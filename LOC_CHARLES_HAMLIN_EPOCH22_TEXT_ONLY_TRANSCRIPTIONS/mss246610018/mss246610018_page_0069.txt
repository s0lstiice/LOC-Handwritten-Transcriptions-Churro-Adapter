Referring to France, Governor Harrison said there was now an
economic crisis there which began only a few weeks ago; that the importation
of gold into France was accompanied by an equivalent increase of currency,
and that much currency was being hoarded; that some claimed that this
increased currency was caused by increased prices, but there was some doubt
as to this; that the only way for a Frenchman to get Bank of France notes
was to present gold; that prices were now advancing very rapidly; that
wholesale prices also increased until about six weeks ago; that the stabilization
of the franc necessitated an increase of prices which were then below
the gold equivalents of other countries; that retail prices reached their
peak about January, 1929, and then fell through April 1930, but that from
April, 1930, there had been a steady increase of prices, which caused an
economic crisis, and that luxury business is practically suspended; that
one dressmaker offered a friend of his one of the best dresses in her
establishment for an equivalent in France of $40, she was so eager to sell
at any cost!
Governor Harrison said there was really no fear in France of war with
Italy; that no one thought of Italy, but everyone feared Germany; that
Mussolini's speeches were purely for home consumption; that a friend of
Mussolini's told him that Mussolini remembered Harrison's statement last
March that Italian bonds could not be placed in America while Italy was
threatening war; that Italy was in a very bad way, as she could not balance
her budget; that Mussolini had generally reduced official salaries, and
reductions of other salaries must soon come, and that this would be a real
test of Fascism; that Italy would like to fund reparation payments coming
to her, but this could not be done at a rate less than 10 or 12%; that it
was generally believed that Italy spoke of this only to bring about a
foreign loan to her.
Parenthetically Governor Harrison told Governor Young that the offer
of the clearing house banks to loan up to 50% of the deposits of the Bank
of the United States cleared the situation very much, but the rate charged —
5% — somewhat dampened the enthusiasm.
Governor Harrison then spoke of Germany. He said that the people
generally believed that the Young plan was the cause of their depression;
that unemployment was increasing very rapidly, and that a socialistic
movement was threatened which might imperil the capitalistic system; that
the party in Germany led by Hugner was not organised, and that he had no
real plan, but that he was supported by the intellectualists and by those
living on fixed incomes.
