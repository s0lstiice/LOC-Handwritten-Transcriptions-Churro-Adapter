Governor Harrison also said that what Germany really wanted was cooperation with the United States in financing countries of Eastern Europe such as Romania, and even South America; that the Germans were angry with France for withdrawing money from Germany, but Governor Harrison pointed out that these withdrawals relatively were so small that they could not explain Germany's loss of capital; that the real trouble was the export of German capital because of fear of Hyner and his policies; that Germany has 6 billion marks in short term currency, or a net of about 5 billions; that the income from Germany's agriculture far exceeded that of industry, but that agricultural mortgages ran up to 8 or 9%, and that agricultural income had very seriously declined.
He said that Mussolini said that Germany was in direct contact with Russia.
Governor Harrison said the object of the central Bank conferences was to discuss world difficulties; that while the Bank of France was glad to help England, yet the Bank of France and the Bank of England had not yet got completely together; that to obtain money from France England would have to issue bonds payable in France, which she was not willing to do.
Governor Harrison said that the Bank of England had been very stupid; that she had plenty of gold more than a year ago, but the coin was of greater fineness than that of other countries; that she paid out coin which France would not accept, but insisted on bars; that this caused a backing up in the Mints, which made great delay.
Governor Harrison explained the call of Governor Noman after he had left for America by saying that he never intended to go to America but simply went over on the steamer to meet Harrison in France and later went with him to Paris, but accomplished nothing.
