Dec 1. Monday
Gen H. said Jefferson was very blue -- and
given up all hope of being assisted S ay --
that he had told Elkins the new Secy
would not take a furnished house of
of the Maney -- that he could not stay
on longer than a few weeks.
But Sat. Dr Laughlin wrote me that he
agreed that it was a fine Italian
house that was behind the street in
N. Y. Times that I had declined the
house; that he could be in town, in a
few days would find out about it.
I called up his hotel to see if he was
there & found that he was; he left
on Sunday without communicating w.
me -- I do not understand this.
Gen H. told me he was at peace that he
would get over that I seemed to be obliged.
He cordially tried to draw me out
but I was very cautious -- I said, however
that no one had offered the house
to me or had even mentioned it -- who
was he?

He also said that if I were appointed the
vacancy should be filled by some one in
the South last as it was not even
presented. I said in the military
event of my return, my successor
should not be taken from N. England.
I think however it could be a mistake
to bill the Board geographically -- it
will tend to make it a long and cold
hunt for a Board. If appointed, I
am inclined to think my successor
should be an Eastern man, not however
from N.E.
Dec 2. Tuesday

talk in the Bd Room. I went in to tell
Gen Glass we were ready and in walking with
him he said -- do you know Mr Morrow, a
Boston bachelor? I said No but I know
a Mr Slavow -- he said -- that is the
name I applied not what I thought
of him. I said he was a fine man.
