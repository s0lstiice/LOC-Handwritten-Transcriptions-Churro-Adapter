I explained to her why I left very early when
at dinner last Tuesday; that I thought
Roy Stanford Baker would want to talk
over with her the collection of all the sketches,
addresses etc of P. S. Wilson which he and
Dr Todd were furnishing, so I left early.
She laughed and said she thought I was
bored with him & his ditzling woodrow, just
married, who called that evening. I annexed
the letter was not so.
She said Baker was much distressed at the
collection of Wilson's addresses etc, which the
Review of Reviews had just published, but that
she told him that there were public addresses
which anyone could print, and that his
collection, with valuable marginal notes etc
would not be injured at all by the Review
of Reviews idiot.
She also said she talked with Baker about
the official life of Paul Wilson and said
she really wanted him to do it but had
not made up her mind, she asked him if
he would be willing to collaborate with
anyone else, but he said no.
She said she went over their house last
Thursday night when I arrived.

I told her I was making Baker 3 volume books,
woodrow Wilson and World settlement, and was
very much informed with it, and that I
believe there was no one as well qualified as
he to do this work; that I thought the
ideal solution would be to have some one
of more distinctly literary ability and business
of view-point, say a man like Blair Perry,
collaborate with Baker.
She said this was her feeling also, and that
she was carefully pondering over the matter.
She asked about Bertie's plans and I told
her she had not made up her mind
but that I did not want her to leave a
house they would, but to leave a friend
not which she sadly needed; that she might
go to Bermuda.
She at once said, "Why can not Bertie come
and spend the winter with me; I
should so love to have him!"
I thanked her warmly but said this would
not be possible. Her kindness was really
touching.
