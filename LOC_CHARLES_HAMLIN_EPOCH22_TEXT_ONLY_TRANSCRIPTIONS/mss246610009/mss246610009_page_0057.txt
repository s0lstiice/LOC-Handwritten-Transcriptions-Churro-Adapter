W.C. demanded to know why, or not the
WBoud or Bumps were to make simultaneous
examinations of State Members' banks as ordered
by the Board some time ago.
James said the Calif. subject was now examined
the Bill of Italy simultaneously and our
Examiners were telling & watching to see if
the State did it properly; that this would
not be finished for some months, but
that, as regards California, at least he
was content to await their report.
Some question was raised as to which, and all
agreed that the W. Press should lay to reach
some agreement with the State on a better
examination, before we took action.

Nov 4. Tuesday, Election day.
I voted the straight Democratic ticket, for
Faulk, Curley and Walsh.
Bertier voted the straight ticket except that she
voted for Gillette for Senator.
I voted on the constitutional amendment:- against abolishing
the Wade Davis Act as a State measure; against the
tax on gasoline; in favor of allowing women to
hold office in Illinois; in favor of the child labor
amendment.
The vote on 1st was:- Coolidge 449; Davis 52; La
Follette 11. In Governor, Sullivan 436; Curley 56.
In Senator, Gillette 382; Walsh 72.
Gillette fell behind Coolidge 67 votes, and thus
explains Walsh's lead of 20 votes over Davis.
Incidentally the Coolidge and Sullivan ballances
number Gillette.
I at once sent following telegram to Davis, refusing
Bertier name:-
"Massachusetts vote:- Coolidge 449; Davis 52;
La Follette 11. Your vote in the largest
democratic vote for a generation, the normal
democratic vote is about 20."
H.H. Hamlin
