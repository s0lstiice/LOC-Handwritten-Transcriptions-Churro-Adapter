Dr. C. J. Smith Esq.
Dear Sir,
Mr. Vail and myself as proprietors of
the Telegraph in common with yourself have given to
the Memoranda of agreement which you handed in yesterday,
such consideration as the limited time we had at command
would allow. And the better to understand its legal
bearings, we took council of a legal gentleman in Baltimore
M. Latrobe, who has promised to send down by the
morning train a draft of such a memorandum as will
embody our views. A transaction of this kind we do not wish
to act upon hastily, and the press of other engagements at a time
when what we have to do must be done quickly, compels us to
propose some simple mode of promoting our general interests
without interfering more than is necessary with our individual
preferences. I have a great repugnance to the management of
such a contract as is proposed, even in its most unobjectionable
form, and while I would accord to enter into such contracts
with the Govt. as you and it can for the mutual benefit of the
proprietors arrange, we wish ourselves to be free from its
moral and as well as pecuniary responsibilities. Now can not this be brought about by the adoption of one
of the following propositions.
