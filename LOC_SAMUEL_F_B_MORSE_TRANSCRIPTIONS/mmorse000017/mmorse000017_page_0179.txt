have at all affected me with that proportion of

the officers named to secure
me from Police justice, or death of the
Board of Aldermen. The former being present,
several letters from both members of Congress and
the most respectable of our citizens, will be handed
the members elect of the Common Council; and if
it is perfectly agreeable, I would be pleased with one from
you, as to my capabilities and my unswerving allegiance
and sacrifices in the party. I would be glad of this
in receipt, to them a favor of the Aldermen elect. You
favor me this particular with gratitude, and you may make
it as strong as you please.
I remain, truly, your friend
J.D. Chapin
