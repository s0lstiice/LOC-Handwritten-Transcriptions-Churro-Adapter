Washington May 7th 1844
Dear Sidney,
I have a moment just before retiring to
bed very much fatigued, to write a few lines.
This morning my young friend Miss Annie G.
Elsworth left Washington with her cousin Mr.
Goodrich Smith for New York to pass a week or
two in the city. She will be at Mr. Manning's
No. 107 Second Avenue, 4 doors above 5th Street
I gave her a letter to Sister Catherine, and for my
sake and her father's & mother's I wish you and
Sister would become acquainted with her, and
particularly my Susan if she arrives in New York
soon, as I expect she will be with you in a day or
two at latest.* Let me know when Susan and
the Charles arrive, for if they come within the
next fortnight, I think I can contrive to run
on and pay a visit of two or three days, unless
my marplot Smith should prevent again, as he
is likely to do if he comes on here. As yet there
is no settlement of that matter, and he seems de-
termined (in his nos.) to be as ugly as he can and
defeat all application for an appropriation if
I am to have the management of it; he chases
like a wild bear, but when he finds he can effect
nothing by such a temper, self interest may often
him into terms. — You will see by the papers
I had a letter from Susan yesterday in which she says she
shall be in New York by willin' to leave Washington, and it is
your four weeks.
