Illinois City, May 29, 1844
My dear Mr.
I have by the papers of this morning that
your magnetic telegraph is now in successful operation
between Washington and Chicago. I hasten to acquaint
you on occasion of your complete success in this most
inpatient and interesting invention, and to add the
expression of my warmest wishes that what has been so
succcessfully commenced, may be continued by the govern-
ment, until the main lines of our railroads throughout the
lands may be applied with the telegraph. If so I hope it
may give you an interesting and profitable employment
for years to come.
We have recently commenced knocking at no
54 Bond St. a short distance from Mrs. Bruce, who
will remain at 35 Broadway. And as you must be
fagged out with your duties anxieties and labour, and
ought to have a little relaxation, I beg that you
will pack your trunk and make an visit for as
many weeks as you may find it agreeable. Mrs B.
will probably drain half of your time, but that is our
little between ourselves.
I sent your book according to the request of
the N. A. Academy to N.York, and much to my Surprise
a few weeks afterwards received it back again.
