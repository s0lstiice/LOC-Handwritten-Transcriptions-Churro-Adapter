Extract. copy of letter to Vail Wash June 1st 1844.
Dear Vail;
I have my hands full in pressing
matters for Congress, and find it hard to get away. I will see
about the woodcuts you speak of, if I can but I may not
be able to get them. As to the preparation of a pamphlet
I think well of it, but it ought to well done, and require
more leisure for that purpose than I have to spare. It will
do no harm to let people wait awhile. The interest is not
likely to decrease.
In haste Yrs truly as ever
L. B. Morse
Copy New York March 21st 1843. From W.Vail.
Prof. Morse, As an assistant in the Telegraphic experiment Con-
templated by the Act of Congress lately passed, I can superin-
tend and procure the making of the instruments complete
according to your direction, namely, The Register, the Corres-
pondents, with their magnets, the Batteries, the Reels and the
paper and will attend to the procuring of the Acids, the Ink
and the preparation of the various Stations. I will assist in
filling the tubes with wire and the resinous Coating, and I
will devote my whole time and attention to the business so
as to secure a favorable result and Should you wish to de-
volve upon me any other business connected with the Telegraph
I will cheerfully undertake it.
$3.00 per day with travelling expenses I shall deem a Satisfac-
tion salary.
A. V.
1843. March 31st. I approved W.Vail of his appointment at $1,000.
per Ann. Salary.
