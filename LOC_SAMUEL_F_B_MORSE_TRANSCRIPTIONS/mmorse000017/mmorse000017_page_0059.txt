Washington Feb 15 1844
Dear Sir
I regret what S did not happen
to beat my lodging, when you called the other
day, both because it would have given me pleas-
ure to meet you, and because S wished to con-
duct you in relation to a matter of some
public interest, which has been referred to
me for examination. S called at the
telegraph office this morning after you left
and, but in inquiring for you learned that
you had left the city. The subject on
which S wished to ask your opinion is this,
Mr. Kening of New York, late a paymaster
in the army, issues the government about
$35,000 of which not more than $1000 is
subject to be sevance. By way of additional
security, he has assigned to the U. States
fifteen or twenty pictures lately exhibited
in a few days in the National at the
Capital, and now at the Patent Office.
