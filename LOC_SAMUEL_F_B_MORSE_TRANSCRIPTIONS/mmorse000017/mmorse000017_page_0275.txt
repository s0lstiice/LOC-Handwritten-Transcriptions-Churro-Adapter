Dear Sidnus,
You will see by the Papers, how
great success has attended the first efforts of
the Telegraph. That sentence of Annie Ellsworth's was divinely intited, for it is in my thoughts
day and night, "What hath God wrought." It is his
work, and he alone could have carried me thus
far through all my trials, and enabled me
to triumph over the obstacles physical and
moral which opposed me; Not unto us, not
unto us, but to thy name O Lord be all the
praise! — I begin to fear now the effects of public fame, lest it should kindle that pride of heart
and self sufficiency, which dwells in my own, as
well as in others' hearts and which is so ready to be inflamed by the slightest spark of praise. I do indeed feel gratified, but I rejoice with fear, and I desire that a sense of dependence upon and increased obligation to the
Grain of every good and perfect gift, may keep me humble, and circumspect.
