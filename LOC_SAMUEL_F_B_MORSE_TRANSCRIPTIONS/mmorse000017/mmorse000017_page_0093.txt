Hon. Geo. P. Marsh
March 6th 1844
Dear Sir,
In compliance with your request that I would give you
my opinion of the pictures recently placed in the Patent Office
I would say that I have this day examined them and find none of
my notes, as they were taken on the spot, I had no Catalogue, and
No. 1. is a picture of some merit but of its originality I could not
judge from the distance I was compelled to see it.
No. 2. Christ standing a good copy.
No. 3 has some good points in color, --
No. 4. Dead Christ, apparently a good copy.
No. 5 Adoration of the Magi, a fair copy.
No. 6. A poor thing.
No. 7. Some good points about this landscape, freely touched
with a full pencil, but ostentations in the display of
this technical excellence.
No. 8. The best picture in the whole collection; full of sunlight,
rich and silver-toned; architecture well painted, the
shipping Claudeslike, but the Sky not Claude.
No. 9. a fair copy, I believe from Nouvermans
No. 10 of the same character as No. 9.
No. 11. A poor thing
No. 12. Christ in the Garden, an indifferent work.
No. 13. A Magdalene, mediocre. -- a copy
No. 14 St Sebastian, a very fair picture, but unequal in the
execution, and bears evidence of its being a copy.
No. 15. Winter scene, good, but not remarkably so.
As a collection it is very indifferent, No. 8 is the only picture
I should select for my collection.
I have been very free, in condemning, Exce ption ly, but to be honest
I cannot avoid it.
Believe, me dear Sir,
With sincere respect
Y. O. B. Sent.
Sam. B. B. Mordy
Washington, March 6th 1844.
