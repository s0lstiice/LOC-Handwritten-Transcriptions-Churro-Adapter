Amico carissimo.
New York May 15. 1844.
Next September I shall be, I hope, at Milan. The scientific Congress of
Italy will meet there on the 12th of that month. I intend to present to it
an Account of the Sentimentary System of the U.S. if you, dear
Professor, are willing to help me, I will lay before it also an account
of your wonderful Telegraph. I think that the description of the
process which led you to the application of the magnetic power to
telegraphic purposes, and of the telegraph itself, would prove highly
interesting and acceptable to my countrymen. I am willing to do my
utmost in this business, but my utmost will amount to very little
indeed: for I have no knowledge of the scientific principles of
language of your electromagnetism, a mechanism. If you can not
spare time enough to draw up a full account, tell me where
I may find the materials out of which a competent person
could make one, & give me the necessary direction.
I write, Professor, because I am not certain whether I shall be able
to come to Washington to see you, before my departure. I am
trying now to collect here money enough to begin the business
of the Christian Alliance; then I shall visit for the same
object New-Haven, Hartford, Boston, & Providence. I have
strong hopes to succeed in this pecuniary mission, & I am
certain that the experiment will be gratifying to all our
friends.
