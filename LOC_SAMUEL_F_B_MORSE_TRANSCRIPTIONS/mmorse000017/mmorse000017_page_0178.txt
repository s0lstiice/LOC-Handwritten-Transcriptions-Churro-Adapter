New York April 26th 1844
My Dear Sir.
The first thought which occurred to me on the morning after our late
mid- pol election was the pleasure with which
you would hear the results of that election.
During the campaign, also, your name was
the subject of frequent mention among our
old and tried friends; and I often endeavored
to induce the Judicating Committee, late in
the month, to say that some of the men of the
day were not such as to recognize the long
arduous and faithful services of the best friends
of the principles at issue; and the recent loss born
respecting those friends having told the National
American Party etc! It is true, no respectable
or high minded person has thus demanded him
self, but the story having been put in circulation
by Byett and one or two others, it was in
justification eliculated to our prejudice. It was
also said that I had written a letter from
Albany stating that I had changed my sentiments
in relation to American principles. This
of course, was as malicious as it was false.

they affect with their poison, have you yourself
ever read the history of the party. But since this
