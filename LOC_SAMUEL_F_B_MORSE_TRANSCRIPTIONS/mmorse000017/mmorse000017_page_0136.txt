Washington March 29, 1844
Dr Lin
The following is an extract from a letter which I received from Mr H. C. Smith on the subject of his claim for damage on the Bartlett Contract, that I refer to in a conversation with you this morning.
"I am willing at any time to refer the whole question to the two Comptrollers of the Treasury, and abide by their report as to the damages which ought to be allowed on the contract, and thus withdraw all further cause of angry feelings between all parties. If Morse and the Secretary will agree to refer the whole question over to Judge Parris (2d Comptroller) and Comptroller McCallough (1st Comptroller) both are honest men impartial judges of the law and the fact, and you may so say to Prof. Morse - I will not open my mouth against any man they shall make on a proper hearing of the case - In that event, the petition and all the papers can be formally withdrawn from the Senate, and when the award, if in favor of the contract, shall be made, the Secretary if he does not feel authorized to pay the amount, shall make a report to Congress of the amount due, if he will do this and attend to it immediately, and if Prof. Morse and myself cannot be found, we will cease to be enemies. Can any proposition be fairer? I will moreover say, that I will consent that Prof. Morse shall have full liberty to use any letter, however private or confidential, I ever wrote him touching the contract, and not consider such use any breach of confidence, for I am so unacquainted with any wrong, or shadow of dishonour in the matter that I will impose no restraint upon him."
I hope this may result in a satisfactory adjustment of all differences.
Yours Respectfully
E. Correll
Prof. S. F. P. Morse
