New York June 1st 1854
My Dear Sir
It is now something since I have heard from
you directly though I hear from your works through the public
prints that you are pushing the Telegraph more vigorously.
The impression in N.Y. is very strong that it will be extended until
it shall spread every important key town in the U.S. My Brother
has just returned from Boston and tells me that it was the most
frequent topic of conversation wherever he went.
It seems you have finally got to Baltimore. Did the appropriation
hold out and what is the condition of the enterprise before Congress?
that is, what sort of a proposition have you made, and what pro-
gress has it made in Congress? I am the more anxious to know as
I have recently received a lengthy communication from Mr. Riggs
attempting to vindicate his own acts relative to the Telegraph and
showing wherein he blames you. I at first thought I would include
to you the communication but on reflection decided otherwise. I will give
in brief his complaint. It is that 1. you will not come to any terms
to settle his claim against the government in the trenching contracts
2. you will not determine even on the mode by which you may
ascertain the amount of any payment which he the contractor ought to
receive and which you as Superintendent ought to apply for
3d. that you have referred him over to the Sec'y of the Treasury for
payment and he cannot to his fault – that the Secretary, without notifying
Mr S. had decided on the case and reported it to government.
