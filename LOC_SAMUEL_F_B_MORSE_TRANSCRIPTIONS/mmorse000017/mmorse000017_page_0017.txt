Philadelphia Feb 2nd 1844
Rf F. B. Moore Superintendent of
Sic.
Sometime since received from you a
letter enclosing a draft on your brother for $30. some cents for
the balance due me on acct of salary as Assistant Superintendent.
Previously to the receipt of that letter I had written to you for the last
deal me for my board in Washington during the winter of 1842-43.
which you had promised to pay me. I have waited some time in expectation
of hearing from you on the subject, but have as yet had no answer
to my last letter, as it is possible that you may have in the hurry of your
business had it pass from your mind, I have concluded to write to you again
on the subject. I trust you will see the necessity to me at least of not
to yourself of attending to it. I am unwilling to suppose that the paragraph
in your letter to me offered to done, in which you put a case where
your firing have been inflicted the obligation of gratitude cannot
have been intended by you to mean that you did not intend to comply with
your promise reflectively made to me, because I believe you to be a Christian
or gentleman, I cannot for a moment think that you would want to
act as a substitute to about the fulfillment of your becoming obligations.

enough to know that gratitude is a very feeble claim to one easily satisfied
where the disposition exists to do so, but I do ask from you as a matter
