Washington City May 2d 1839.
My dear Sir.
And you are back again at last?—
I want to see you more than old the mail, I have a forge
and hut for three steel plates and as many bits of canvas,
that I am old to put now I would move myself along
side of you for an hour before many days— Can't
you come down this way?— I knew all about you
by the papers— so much for being distinguished— but do
you get up one morning fine instead of four hours be-
fore day and write me a letter or I'll complain to
the Pope against you— Tell me first all I don't
know (3 that all about the Thunder Lightning machine)
about yourself— What have you been about in the
way of paint etc etc— Tell me about Bellin, that
good old soul— did you see him?— How does he
thrive?— I began a letter to him tonight but laid it
by until I could know if you could tell me any things of
his whereabouts— I have not heard from him for a year
and a year can work odd changes as I've well known
from letter experience—
How goes on the N.A. this season, they
haven't even sent me a Catalogue— "out of sight out
