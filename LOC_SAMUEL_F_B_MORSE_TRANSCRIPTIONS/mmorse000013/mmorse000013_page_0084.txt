Baltimore, Nov. 14. 58.
My dear Sir,
You must have thought me very impossible,
or very impossible to an act of kindness from myself having
long since obliged, to your late letter which asked an inter-
duction to Mr Wharton. But the truth is, I have had an
interview with him every morning here, and I have not
seen him since, and I have only this moment
opportunity to write a few lines by way of apology for a letter.
It was a very kind thought in you to address that to
introduction to our ambassador here, and I thank you
ample for them, -- we had indeed, and their introduction from
President Linney, but as that was written nearly three years
since and when Mr Wharton was Chargé d'Affaires, your
came into excellent play. Mr Wharton has been
only about two days since his return from Paris; on leave
however, and the pleasure of making him acquaintance,
Mr Calhoun is highly pleased with Mr Wharton, and can
say nothing more complimentary of his conduct than
that he is to me, in many respects, a pleasing souvenir of
any country Mr Finley wrote, and indeed his time is so little given
