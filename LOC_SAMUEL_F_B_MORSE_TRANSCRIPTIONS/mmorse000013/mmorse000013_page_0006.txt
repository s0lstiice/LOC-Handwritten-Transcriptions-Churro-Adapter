Dear Sir
The undersigned; Academicians of the Royal Academy
of Design, having learned with sorrow, your views, with regard to the
proceedings of the Academy, at their late annual Meeting, are anxious
to remove any sinister impressions or misconstruction, with regard to our
motive and intentions; and at the same time, being actuated
by feelings of the greatest respect and regard, beg leave to address
you in this manner, for the purpose of expressing our regret, that we
should appear in so unfavorable a light.
We disclaim any intention of disrespect to yourself or to any of the gentlemen who were not
re-elected; on the contrary, we have the greatest regard for their
private characters, and are perfectly sensible of the services they have
done, and the great services they have rendered to the Academy.
Notwithstanding, we feel the impression, that it would better promote the welfare of the Institution, by having changes from
time to time in the Council, thereby encouraging the younger members,
by allowing them to participate in the management of the Academy, and
stimulating them to greater exertions for distinction.
It has been objected to our proceedings that we have removed
without giving them any intimation of what we were about to do.
In reply to this objection, we beg leave to state, that we have
removed no one; but merely elected some new members to
supply the places of those whose term of service expires in July.
And as for the manner, we had no precedent for doing otherwise.

the reasons which have had an influence in bringing about
the change in Council; but merely to state in this manner, some
