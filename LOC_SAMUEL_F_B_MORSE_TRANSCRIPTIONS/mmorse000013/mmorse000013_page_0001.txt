NY University April 24. 1838
United Rail way
Colin.
I will in greatest haste first to say that the
boxes have safely arrived, & we shall proceed immediately to examine into the difficulties which have
troubled you, but about which we suppose there is no serious space. I received a letter from Mr Smith in
which he regrets very much not having the circular Portmule, but as you are proceeding to make it, & as we
shall so in one of the ordinary packets, you may get it done I still think, by the 20th of May, at which time
you will perhaps have the opportunity of sending it by one of the rapid steamships in time for our operations
in London. I will see about the light metal, in season for you would; if you can possibly get the Circular
Portmule completed before we go, it will be a great convenience, not to say an indispensable matter, for
I have just learned so much of Wheatstone's Telegraph as to be pretty well persuaded that my superiority
over him will be made evident, more by the rapidity with which I can make the fabricule work than in
almost any other particular. Mr Smith will probably be here tomorrow, & I shall then be able to send you a report, if not before, as I expect some copies to day. I am sorry when I enter into business matters, to have my regards to him & family & boys
to take a mother in great love but as ever
