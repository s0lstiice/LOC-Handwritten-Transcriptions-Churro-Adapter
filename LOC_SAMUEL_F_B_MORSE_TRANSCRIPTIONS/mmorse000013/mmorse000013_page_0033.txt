14, Bridge Place,
July 12, 1868.
To John Campbell,
The Majesty's Attorney General,
Dear Sir:
I am strongly distressed that in my own
opinion at the interview of this morning in the
subject of my application for a Patent for an Ele-
tric Telegraph certain points in my case, of ma-
tterial importance, were misrepresented by me, or
were misrepresented by the honorable Attorney
General, from the imperfect manner in which
I presented them. And although it may seem
improper for me to apply to the Attorney General
to consider the subject in a more intelligible
form, after we hearing has been had of the
case, inasmuch as an appeal is open to another
tribunal, presuming, nevertheless, that it will
be the desire of all interested to have matter
true and correct, as well as this preliminary
stage as at any other time, I respectfully refer
the honorable Attorney General's attention to the
following considerations:
1st. I intended to pursue my application
in the opinion that my invention intends to employ
one of different modes of accomplishing a particular
result.
2nd. I understand the law to be settled,
that patents may be granted for any number of
modes, or means for accomplishing any desired
end, provided there is a distinct & essential differ-
ence in each of the modes or means in being susceptible
of, of course, that end. It will be seen.
