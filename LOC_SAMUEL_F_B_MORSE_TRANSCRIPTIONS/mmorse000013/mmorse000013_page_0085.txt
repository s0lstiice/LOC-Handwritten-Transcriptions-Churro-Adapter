as I do how much of the benefit of foreign trade I have
by my individual life, that cannot come again to myself
to reap those fruits, which I am now obliged to pass by
unattainable. But for what little progress, at the best, in this
life is sufficient, and how comforting the reflection, that
if we are true to our fellow men, and do make known all
the deficiencies of our present limited and condition state
will be filled up by the full appreciation of souls free
in all their instincts, and developing themselves everywhere
equally!
Disjoin, for your own sake and for our country's sake, that
your telegraph invention is so highly appreciated; as it
will probably detain you in Paris during the winter. I hope
we may yet have the pleasure of seeing them company on
the voyage home, but you will let me hear more particular
details about your plans for the spring? With devotion
to you but regards to your, and I must give them hearty
with the assurance that I am, yours affectionate
Ever, J. Schuyler
