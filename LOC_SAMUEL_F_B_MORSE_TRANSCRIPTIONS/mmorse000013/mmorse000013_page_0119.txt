In this same year Ampere who was ignorant of the
labor of Soemmerring in making known to France for the
first time the discovery of Deville upon the movement of
the magnetic needle under the influence of the electric current, divided with that precision which characterized his
scientific talent to make it serve the purpose of constructing
electric telegraphs, and in page 19 of his memoir he thus resolves the problem. — "As many magnetic needles as there
are letters of the alphabet," says he, "which may be put in
action by conductors which may be made to communicate
somewhat with the battery by means of keys which may be
tricked at pleasure would be able to give plan to a complete telegraph which would transcend all distance and would be as
effective as a writing or a word to transmit thought."
