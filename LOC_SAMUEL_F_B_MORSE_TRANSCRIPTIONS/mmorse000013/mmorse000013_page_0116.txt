Academy of Industry
Agricultural, Manufactural and Commercial
Report
made to the Committee of arts and manufactures by
M. Odolant Desnos
Secretary of this Committee upon the
Electric Magnetic Telegraph of
M. Morse
Professor in the University of N.Y.
Mr. Morse, professor in the University of New-York,
having invited, during his stay in France, the Academy of
Industry to do him the favor of paying its judgment on
the merit of the electromagnet, which he has duly
examined, we have the honor to present to you the following report.
The application of electric currents to the transmission of thought being at
present the object of the researches of a few
brilliant men, and among them, believe it not, Mr. Morse, a perfect solution of the problem, we intend to
examine the application of the electromagnet upon the transmission of thought, and then we will point out to you the character of the telegraph of Mr. Morse, the principles upon which it is based, and the advantages or disadvantages which its adoption or omission would involve. The first idea of establishing a correspondence between
different points of the globe, though very distant from each other by means of electricity, was communicated to Franklin, who transmitted this fluid by means of a metallic wire from San Juan miles distant. The celebrated musician Benten is said to have had this same idea towards the year 1780.
