Under date of August 12, Mr. Merse
New York August 15th 1839
again writes to Mr Smith:
My dear Sir
I received yours of the 2nd inst. and the
paper accompanying it containing the notice of Mr
Chamberlain. I had previously been assured that
my forebodings were true in regard to his fate. I
sent you a New York Observer a few days ago giving a
more full account of him which I hope you
have received.
We shall hear double lies from Mr
Brown, when he returns, what was done by Mr C.
in regard to the Telegraph in the East. Our enterprise
abroad is declined to give us anxiety if not to end in
disappointment. I have just received a letter from
Mr Amyott who was to have been my companion to Rus-
sia, and leave from him the unwelcome news that the
Emperor has decided against the Telegraph. I
have been expecting a letter from the Baron Meyen-
dorff every day, for Mr Amyot informs me that he in-
tended writing me. The Emperors objections are, it seems,
that "malveillance can easily interrupt the communica-
tion". Mr Amyot scouts the idea and writes that he
refuted the objection to the satisfaction of the Baron
who, indeed, did not need the reputation for himself for
that whole matter was fully discussed between us when
