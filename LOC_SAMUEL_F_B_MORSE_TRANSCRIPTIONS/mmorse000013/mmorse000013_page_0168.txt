must abide the consequences. I shall have done all I feel
in conscience bound to do to prevent litigation. My brother
Sidney E. Morse of New York has the charge of this matter and
unless the terms herein stated are complied with, he will at
once proceed to publish my defence from the false charges
of that paragraph. Your pride will perhaps revolt at this
Proposition but I beg you to let prudence in this case take the
precedence of pride. The following is the disclaimer which
you must sign
"Having seen a paragraph in the Boston Post of
1839, in which the Electro Magnetic Telegraph
"invented by W. Samuel F. B. Morse of New York is erroneously
"claimed for me as my invention, I therefore, the subscriber C. J.
"Jackson of Boston do by this instrument disavow any claim
"whatever, either to the "first idea," or "entire invention," or to be
"a participator in any sense in the said Electro Magnetic Telegraph
"either as inventor or proprietor. I hereby further declare that
"my claim that I have heretofore preferred either in private
"or public has arisen from mistake on my part, and from
"a misconception of the nature of the invention of the said
"Sam'l F. B. Morse."
C. J. Jackson.
