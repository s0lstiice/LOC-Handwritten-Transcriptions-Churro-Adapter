Paris Feb 213th 1839
Hon F.O. Smith
Dear Sir
I have been wholly occu-
pied for the last week in copying out the correspond-
ence, and other documents to defend myself against
the infamous attack of Dr Jackson, notice of which
my brother sent me. I have sent it this day by D-
Mitchell who sails in the Ville de Lyon on the 16th the
packet that takes this; I have sent a letter to Dr
Jackson calling on him to save his character by a
total disclaimer of his presumptuous claim, with
in one week from the receipt of the letter, and giving
him the plea of "mistake" and "misconception of my
invention" by which he may retreat. If he fails
to de thes I have requested my brother to put
lik immediately my defence, in which I give a
history of the invention, the correspondence between
Dr Jackson & Myself, and close with the letters of
Gen. M. Reeves, W. Fisher of Phila and Capt. Sill.
I cannot conceive of such infatuation as has pos-
sessed that man. He can scarcely be deceived; So
must be his consummate self conceit that deceives
him, if he is deceived. But this cannot be, he knows
he has no title whatever to a single unit of any
kind in the matter. It is pure wickedness.
