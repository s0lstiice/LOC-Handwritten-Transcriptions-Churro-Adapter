Paris, (France) Feb'y 13. 1839.
To Dr. Charles T. Jackson
Sir I have just been shown a paragraph
in the Boston Post copied into the NewYork Courier &
Enquirer of Jan'y. 12th. 1839, which cannot have escaped
your notice, since it claims for you, the "first idea" and
"entire invention," of the Electro Magnetic Telegraph which
I have claimed for mine. Knowing as you must know
the utter groundlessness of the claim made for you to own
the slightest participation in this invention of mine, I hope
that long before this reaches you, you have disabused the
public mind, so erroneously influenced by the false and
injurious representations of that paragraph.— If you
have not, I wish to give you an opportunity to es-
cape the exposure which in self defence I shall be com-
pelled to make of the whole matter, an exposure which
however you may deceive yourself to the contrary will
disgrace you before the public.— For your family's
sake, and for your friends sake. I would wish to spare
you this mortification; an indulgence on my part, which
you must be conscious you do not deserve. There is one
chance remaining for you to escape this exposure. If on
the receipt of this you shall immediately cause the
