19. C.M., to J. F.H.M.
New York Feb. 24th 1859
Dear Brother
Last year has been a year
of very great expense, an immense sum
having been required for the new buildings; while it has been a year of little
income from rents This year, if we meet
with no disaster, the tide will begin
to turn, and it would not be surprising, if the income from rents and the
paper in three years enabled us in time
to pay all debt of every description,
mortgaged and floating debt, and left
us an unencumbered property (real
estate and the paper) yielding an income of $20,000 per annum. I say
this to encourage you when you feel de-
pressed in view of pecuniary matters;
for if you fail to realize enough for
your support from your own projects
you may calculate confidently, if I am
succesful, that I will make up the defi-
cency I shall feel very poor and stingy
