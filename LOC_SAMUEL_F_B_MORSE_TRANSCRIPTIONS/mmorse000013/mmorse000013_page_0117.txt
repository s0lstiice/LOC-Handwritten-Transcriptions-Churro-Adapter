Chappes, who after having perfected the project of the philosopher
Amontons, constructed their mechanism by visible signals, being able
to show 196 configurations. It was on the 24 August 1793 that the
Convention gave them an order to establish a Telegraphic line
of this kind from Paris to Lille; so that in spite of the besieging
army which surrounded that city, the orders of the Committee of
Ruble safety could be transmitted with the rapidity of light, and
accomplished in two or three minutes these 60 leagues of
distance. This happy application of the mechanic Telegraph of
the brothers Chappes caused the German Reeiser, to conceive
and to give a plan in 1794 of an electric Telegraph, mounted
by the ordinary electric machine. This plan which may be found
in the "Majasin de Voigt," presents a table of glass upon which
are engraved some metallic characters representing the letters of the
alphabet and at each of these letters a tube of glass, a wire which draws a spark from each character
as soon as its other extremity receives from the machine a sensible
quantity of electricity.
In the same collection it is found that in 1798 the Doctor
constructed a Telegraph altogether analogous in Spain and
that the Infant don Antonio saw it act, and made use of it
to transmit a piece of news to a very great distance.
The end of this apparatus was thus to take advantage
of the speed with which the electric spark transmits itself, a speed
which it is believed equals that of light, which traverses at
least 81,000, leagues in a second, and to make it move with
the rapidity from a point of departure to a point of arrival
very distant making it follow a metallic conductor necessarily
insulated.
