Dear Brother,
Your letter by the Great Western, for myself & Susan, was
duly received, and we both return answer by the same ship, which sail's tomorrow
I can easily conceive that your situation in Paris, away from friends & family,
must be a trying one, and am sorry that it has not often been cheered by
letters from those who cherish the recollection of you on this side of the water. I am
sensible that I am very deficient as a correspondent (or rather, am not
corresponding) with absent friends; but you must not lay all the fault
on me this time. When you went away, you expected to be absent but 3
or 4 months, & it seemed so uncertain whether a letter, if written would
reach Europe before you embarked that I had not much encouragement to write.
Afterwards, you announced that you would be on your return before
letters could reach you, & that we need not write. It was then several
weeks before we heard that you had changed your plans, & determined
to remain. During all this time, the suspense & uncertainty kept me
from writing. I have written, however, certainly two & I think three
if not four letters, this being either the fourth or fifth. Susan has written
quite a number, and has you so constantly in number mind, that I
am confident she has not been at all remorse; certainly not, since
you informed us that you were to remain in Paris during the
winter.
The boys are still at Hudson, with their uncle Richard, whose
health I suspect is somewhat improved, but not yet radically restored.
I have been full of cares & perplexities of late, and sometimes they
have been almost too much to bear. The new buildings on the corner have
been under roof the for three months, & during the winter, I have been slowly
finishing inside. The walls stand well, remarkably well, & Judicious building,
