My Dear Morse
Though it is only a short time since I saw you
there are several matters that indeed I wish to write - I hope you
may be not made his own apology you will be kind enough to find me for your friends - (to the first place for
begin self-interest) I am again engaged in matters that will require both time of the Judicial service - Do not know when
it will be to you - If you have not passed away your
Brother can you conveniently let me have the situation that
you before furnished me with? If you can please to leave it
in such condition as I am at the City, till Duncan
is from or returning when I can get it - If you are on the eve
of departure I know that your time is precious. In this matter
cannot be done easily, leave it and me - There is one
thing that has weighed heavily on my mind since
I saw you, & although writing to you on the subject is
only troubling without the possibility of being serviceable
find it difficult to deny myself the expression of my satisfaction
with disappointment at the result of the last Annual Meeting
of the Academy - I received a letter from his Excellency yesterday
which he says he had to give, expressing my opinion of the
Black Ball -- He speaks with his usual warmth on the
subject, but not more warmly than I feel in addressing to the
League Black Ball must go with him. I see no plausible
reason for a change at present, & many things one as soon as
change -- I do not think the Academy that I
should wish in it as Academic officer only one as
