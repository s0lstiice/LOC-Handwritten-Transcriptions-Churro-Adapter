N.Y. Waverly Place N.Y. Feb 21st 1839
Prof. Moser,
My Dear Sir,
I regret to hear by your letters to
your daughter and by the Southwestern arriving here about
the 16th inst that you have not read my letters of Jan 27th
which I have written two besides what I have written in
the same letters (2) three) of Jan 27th during the winter
but I suppose you have read the information through
other channels. Mr Smith is managing the telegraph in
Washington but I can give no idea of what but
will be the result. Dr Jackson has him silent since
his first blast claiming the sole honor of the telegraph
of which you have I presume already sufficient
information. Your brother wrote some weeks ago a
good refutation of the article by Jackson in the Boston
Post of which I copy was sent in one of your
letters. Since which Dr Jackson has said nothing
in the papers. The piece by Sidney E. — required the
public to suspend judgment till your return when they
should be satisfied with regard to the whole matter. Let
me at the same time that you had all the evi-
dence in your favor and that in writing that
Dr Jackson claims on his side.
The University has gone through many
changes since you left. The Chancellor
has resigned at Cadiz but it was a hard death
but the Council of Siciliens compelled him to
and they have appointed Mr Frelinghuysen
of N.Y. to fill his place. He requires that a
salon of $30000 in Chancellor's hands and a pledge
of 100,000 dollars of the debt to be liquidated before
he will accept. Already $20,000 has been collected
toward it but it will take him another $80,000 will be obtained
