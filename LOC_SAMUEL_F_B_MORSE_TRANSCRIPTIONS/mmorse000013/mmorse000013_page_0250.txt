New York Apr 26th 1839.
F. O. J. Smith Esq
My dear Sir,
Iake the first mo-
ment of rest from the fatigues of my business engage
to apprize you of my arrival yesterday in the Hotel Weiler.
The day before I left Paris I concluded the arrange
ment with the Russian Government through the Baron
Meyen deff, so far as he had power, and shall expect
through the Russian Ministers the answer of Govt
at St. Petersburg by the 10th of May. There are some
points different from those I believe I sketched in
my letter to you of Feb 22d. The second interview the
Baron believed that he had limited the compensation
"half the economy," to 3 years, instead of 5, as both
Mr Amyot and myself understood him to say. He
seemed a little troubled at this, and reproached him
self for not pulling it down in writing at the time,
for he had written 3 years to his Govt and it was too
late to rectify the matter; but he observed that if I
was successful, I might safely rely on the liberal
disposition of the Emperor. It is limited also to the
route from St. Petersburg to Warsaw 800 Miles. I
wish much to see you and with as little delay as
possible, for the time is very limited on account of
the season of the year in which it is necessary to be
in St. Petersburg in order to labor at all. I
