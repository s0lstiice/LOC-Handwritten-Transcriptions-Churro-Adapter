65a
Paris Oct 1, 1835.
My dear brother.
It is a long time since I had written you
and in my last I led you to expect that about this
time both Richard and myself would be on the
water on our return. I have not wished to raise
expectations in regard to my Telegraphic
matters, for I felt and still feel in this this
world of disappointments, that flattering
appearances should not prevent us from
setting bounds to vainqueur expectations.
Perhaps I have erred on the other
side, and have fallen into distrust which
Providence in a thousand ways was calling
for my confidence and gratitude. Certainly,
now everything looks successful, yet as nothing
is consummated I hold myself loose and keep
prepared for disappointment. I will tell you
how matters stand. I have obtained a patent
in France, although Wheatstone has also here a
patent for his invention. They do not
interfere except that my invention is
pronounced far superior in simplicity and
practability. They are different inventions.
