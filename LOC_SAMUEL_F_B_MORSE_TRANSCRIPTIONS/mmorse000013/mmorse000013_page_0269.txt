to Monsieur Desguerre.
My dear Sir,
I have the honor to enclose you the
note of the Secretary informing you of your election
at our last annual meeting, at which the body of Honorary Members of our Academy, when
I proposed your name, it was received with enthusiasm, and
the vote was unanimous. I hope, my dear Sir, you will
receive this as a testimonial not merely of my personal esteem, and deep sympathy in your late losses, but also
as a proof that your genius is in some degree estimated
on this side of the water. —
Notwithstanding the efforts made in England to give
you the credit which is justly due; I think I may
with confidence assert you that throughout the United States
your name alone will be associated with the brilliant discovery
which is called by your name. The letter I wrote from Paris
the day after your sad loss, has been published throughout
the whole country, in hundreds of journals, though any attempt
to give to any other the honor of this discovery, I
myself will be ready to your defense.
I hope, before this reaches you that the French
Government, long and deservedly celebrated for its generosity to men of science, will have amply supplied its young talent, when
the proper remuneration shall be decided to you in France,
you should think it for your advantage to make an
arrangement to hold back the secret for 6 months or a
year, and would consent to an exhibition of your results in
this country for a short time, the exhibition might be managed
to your advantage, if you should think favorably of
the plan, I offer you my services gratuitously.
May 20th 1839.
