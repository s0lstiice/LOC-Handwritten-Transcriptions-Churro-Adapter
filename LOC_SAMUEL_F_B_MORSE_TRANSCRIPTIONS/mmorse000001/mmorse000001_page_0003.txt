My dear Son, You do not write me as often as
you ought. In your next you must assign some
reason for this neglect. Possibly I have not
received all your letters. Nothing will improve
you so much in Epistolary writing as practice. Take
great pains with your letters. Avoid vulgar
phrases. Study to have your ideas pertinent &
correct, & clothe them in an easy & grammatical
dress. Pay attention to your spelling, pointing,
the use of capitals, & to your hand writing. After a
little practice these things will become natural & you will thus acquire a habit of writing correctly & well. General Washington
was a remarkable instance of what I have now
recommended to you. His letters are a perfect
model for Epistolary writers. They are written
with a great uniformity in respect to the hand
writing & disposition of the several parts of the
letter. I will show you some of his letters when
I have the pleasure of seeing you next vacation; & when I shall expect to find you much
improved in your natural disposition, my dear
son, renders it proper for me earnestly to recommend to you to attend to one thing at a time.
