My dear Wife
I am impatient to receive a
Letter from you, I hope to find one in the morning
at the Post Office in Utica Eliza has no one
from Mr R. who informs us you were well 3
days after we left home; but that you were dull
after having been for so long a time accustomed
to such a family around you as we had to be left
almost alone, it is not surprising that you should
feel the change but I hope you soon recovered your
sprits, I that you have experienced the liberty
you hoped to enjoy in your comparative soli-
tude. Not an hour passes except when asleep, that I
do not think of you & the dear boys I hope they do
all they can to make you happy & that you all enjoy
health. The time now seems long since I saw you,
& I am anxious to see my return
As to myself I am made very happy by our
friends here I specked last Sabbath in the morning
& in the afternoon at New Hartford 5 miles for Mr
Rowden, I spent the night & next day with him
All the company came out to dine on Monday at
Mr. Rowden's with Mr. Sidney who rushed Whiter-
