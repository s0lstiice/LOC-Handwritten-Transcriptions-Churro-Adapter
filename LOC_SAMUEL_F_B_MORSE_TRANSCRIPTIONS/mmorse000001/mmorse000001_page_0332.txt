Yale College New Haven June 18th 1807
My dear Parents,
I have just rec'd your letter informing me
of my sisters death, altho' it is very afflicting to me, yet it
was not unexpected, I have been expecting this long time to
hear this news, my aunts letter raised my suspicions, &
I then thought she could not live long; I am very sorry, & will
try to give you some comfort, by applying myself to my
studies, more diligently; I intend (if possible) you shall hear
no complaints of my idleness or irregularity.
Last week I went to Mrs Beers & saw a sett
of Montaigne's plays in French in 8 vols handsome hand-
somely bound in calf & gilted -- for 2 dollars; the reason they
are so cheap is, because they are, wicked, & bad books for me to
read, I got them because they were cheap, & have exchanged them
for a handsome English edition of Gil Blas
price $4,,50 -- I am about to get a desk & bookcase
made of Cherry wood, the price to be 12 dollars, which will last me
tho' I then I can sell it for the price I gave for it, if
I was to get a desk made of stained wood it would cost
about 8 or 9 dollars, & when I am thro' college sell for
11 or 5 dollars but Cherry wood (like mahogany) is handsome
the longer it is kept. I hope you will not think
this extravagant. I will endeavor to be as economical as possible. In great haste. -- Your affectionate & obedient son
S. S. B. Morse.
(I am in good health.)
