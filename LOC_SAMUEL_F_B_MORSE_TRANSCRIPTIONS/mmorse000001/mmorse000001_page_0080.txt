Dear Son
We have the pleasure of a letter from you, which has gratified
us very much it is the only intelligence we have had from you since Mr Brax-
ton left you. I began to think that something was the matter with respect to
your health, that occasioned your long silence. You must have your letters
written and have them at Mr Phillips, as the frequently has opportunities
of finding them that you do not know of. We are all pleased to find that
you were so happy in your new situation; we thought that it was a sign that
you behaved well yourself to find you satisfied. Mr and Mrs Abbott we know
have always sustained the character of very worthy excellent people, and we
felt pleased that you was not in humble to their merits how is Erskine?
you say nothing of him in your letter. I hope he is well, give our love to
him and tell him we wish him to write us and let us know whether he is
happy in his new situation, and whether he hears frequently from home?
Do you need an Umbrella? if you do we will send you one by the first good
opportunity. Hope you will be very studious and be well fitted for Collledge by
the time you enter that you may pass your examination with honour
to yourself as well as your instructor. We are very anxious now for
that you should excel in every thing that will make you truly happy
and be useful to your fellow men. In particular by no means forget
your duty to your heavenly Father. Remember what has been said with
great truth, that he can never be faithful to others him who is not so, to his
