Charleston Nov. 18th 1805
My dear Son,
We are extremely anxious to
hear from you, having received one letter only
from you since you left us, & that written the
day after your arrival Your Mama is
distressed & almost sick with anxiety lest
you are sick, having heard that it is sickly
in a part of the town. You must have re-
ceived a number of letters from us, in which
we have enjoined it on you to write us
often at least once in a fortnight by
post. Don't fail to do this as you regard
the happiness of your parents, especially
of your affectionate & anxious Mother.
We wish to know particularly how you
are pleased with your situation, & what
is your progress in your Studies, whether
you keep pace with your class &c &c
If you shall not have written when you
receive this, write immediately by mail,
& afterward once in a fortnight, by private
intimation, whenever they offer. The family
are all well. Nothing very important has lately taken
place in town. The prisoners are coming into the new
prison the first of December. Your brothers are to be at home
