Charlestown August 27th 1803
Dear Sir,
In June last your brother John
called on me, in great distress to indorse a
note for him, at 90 days, for $257. His wife
at this time lay at the point of death; his goods
were all attached, & a man watching them
in his house, at his expense. I told him
that I could not be obliged for him without
security, as I had promised Mrs. Mosey, I never would be.
He said he was willing to make me secure, & the
sheriff & he made out a bill of sale of furniture
to the amount of the debt, he signed it, & the
sheriff witnessed it, & I endorsed the note. I believe
it a means of saving the life of Mrs. P. it is
so considered by others. I had hoped by this act
to have continued your brother in the Bank,
till your return.
A strong inducement to indorse
this Note; in addition to the above, was
your brother assured me repeatedly, that he had
just received a letter from you, requesting him to
get some one to assist him till your return,
& that you would be given me immediately on
your return. I had no opportunity to see you
