It is impossible that you can do two things well
at the same time, & I would, therefore, never have
you attempt it. - Never undertake to do what might
not to be done; & then, whatever you undertake
endeavour to do it in the best manner.
It is said of Mr. DeWitt, a celebrated States-
man in Holland, "who was born to success in the
year 1672, did the whole business of the republic,
I yet had time left to go to assemblies in the
evening, & sup in company. Being asked how
he could possibly find time to go through so
much business & yet amase him self in the even-
ings as he did? he answered, there was nothing
to do but, for that it was only doing one thing
at a time, & never putting off anything till to-
morrow that could be done today. The study
I am dissipated attention to one object, is a fine
mark of a superior Genius; as hurry, bustle,
agitation are the never failing symptoms
of a weak & frivolous mind."
I expect you will read this Letter over sever-
al times, that you may retain its contents in
your memory & give me your own opinion
on the advice I have given you. If you improve
this well, I shall be encouraged to give you
more as you may need it.
