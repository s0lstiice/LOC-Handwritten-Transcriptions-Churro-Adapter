my Dear Husband
Charleston August 9th 1806
Yesterday I received yours of the 1st inst from
Belle town Springs was very happy to find by it that you were as
well as you never, when you consider how much fatigue you
had gone through before you set out on your journey and the
rashness with which you performed it your firmness you can
hardly wonder at your spirits flagging after you had gotten
to a resting place I was sorry to find you had determined
to leave the Springs so soon I feared your being out of employ
so long would make the time pass heavily but you should
recollect that it was Duty to your family & Friends that all
you have at this time and Duty should make you willing
to stay and give them a full and fair trial. by the
time you receive this you will have made your visit
to White town and be on your way back as far as
Auburn you will have received two letters from me at
White town Hope one of which I have written I wish
I had written one to meet you at the Springs it would
have relieved your mind while there but I did not know
till it was too late how long you would remain there or
when you would arrive there. In my last letter I gave
you Arthur's opinion of the Chevau legs transfere which
if you have not received you doubtless will have converse
with him on before you receive this I hope you will talk
with Whiting on the subject and endeavour to adjust the
matters with him as it is a miserable take in I find Arthur
are good for nothing. – Mr Racketwood informs me that
