My dear boys,
We yesterday rec'd letters from
Finley & Richard, of the first inst. It is always
pleasing to hear from you, but especially when
hear good news. If you are obedient
to the rules of the Academy, I attentive to
your studies- We are gratified to find you
contented to remain at the Academy on the
day of Independence while so many go
home on that occasion. The improvement you
will gain during the absence of your school
mates, will be of more advantage than the
satisfaction they will receive- And we shall
be more ready to indulge you some more
reasonable & useful gratification hereafter.
Richard may get a Greek Grammar if
Mr Newman. We shall send him a pair of
shoes. If you do not add to your black marks,
Mr Newman, I presume we'll have the good
news to take it off at the end of the term-
If there be but one left you- We are much gratified that you have conducted so well-
I send Finley the Panoptick, with your money, all
read in time- We shall be very glad to see Elisha
come down- My dear boys- consider that time is
precious- learn then to improve every moment. Get to some reliable purpose from an early period. The situation in industry demands all your time & attention. I would earnestly recommend to all to believe in all to believe in God. I have read all your letters & am very well in all respects. I am your affectionate parent & more
