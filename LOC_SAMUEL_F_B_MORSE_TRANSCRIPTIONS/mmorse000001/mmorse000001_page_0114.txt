My dear boy,
Your Mamma has left me this page to
fill it with pleasure, as I am fond of writing
to those who are pleased as you seem to be with reading my letters. Mr. Abbott, who dined with us today,
gives a good account of Finley's behaviour in the family. I say he is a boarder, in general are very studious -- Hope Finley will learn to fix his
attention to his proper business, he will soon find his prospects will reward him for all his labour. If Mr Newman thinks it proper he will
furnish Edwards with a suitable piece for him to speak at exhibition -- I think it will not be best for him to go to College this fall -- to leave Rochester
alone -- He is young enough yet -- I am sorry to hear he has been unwell -- You must all take care of your healths -- & not play hard this last in
& especially take care not to go into the town -- You are warned, now in the middle of the day -- I am more than once in the week -- Your Mama has
told you what happened to young Willard who was buried yesterday -- The yellow fever is in Providence & St. Haven -- The College at U. S. is on that side
broken up for the present -- I hope the sick keep there will soon subside -- The H. M. of the Penophilist is not quite ready the last half of the flowers are not stuck off.
The paper for Andover may be sent on Saturday --
The season is very dry, melancholy in that respect but the town is very healthy at present -- Your Mama sends you
some lilies, which she has written from memory, with the wishes you all to remain up in your memories -- We send you all
dear Cousin Hutchinson, a great deal of love -- Your affectionate father J. Mosse
