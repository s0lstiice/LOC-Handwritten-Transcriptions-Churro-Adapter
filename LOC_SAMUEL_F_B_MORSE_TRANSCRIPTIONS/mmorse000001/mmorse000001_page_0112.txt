My Dear Sons
I have had a few lines from Finley to day which gave
me pleasure to hear that you were all well he mentions Edward, not
being quite well a few days before but never said what was the matter
with him; have a grand best wishes too hand if this is the cause of his
not being so well he must not continue it his health is of
great importance to him, as without it, he can do nothing
to that advantage, that he otherwise would. I shall send
Finley affairs from Paris will send the 2d No of the Transplantist
and if I can find it will send the Music Book all by Mrs
Abbott, to morrow morning. You have not told me whether
you received the two bundles of papers sent you by Capt Phillips
Have you heard of the death of Young Williard of Cambridge
the late president Williard son he died of a violent fever occasioned
by going into Eaton when he was very hot in the middle of the
day he also pumped a great deal of cold water on him had
it not been for his being so very hot this be a warning to you all
not to be guilty of the like Indiscretion which may cost you
your life Dreadfull indeed would this be to all of us. Drink
you would not go into water afterer than once a week and
then either early in the morning or late in the afternoon and
not go in when hot nor stay long in the water remember
these cautions of your mama and obey them Strictly. A young
