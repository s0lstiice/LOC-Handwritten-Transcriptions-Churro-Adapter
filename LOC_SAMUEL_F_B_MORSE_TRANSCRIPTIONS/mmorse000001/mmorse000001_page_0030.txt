Charleston Feb. 9th 1865
My dear Sir, I am deeply in your debt, but find it extremely difficult to redeem time to pay up your letter by the Ward & two by the Butler are now before me Before I proceed to say any thing in reply let me say, that I received a polite letter from your son containing a request to which I have faithfully attended & I am sorry to say, without success there is no such birth unoccupied that I can find in any office in Boston or this town He will I know excuse my answering his letter in this way as he can so prepared for time
The 50 Watt & 50 Columbia shell be ready in the Spring also the two sheets for Vincent, which I think he desires bound as it is probable the doing up & sticking of many wood sheets will cost as much as the binding a thousand sheets of Vincent if not I will pay the difference
My extreme fear for an end of the potentious aspect of our political affairs are almost swallowed up by still greater fear for our University which is most imminently by threatened with a revolution which will deeply & lastingly affect the cause of even Federal truth. The Corporations have at length by a majority of one chosen in Mare, next Thursday the Overseers meet to sanction or negative the choice I fear however not by a majority
