Copy to Alfred Vail
Extract.
My dear Sir,
You will perceive by the paper to-morrow that
my bill appropriating $30,000 for a trial of the Telegraph
has just passed the House by a vote of 89 to 83. It is
real a second time in the Senate, & I am now strongly
in hopes that it will be carried through the latter body &
become a law before the 4th of March.
You can have but a faint idea of the sacrifices
& trials I have had in getting the Telegraph thus far
before the Country and the world. I cannot detail them
here, I can only say that for ten years I have labored
all my time, and at my own expense, without assistance
from the other proprietors (except in obtaining the iron of
the magnets for the last instruments (which if you)
to forward our enterprise, my means to defray my
expenses to mutual loss every cent I owned in the world
was collected, are nearly all gone, and if by any means
the bill should fail in the Senate, I shall return to
New York with the fraction of a dollar in my pocket.
Write to Mr Smith some time since complaining of
his not coming on to assist me as he promised.
He wrote me that he was invariably prevented
and closes with these words "But let me say
"as one of the parties interested, I have so great
"confidence in your own sense of justice, and of
"your sacrifices in the enterprise that I shall be
"at all times most willing to have you fully
"reimbursed and recompensed before any one
"shall participate of a single cent of profits, or
"beyond their actual expenditures. Since your return
"from Europe, yours has been the whole expense -- your
"the whole cost of keeping the principle of life, I hope
