New York soon after established the American Academy of Fine Arts, which has successfully competed with Philadelphia and Baltimore in the taste and zeal for the fine arts. But since the formation of the Academy of Design, however, a manifest revolution has taken place. New York now takes precedence of all cities in the country, in furnishing the simplest means of study to the artist or amateur. There are about 100 artists in the different cities of the United States, and about 70 artists in this city. While in Boston or Baltimore I was in Paris the Arts languish, in New York they flourish; and although they suffer individually with the rest of the cities in the general absurdity, yet they are able to live. The Academy (as is shewn in the Preface of the Catalogue of 1842) has created an embarrassment, and while the effect of the general admiration in diminishing the receipts this present season, gives them some consolation, it amounts to about $10000, while the receipts of the Exhibition at Philadelphia have amounted to $8000. Our Schools of Fine Art are scattered over the country, and if managed with the prudence of real artists have not distinguished themselves. The Academy of Design, if its founders will be accomplished, in making New York the central point of the Arts as it has been of the Common Schools of the country.
