New Haven Nov 28th 1847
Dear Father,
I have asked Prof Goodrich concerning my
guardianship & he says he would rather have me put one who
has little more to do with college than he. Prof Olmstead
no doubt would take that charge as he is the guardian of
one of my class now. Prof $200 should be sent by Truck R
is gone $25 for board $29.57 for coal $1 for oil, candles, a
few medicines postage etc. All that I owe now in New Haven is my
stock bill $3.25 & the washerwoman $1.18.74. I purchased a necktie for
$1.50 to the one I got in NY being rather too much worn. I
received a vest from Bridgeport one that I thought I had lost.
My overcoat that you purchased for me in Madison this last
fall is in want of repairs the cloth is very weak & the velveteen
collar has split & worn in several places. It will cost me
$2 or 3 dol. to get it fixed over re buttoned etc. I really want
a new one but will wait till next winter, I can make
this one last through this winter. I am well situated for the winter now and
only want a little money to keep me in coal, oil, & clean clothes.
This finding a guardian is a new business to me & I hardly know
how to go to work, I am sorry it has given you so much
concern & yet the matter is just where it was at first. Prof
Woolley told me he would not like the charge & Prof Goodrich
98970
