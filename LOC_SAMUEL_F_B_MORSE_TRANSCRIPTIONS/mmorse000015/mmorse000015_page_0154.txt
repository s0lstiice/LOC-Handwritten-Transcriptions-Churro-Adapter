March 26. 1843.
My dearest Sir.
State the liberty of sending a letter for my
friend Brown, which I beg of you to forward to his Excellency.
I desire to trouble you with these little commissions, but the
truth is that you have so benedicted a disposition that you
must not marvel that it is likely to be seriously taxed.
When you have occasion to expedite letters to the Rocky
Mountains you must use my services in a similar way.
I send you a daily Gazette of yesterday Containing
an article published over the actual W. which you
have probably noticed – The article was written by one
to assist the Community of a great fraud upon their
Common Sense, which has been attempted by a travelling
picture exhibited in Advertising for exhibition the
original picture painted by Mr. Clark for the British
Institution in 1846 or 1847. The article has drawn out
a reply in which the picture is unblushingly
claimed as the original painted by Mr. Clark. I
think I never knew a case of such unparalleled
impudence – The picture is certainly the oldest I
can saw, for a picture of any pretension. It is
absolutely beneath Criticism – The owner of the
picture is in Mr. Clark of your City the same I
