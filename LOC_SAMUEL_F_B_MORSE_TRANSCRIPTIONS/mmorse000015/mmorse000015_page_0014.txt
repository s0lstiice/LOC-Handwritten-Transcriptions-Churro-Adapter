My dear Sir, I have received your letter of the 13th ult. and am much obliged for your kindness in sending me a copy of the telegraphic instrument which you will receive notice of my Telegraph, I showed it to a few friends
occasionally within a few weeks, among others to Prof. Henry of Princeton (a copy of whose letter I have sent you some time since) he had never seen it in operation but had only heard of it from description the principle on which it is founded. He is not of an enthusiastic temperament but exceedingly critical of giving an opinion on Scientific inventions, yet in this case he expressed himself in the warmest terms and the telegraphic Telegraph (who informed me) that he had found it the most beautiful and ingenious instrument he had ever seen. Indeed since I last wrote you I have been wholly occupied in perfecting its details and something with the whole system. There is not a shadow of doubt as to its performing all that I have promised in regard to it and indeed all that has been conceived of it. You can understand the difficulty arising from want of means that I have had to encourage, I had hoped to be able to show my perfects instrument in Washington last before this and was sent to this morning contemplating its perfection last week. The result of the proposed arrangement of Congress has stopped my preparations and introduces I fear another year of hardships and anxieties.

I am
Very truly yours
Abraham Lincoln
