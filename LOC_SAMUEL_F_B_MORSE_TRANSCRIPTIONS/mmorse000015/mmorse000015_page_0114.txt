New Haven, Conn. July 1848.
Dear Doctor,
Your letter was received yesterday and as it
was most convenient for me to write to day, I did not exactly
comply with your request to write immediately. Our studies this term
are very hard, and they continually grow harder. I must say I
am not very fond of Algebra, we are hurried on so fast that I
have hardly time to understand it, in many places I do not under-
stand. Most of my class have been through the Algebra
before and even they sometimes flunk one or two. If the class went
off of their own accord because the lessons were too hard for them.
My other studies are easy, of the two Latin & Greek I like Greek
the best. I have changed my room and got a room mate. Boston
is my room mate the one that was examined with me we pay
$11.50 per week for our rooms and it is a week of peace to have them
gone. I have been at Mrs. Wilhelly's not more than 10 days and I paid for
them in vacation as an arm ame but now we are not charged for
vacation. I had to get some new furniture chairs, tables, etc. as
I had none of my own. I received the Public Document you sent
me, you must write when you have complete success. Nothing of any
interest has taken place here, our friends are all well. One of my
old schoolmates who was a V.P. in Kentucky, I knew him till he died, he
was very much liked by his class, and a good scholar.
