F. C. J. Smith, Esq.
New York July 16. 1842.
Dear Sir
I am just now in a dilemma in consequence of an application from an energetic and enterprising engineer Mr. John P. Manrou who is so delighted with the operation of the Telegraph that he desires to form a company and at once put it in operation from New York to Washington. He thinks there is no doubt but a capital could be raised for this purpose.
He is just the person to enter upon the plan, Mr. Manrou is a substantial man, a successful contractor on the N.York & Erie Rail Road. He is so earnest, I promised to write you on the subject. If you could come on and apprise me when you can be here, I will endeavor to have Mr. Tail here also and as F. Gale is on the spot, we could put all our matters into a state less embarrassing than they are at present. It seems to me that there is now an opportunity for doing something advantage to all. You must perceive at what disadvantage I do business, when before I can make any answer to queries from persons who feel disposed to take hold of the enterprise, I must write two or three letters of particulars to different parts of the country, and wait days for an answer. The necessity of our Telegraph is made evident in this our case. If you had in your pocket one of my Reports there would be no need of a long journey, or of writing 3 or 4 days for an answer.
In brief I can say that the cost of mails alone have ascertained to be as fellows, and the wire you will perceive, is to be laid the most substantial manner.
(Laid pipe & see over)
