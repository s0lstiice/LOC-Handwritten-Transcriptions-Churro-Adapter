Spadwell Feb 29 1843
Prof. Stone
Dear Sir Yours of
the 23d int came to hand yesterday with
the cheering intelligence of a successful
application to Congress for an appropriation
for testing your Telegraph. After so
much labour, and persevering to
accomplish your object and our object
I feel more grateful to that kind providence
on your account, than my own, for
previous, indeed, would have been the dis
appointment had your unceasing exertion
only failed in hopelesst procrastination.—
I could willingly have shared with you this
labour, could I have ordered means to do
so, or could I have shaped my circumstances
But it has been out of my power.— Although
disappointed in not accompanying you to Washington
my best wishes followed you and since I
for the first time learn, that Peter is with
you, I promise you are better satisfied and
upon the whole, it has been all for the best for
you and all concerned.— While I could, you
had the evidence I think, that I have done
all in my power,— and am now as ready
succesful or not to do all in my power.
I fully concur with Mr Smith in all he has
expressed, and you will perhaps call to mind
