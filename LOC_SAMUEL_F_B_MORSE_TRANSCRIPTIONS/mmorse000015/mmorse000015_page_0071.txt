Washington Dec. 18. 1842.—
Dear Sidney:
I have not written you since my arrival
as I had nothing special to say, nor have I now any-
thing very decided to communicate in relation to my
enterprise, except that it is in a very favorable train.
The Telegraph as you will see by Thursdays or Fridays
Intelligence of last week is established between two of
the Comtesse rooms in the Capitol, and excites universal
admiration. I am told from all quarters that there is
but one sentiment in Congress respecting it, and that
the appropriation will unquestionably pass. The discovery
I made just before leaving N.York of the fact that two or
more circuits will pass without interference at the same
time on the same wire, excites the wonder of all the scienti-
fic in & out of Congress here, and when I show them
the certainty of it, in the practical application of it to
simplify my Telegraph, their admiration is loudly expressed
and it has created a feeling highly advantageous to me.
I believe I drew for you a method by which I thought
I could pass rivers without any wires through the water,
I tried the experiment across the Canal here on Friday
afternoon with perfect success; this also has added a
fresh interest to my favor, and I beg you to hope that I am
on the eve of realizing something in the shape of Compen-
sation for my time and means expended in bringing my in-
vention to its present state. It does not be sougains,
