New York, May 17th 1843
Copy to F.O.J Smith,
Dear Sir:-
I have this morning received enclosing the
contract for treading. I have examined
the contract and I must say I am not
exactly pleased with the terms. If I un-
derstood you right before you left for
Boston, you were confident a contract
could be made for within the estimates
given in to the Govt, and I had hoped
that something could be saved from
that estimate as from the others, so as to
present the experiment before the country
in as cheap a form as possible. I have
taken a pride in showing to Govt how
cheap the Telegraph can be laid, since
the main objection, and the one most
likely to defeat our uttermost plans, is its
great expense. I have in my other con-
tracts been able to be far within my esti-
mates to Govt and I had hoped to be
able to present to the Sec'y the contract
for treading likewise reduced. There are
plenty of applicants here who will do
it for much less and one even said he
might let for one half. I shall do nothing
in regard to the matter until I see you.
