Under standing that it is a desideratum
in relation to your Telegraph that it should be capable of
transmitting dispatches public & private that can be read only
by the person to whom addressed, I take the liberty to in-
form you that I am in possession of an instrument for
writing in cipher any communication whatsoever, in such
manner that it can be read only by means of a similar instru-
ment, similarly adjusted.— The changes of adjustment of which
it is susceptible are almost infinite in number.— So that
every person on the Globe may have a different key by which
to adjust the instrument.— To transmit communications
thus written, the Telegraph needs only to possess the power to make
35 distinct characters or signs which may be readily distingu-
ished from each other.— Single instruments at each Tele-
graph Station, to which correspondents may have access will
be sufficient.— The Instrument is not very expensive; & any
person may be taught to use it in five minutes.—
The Instrument is constructed ready for exhibition; &
should you think such a device would be available for
your purposes, I shall be happy to show it to you
at any time.— Both the instrument & the device are
my property though not my invention.— I would dispose
of it to you for a moderate consideration in money, or on
some terms conditioned on its being ultimately adopted & made
available.—
Very Respectfully Yours
Eli W. Blake
