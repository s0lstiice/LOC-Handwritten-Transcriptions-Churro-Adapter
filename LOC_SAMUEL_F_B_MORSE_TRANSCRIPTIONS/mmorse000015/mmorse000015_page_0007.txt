Coppy to Gov. Lenard July 1, 1842
My Dear Sir,
In reply to your last letter of the 23d
instant I would say, that I fully intend to comply
with your request to make such and a catalogue
of the Artists and their works as you desire. I regret
that my health is at this moment such that I shall
be compelled to decline, as the little strength I have
is necessarily devoted to some pressing domestic matter.
But while I feel unable to do what I could wish
I am happy in being able to refer you to a work which
contains a very full account of both artists and their
works. I allude to "Dunlap's History of the Arts
" of Design in the United States, Vol. 2 Vols. large 8vo.
If this work is not within your reach, I have a
copy which is at your service for that purpose, and
which I will immediately forward to you upon
the expression of a wish to that effect.
Very respectfully
Y. M. C. A.
St. B. M.
I would add however that since the publication of
Mr. Dunlap's work, there are several Artists of great promise
coming from the school of the National Academy,
D. Huntington of this city, a pupil of Mr. Morse, exhibited at
the Exhibition of 1841, a picture of great merit Mercy
at the gate from the Potomac Prophets.
H. Peters Gray, a pupil of Huntington son, also of Mr. Yost, portraits
and ideal heads of great beauty particularly in colouring.
