Paris November 10. 1812
Dear Friend,
I have enclosed
with this an introductory letter to A. Cooper Esquire
my object, in so doing, is to obtain a passport from the
British Government to go to London to pursue my pro-
fessional studies under the greatest surgeons of the
day. As you are perfectly acquainted with my pro-
suits as well as with me; and can answer, that it is
only as a student of surgery, I ask this favour; I as
our former habits of intimacy and friendship will I
trust, interest you in my behalf; I venture to ask
you to call on Mr Cooper with my letter and comple-
ments, state to him my wishes and request of him
such aid in obtaining me a passport as he can
give consistently with his feelings and occupations.
Finally, if you can obtain me, by the assistance of
Mr Cooper or any other friend, an passport, and give
me notice of the same, in a letter addressed to the
United States Paris, you will do me a favour which
will be considered as of the greatest importance. Mr
Warden has assured me that if you give a letter, addressed
to his care, to Mr Driekens or leave it at the office of
Mr Beasley, agent for American prisoners, he shall
receive it in due time. As the thing is important to
me, have the goodness to write me by two or even three
opportunities if they present, I inform me if my passport
can be deposited for me at the landing place of the
French cartels. With respect & esteem I am as heretofore yours
your friend W. Hayward has politely offered to add his advice on

William J. Walker
