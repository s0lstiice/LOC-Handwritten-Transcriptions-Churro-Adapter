Simmonds also of the Class before you, died
in a most shocking manner. He was pri-
soned by a mistress of his keeping, in revenge
for his having married another woman. —
Your classmates are all well as far as my
knowledge extends. Northrop & Bill Huntington
& my class are Officers in our Army. —
Isaac Larkin, & John Larkin (who used to live
at the bottom of our garden) are Midshipmen
in the navy. — Boston looks quite dreary & desolate.
No American shipping in the harbour, except
ships stripped of their rigging and by nothing at
the wharves. It is no uncommon thing
to look round the harbour and not see a single
American flag. The town is depopulating
rapidly. Charleston is a little more busy.
The 7th A 7th, & a shop of war are building
here? — The season here is uncommonly fine.
The crops of grain & grass are immense. A good
season was never more needed. Our coasting
trade is entirely destroyed. All the flour from
Pennsylvania & Maryland is carried to Boston
over land, a thing unprecedented in this
country till since the Declaration of War.
Papa & Mama have gone to Andover to spend a
