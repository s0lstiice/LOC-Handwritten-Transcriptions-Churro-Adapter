side would probably have been an inconvenience. The
event of the engagement may be attributed to an accident
which befel the Chesapeake in one of her manoeuvres. Her
object was to shoot athwart the bow of the Shannon, and assume
a raking position. In executing this manoeuvre, her
rigging caught in the anchor of the Shannon, and spoiled
her plan.— The Shannon immediately threw two hundred
men on board and the Chesapeake surrendered. It should
be stated that every officer on the Chesapeake, upper
deck was either killed or mortally wounded in the early
part of the action; The confusion which this occasioned hastened
the termination of the action— battle. The stories about
infernal machines &c and by the British are all false.
The battle was fairly fought.— You may think from this
statement that Capt. Lawrence, was unforgonable in
raking a battle—but you must allow for his peculiar situation.
He was challenged.— A few months before he had himself challenged the Bonne
Bétoynne, as vessel of superior forces, and the refusal of
the Bonne Bétoynne to accept this challenge was announced by
Captain Bainbridge, in an official letter as equivalent to a victory on the part of
Capt. Lawrence. In this situation, if Lawrence had refused it would have resulted
that he acknowledged himself inferior to the Shannon. As his refusal was
