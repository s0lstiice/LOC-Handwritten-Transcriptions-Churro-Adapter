Dear Brother Charleston July 8th 1813
We received a long letter from you this
morning, dated April 25th. The information it contained
was very gratifying to us all. -- I have received your
letter a few days since on the subject of the War,
but have understood since that no letters on political
subject would be received. In that letter also I gave
several particulars respecting the capture of the Ches
apeake which you will probably meet with either
in the English or American papers. An abstract of
these remarks I here insert: -- The Chesapeake sailed
from Boston harbour, the Shannon in full view about
9 miles off -- We saw the two vessels till 1/2 a few
from the top of the Exchange, till about an half
hour before they engaged. -- The circumstances of
the Chesapeake were these: -- In the first place,
she was notoriously the poorest vessel in our Navy,
very unpopular with the sailors -- She lay in this
harbour for months last fall after she was ready for
sea for want of a crew. The sailors would not enter her,
and the rendezvous was finally opened in New-York
where the vessel was unknown; and there they obtained
their crew. -- In the second place, Capt. Lawrence,
her commander, had been on board the frigate
