Sunday evening or rather Monday morning 1. o’Clock
London May 30th 1813.
My Dear Parents,
I just have time to drop you a line by an
opportunity which I learnt, but a few hours since;
and am sorry I have not a longer time to write you
a longer letter; this is just to say that I never was
in better health and spirits, that as far as I am
able to judge I am going on prosperously, and that
I have commenced writing you a long letter giving
an account of my journey to Oxford etc: the ceremony of delivering the Gold medal to me a few
days ago, and various Yes. I get so little time
to write that I am a great while in finishing
a letter, for I think a letter ought to be long
that is to go so long a voyage.— I will now
tell you the reason of my not writing so of
late, Mr Allston has been extremely— and
Mr Allston feared if I wrote and did not mention
they were well, that it would create anxiety in
his family at home & wish’d me to wait till he
was better, he was ill for 2 or 3 months; but I
am now happy to say he has not only completely
recovered, but is in much better condition than
he has been for many years, he has abandoned
smoking altogether, and is now working upon his
great picture, which will be completed in a few
days, and open to the public. Owing to his illness
he was unable to finish it for Somerset house.—
I never felt so low spirited as when he was ill, I often
thought if he should be taken away at this time, what an
uncomparable loss he would be not only to me, but to America, to
the world.— Oh! he is an angel on earth, I cannot love him
too much.— Excuse my warmth, I never can speak
of Mr A. but in rapture.— More anon,
Remembrance to Miss Roswell etc.
Love to all. Adieu
Sam! H.B. Morne.
