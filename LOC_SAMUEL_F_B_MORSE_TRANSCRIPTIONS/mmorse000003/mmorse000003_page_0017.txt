is bad, it is a mortifying thing sometimes to me, when I have
been painting all day very hard, and begin to be pleased with
what I have done, and on showing it to Mr. Allston with the
expectation of praise, and not only of praise, but a score
of "excellents," "well dones," and "admirables," say it is mostly
flesh, it is indeed "Sir, it is painted with brick dust and clay,
I have felt sometimes ready to dash my pallet knife thro'
it, and to feel at the moment quite angry with him, but
a little reflection restores me, and I see that Mr. Allston is
not a flatterer, but a friend, and that really to improve
I must see my faults, what he says after this always puts
me in good humour again, he tells me to put a few flesh tints
here, a few grey ones there, and to clear up such a
part, by such and such colors," and not only that, but takes
the palette and brushes and shows me how, in this way he
aposts me, I think it one of the greatest blessings that I am
under his eyes I don't know how many errors I might have
fallen into if it had not been for his attentions. He and Mrs.
Allston are very well and desire particular remembrance to
you. I rise between 7 & 8 o'clock, breakfast, then set my palette
(with you know is to prepare all my colors, and about nine
o'clock I am prepared to begin I am painting portraits alone
at present, our sitters are among our acquaintance, we
paint them if they depray the experience of canvas and colors,
the improvement is the only profit we can expect
for some time) our sitters come at nine and stay an hour
or two each; since the days have become so long, we dine at 2.
o'clock; after dinner, paint till dark what is not till 9 o'clock,
in the evening draw from plaster casts of hands and feet, or call
on some friends, Mr. Allston, &c. or else see company at home.
