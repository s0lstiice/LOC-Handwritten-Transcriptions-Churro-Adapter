Washington, January 12th 1840.
Esteemed friend,
I was fearful you might think me negligent in
not writing agreeable to promise, and obtaining the registered
certificates from the Office at this Depot. I would
have the goodness to excuse me for that neglect when I inform
you, that as I forgot my valuable friend in the Office, I returned
to me his intention of writing upon immediately, and I consider
it unnecessary for me to write until I heard from you upon
the subject. I expect you have received his letter, with
its valuable advice, in which much confidence of success may
be placed. I hope not to be found wanting in any thing
that may contribute to your benefit. I am human in demanding
for my removal from the unhappy place. The business of
the Office might have prevented Mr Sergant from writing,
so soon as you wished, which was the next morning.
As some time has elapsed, my anxiety compels me to
write, hoping it will not be considered premature.
As soon as convenient undoubtedly you will let me know the
prospect of success. – The money which I received was a
step to make me comfortable in the prison, during this
inconvenient season. – In case you should not succeed in
obtaining my liberty of parole, I have a prospect of going
into some little bump of malnage which will support
me during the winter if you can let me have about
five pounds, and I shall not be necessary to trouble you
