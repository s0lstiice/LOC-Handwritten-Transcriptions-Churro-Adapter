of Mr. Stephen, the author of the odious Orders in Council,
Mr. Thornton asked me at table, if I thought that if the
Orders in Council had been revealed a month or two sooner
whether it would not have prevented the war, I told him
I thought it would, at which he was much pleased, and
turning to Mr. Stephen, he said "do you hear that Mr. Stephen,
I always told you so" -- Last Wednesday I dined at Mr.
Witberforce's I was extremely pleased with him, at his
house I met Mr. Thornton & Mr. Grant members of parlia-
ment, in the course of conversation, they introduced America
Mr. W. regretted the war extremely, he said it was like
two of the same family quarrelling, "that he thought it
a judgment on this country for their wickedness, & that
they had been justly punished, for their arrogance & insolence
at sea," as well as the Americans for their vaunting
on land -- As Mr. Thornton was going, he invited
me to spend a day or two at his seat at Elpham, a
few miles out of town, I accordingly went and was very
civilly treated; the reserve which I mentioned in a former
letter, was evident, however, here, and I felt a degree of
embarrassment arising from it, which I never felt in America;
the second day, I was a little more at my ease: at
dinner were two sons of the Mr. Grant I mentioned above
they are perhaps the most promising young men in the country,
and you may possibly one day hear of them as at the head of
this nation; after dinner I got into conversation with them,
and Mr. Thornton, when America again became the topic
