I fear, unless there is more exertion made to forward
the art than at present. I wish Edwards would
extract the critique on Mr. Allston's pictures, and with
some few prefatory & complimentary remarks, have
it inserted in one of the papers, it would be grati-
fying to Mr Allston's friends I think, and it would also
be to me. I could write you longer, for I have many
more things to say to you, I wish to see you very much,
but I must forego that happiness, for my future
advancement in the art, which I have chosen, my love
for it is unabated, or rather I may say increased,
for the more I study it, the greater reason I have
to admire it, & to bless heaven for giving me
parents who have thrown no obstacle in the way
of my unlimited pursuit of it.
I wish you to remember particularly, that
although (through want of time to re-write this) I
have confounded the political part of this letter
with the rest, I intend the political for my brother,
and do they shall be the last opinions I will write to
any of you; for upon re-reading them, I see the
inutility of telling you them, for it is impossible, you
should see what I do, there, nor you cannot straight
the matter properly: I may be wrong & you may be
wrong, but as different circumstances operate to
form different opinions in each of us, we had bet-
ter not waste our time, and destroy our mutual
pleasure in receiving letters by discussing, so endles
a topic as politics, and I agree to drop them altogether.
