My dear Parents,
I have just received your letters of Oct.
7th by Mr Lyman, with a found of Chinese Vermillion. Capt. Langdon has not yet called on me,
nor Mr Parkman, by whom I suppose I have also some things from you; they have not yet
I believe arrived in town. The Vermillion is of the quality I wished, and I cannot sufficiently express my thanks to Mr Russell, whose kindness & attention to me I hope one day to
deavor to repay, though I fear it will give more & more impracticable, while she lays me under
such repeated weights of obligation; in the mean time express to her my warmest thanks.
Janr 1st 1813.
I was obliged to leave my letter unfinished till now, since writing the above, Mr Parkman
has called on me, he has not, the edition of George & Atlass, for which I thank you Capt. Langdon
has not yet arrived. Just Thursday week I received a very polite invitation from Henry
Thornton Esq to dine with him, which I accepted, I had no introduction to him, but hearing that your
son was in the country he found me out, and has shown me every attention, he is a very pleasant
sensible man, but his character is too well known to you
