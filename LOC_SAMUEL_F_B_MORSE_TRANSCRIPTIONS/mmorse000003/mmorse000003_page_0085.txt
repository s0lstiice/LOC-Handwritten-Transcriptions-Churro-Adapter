their spirit seems to be, the sacrifice of the public interest
for that of the individual; who are these men that
make the greatest outcry, are they not men on whom
the war bears some what hard, in account of their
great commercial concerns; is it not therefore natural
they should be displeased with the war, they look
no farther than the present moment, because the war puts
a stop to their business for a time, they consider
all as a gone case with them, and raise up the phantoms
of French alliance, sack'd towns, and total destructions
to frighten themselves and their neighbors, into resistance
to the war; if, when a nation treats us with the
greatest indignities, when the private, as well as public
voice breathes forth nothing but contempt towards us,
when we are prohibited from trading to countries
under false pretences of assisting an enemy, while they
trade themselves direct to enemies ports, when the savages
on our frontiers, are excited against us (of which there
can now be no doubt) when our seamen are taken, and
presed into the English navy, (a part of which are American
seamen; I say, when this is happening every day
before our eyes, too plain to admit of contradiction,
if it is not time to declare war against a country,
when is it? I well know what answer I should receive
from some federalists, or rather question by way of answer,
