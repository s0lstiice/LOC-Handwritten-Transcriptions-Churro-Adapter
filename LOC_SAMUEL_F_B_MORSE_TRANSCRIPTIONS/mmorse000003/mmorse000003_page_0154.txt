Before this can reach you, will doubtless have seen American papers announcing the
marriage of my sister to Capt. Houlf of the navy. They are at present at Boston, but it is not
improbable that they may reside in the navy house at Charleston. My daughter too
is married to a Mr. Watson of New Haven. But Jannette is writing to you at this moment,
and I have to hear all domestic news. We hear much of the war and an intention of
both countries to prosecute it now vigorously. God grant some happy event
any turn so bloody and unnatural a purpose.
Mr. Jarvis will fill up the paper, and nothing remains for me but to assure you that the friendship which you awakened
in my bosom on our first acquaintance, is not weakened by the lapse of years, neither by your silence. I am, my dear friend, deeply interested in your welfare, and what ever your situation in life may be, do not forget that in this little corner of the world, there
is one who loves you, and who will ever rejoice in your happiness.
Heaven bless you
Sarah W. Jarvis
My dear Sir, As I presume your two female friends have exhausted every topic of domestic news, I have little to add, excepting my thanks for your favour of June 29th 1812, presented me by Mr King & also for the pleasure which it afforded me
to converse with one who had been your very intimate intimate friend & of course was able to answer the numerous inquiries of your friends at Bloomingdale respecting your
health, your happiness & the nature of your pursuits. Your continual increasing success. You mention an answer to my letter of June 1812—that I had never the
pleasure of receiving, and I feared that you had relinquished a correspondence which did not make you an adequate return for the entertainment you afforded.
