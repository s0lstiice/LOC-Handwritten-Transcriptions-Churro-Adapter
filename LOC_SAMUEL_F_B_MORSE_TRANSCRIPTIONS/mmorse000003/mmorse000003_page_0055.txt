My Dear Parents,
I have a few moments to write you a
line by Mr West of Salem, (Mass.) who goes to
mortgage to Liverpool to embark for NewYork; he will
tell you that I am well, — My last letter was by
a Mr Wheeler, and I expected it would be the last for
some time on account of the state of affairs; but I
am glad to have an opportunity again to write you.—
I have had no letters from you for a great while, I wish
to receive a good family letter that I may know how
you are situated.— I am pursuing my studies, and
am convinced you would find no fault with my application
I employ all my time in acquiring something that is necessary
to my improvement in my art.— I have just returned
from a little excursion on the sea coast, whether I went for
a little relaxation, and to see the country, will a view
to my profession; I first sailed down the Thames to Margate
which is on the coast and upwards of 70 miles
from London, Margate is a celebrated watering place, and
in the summer season is the resort of many hundreds
from the city of London; the common conveyance is by
a hoy or packet vessel which carries from 50 to 80 passengers
at the first scent of the sea air, many of them are seasick
and if the water is at all rough nearly all of them: on
board the hoy in which I went, there were about 50
Georgians, the air was very clear, and a strong head wind
