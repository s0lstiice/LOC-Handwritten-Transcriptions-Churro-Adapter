London June 27th 1812.
My Dear Sir,
Permit me to introduce to your acquaintance
Mr. Cha'r King, an American artist, who has resi-
ded in this country four or five years for the pur-
pose of turning his studies with Mr. West, he has been
a very intimate intimate friend of mine; he now returns to
America to reside, and as he visits New-York on his way
to Phila. I could not let him pass through your city
without becoming acquainted with one of my best friends,
any attentions shown him, will be considered by me as
shown to myself, & I shall be proud to show every atten-
tion in my power to any whom you may think proper
to introduce to me while in this, or any other country.
Your letter of march, accompanied by one from
Mrs. Lannette I received a short time since, and I
answer them immediately. Your letter of Jan. I received
some time ago and answered it immediately directly after
I received it. Mr. King will tell you how difficult
it is for me to find a leisure moment, this I hope will
apologize for my not writing you often. You must
not be ceremonial but write whenever you feel an inclina-
tion, a letter from any of you will always be
very grateful to me.
Remember me affectionately to Mrs. Jarvis
Mrs. Lannette, and the rest of her amiable sisters;
the to the Misses Stuarts & Miss Isaac. I have not yet
received an answer from Miss Isaac to my letter all
who inquire after me.
Yours very sincerely
Sam'l P. B. M'cormick
