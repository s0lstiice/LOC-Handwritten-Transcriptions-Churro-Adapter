Last night as I was passing thro' Tottenham Court Road, I
saw a large collection of people of the lower class making
a most terrible noise by beating on something of the sound-
ing genus, upon going nearer and inquiring the cause I
found that a butcher had just been married, and that
it is always the custom on such occasions for his the couple trade, to sevenade
with marrowbones & cleavers, perhaps you have heard of the phrase "musical as marrow-
bones and cleavers," this is the origin of it; if you wish to experience the sound, let each one in the family take a pair of
longs and a shoeel, and then standing all together, let each one try to outdo the other in noise, and this will give you some
idea of it how this custom originated I don't know; I hope it is not symbolical of the harmony which is to
exist between the parties married --
Mr. Van Rensselaer has gone to France he was the bearer of dispatches from Mr. Russell to Mr. Barclay, he went
on Friday last. I am very well acquainted with Mr. Russell our ambassador, he is a very pleasant man &
is attentive to men --
The days are very long here now, it is twilight all night long you can see to read at half past 10. and at half past 2 in the morning.
I walked to Greenwich a few days since I was at the observatory, on the spot where longitude commences, I went
thro' the hospital it is a noble building, one of Mr. West's finest pictures is there -- Excuse the incoherence of this letter
it is made up of a little of everything, write me soon a good long letter. -- Love to all. -- Good bye --
Mr. Edwards Mosse --
Sam'l J.B.Mosse --
