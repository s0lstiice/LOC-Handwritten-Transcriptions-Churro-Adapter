My Dear Twenty,
I have had for writing a long letter, the twelve months, Mr.
Barney Smith of Boston, and family, are going in the fair
American capital and have kindly offered to take a letter
to you, for me, as the vessel I understand is not allowed
by government to take a letter bag. Mr Smith and family
have been extremely attentive to me since I have been
here, notwithstanding the great difference in our political
sentiments; Party distinction among Americans
in this country is soon lost, and their only ambition
is to see which can be the most hospitable to Americans;
I wish it was so at home. Mr Smith will call on you and tell you all about me. Mrs
Lydia Smith, (the daughter) is a very accomplished
girl, among other acquisitions, she has a good knowledge
of painting, and her talents are such for the art,
that she bids fair, not only to excel, but to be
another ornament to American genius in the
arts, and to have her name on the list as the first
and female artist of any age.
I have been to the Plantation office for
Papa, and the clerk is copying the record for me,
which if done in time I shall send with this
it is of some import.
I have just received your letter by Capt. Patt which
he sent me from Liverpool; the parents I suppose he will
be here in a few days.
London March 1st 1814.
