Charleston July 10th 1813
My dear Son, yesterday we had the highest satisfaction
receiving your good long letter which was concluded
the 27th of April. We all thank you for it. The serious
matter it contained gave us much pleasure, except
your disappointment relative to the Jacket sent by
Mr Woodson. Possibly he may have left it with
some friend at Liverpool who sent it into the office.
Whoever did it was to say the least, very inconsiderate
& disabling. I do not particularly mention its
contents as I have sent you so many letters since
July of which I keep no record or use.
Your dialogue with conscience in a good one
I am glad you have so faithful & good a friend
always near & that you are disposed to listen to his
reproof & yield to his arguments & monotonous.
I hope my dear son you will always be enabled to conduct
in such a manner as to have a friend in your
own bosom in an enlightened tender conscience. If
you have spared with your God, your condition on
Spirits grounds the highest happiness will be your
portion let what will happen to you in this changeful
& mixed state.
Your becoming industry & success in performing
your professional studies give your affectionate parents
the highest gratification & reward. We wish you to avoid
carrying your economy to an extreme. Let your appearance
be suited to a respectable company, you keep, & your
living suit, as will conduce most effectually to presence
health of body & vigor of mind. We shall all be
willing to make sacrifices at home, so far as may be
necessary to the above purposes. You did right to make
the excursion you did after so long confinement & must
be successful & free of your winter's labor. I hope to be
glad to have you visit Edinburgh if you can before
