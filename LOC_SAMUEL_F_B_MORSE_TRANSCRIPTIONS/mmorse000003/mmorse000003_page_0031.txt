London, June 25th 1812
My Dear Father,
Permit me to introduce to you Mr. Cha.
King, an American artist, who has been in this country
four or five years to improve himself in painting
with Mr. West, he has been one of my most inti-
mate friends, and has been very useful to me, when
I first came he was very attentive to me, in find-
ing me lodgings, and in seeing me comfortably
settled; he arrived in London on Saturday on
his return to America, if you can render him
any service, or show him any attention, I shall
feel very particularly obliged to you; I esteem
him very much, and it is like parting with a brother
to part with him; Mr. Allston, Mr. King, & Mr. Leslie,
were my most intimate intimate and confidential associates,
we used to see each other every day, and we became
all of the same family: Mr. King is from Newport
(R. I.) he is not in very good circumstances as to prop-
erty, and returns to America to settle with Mr. West
and Mr. Allston have a very his opinion of Mr. King's
talents.
I wrote you a long and particular letter by Mr.
Hurd a few days ago; Mr. Hurd returns in the Galen &
you must recollect Mr. King is democratic; I mention it that
you need not make any remarks that might hurt his feelings;
he will tell you many truths, however, in politics & in
remembrance to all friends.
My love to Mama, brothers, relatives and family
and remembrance to all friends
Adieu Your very affectionate Son
SAM: J: W: MORTON
