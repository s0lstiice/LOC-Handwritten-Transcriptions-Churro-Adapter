Boston July 12. 1813.
My Dear Friend,
You perceive that I still reside in Boston, while
you have traversed ocean and an now spending and delighting every favorite
in the metropolis of Europe; whose license and the Art have been stealing for
ages. -- I sometimes envy you your fate, and feel a rising murmurs at my
ear. I have been intending, and intending to unite to you, ever since you left
home; but the Genius of travelling never happening to enter my fingers at
the precise period during which a ship was up for London, and having a
young convictor that I could tell you nothing with postage across the Atlantic
have thus far failed in my design. -- Except this as a testimony that all
your silent friends have not been forgetful ones.
In Saturday afternoon however, the beauty of the weather induced
my cousin Catherine Roland, my sister Mary (who is here on a visit) and
myself to take a walk over to Charlestown for the purpose of paying a visit to
your parents. We found them just preparing tea, and it once concluded to
join the family party: meanwhile you mother produced your last packet, and
delighted us by reading it here. There are all much pleased with its contents
and in various reasons. In spite of the numerous testimonies you proof of
rapid progress in your profession, and still more in those you furnish of
the loyality of friend Conscience, whose minions will, I doubt not, at
ways be indebted to you, even though attend in a still small voice. I
have inquired from time to time, what at your father's, for particulars of your
situation, success and happiness, and your mother has been kind enough to show
me many of your letters, so that I knew pretty well what you do and
how you feel. -- But to return to the tea party. I consent to the eye of your
