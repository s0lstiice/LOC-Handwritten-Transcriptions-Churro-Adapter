Dear Brother
Charleston Novt 17th 1813.
We received your letter and medal a few
days since, and were highly gratified with the tokens
of your improvement in painting. The engraver of the medal was the handsome Mr. Stanhope, and
Goddard had not arrived. He transmitted the letters of from
Washington, and stated that he should sail to bring with him
the statue by the next ship. Allston's Poems have been re-
published in this country, and are very well spoken of. They
have been out of print but a few days, and have therefore
not yet seen recrud. I have read them & was much
pleased. Great expectations are entertained of his future
eminence.— About a month since I entered my name
in Mr. Rockwood's Office, where I shall stand six or eight
months.— Rich spent the winter with us engaged in miscellaneous
Mama, Richard & Nancy went to Woodstock last week to see
Grandfather. He is now 88 years old. He had received
from the train which he received by his fall, so far as to
go from one room to another. Mama showed him the golden
medals. I read your letter. He was much delighted with every
thing but the politics. Charles & Jedidiah to have gone with
their families settled in the Genesee country in the
west of N York. Lyman stays at Woodstock. I will visit
the homestead of the Moore family. Lyman had been in the
service of the State of Conn. to defend Delawors. Lyman
you have doubtless long since heard of the gallant exploit of
SPERRY in his crossing on Lake Erie. Our regrettings on this
occasion are mingled with sorrow. By the last mail from
the North Wilkeson with from 6 to 10,000 men was sailing
down the St Lawrence for Montreal. It was 100 miles distant.
Hampshire will join him with 4000 in the neighborhood of that
city. He ought to succeed. Our weather on this subject I
know differ from mine, but I shall not cease to desire it.
