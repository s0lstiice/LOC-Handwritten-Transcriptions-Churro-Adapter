opinions of politics, because I have completely diverted
myself of party, therefore I must be a democrat, this I
think is very unfair that I cannot differ at all from
Yoderahits but with one jump you set me down on the
other side. With out entering into the subject of politics
or giving you a detailed account of my reasons for think-
ing as I do, I declare once for all, and solemnly, that
I am as far from one party as the other, I despise them
both, I consider them both mistaken, each endeavoring
to establish a superiority over the other, without regard-
ing the the interest of the country at large, each would
rejoice, at the downfall of its opponent, events were
the disgrace of the United States the consequence, each
calling the other hard names, tell the whole Billingsgate
vocabulary, exhausted of its most vulgar epithets.
This may appear as were opinion, but it nevertheless ex-
actly my sentiments, I consider myself as old except
to have an opinion of my own, I can see and judge
for myself, but as I said in a former letter, my heart
is often to conviction, and whenever I find reason to
alter my opinion, and my conscience shall tell me I
am in the right I shall do it & not till then. At present
I see no reason to change my opinion, I am not Federal-
ist enough to wish for the disgrace of my country, nor
Democrat enough to wish for the destruction of England, but
I am just American enough to wish for a speedy and
honorable restoration of peace between us & Great Britain.
Thus endeth Politics.
