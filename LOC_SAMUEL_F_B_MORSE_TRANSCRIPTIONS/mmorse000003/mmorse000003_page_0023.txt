for nothing, or rather worse than nothing, in supporting the
pride of party, if you could be at this distance and see
how they appear here, you would throw off all
party feelings and be neither democrat nor federalist but
an American -- You can know more of this subject by con-
versing on it with Mr. Hurd, Mr. Emery, or any other American
who has been here and they will confirm what I have stated,
I could write more on this but I fear you will not be
pleased with it, unless you have the opportunity of knowing
so much on it as I have, who am in the centre of political
discussion. -- I write these sentiments the more freely as this letter
goes by Mr. Hurd, and of course cannot be in any danger of
being intercepted. I should not dare to write this thro' the post-
office --
I am in excellent health and spirits, and more
pleased with the profession I have chosen --
I wish I had time to write you something interesting &
new -- I could write volumes on what I have seen
in this strange country but it would employ so much of my
time, that I should neglect my studies, I shall write Edwin's
an account of some things; which as you will all see letters
that are in the family, I need not put in this letter.
I must now bid you adieu!
Remember me suitably to all friends, always, particularly
Grandma, Aunt & Uncle Salisbury, brothers Russell, Mr
Brown, Evarts, J. Bartlett's family, Nancy & in short to all to
whom you had this letter you are at liberty to make particular
mention. -- Hoping that my country will maintain her honor
in spite of party spirit; I remain yours very affectionately
Saints J. D. Moser
