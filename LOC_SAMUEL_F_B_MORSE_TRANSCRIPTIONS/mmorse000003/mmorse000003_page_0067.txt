Wholesale, Oct. 7th, 1812.
S. F. B. Morse
Sir,
I owe it to the attention of your
parents, that I have been informed of the
present opportunity to write to London. They
will write every thing particularly interest-
ing to you. I am happy that you are
so industriously & prosperously engaged
in the prosecution of your profession. I
hope you will let politics entirely alone
for many reasons, not the least of which
is a regard to the internal tranquility of
your own mind. I never yet knew a man
made happy by studying politics; nor use-
ful, unless he had great duties to perform
as a citizen. You will receive this advice,
I know, with your accustomed good nature.
I take the liberty of enclosing sev-
eral letters to your care, as I suppose the
bearer will deliver this in person. I do
not expect you to deliver them; but only
put them in the way to be delivered, either
by the penny post, or in any safe way.
The address of the letter to Mr. Bender you
will fill up if necessary, as you proba-
bly know where he lives. That letter has
a bill of exchange enclosed.
The letter to Collettta you will
put in a way of being forwarded through
Mr. Evarts joins me in best regards.
Yours sincerely,
Jeremiah Evarts
Oct. 10, 1812.
