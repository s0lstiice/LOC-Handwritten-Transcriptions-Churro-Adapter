the cliff with my two or three men who are constantly on
the look out with glasses and give notice of every thing that
passes in the channel, they lent us their glasses a few minutes,
through them we saw the houses on the shore, windmills in
motion, and men on board a French lugger, who were close
under the shore and towing an English sloop from Boulogne
to Calais, the cathedral of which latter place, we could just
discern with the glass: towards the north were seen the castle,
and the fortifications of Dover, which completed the view;
after having spent the day in looking at the different objects
worthy notice in and about Dover we retired to rest, determining
in the morning to return home to Margate, in the morning
however, I thought I could not leave Dover without paying a
mother visit to Shakespeare Cliff, this detained us till 10 o’clock
when we commenced our return, dined at Deal & spent 2
hours there, and arrived at Margate, a distance of 30 miles
a little after dark, this for experienced pedestrians, I
think is something of a fete; when we arrived at Margate
we found the hoy had just sailed so that we were under the
necessity of waiting another day, this day we employed, on a
fishing party which was very pleasant; the next day we took
our passage for London, where we arrived safe after a passage
of 30 hours, if I had picked the most pleasant days out of
the season I could not have pitched upon any more delightful,
the days were all clear, and not a drop of rain during
the whole time, a thing very remarkable and uncommon
