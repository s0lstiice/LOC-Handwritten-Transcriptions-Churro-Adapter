I am happy to hear that my last visit to Mr Taylor has at
length been brought to a settlement. I have not had time yet to
examine & compare it with my own, but think it is probably
correct. There is property sent me by Mr Taylor to a considerable
amount which remains uncollected in the greater part of which
the principal works have been forwarded regularly. Give my regards to Mr Taylor, inform him that I will
write him soon in answer to his letter, that I shall be glad to
receive the Panorama beginning with April 1808 (to which
time I have red them) on the terms he mentions, I will send
him the Panoptist regularly, & other publications to the time
of the Panorama. Would not Mr Taylor republish the
Georgian Epic? If you may confer with him on the subject.
Mr Smith is apt to make it inconveniently frequent to call in at
Mr Taylor's as he appears quite disposed to be friendly
to him. I request him to send me the continuation of Milton's
immediately after its publication. Many other valuable works
that it is to reprint here. A little attention to these things
will not too much divert your attention from your proposed
journeys, without greatly oblige me, I contribute to enable me
to transport you with in the present state of our affairs in
subject to as little inconvenience as I submit to
knowing your advantages are disposed to be found in your
expensurables.
You did right to take the package from the
post office. It was given to Capt Holmes in this town in the full
expectation that he would deliver it himself, or send it to the post office. I shall take a notice of the face of the
letter and send it here. He is a favorite writer with her and she has a great
desire to see the title of his countenance. I am confident I shall find it in a gentleman
with a tract about my business, his age & character.
