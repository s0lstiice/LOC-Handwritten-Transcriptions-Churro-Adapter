Rogers died suddenly last week of a bleeding and apoplectic
death. I do not now recollect any other deaths
in this town or his neighborhood among your acquaintances
or kindred of Weston died lately.
The business usual at our spring elections is commencing
little warmly, however, is yet visible. The controversy with Mr. H. Adams is up as I have
long appealed to the public in the last Centennial Edition
you may possibly find in this same paper you may see
a parallel, which may interest you.
Mr. H. Adams is well, I have done you often
friends here in Boston, your Uncle Robert Talbott, a
firm Cott. Martha Van Rensselaer family, I am
ill interested in your welfare & success, I recognise
unfortunately often you, when we see them -- I hope
Capt. Pratt has long since then answered safely -- I wrote
by him long letters let me know if you received them.
Remember me to my friends & correspondents as
you see them. I hope you call on them frequently --
Particularly to Mr. Alden, I am well -- he likes you
from either of them -- I am sorry they do not drop
me a few lines -- I believe they are well at
the Clamings -- He is to be married to his cousin
Gibbs, I am told next month -- Col. Gibbs is selling
his furniture today, is going to remove to Long Island
I must refer you, my dear son, to the Col. & your
Mama I have formerly written you
relating to your most important -- your ever lasting
concerns -- They are with great increasing weight
on our minds. We cannot not daily, from every day
to remember your affectionate, in our presence
