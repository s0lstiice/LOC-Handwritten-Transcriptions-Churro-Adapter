I shall follow your advice in not
being too sanguine respecting its success.
But hope is pleasant, and I shall therefore
indulge it until I hear from you again.
I am quite satisfied that it should go
at five hundred; and as soon as it is
sold I shall, according to my promise, be
at work on an elegant frame for it.
I have at last the satisfaction
to inform you that my large Picture
is in the British Gallery, and, more
happily in the place where Mr West's was.
You have added much to the above
agreeable intelligence by your favourable
account of the invalids at Mr Dungers.
Give our love to them all.
Yours with as much sincerity
as possible, H Hinton.
P. I. On your letter to me (received a week since)
you omitted the Fitzroy Square in the superscription.
You must recollect it in future; as some of
my letters have been lost in consequence of that
misfortune; there being several London Streets in London.
I wrote to my good Mr King the other day.
