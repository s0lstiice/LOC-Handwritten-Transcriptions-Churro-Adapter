and Thursdays we draw at the British Museum from 10 to all 5
o'clock: we got admission there thro' the influence of Mr. West.
The Museum contains a superb collection of antiques, and
other things very useful and necessary to be studied by artists.
Mama wished me to send some specimens of my painting, hoping
that you might see my improvement; the pictures that I now
paint would be uninteresting to you, they consist merely of
studies, drawings from plaster figures, hands and feet &c. Such
things; the portraits, are taken by those for whom they are
painted, I shall soon begin a portrait of myself, and will
try and send that to you, it will be an experiment, I wish
to try the effect of candlelight upon flesh, and shall take
no own head for the purpose, so if it should not be such
a thing as you wish, you must excuse it as it will be an
experiment. I feel that I am improving every day, for every day
gives me a greater insight into my art, and shows me how much
study and perseverance it requires to be a painter.
You sometimes mention the American hotel; there is
no such hotel in London, there is the N. England Coffee house, the
New York, Virginia, & Carolina. Your last letter mentions
not having received information respecting the draft on
Mr. Hope, it has been received and accepted; I wrote you
immediately on receiving it, and have mentioned it in 3 different letters;
the allowance for this year will carry me through
it I think clear, but I have been obliged to restrain myself
in many things which at home I could have indulged in:
the price of provisions in this country at the present time
is enormous, it requires the greatest economy to live in it, the
poor are greatly distressed for bread which is growing dearer
and dearer every day; I don't know what will be the consequence
if our embargo continues much longer, it distresses the people
here six times as much as it does you in America.
