at my writing in this strain, and may think some persons have put
these sentiments into my head, but I assure you they have not, for it
would have been as easy for a democrat to convince a federalist
in America as for me to be charged, when I first came, I only wish
you, and all those who are federalists, would come and stay on
London a month or two, if their party enthusiasm was not at
that time a little cool'd. I would engage to forfeit my right
hand, to observe the contempt, with which America is spoken of,
the epithets, of a nation of cheats, scroungers, pusills,
imboys, cowardly, and such like, these I think are sufficient to
make any true American's blood boil, these are not used by
individuals only, but on the floor of the House of Commons.
The good effects of our declaration of war, begun to be seen
already, the tone of their public prints is a little softer and more
submissive; not one has called in question the justice of the declaration
of war, all say "we are in the wrong, and we shall do to get out of it as soon as possible;" I have had conversations with
strangers who have not known I was an American, they put on
long faces and say "we are a ruin'd nation, america has been
too lightly treated by our government, unless america will retire
after the receipt of our revocation of those foolish Orders in Court
I do not see a single hole for us." I always acquiesce. I could
tell you volumes, but I have not time and it would perhaps be in
politic in the present state of affairs. But I only wish that among the
infatuated party men I may not find my father, and I hope that I
will be neutral, rather than oppose the war measure, for, (if he will
believe a son who loves him and his country the longer & farther
(from them) this war will reestablish, that character for honour,
