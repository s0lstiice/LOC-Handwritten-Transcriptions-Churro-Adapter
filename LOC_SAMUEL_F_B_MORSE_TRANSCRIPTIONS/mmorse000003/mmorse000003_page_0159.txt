Hope you have long er this called on Mr. Wilkerson
to Mr. Macaulay -- if not fail not to do it without
delay, I make to them your humble apology for your
neglect -- Present my respects to them both -- I will
write them when we have peace. With I hope will
be effected in the course of the Spring -- I presume
on your prudence, my son, in the then critical and
unhappy state of affairs between the two countries. Mind
your own business -- I leave political concerns, with
whose proper business it is to manage them. The New-
papers, with you will have only to read well in form
you of the train of events of every kind, in this
Country to them I refer you -- Young Lt. M.
Walker is to sail for France in a day or soon --
he goes out to spend a year in order to complete
his medical studies -- He may visit you in
before his return -- or Mr. Sp. the time, admit, you may
see him in Paris -- but you will not so think but
under the most favorable circumstances -- till
affairs shall become settled in that country -- I doubt
you by Mr. Walker but know not that it will ever
find you through that channel.
We are all pretty well -- Your Grandson Breese is
with us -- Your brother Edward is at home commencing his
law studies -- I am going to Lititz Field in a few months -- Ruth
was well at W. Haven a fine day ago -- Nancy & Bethey Cannon
are still with us -- All join in love to you, with your love
to me. Remember us to Mrs. H. Wilkerson, Mrs. Thomas Brownfield
(whose friends are in unusual health) & Mr. West -- Dr. Leake -- Your ever
affectionate father E. Morse
