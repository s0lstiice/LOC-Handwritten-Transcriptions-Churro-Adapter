You shall I thank Miss Russell for all her attention
and kindness to me, present her with my best respects for
the present, and assure her I feel grateful for her gifts, and will
ever long show her some testimony of my gratitude.
My good friends Mr. Brown & Mr. Evarts will excuse me for not writing them when they know how I am pressed for time, they cannot think it neglect when they
bare that my parents are the only persons with whom
I correspond.— If I had time I would willingly hold a
correspondence with them, and with many other dear friends
whom I shall never forget.
You mention that some of the Ralston family are
in Boston on a visit, and that Mr. Codman, is attached
to Eliza; once in my life, you know if you had told me
this, and I had been a very blood-sinned young man, who knows
but Mr. Codman might have been challenged, but I suppose he takes the advantage of my being in England; if it is
as you say I am very happy to hear it, for Elizabeth is a girl
whom I very much esteem, and there is no doubt but she will
make an excellent wife, if she is still in Boston remember
to hear of Mr. W. Ralston's death, it must have been a great
shock to the family, as I know he was a favorite; I sincerely con-
dele with them.
