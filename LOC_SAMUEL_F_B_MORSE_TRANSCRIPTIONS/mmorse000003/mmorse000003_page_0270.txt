My dear Brother, Your letters by the September & also the
medal gave us great pleasure. The politics however were
very disagreeable & occupied no inconsiderable part of your
letters. Your kind wishes for our reformation we must beg
leave to retard by hoping for your speedy amendment.
A friend who was here & heard the letter in which you
long'd to be in the navy, suggested it as a good subject
for you, to represent yourself "hastising the insolent
Englishmen." We do not however read the political
tracts to your friends. They are marked off & always passed
over. Your accounts of your excursions of your em-
ployments are what we like to hear. Indeed every step
you take gives us pleasure to know.
I am spending the year at home recruiting my health
which suffered from too close confinement last year. I gave
you an account of the journey we took with Dr. Dwight
since that I have taken another with Mann & Nancy
to Woodstock. We went in a hack, were gone 5 days. Mann
raps was comfortable. He has Aunt More to take care of
him. He moves from his room to the other to meals
by taking hold of the arms of his rocking chair & having
it between him. He has a stick suspended from the
wall over his bed & by taking hold of this with both
hands raises himself up from the bed. Walter is a
curiosity for his height six feet four inches & very slender.
He is quite a mechanical genius & is at the shoemaker's
trade. Charles & Peddicial had lately moved old
