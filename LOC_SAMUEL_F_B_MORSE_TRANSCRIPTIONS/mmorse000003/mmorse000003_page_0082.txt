chances to be in their company, the abuse of america is the
common topic of their conversation; This I am also a witness
of, for when I dined at Mr Macauly's a gentleman who
dined there, with me, while at table addressed some most
impertinent remarks to me, on the literary characters of
my country, saying that it would be long before we could boast
of any literary characters worth the notice of Englishmen, and
appealed to me for the truth of the observation; another young
gentleman at the same place, the moment he was introduced
to me as an american, addressed this as his first question
to me, "Pray how many ships have you in your navy?"
I answered him very candidly, repeating their names, "is
that all?" said he, and laughing in my face turned on his
heel and walked to another part of the room; Is this not
enough to stir up all the American blood in me? Judge then,
how can an American enjoy himself in the company of En-
glishmen! These are not exaggerations, I could relate instances
of the same kind without number which have happened to
Mr. A --- in to my roommate Mr. L ---, and to almost every
american with whom I am acquainted; Now is it possible
for a man born in a free & independent country, of
connections as good as any in England, to sit calmly
and hear his country abused, in so indecent a manner
as is so generally the case in the company of Englishmen; if so
he must be made of a more gentle spirit than mine; --
