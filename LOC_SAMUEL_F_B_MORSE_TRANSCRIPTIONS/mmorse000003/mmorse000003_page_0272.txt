Mr. Rockwood's office, mayt spring he goes to Litchfield &
probably will after that study in Connecticut or New-
York. What serious business I shall pursue at the end of
a twelve month is quite uncertain.
Papa keeps a horse so that we are able to ride. Edw.
spends much of our time at the Reading Room. a sort
of office where the respectable gentlemen meet.
Cousin Betsey Broce is spending the day with us &
sends her love to you.
The 74 will be launched by next June. The Constitution
is ready for sea. & the Trolic Hoop is in our harbor.
There is a great deal of smuggling. Many merchants
are engaged in manufactures & some loan their
money to Gov. By these means the war is opposed becoming less opposed
by the wealthy but the poor & the common people
will be the next to complain. There has been a surpris-
ing number of tragical events a consequence of the
war. An Indian & his squaw were wantonly shot at
Malden a day or two ago. Many soldiers shot for deser-
tion & many murders are committed.
I saw Edw. Cotton your former master in the avenue of
the Exchange Building. He is quite a sputtering politi-
cian & has changed his principles for he was labouring
to prove that the Canadians would not fight for their
Government but were quite willing to submit to the
U.S. I have nothing more at present.
Your affectionate Brother
R.C. Morse
