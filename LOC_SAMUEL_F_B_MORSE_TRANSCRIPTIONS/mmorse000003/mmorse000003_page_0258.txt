The Public Room, New Bond-Street,
Nov. 17th, 1813.
SIR,
THE Artists of the United Kingdom are respectfully invited to join a
select Number of Painters in an Exhibition of native Talent, at the commencement of the
ensuing Year, being a Period when only one Exhibition of Art is open, although the
Attendances required upon Parliament, the Courts of Justice, the Vacations of the
Universities, and all the Seats of Learning, cause the Metropolis to have a great influx of
its Residents and Visitors; they therefore purpose sending their Works here on the 5th
and 6th—open the 15th, of January; and close precisely on the last Day of March.
They feel anxious to receive Pictures which have not been exhibited;
but that Circumstance will not, in the present instance, be considered, as an Assemblage
of Talent is their chief object.
The Names of the associated Artists may be seen at the Room; no
Expense will attach, except a Commission of 5 per Cent. upon Works sold. Should this
Exhibition meet the expectations of the Public and Exhibitors, it is intended to be
continued Annually.
I am,
SIR,
Your respectful and obedient Servant,
WILLIAM LAXTON,
SECRETARY.
Printed by J. Furniss, 91, Wardour-Street, Strand.
