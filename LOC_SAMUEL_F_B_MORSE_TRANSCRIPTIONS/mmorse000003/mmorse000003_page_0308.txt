Chapleton Prison February 22d 1844
Respected Friend
I received your letter of the 19th. Your kind efforts for
my release, having failed, yet I shall hold in grateful remembrance
the exertions which you have made, and your solicitude for my
welfare. It is indeed a consolation to think that negociation
is going on for the purpose of putting an end to the miserable war.
I feel interested for my country's welfare as well as desire for
liberty from Prison. As to the probable result it is not for me
in the situation to form an opinion as means of information are cut
off. — No letters have as yet been received from America
by any of the prisoners from their friends arriving here expect
by the arrival of the Fair America at Liverpool. — A letter
has however been received from Mr Brown, harbour Master of
this port stating that he expected we would be on our passage home
before his letter reached England from this we expect something. —
I am in daily expectation of letters. — You mention that it
will be as late as May before you shall see me again.
I hope you will not think I trespass on your goodness in
aspiring the further assistance of two pounds, as I had made
some calculation, and if I can have it soon I will put me
in the way of some little business, by which I shall be able to
support myself comfortable, and will trouble you no further
until assistance from home. By enclosing it to the care of
Mr Sergeant it will come safe as many prisoners receive money
the way from different parts. — any way you may find out by a
receipt or an order shall be punctually attended to & as soon as I receive
letters from home by your grateful concern find a seat.
Mr S J B Morel.
Benny Burwell
