the palm of painting still rests with America, and
is, in all probability destined to remain with us; all
we wish is a taste in the country, and a little more
wealth; taste is altogether acquired, any man of good common sense, who will interest himself in
the thing and study, closely apply himself to acquire it,
may in a short time become, a man of taste, be able
to discover the different kinds of excellence in art,
separate the real from the fictitious, and justly to appreciate
merits. In order to create a taste, however, pictures, first-rate pictures, must be introduced into the country, for
taste is only acquired by a close study of the merits of the old masters; In Phila. I am happy to find they
have successfully begun; I wish Americans would
waste in the thing, throw aside local prejudices, and
give their support to one institution, let it be
in Phila. since it is so happily begun there, and
let every American feel a pride in supporting that
Institution, let it be a national not a city business,
then might the arts be so encouraged, that American
artists might remain at home, and not, as at present
be under the painful necessity of exciting themselves
from their country and their friends. This will
come to pass in the course of time, but not in my day,
