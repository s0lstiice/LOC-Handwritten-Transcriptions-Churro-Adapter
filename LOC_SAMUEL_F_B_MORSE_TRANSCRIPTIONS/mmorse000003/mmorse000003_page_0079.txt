one; 'tis not like making an acquaintance in America,
before you can feel at ease in an English family, you must
visit them constantly, almost beg for a shake of the hand,
and be met with such cool, suspicious, looks as is enough
to freeze the warmest heart; I do not blame the English for
it; they have so often been deceived by impostors that they
must be on their guard; but for all this I cannot relish it;
I can't go into a family, where at the first, they look upon
me as an impostor, or are prepared to think so; I, who have
been accustomed in my own country to be received in so warm
a manner; this objection, however, might be got over, did
it not take up so much time, every moment is precious
to me now, I don't know how soon I may be obliged to return home,
for want of means to support me, for the difficulties which are increasing in this country, take off the attention of people from the fine arts, and they with hold that
patronage from young artists, who they would, from their liberality, in other circumstances, freely bestowed. -- You may
say that I need recreation, I find recreation enough in the various parts of my art; if I want exercise, I walk
to the different galleries of the artists, & replenish my mind with images &c, and at home I always find employment,
in studying out my pictures, and in writing to you. -- With regard to the Geoq. I shall not be able to realise any thing from it;
