three, and being accustomed a little to the sea, I soon found
my sea-legs, while the rest of the passengers were tumbling
about at a pitiful rate, we got into Margate after being out
26 hours which is considered a long passage.— Margate is a
gay place and it was crowded with visitors while I was there;
My companion was a Mr Earl an artist whose father was an
American, we staid in Margate two days and then proposed
an excursion to Ramsgate about 4 miles from Margate, this we
accomplished in an hour; we staid but a short time in Ramsgate,
it is another watering place, and I think a much more beauti-
ful place than Margate; its pier is one of its greatest artifi-
cial curiosities it is a quarter of a mile in length, is circular in
its form, and encloses a large basin for the reception of vessels,
from this place is seen the Downs so celebrated for its ships
anchorage; (our excursion recollect is pedestrian) we went from
Ramsgate to Sandwich along the cliffs which bound all this
shore composed of chalk, we dined at Sandwich, it is remark-
able only for its age, having been built in the time of the Ro-
mans; just before we entered Sandwich, we saw a pile of
ruins about a mile on the side of the road; and we crossed
over the fields to see it, but just as we came within a stone's
throw of it, we were stopped by a wide river which runs
by the foot of it, so, finding no possible means to come at it
we were obliged to return without gratifying our curiosity,
I have since heard that it was a famous Roman & Celtic ruin
called now Richborough castle; after dinner we pursued our
