of his poetical effusions, I will transcribe a specimen
of it which will give you a better idea of the man
than all I can say of him: the following he wrote on
our leaving NewYork;
Farewell to NewYork Manhattan's fair isle
May plenty and trade make the citizen smile
On board the Lydia each man takes his glass,
The next, sweethearts, and wives, is the theme of our toast
The cocks proclaim the morn, and the ducks quack the worst
With fortepiano & flute, we charm the gods where Neptune
reigns
Whilst gliding over the watery main
This is a true copy & word for word as he wrote
it; the two first lines are uncommon for him they really
do him great credit -- the sixth line was our phrase
which we were constantly repeating afterwards through
the whole voyage -- As to the rest of the passengers none
were so eccentric as to need particular description
they were gay, civil, & accomodating, each seemed desirous
to contribute his share to the diversion of the whole;
some parts of the day we were entertained by music; one
