New Haven February 15 1811
Dear Kinley
Yours of the 6th ult. I received together with
the books enclosed, which I delivered personally, according to your request.
Did I not know the nature of your desire & the state of your
govern'd I should really be surprised at the commencement & indeed
the whole tenor of your letter but as it is, I can excuse & feel for
you. Have I commended a letter with the French Helas! it
would have been no more than might reasonably have been expected
considering the desolate situation of NewHaven & the gloomy prospects
before me but for you, who are in the very center of fashionable life
I surrounded by the amusements & bustle of the metropolis of New
England, for you to proclaim "how lonely I am" is uncomparable or at
most admits of but one excuse (to wit) that you can plead the fulness
of the youth who exclaimed "Gods annihilate both time & space & make
two lovers happy". You suppose I am so much taken up with the
ladies & other good things in N.H. that I have not time to think
of one of my old friends, - shall I Morse, there are no ladies or any
thing else to occupy my attention they are all gone & we have no
amusements - even old Value has deserted us, whose music though
an assembly of unharmonious sounds is infinitely preferable to the harsh
grating thunder of his Brother - NewHaven is indeed the worst place
