My Dear Parents,
It is an opportunity to write to you a few moments for the purpose; I wish I had been able to write you a longer and more particular letter, which I intend doing soon, for I can scarcely find leisure at present to eat my meals; I feel every day an increased enthusiasm, and encourage you that I had received your leave by the Baltimore and Liverpool packet, and have also sent you letter No. 12, by the Miss Lynders, Gen. Lemans daughters who embarked for Philadelphia in a few days in the ship Aurora Maria.
I delivered your parcels to Mr. Burder, he is a very friendly good man, who receives me very kindly; -- Mr. Duncan Smith likewise, Mr. Evarts's clasmate, is very attentive to me, and desires remembrance to Mr. Evarts. On Tuesday last I dined at Mr. Wests, who requested to be particularly remembered to you he is extremely attentive and polite to me; I have just purchased a sketch of his face from it and send it to you; when I return home I shall bring it with me as I consider it very valuable -- Mr. Hurd and Mr. Parkman are still here, they are often in and out at my lodgings and are well. -- I have changed my lodging to No. 82 in the same street, and have room with Mr. Leslie of Philadelphia who has just arrived, he is very promising, and a very agreeable gentleman, we are in the same stage of advancement in the art. I have painted 5 pieces since I have been here, 2 landscapes and 3 portraits, one of myself, one a copy from Mr. Wests copy from Van Dyke, and the other a portrait of Mr. Leslie, who is also taking mines.
