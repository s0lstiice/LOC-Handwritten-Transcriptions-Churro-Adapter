my own interest to be at home; if I stay here I
shall certainly do nothing, but if I am at home
I shall soon feel inclined to study. However, in this
business I shall not proceed rashly, I intend going to
consult Dr. Dwight concerning it, and shall do as he
advises me; I think I would give you this informa-
tion lest you should be surprised at seeing me of
come home in such a sudden manner.
There is nothing of importance that I can
mention to you, the College is in a prosperous state;
There has been one instance of the yellow fever in this
city & four or five of the putrid fever, it is now in
this place, but I know not whether we are in much
danger of catching either of them -- Adieu!
Yours etc.
P.S. Edwds is well.
J. S. B. Morse
