The accounts lately received from America
look rather gloomy, though thought here to be
more threatening, from my own observation and oppor-
tunity of hearing the opinion of the people generally
they are extremely desirous of an amicable adjust-
ment of differences, and seem as much opposed to
the idea of war as the better part of the American
people. In case of war I am doubtful whether
I should return home, or go to Paris (as Americans
would be ordered out of the country) but that probably
should choose the latter.
In this letter you will percieve all the variety
of feeling which I have had for a fortnight past, sometimes
in the balance of equality of each, which, latter tho' most desirable
I seldom have, but generally am at one extreme or the other. I wrote this in the evenings of
the last two weeks, and this will account, & I hope, for a fortnight past, sometimes
apology for its great want of connection; I know I am writing to my friends, & such friends, as will
overlook its faults, & I must now conclude my dear friends, by requesting you to remember me to all
my friends; to your parents, respectively, to Miss Ann Hart, Mary Ann, Jennette, Augusta, & the others, to
the Miss Stuarts, Miss Isaaces, to my cousin Capt. & Mrs. Stout and in short to all who inquire after me; & receive the further assurance of the lasting
friendship, & esteem of your sincere
Miss F. with your affectionate regards
Friend Bancroft J.B. Morse
