vailed, but still I do not feel the less desirous to hear
from you -- you may always be certain of this, that I
shall never forget you, it is as impossible, as for you
to forget me -- Mr. Allston mentioned to me that he
was once eighteen months without hearing a syllable
from home.
I mentioned in one of my other letters that
I had drawn a figure (the Gladiator,) to admit me
into the academy; -- after I had finished it, I was
displeased with it, and concluded not to offer it, but
attempt another; I have accordingly drawn another
from the Laocoon statue, the most difficult of all
the statues, have shown it to the keeper of the acad-
emy, and am admitted for a year, without the
least difficulty; Mr. Allston was pleased to compli-
ment me upon it by saying that it was better than
two thirds of the drawings of those who had been
drawing at the academy for two years; -- I feel en-
couraged likewise when I think that those who have
