During my first night at the camp, I was much at a loss to find a subject on
which I might write. I composed myself to sleep, in
expectation that a dream would disclose to me some
incident from which I might be able to extract matter
sufficient to form a passable composition. Accordingly,
after the usual confusion which takes possession of the
senses immediately on falling into a sleep; a beau-
tiful prospect of New Haven and its delightful harbour
gradually opened to my view; from surrounding objects
I soon perceived that imagination had placed me on
the summit of East Rock. Among the various scenes
which then attracted my attention, the sun half sunk
beneath the horizon, gleaming with its setting rays a
dreadful cloud, was among the most interesting. I had
scarcely time to contemplate this awful sight before
the approach of the threatening storm warned me
to seek some shelter from its fury; I accordingly bent
my way towards the thickest part of the forest, in hopes
that I might meet with some humble shed in which
I might repose myself until the strength of the
blast had spent itself; but I searched in vain; the
darkness increased; the tempest roared; and the light-
ning, flashing at short intervals, served but to disco-
ver to me the gloominess of my situation: I thought
of returning, but instead of pursuing the way thro' which I had entered the wood, I turned into a different
path.
