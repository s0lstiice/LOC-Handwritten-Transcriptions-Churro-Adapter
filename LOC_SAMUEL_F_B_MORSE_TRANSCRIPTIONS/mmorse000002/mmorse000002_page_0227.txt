on the road; The first place after leaving
Liverpool was a small village called Prescot
from which we proceeded to Manchester thro’
two other small villages, Warrington & Golam.
Manchester next to London is the largest city
in England, it is handsomely situated and we
had a beautiful prospect of it about a mile
before we entered it, it is celebrated for its
manufactories of cotton stuffs, such as cambrics,
mustings, dimittes, lace, tape, &c. on every side we
passed we saw them advertised; all those kinds
of goods which we have in America come from
this place. The next day we pursued our jour-
ney, the country thro’ which we passed was de-
lightful beyond conception, every thing, & every-
thing, the appearance of industry and prosperity, cultiva-
ted fields, as far as the eye could reach, with
here and there an old Gothic tower rising thro’
the trees, & the cottages of the peasantry clustered
together in picturesque villages, all formed a
scene in the highest degree romantic & beauti-
ful. We passed the Stockport, Macclesfield,
