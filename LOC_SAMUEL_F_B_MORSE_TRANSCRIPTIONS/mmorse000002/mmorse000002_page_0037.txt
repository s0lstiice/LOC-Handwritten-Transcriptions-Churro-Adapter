Goddard has been here, and behaved very well; I write
however, give up his company, for I see many things in him
which has disputed me. His father wrote him a very
long & serious letter; he read it to me, it was similar
to many of your letters to us, giving him a great deal
of good advice, and one would suppose from the contents
of the letter that he (Dr Goddard) was a Christian; I did
not know before, that, Dr G. was such a serious man, if Elisha
is not entirely hardened. That letter must have affected
him some -- I suppose the family must be in great distress,
for I really believe they are a very amiable family,
to take them generally -- Elisha's letter was enclosed in
one to me, written by Dr Goddard, thanking me for the
good advice I had given Elisha, & wished me very much
to give him some suitable to his situation, &c. I accordingly
did, but I doubt whether it will have any good effect;
I feel very well today, and am now pursuing my studies with redoubled ardour to
make up for the time in which I have been unwell.
I should think you ought to make some allowances
for my not writing you; I have been so unwell that
I have not been able to do any thing at all, for two
or three days, at a time -- Edwards has not written
but one or two letters this term; & he is not censured,
whereas I have written 6 and it appears that 3 have
miscarried & 1 is on the road; I hope you will get
them all soon, & relieve me from my pains, I wish you
would answer this as soon as you receive it; for I
shall be very much hurt until, I know that you
are convinced that I am not to blame -- I am now
perfectly contented & happy, am more & more pleased
with a college life; have not missed prayers nor recitation,
for our week past (the time I began to feel better) and
shall not again till commencement.
