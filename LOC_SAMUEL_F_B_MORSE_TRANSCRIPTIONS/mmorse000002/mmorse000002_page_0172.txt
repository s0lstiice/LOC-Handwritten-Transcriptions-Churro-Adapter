the arts, there are few means of improvement.
These are to be found in their perfection, only
in older countries, & in none perhaps greater
than in yours. In compliance, therefore, with
his earnest wishes, I hope if his friend & patron, Mr
Alston, (with whom he goes to London,) we have
consented to make the sacrifice of feelings (not a
small one) & a pecuniary exertion, to the utmost of our ability, for the purpose of placing him
under the best advantages of becoming eminent
in his profession, in hope that he will consecrate his acquisitions to the glory of God &
the best good his fellow men.
You will easily conceive, Sir, what must
be our solicitude for & for his at critical age,
removed at such a distance from us, with little experience or knowledge of mankind, in a land of strangers, & exposed to the various evils & dangers of the world. The same kind consideration however
that kept him at home is able to preserve him
abroad, to him we have committled him.
It is a great satisfaction to us & to him, that
he is to reside in England, where the same language is spoken, & the same religion is
practised.
