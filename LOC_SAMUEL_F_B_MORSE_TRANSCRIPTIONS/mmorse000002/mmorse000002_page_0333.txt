Mr. Allston is very busily engaged about a very large
picture, and requested you to tell you that if he has
not received the other mention in my letter as soon as he does
he will write again, he and Mr. Allston desire particular remembrance to you, they are both in excellent
health, and spirits. -- Mr. Allston regularly meets all men, that aim improving
very fast, and getting along very well.
I have called twice on Mr. Taylor about Papa's business, he is a man whose time like Papa's is very precious, he
has appointed, next Tuesday a week, to settle the business I shall call on him then and inform you of the result immediately after, he is an honest, good man, from all accounts
and will act honorably.
I thank you for the letter from Mr. Filliman to Col. Inman
bull, but I should wish not to have any more letters of introduction to any body else, I have not yet delivered those which
I have received. I find they only retard my progress, for were I
to pay the due attention to every one I have had letters to,
all my time from morning till night would be taken up in preserving the acquaintance, I begin to wish I had
brought but one or two; I think letters of introduction are less necessary to a young artist than
to any other class of people, for his attention to his art ought never
to be diverted; now a young man studying divinity or physics
or any thing else can bear to have his mind diverted, and it
would seem necessary, but it appears to me it is not necessary for an artist, he can find amusement enough and dancing
too, in his art, for my own part I feel sometimes, that I should like to live entirely abstracted from society, and devote myself solely to painting, but that I know it is impracticable. If I should wish for letters of introduction to any
particular person, or to any particular place, I will write you
