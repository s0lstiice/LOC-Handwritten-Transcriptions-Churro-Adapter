Mr Isaac Hurds is in London and also Mr Parkman the Clergyman, they will spend the winter here and return home in the spring, they are very friendly and sociable, we shall probably meet every day at our chef-house to dinner, as it is situated between us, we live about 3 miles apart, they have taken lodgings at No 36 Norfolk street, Strand, a few moments walk from Somerlot house, where I go every evening to draw, so now I have company and it keeps me in excellent spirits, Mr Parkman has just returned from Paris. The Higginsons here are bankrupt which will undoubtedly cause the same in tune to the partners in Boston, his B2
Stephen &c. The circumstance reminds me of an observation of Mama's, that those who endeavored to injure Papa, were sure to meet with some punishment, and I consider this in that light, tho' I feel very much for the Higginsons here as they are very benevolent and are considered excellent people.
I have delivered my letter to Sir Joseph Bankes he received me very kindly. I must close this letter as it goes via New York. if you have any more orders for books or the like, I will get them ready to send by Mr Parkman or Mr. Keene in the spring as now it is impossible to send anything on account of American measures. Distresses are increasing in this country and disturbances, riots &c. have commenceded, as you will see by the papers which accompany this, they are considered very alarming.
I can only add give my love to all whom I mention in the other letters, especially relations, and Mrs Russell, and Mrs O. Brown, Nancy &c. &c.... good bye. Your affectionate son
Sam'l F. B. Moslem
