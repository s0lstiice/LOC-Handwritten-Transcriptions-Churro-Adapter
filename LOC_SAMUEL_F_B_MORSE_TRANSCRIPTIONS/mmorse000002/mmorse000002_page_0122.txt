Yale College. N. Haven. Jan 29th 1811
Dear Parents.
The vacation is about to close - a vacation
which has been of little use to me in the way of improvement. For the first few days I was occupied in arranging
the books belonging to the Brother's Library and preparing a Catalogue for the Prep - After that business was accomplished
I attended the Superior Court for a week, and heard several lawyers upon interesting cases - Since that time I have
kept my room, and spent my time in idleness. Richard has been confined there with a sore swelling upon his
seat - The Doctor has seen it and says that it is a swelling which goes by no particular name - that it will break
in the course of a day or two, and discharge - As soon as Richard recovers from this sore he proposes to start
for Charlestown - thinking I suppose that home will be a cure for every disorder. I have so little confidence however
in this sovereign remedy, that the next blood is not in the best state, and tho' I suspect I am possessed of the State, I
shall nevertheless remain at N. Haven. If my suspicions respecting this doctor should prove true, what would
you advise me to use for a cure - No mercenary interest of course. I wait your orders. Safford arrived on
Saturday evening, and brought us your very desirable present. Richard was then confined with his son and the
reception of such articles at such a time was to him peculiarly pleasing. The pie we ate was a rich pie - I
think I shall not go to the office again until the end of the month. I am your affectionate son
John H. Safford
