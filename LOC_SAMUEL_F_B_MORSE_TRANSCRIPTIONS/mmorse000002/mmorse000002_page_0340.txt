Mr. and Mrs. Parry.
My Dear Friends,
I have long been waiting in anxious expectation
for an answer to some of my letters, this is the fourth I
have written to Miss Dannette and an one to Miss Mary Isaac, the two last with one other to
you went by the ship Louisa for NewYork in Octr. last, we
have heard of her arrival at this port, I hope you have received
some of them, I wrote one by the Lydia the ship in which
she has been to NewYork, and has arrived again at Liverpool
but I have received no intelligence since I have been here from
NewYork altho' I have no less than 6 correspondents and some
too my clafmatip and have written them all once and
some twice -- but I will not complain, I never can suppose I am forgotten; the distance is great and there are thousands of accidents to which a letter is liable in passing such a
distance; my letters have probably miscarried or are delayed on the way, I am still on the expectation I am
not so particular as some who never write a second letter till their first is answered; so I will improve an opportunity that offers for the purpose, and write my fourth
letter.
I have been employed for my own part in pursuing my
studies, in my art, for which I feel greater enthusiasm than
ever; the fine collections of the nobility open always to artists,
the conversation of men of the highest rank in the art, and
especially the advice and hints of Mr. West, all conspire to keep that enthusiasm alive, and, I am as happy as
any one can be who is absent from his native country and
friendship. I dined with Mr. West last Tuesday with a
number of artists, Mr. West is now in his 6th year, from his appearance you would not suppose him to be
less than 50 years of age, his white locks give a venerable appearance, he has a countenance full of dignity and
his manners are polished, and the moment I am in his
company, he is a very mild affectionate domestic in his attentions and affection for Mrs West who is in her dotage
still remains as ardent as ever, he has declined many invitations I foregone many pleasant engagements for the purpose of affording comfort, by his company, to his wife
