the 1st Sunday in the month of February, 1820, in search of John Cooper;
I now despair of reforming him; I christen great hopes of
making him, in process of time, a perfect companion for myself;
But these hopes are now blasted; However, I do not know
but, reflection, (if he is not entirely hardened) with a parting letter
from me, may not have a good effect on him, if it does
I hope you will not deprive me from encouraging him to
ample & advice when I come home. In any rate I hope
you will not forbid my visiting the family, you will not
hear my being led astray by Eliska, & I have too much regard
for my own character, to be seen walking the streets
with him. I shall at first receive him very coolly, and if this
has no effect on him, I shall abandon his company altogether, or
special with him no farther than is consistent with civility. Ward & Walker
shall in future be my most intimate intimate friends; I wish you would
tell them by some means, that I do not forget them as little
I do not write them; they will see me as receive a letter
from me in a short time. Campbell has been here, and
has obtained liberty to be examined for my class, he has since
returned home & concludes not to be examined until
commencement. Barnes & Edmonds are well, I am still
the same as to my health & think it very probable I shall
be at home in a short time. I feel myself very much
to blame for not writing you often, than I have done
hitherto, I will mend in this particular.
Please excuse me from writing more
often. Yours, etc
S.S.B. Morre
