My dear Son,
I write you a long letter (accom
panied with a package of newspapers, pamphlets & pamphlets) by the Galen, which was to have sailed yesterday,
but the aspect of things at Washington has detained her
(which I suppose is permitting) the expect to sail on the next Sabbath. I have
written to-day, a hearty letter to go by the Brig Amherst
(which I suppose is permitting) for Liverpool, with advertisements to sail this forenoon, to Mr
Brownfield -- Perhaps that will reach London before this,
or the one by the Galen. -- I send this day young Tom
Edes, a son of the late Thomas Edes, who will go to
London, I probably see you -- I have little to add to
what I lately wrote by the Galen -- Your good mother intends writing you one of her good letters
to Governor by this officer -- I shall leave to her
to say many things, what I might otherwise write you.
Mr. Hunt has received two letters from his son in
London, since your last received (of Nov. 25th) one dated in Dec. the
other in Jan. 4th in both he mentions you as well -- He began
to feel quite anxious, that we have not heard from you
for so long a period -- longer than any time you left us -- We
hope there is not to be any falling off from your good beginnings, we don't suspect it but as in other causes for
the delay, such as you have often suggested in your letter --
We wish to know whether your orders for printing, &c
