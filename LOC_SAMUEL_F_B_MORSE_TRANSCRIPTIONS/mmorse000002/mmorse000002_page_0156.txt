I take very good care of myself by keeping
very quiet in the house. Malina will not be
anxious on that account. My ride to New-Orleans
was beyond every thing disagreeable; the sun
beating down upon the stage (the sides of which
we were obliged to shut up on account of the sun) was
like an oven, and the wind, instead of being
in our faces as Papa supposed was at our
rack, & brot' into our faces such columns of
dust as to hinder us from seeing the other
side of the stage; I never was so completely
exhausted with dust before in my life. Men
perhaps will think that I experience some little
revenue from such a fatiguing journey; but I
never felt better in my life than now. We
have 14 passengers in the Lydia among them 4
ladies; none at present except Mr. Allston & Lady
with whom I am acquainted. Will write you
soon again; till then adieu!
Your very affectionate
Son
Sam'l F. B. Merrill
N.B. Your letter enclosing Mr.
Grays came safe to hand this
morning.
