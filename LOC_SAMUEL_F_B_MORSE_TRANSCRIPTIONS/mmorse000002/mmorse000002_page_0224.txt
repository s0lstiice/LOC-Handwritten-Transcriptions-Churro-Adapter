Thus ended a voyage thro’ the whole of which I
cannot recollect a single disagreeable circumstance
and which was performed in the remarkable short
time of 22 days. I then went with the rest of
my fellow passengers into the city to look out for
lodgings, we at length fixed upon the Liverpool
Arms, the same hotel at which Professor Silliman
staid while in Liverpool. I saw the same French-
man whom he describes in his journal, he did not
seem to show the same attention to his guests as for-
merly. What little leisure I could get I employed in
looking round the city, it is situated on the river
Mersey, the streets are very irregular and dirty,
it is about the size, and has the same no. of inhab-
itants as NewYork, but I think NewYork far surpasses
it in beauty; the public buildings are in a
magnificent taste, but have a black, smoky ap-
pearance which arises from the custom of burning
sea coal almost altogether, the private dwelling
houses likewise are very plain, there is scarcely
to be seen throughout Liverpool a 4 story house they
rarely ever exceed three.— I met with no difficulty
