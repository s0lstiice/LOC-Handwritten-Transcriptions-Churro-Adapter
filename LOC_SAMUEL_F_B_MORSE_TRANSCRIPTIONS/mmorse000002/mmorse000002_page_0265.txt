My Dear Friend, I have heard of your safe
arrival in London, and that you had a
short and pleasant passage, on which event
give me leave to congratulate you. — Also
I have received nothing from you, the which
I attribute to your having been occupied in
writing to your family, and those who perhaps
have a greater claim on your favour than
myself, yet I do hope you will have written
me before you receive the present, it will at
least manifest a disposition on your part
to cultivate a correspondence from which I
assure you I should promise myself much pleasure.
I saw your father a few days
since, he desired me if I wrote you to say the
family were well, your friends here generally
I believe are also all well; I wish I had any
thing else of interest to communicate, picture
to yourself Charleston and every thing that appears therein following its old routine without
interruption and you will see its present state,
it is as pleasant this year as it was the last, tho'
the society has rather thereand, that is the society
of young ladies, which I suppose is all young men
