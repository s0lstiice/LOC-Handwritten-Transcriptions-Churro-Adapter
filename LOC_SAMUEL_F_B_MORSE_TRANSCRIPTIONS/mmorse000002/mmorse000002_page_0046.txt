explain to you hereafter. Vacation commences in a week from
next Wednesday; & then I shall have some respite from my
labors; there will be a great number of students who will re-
main here & keep me company, so that I shall not be liable
to feel homesick.
I hope you will excuse me for not writing you often
than I have done this term, for you know how busy I am
employed in studying. You mentioned in a former letter that
you hoped we visited at the places you told us, but I had
not been out of the college yard before I came here, when I
went to see him at Butler's tavern it was the first time I
had gone out of the yard. So you see I have no time for vis-
iting; indeed it is not a popular thing among the students to
go on visiting; they think the person is more pleased with
company than his "Books"; now I don't wish to be that
of in this way until I get out of college I will then
visit as much as you desire me to but whilst I am
in college, I should desire to be free from such cares, for you
have often inquired upon me this maxim, "Do one thing first."
now whilst I am studying I wish to make a business of it
and when I visit them I will visit.
Give my love to all the family especially Nick,
his clap is a very good one, & makes progress as I have no doubt
he does; Edw's will inform him of their studies as I have
other business to attend to. This letter must last a long while
for it is a long one. I wish you all a happy new year.
I hope mama is well; write me soon. Edw's & I am well.
I may possibly find a letter in the office for me from you I have
not received one this long time. I must bid you Adieu as it is late
and I am considerably fatigued. Your affectionate son
Samuel F. B. Morse.
