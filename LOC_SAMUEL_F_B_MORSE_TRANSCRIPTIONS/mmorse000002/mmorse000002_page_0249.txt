In my last long letter I told you I believe every
thing that was of importance; I have but little opportu-
nity of seeing any thing being shut up from morning till
night. One thing which annoys me very much is the
various cries of London; you may perhaps recollect I
was describing to you one of Hogarth’s pieces called the
enraged musician, in which every noise was collected under
his window; but I have experienced every day the same
noises, for at a certain part of the day the whole variety
of chimney sweeps, ballad singers, dust cart men with bells, beggars
by hundreds; and old women with wheelbarrows, some with
potatoes, some with apples, some with fish, rabbits, fleurs, &c
in fact every different article you can think of, & all
these crying at once their different cries; when I first came
here, I that some of the cries were made by boys who were
mocking these old women, they were such terrible noises, but
upon looking out of the window I found it was made by
half a dozen old women collected together at the corner of
the street to try to see what could scream the loudest.
I find I cannot send any thing home, not even
Mr. Collier’s books which he wished me to forward as soon
as I could, when the nonintercourse is taken off I will
send them; I endeavor also to send you a Bilton cheese
which are the finest that are made in this country, I would
suit Papa’s taste exactly, as they keep sound but a short time,
