Things remain in this town pretty much
after the old sort. The talk made for a time
some noise in consequence of the violent opposition
made to it by some individuals. And
within a few days I learn that a petition is
in circulation, to be presented to the Legislature,
to let aside the act incorporating this Parish.
I have very little anxiety about the issue,
it will be what it may. Every thing appears to
be in an unsettled & in calculable state.
Our children are our greatest earthly comfort.
Your affectionate concern for our happiness has returned
for ours in regard to you, yielded us much
consolation under all our disappointments. Truly
what are not a few -- Your letters have afforded
us much satisfaction -- continue to write us -- We
cease not daily to remember you in our prayers. For
if not my son, to remember your parents, brothers in
spirit, be virtuous & be happy.
Your friends here remain much attached to you &
enquire after you affectionately. I wish to be remembered
to you. I enclose you the $100.00 bill -- 1st P. J. Ford -- before I shall write
you by the Galen -- Regards to Mrs. H. Clinton. Your affectionate father
Emerson
