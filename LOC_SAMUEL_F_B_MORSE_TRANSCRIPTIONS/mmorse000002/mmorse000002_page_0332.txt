My Dear Parents,
I have the pleasure to inform you that I
have just received your good letters by the ship Pluto
for which I thank you; I must relate a circumstance
however, with respect to my getting them; Mr Brownfield wrote
to me and said there was a parcel for me, in the Post Office
which he supposed contained newspapers only from home, and
that the charge of Postage was 2s 5d or 10 dollars, wishing
to know if I would take them at that enormous charge
or let them remain in the post office; I immediately
waited on him to see about it, and told him there were
several letters in it, wishing to know what I could do; he
told me he would go with me to the post office and see
what could be done; I then went with him, and stated the
case to them, telling them, I was willing to pay the postage
of all letters contained in it, and told them they might open
it, which they did, but, as good luck would have it, they
found a corner of the letters which were in the newspapers, they
told me as there were no letters I might have it by paying
15 shillings, I was going to decline, when by accident I discovered
a corner of the letters, and wrapping it up, lest they
should make another search, I paid them the 15 shillings and
took it, although that was a great sum for postage being about
a dollar a piece for each letter; I hope you will not blame
me for paying this neither than let them stay in the office, when
you consider the case, that I do not very often hear from home
and how great the temptation was, knowing that there were
several letters directly from home, and that if I paid 15 shillings
I could take them and not without, but I will not palliate,
I know you won't blame me, they were letters from home.
But to remedy this in future, you will find it the
least expensive to put the letters separate into the letter box,
the postage then is only 1 penny each letter, and perhaps you
will not expect me to pay postage on letters which are
not intended for me.
With all my love to your family
Ever affy
W. B. A.
