I shall on the strength of this go to work and con-
tend for the prizes which will not only gain me
credit but money; the prize for the best historical
piece on a given subject is upwards of 200 guineas
beside the piece which remains with the artist; this
prize however is at present beyond me, I must
creep along gradually towards it -- I have
completed a landscape, which I shall show to Mr.
West in a day or two; I am at present copying a
portrait of Van Dyke's from a copy by Mr. West, a
fine picture; I shall probably have finished
that landscape by the next exhibition at the
Royal Academy, and may offer it for exhibition, but
it would indeed be a wonder if it should be accepted.
I can however but try -- I am getting along com-
fortably, I once in a while am troubled with the
return of my colick -- This is but a stupid letter
for to tell the truth, I feel very stupid, owing to a
cold I have had for some time; my next letter
shall be more particular and longer -- This will
probably go by the Orion via New York, and as she sails
to-morrow, I must close. I send a file of Sunday
newspapers, which contain a summary of the week's news:
I shall write you again soon; so good bye; give my love
to all those whom I have mentioned in my other letters,
In haste Your affectionate Son Sam'l F. B. Morse
