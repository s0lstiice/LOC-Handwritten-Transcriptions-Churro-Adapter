Of all the subjects, which are discussed by mankind,
no one affords more real satisfaction than the contempla-
tion of Beauty. Whether we consider the beauty of the
human form and features or the beauties of nature &
art, each of them are equally interesting. However with-
out dwelling any great length of time, on either of
these abstractedly, it is my intention briefly to con-
sider them all promiscuously.
The principles of beauty are three, viz. Variety,
Proportion, and Uniformity. It needs little
demonstration to convince a person that the continued
repetition of any one thing, is disagreeable; a person
cannot view with pleasure, a painting, where the only
object that strikes his eye is water, he would imme-
dately pronounce it insipid; but if a ship, or a rock
were introduced, it would then afford variety, and
of course have a great claim to beauty. From this
it might seem that the greater the variety, the more
beautiful the scenery would appear; but this is far
from being the case, for a confused assembly of
objects, without fitness or connection is quite as dispris-
ing as an unvaried sameness. The eye with respect to
variety is very analogous to the ear: you may all have
