My health is very good and has been so since
I have been in London excepting a few dull turns
which are natural to me, you know, they seldom
however, last more than half a day; it would
indeed be singular if I should not have them
in November, as you probably know it is called
the Englishman's month, in which more
suicides are committed than in all the other
months together; this November however, has I
understand, been unusually fine, and if it
has, the month of November in its true state must
be dismal indeed, for almost every morning as
yet, till 12 o'clock it has been so dark, that one could
not see to read without sitting by the window
and the appearance of objects, in and out of doors,
was the same as appears on looking thro' a piece of
a green glass bottle; but I must give November its
credit, for some days have been remarkably clear &
beautiful.
I mentioned in my last letter that I had entered
the Royal Academy, which information I
hope will give you pleasure; I now employ my
days in painting at home, and the evenings in
drawing at the academy, as is customary; I have
finished a landscape, and almost finished a copy
of a portrait which Mr. West lent me, Mr. Allston
has seen it and complimented me by saying it was
just 100 times better than he had had any idea of
