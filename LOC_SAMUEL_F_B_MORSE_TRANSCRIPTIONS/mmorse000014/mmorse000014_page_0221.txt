My Dear Kathen,
I have this moment read the dress
and letter you were so kind as to send me by Uncle
Lancry. Many thanks for the dress, although not the
kind I meant, it is very pretty & suits me well.
As to the letter, the father it was read with various
emotions -- you are indeed a better parent than I deserve,
thus to accede to my wishes, when so contrary to your
attitude -- But I must all will come right one of these
days. George, I well know, is not so healthy as some,
Still he has a strong constitution and these rheumatic
attacks are practically wearing off. If I have written him
that I shall not marry him, unless he becomes a Chris-
tian and dear father you may rely upon it, even if
what it will, I shall adhere to my resolution.
I will also follow your advice in regard to engaging
myself dear father, George is coming up in the Monday
evening boat to pass a day or two -- I tell you this
because I wish to keep nothing concealed from
you, and I intend to behead with the utmost
prudence -- in all my intercourse with him. I feel
dear father, as if I could never thank you enough
for your kindness, in thus gratifying my wishes -- but
if I am to travel a thorny path as you fear, then
it will come all on my own head.
