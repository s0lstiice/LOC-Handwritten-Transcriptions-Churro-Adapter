it -- but I suppose you will see an account of it in this paper.
I can only say, it was a day I shall not soon forget, & was a
light which I would not have missed being. -- The day was
very sultry, and I got very tired, so that I could scarcely walk
but the next day -- Mrs Milhessen was very anxious to have
us all come & take tea with her, but we were so tired that we
declined. Uncle & Aunt did not go with us -- they chose to remain
at Jackson's & content themselves with the view of the succession.
Thursday morning, we took a walk up to the Capitol again -- visited
the Rotunda -- the Senate Chamber & House of Representatives -- but did
not go up to the top of the great dome -- we were all too tired -- and
for the same reason I had to give up visiting the Patent Office,
which I regretted much. Uncle went alone & also called on Mrs.

We were not pleased at all with Jackson's House -- so yesterday
at 1/2 past 5 PM we returned in the car to this city, & are in
our old quarters again -- Tomorrow morning it is Uncle's plan, to
leave for Philadelphia & either Monday or Tuesday return to N.Y.
