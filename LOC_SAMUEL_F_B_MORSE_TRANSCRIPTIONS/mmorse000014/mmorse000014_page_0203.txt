Memphis, Tenn. May 19, 1861.
Inspector- General
Gen. Slocum is for some months
been occupied with experiments in photography,
after the method of Mr. Daguerre, with a successful
time very encouraging, and at this being rather
successful. In the month of April of this
year, the husband of H. D. informed me that you
were willing to give instruction in the best modes
of practice in this art, and I have been so for a
sufficient consideration in several instances. I
immediately wrote to him under date of May
29 inviting a letter to you soliciting instruction
on the subject and mentioning my intention to
send a sketch (one of several) of
binding a draft payable to him order, to ascertain
other improvements in copying of such item
as in a mobile. About the day received
a letter dated May 5, from a friend in New
York stating that Gen. Slocum had left the City for
New Orleans.
The army of 20000 was quartered
and provided with provisions on the 7th day
of the current month. Their movement able to the
order of Mr. V. N. was delay may take place
at any time. I am, etc. Your obedient Servt. A. H. Slocum
