but I cannot run the risk. I would write a detailed
history of the invention which would be an interesting
document to have printed in the Congressional doc-
uments, and establish beyond contradiction both pri-
ority and superiority of my invention. Has not the
Post Master Gen. or Secretary of war or Treasury the
power to pay a few hundred dollars from a contin-
gent fund for such purposes?
Whatever becomes of the inven-
tion through the neglect of those who could but would
not lend a helping hand, You, my dear Sir, will have
the reflection, that you did all in your power to aid
me; and I am deterred from giving up the matter as
desperate, most of all for the consideration that those
who kindly lent their aid when the invention was in
its infancy, would suffer, and that therefore I should
not be dealing right by them.
If this is a little Blue forgive it.
Truly my dear Sir
Your friend and Servt
Sam'l S.B. Morse
