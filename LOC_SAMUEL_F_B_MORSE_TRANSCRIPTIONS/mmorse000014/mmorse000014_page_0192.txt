Baltimore - 9th 1841.
Dear Father
It was very fortunate that we decided going
home by the way of Baltimore, else I should not have been
master so happy by the receipt of your kind letter.
We were all glad to hear New York friends were well, and it
will not be long now before we are in the city again, as Uncle
has decided to return either on the ensuing Monday or
Tuesday - and Mrs. Hooker will probably go with us, and
perhaps Mrs. Littman. It was quite a surprise to me, when
Mark said he thought he should return to Iowa, however
I expect you, dear father, that for my part I shall be
very glad to get back again - although I have enjoyed
the journey exceedingly. - But I want to see you
again, and I am tired of living in a trunk, of packing
and un packing - & so is Aunt Kate - I often think - if I
get weary now, that will it be when I get abroad - but
I do not mean to borrow trouble - I know I shall have
many home sick hours, when separated so widely from you,
dear father - for even now there are times when I get home
sick, and feel as if I could not hear the thought of leaving
friends - and country. I know it will be for my good,
