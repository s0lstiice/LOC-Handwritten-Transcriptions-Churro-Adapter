yet we have had to honor one a good wool fine - really

riles - and this afternoon one on horseback which I
enjoyed highly - because the day has been unusually
fine. Bowen Joe has gone to visit some relatives in Boston
& Salisbury - but will be back next week. I feel united
obligations to him for his sincere attentions on my journey
here. The lays if you do not come on for me - he will
Robert me by the way of St. John - but I do hope dear
father you will be able to come on. Do write soon & let
me know, for if you do not - I must arrange it about
visiting Ireland. - Besides I shall have to ask you
to restore me 12 $ in your letter, as my money will fall
short. Nearly fourteen of the twenty Shells gave me, went for
my travelling expenses - and now I have my washing done
out, he saved about has but one gal &c. I will
it carefully - dear father - too - or rather four & I fear I
cannot pass him - and that will make my allowance short,
so pray send it in your next, especially if you do all
on me. - Perhaps here is a letter now on the way for
me - and I dare say just after I have sent this - I
shall be made Lafayette by the receipt of one from you.
Then is Uncle and Aunt Kate? When do they expect to
sail? I should have written the letter, but feared she
would be out of town. Just think, not one word from this
home I heard from any one, since leaving - but I
wrote not Seola - Aunt & Uncle Fish & Bowen Lucius
are well & doing fine. Lucius would be very happy if
indeed I would they all, to have you bring Charley too.
