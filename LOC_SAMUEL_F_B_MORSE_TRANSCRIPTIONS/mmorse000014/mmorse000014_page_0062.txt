Sussex Cottage, Stought near London
17th January 1842
Mr,
I address you on the subject of the Electric magnetic
telegraph, of which as you are aware Professor Wheaton and
myself are patentees in this country. We consider that its
efficacy and value are now fully established here – it is in
constant and successful operation for a distance of 1000
miles upon the Great Western Railway; and we are about to
lay it down, under Mr Robert Stephenson's sanction, upon the
Blackwell Railway, a line on which its certainty of action
may be essential to the success of the undertaking.
It has always been our wish and intention to
introduce this invention into the United States, and with that
view we made arrangements rather more than two years ago
with three American gentlemen for taking out a patent there.
In consequence of their not having done so, we are under the
necessity of bringing into a new arrangement, and I have therefore
to profess that you should join us upon similar terms – or
that you should be entitled to a half share of our American
Patent, upon exerting yourself to obtain it, and leaving all
expenses connected with the invention, so far as regards the United States,
your own Patent, if you have obtained one, and all improvements
98742
Professor Wheaton
