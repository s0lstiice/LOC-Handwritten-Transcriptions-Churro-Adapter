New York. June 18th 1841.
My Dear Father,
I have delayed writing you, not because
you have forgotten, but in hopes that Uncle Charles
would arrive, & I should be able to inform you, that
we were coming on to meet you. – Alas, no Uncle comes,
and I begin to feel quite depressed in spirit. This hot
dusty city wears upon me, I find, and I feel sometimes
as if I had no home whatever to live – Life seems without
one cheering spot to me. I know, dear father, it is very
wrong to indulge such feelings, and I pray that my
Shannon City father will take such mischievous thoughts of my
heart, and make me a better Christian.
Much did say, now, he shall not sail till fall, and indeed
from some things he said, I should think it quite doubtful
whether we get off at all. This is quite a disappointment
to me, and more at first I thought it would be, after
having talked so long about going – bidding friends good
bye – I feel as if I thought the off – however
disappointments are the lot of mortals – and no doubt
it is all related to the best. It is for such and such
kind talk of going to Texas still lasting before long to
break. I would like to have me accompany them:
