New York Dec 3d 1841.
My dear Sir.
I have just received another letter from Mr. Isaac
A. Coffin from Washington in relation to the Telegraph.
I gave him to understand last Summer that it was
favorable to his proposition but that I could do nothing
without consulting the other proprietors. From you I
received a definite answer and power to propose certain
terms, but not from Mr. Alfred Vail who wished to con
sult his brother. It is perhaps my fault that he has
not answered definitely as I penciled on recently read
ing his letter he waited for a reply to my letter, so that
I have not a power of Attorney to act for him. From Mr.
Galt I expect one in a few days. Indeed my dear Sir
Something ought to be done to carry forward this enterprise
that we may all receive what I think we all deserve. The
whole labor and expense of moving at all, devolves on me,
and I have nothing in the world (completely crippled
in means, I have scarcely, (indeed I have not at all,) the
means even to pay the postage of letters on the subject.
I feel it most tantalizing to find that there is a move
ment in Washington on the subject to know that the Telegraph
will be before Congress this session and from the means
possessed by Lord & Wheatstone (yes Wheatstone who
successfully headed us in England) one or the other of
their two plans will probably be adopted Wheatstone
