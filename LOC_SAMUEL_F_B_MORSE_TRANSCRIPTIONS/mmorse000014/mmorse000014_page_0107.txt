New Haven July 20th 1840.
My Dearest Father,
Your letter dated the 9th only reached me, if you
will believe it, last Sunday eve; and to day, upon returning from a
delightful ride to East Rock I was rather very happy by the receipt
of another epistle from you, which was delivered by Mr Davis, during
my absence. I have been wondering for a long long while, why
your father did not write me, but presumed it was owing to your
being so busy. While I thought Charley might have at least sent me a
few lines, to let me know you had not quite forgotten your runaway
daughter. I was so disappointed in the other day in regard to sending you, in
several of my friends, a letter. My package was all in readiness to be
sent by Boston Edward Salisbury and Cally, who were going to the
city, I shall very probably appeared to take letters, when they went.
They were entertained for a three days on account of the illness of two
Worleys little daughter Agnes - do I keep my package for them when to
my surprise and disappointment on calling the same day, I found
they had gone. It seems Captain C. Hall sent me word by his man, that
he should go on the morrow - but the servant says it to the men - as
thus they were all written for nothing. - I wrote Charley until
you a letter together - but as it was full of inquiries why you did
not write etc. I understand upon receiving yours of the 9th not to
send it, but write another - so here I am at my third epistle.
I mean to send this in the office to make sure sending it.
