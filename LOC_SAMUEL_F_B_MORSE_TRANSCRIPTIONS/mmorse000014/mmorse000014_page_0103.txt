New Haven, June 31st 1840.
My dear Father,
I suppose you would like to be informed of your
news. I suppose my daughter Susan's safe arrival, is it down to
write you the very next day after I arrived. -- We had a delightful
passage up the Sound. -- Mr. Luelton introduced another lady,
who was placed in his care, with three little boys. Her name
is Mrs. Richards, a very sweet lady from this city, and I became
quite well acquainted with her, before we reached N.Y. Heaven.
The sun came out quite bright, when we were about half way
here, so after all we had a delightful day. -- Mr. Luelton rode
up with me to Mrs. Deverson's, when we arrived about half past
one, and found a warm welcome. I am sure I thought to be
very thankful that I found such good company, for Mr. L.
was very attentive to me, and I felt as if I was well provided
for. The family were at dinner when I arrived -- they said
they had quite given up in despair of seeing me, but were very
glad to see me. The father, you do not know how beautiful
New Haven looks, from an unvarnished view, and the fine elm
trees in their glory; the new houses that have been erected since
I was here, are very handsome. Give me N.Y. Haven for my home.
