broken down, and I feel sad at heart as you can
imagine from the tenor of this letter. - Concerningances I
can give you much to do with it - the weather,
and your absence for I miss you and doubt a great
deal I feel quite alone in the world at times. -
I am up to you Uncle & Aunty often - but the last
is most always out; I hope if they conclude not to go
abroad, that they will go to housekeeping, and for me
into home some abetting place. Uncle is very kind to me, &
he went too - and I live upon the hope of their sharing a home
for me, at some future day. - Louisa Sue Bartlett has written
me, and wants me to come back & finish my visit in Boston,
but I prefer going back to San Francisco. Will you have a
pleasant journey father - and has all your friends?
Last eve I drank tea at the Peters - they had the Feats, Stones &c,
and I spent the night - but it was because I knew you would
not wish me to have a home home - and I do not mean to
do any thing contrary to your wishes. - I am most love to
all my Sisters relatives, & say how much I long to see them.
When I am in New York I will immediately make him go.
