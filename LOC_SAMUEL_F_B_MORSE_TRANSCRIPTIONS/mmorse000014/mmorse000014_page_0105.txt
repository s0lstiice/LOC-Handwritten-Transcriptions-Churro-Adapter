Mrs. Merrick's to same afternoon I arrive at
it looks very much like rain, this morning, and is quite
clampt and foggy. - How do you get along without your
tending them? - I have much love to this Washington for me, when you see
them. - If you will allow me, I will add a few words to
Brantley before I close. - Love to Uncle Sidney - & write Sara - Dear
father to your very aff. daughter Susan.
To Chas. W. More-
Dear Sister- Your wanting Toby is at last out of the way, &
how do you get along without him & we are going to take up lodging
until letter to P. Haines to day, if it does not rain.
Do not forget to send me observis every fortnight by Mr Stanley
opportunity - and I should like to leave you next Thursday eve. if
any letters come for me during the mean time, do them up in a
package and carry them up to Abbey Washington and ask
her, if she will not send them in for Mr Toby to bring her
to me. There are so many opportunities constantly occurring
now to send him, it is too well to inform you, and I am
portage. Don't forget the widows woman also, Channing.
I hope you will write me from also, for I want to hear how
things are going on in New York.
But I have not time to say more at present, must go
over to say things.
Remember me to all my friends - and
be a good child - Believing
your aff. sister
98768
Fr. W. D. B.
