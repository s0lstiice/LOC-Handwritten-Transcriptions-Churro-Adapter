I feel assured we could have shown him a Telegraph worthy
of his encouragement. I feel highly gratified with the
kind interest which the Baron Meyendorff still manifests for
my invention and I approve of the course you have adopted of
confiding to his care one of my Telegraphs made under your
direction. I but not need your declaration to my dear Sir, that you intended that I should partake with
you of any benefit which might be accorded by the Emperor when he shall see my instrument, with your improvement, acting
under his own eyes. I saw sufficient of M. Angot in Paris, to assure me that I had made the acquaintance of a truly
honorabile and upright man, and I therefore have not
neglected to secure myself a proper share of any remuneration which the Emperor may think proper to accord.
I am preparing a complete apparatus with which I hope to visit Washington during the present session of our Congress
and confident, however the Telegraphic enterprise may be delayed, it is yet to pervade the world. Have you any news of the
success of Prof. Steinheils Telegraph at Munich or of which stores of England? Of the latter I learn that 13 miles is in successful
operation.
