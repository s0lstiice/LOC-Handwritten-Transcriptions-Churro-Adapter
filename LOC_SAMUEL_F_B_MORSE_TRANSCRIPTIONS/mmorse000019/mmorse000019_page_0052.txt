New York Nov. 13th 1844.
Dear Sir.
I have received yours of the 4th & 5th inst. and
reply in relation to the several subjects you mention in
that order.
I like very well the suggestion in regard to the pre
sentation of a set of the Telegraphic Dictionary, you are
publishing to each member of Congress, and when I return to
Washington will see the Secretary and let if he will about it.
As to the dedication to me, since you have asked
my opinion, I must say, I should prefer to have it much
curtailed, and less laudatory; I must refer it entirely to
you, however, as it is not for me to say what others should
write or think of me.
In regard to the Bartlett claim, and your
plan for settling it, I cannot admit that as proprietors
of the Telegraph we have any thing to do with it. I
regret that there has been any mention of it, and I had
hoped that you yourself would have come to the deter
mination to leave that matter altogether, or at least
until the Telegraph bill had been definitely settled in
Congress. However much I regret the determination made
to man and probably to defeat all our progress in
the Senate, it is a matter over which I have no control
and therefore in the aspect that has been given to it, the suppression of
details which had better not be pushed to a decision, does
