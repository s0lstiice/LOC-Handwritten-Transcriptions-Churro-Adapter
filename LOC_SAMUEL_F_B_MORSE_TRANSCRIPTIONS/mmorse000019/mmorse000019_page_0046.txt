and morality have been making much progress for
many years in France. This I attribute in a very
great degree to the peace it enjoys and the
consequent absence of the demoralizing influences of
war. — When in Rome last spring I had a long
interview with the Pope who said to me he was
satisfied with the condition and prospect of the Catholic
Church in America. To me at a distance, the public
mind in America appears to be in a strongly unformed
state—what with Millenium, Mormonism, etc., and
ought I not to add Presyison? it would not
suffice me if the Pope should come to possess so
authority among you. Where are the three Powers
the school though, the printing press and the Pulpit?
On my way through the United States to Texas
I hope to see you—I want to confer with you on a
topic too extensive to be introduced into the door of a
rambling letter.
Truly and sincerely
Your
