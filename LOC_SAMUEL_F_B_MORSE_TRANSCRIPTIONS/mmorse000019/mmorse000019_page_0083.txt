My dear Sir
Baltimore Oct 5 1844.
You just received a letter from Mr Smith requesting
wire for 16 miles at 40 the says, "which he (Joseph M.) means to send
side the weight per mile, the same as it weighed before preparation
I will take it," and will pay me the cost for it. When he comes on, which
will be as soon as the printing runs off the balance of his vocabularly, 1/2 of
which is done, -- I do not feel authorized to send it without further
advice from you, on case of the above, that is, the weight originally as to the pay, his promise is made to me I will be furnished.
Perhaps on the belief that there should be some modification in some useful, as in a business point of view, I think it
likely he will omit the cost of the wire, say at what it is worth, 28 and
and the addition of 10 or 12 to make up -- I am from Amy
that the preparation adds one 1/2 in weight to the wire, and
again in this view, Mr Smith may think he would not only pay for
a profit on the original cost of the wire in paying up, so far as
the wire weighs, but also would be paying up for the preparation
also, -- but there are suggestions of my own, and his, I only throw
them out between you and me -- I will be glad to hear from
you respecting it, and will follow your advice. Or if it will aid
Joseph M., any more, I will admit it, I would rather do as an
Agent for you, what in a memorable view should be done in relation
to the same. I have merely suggested this last, so that if possible you
may be relieved from any embarassment of mind, on the case, believing that
a fair disposition would be made of it.
I will forward the case in a day or two
Yours respectfully & truly
David Bannock
