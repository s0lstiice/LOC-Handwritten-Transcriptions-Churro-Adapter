Mr. Speaker, I have the honor to submit to you the following letter from the President of the United States, dated Washington, Feb. 25th, 1845:
"Sir, Allow me to draw your attention a moment to the subject of the extension of the Electric Magnetic Telegraph, from Baltimore to Washington. The Bill before the House from the Committee of Commerce, appropriating a sum not exceeding $60000000, is in the Committee of the Whole on the State of the Union and the Committee of Ways and Means, tomorrow, in conformity with a resolution passed in the House, in opposition to the Bill in the Senate, in the Committee of Commerce.
This is the State of the matter Bills in both branches of Congress; But such is the business with which the House is occupied, that although the favor of the House is undoubtedly toward the Bill, I am advised by its friends there that the best and only probable chance of action upon it in the present session is to have the Electric Telegraph Bill attached to the Civil & Diplomatic Appropriation Bill, which has passed the House and is now before the Senate. In that Bill an amendment was concurred in by the House appropriating 800000000000000000000000000000000 dollars for maintaining the Telegraph between Baltimore and Washington. The Hon. Chairman of the Committee of Commerce suggested that to this amendment by the Committee of Finance might be introduced, an amendment that $90000000000000000000000000000000 be appropriated to extend the Telegraphic line to New York introducing of course the other provisions of the Telegraph Bill, except the latter clause may be struck out as the subject of the vote, which is provided for by the 800000000000000000000000000000 dollars appropriated by the House. Should this course meet the approbation of the Committee, the friends of the Bill in the House are ready to give it their hearty support when the bill thus amended shall be returned to the House for its concurrence."
