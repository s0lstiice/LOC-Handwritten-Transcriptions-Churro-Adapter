Confidential
Baltimore Feb 14. 1865
Dear Sir
Mr Smith came today, on his way to Washington, &
I had conversation with him about all your affairs. He feels very strongly
I think, I say so far as the Bartlett contract is any obstacle, it
will be amicably removed if the bill reported at the last session
passes, because it embraces an appropriation that was particularly
designated to cover that outstanding claim. The only thing in
regard to which I think he feels dis-appointed is that when that
bill was passed, he says it was understood that it should provide
a permanent position for you, & Mr Vail under you,
& that he was to have the construction of the lines. but that from
conversation you had with him at New-York last fall, he infers
that you now go in for both a proportional part of the construction for your position under government. However he
says he shall not guard with any one about the matter, as
he desires the work to go forward with the greatest dispatch.
He also says that any arrangement I can make with the
other proprietors about constructing trains, he will concern in,
being willing he says I shall make a job in the matter to
my advantage if I can. I shall be obliged, I like to have
you improve an early opportunity to converse with him on
the subject while he is in the mood of it, and if any conclusion
be come to please myself me a line inspecting it for sports,
I speak of your satisfaction both in regard to the book
& that last article in Hunt Magazine. - This is about the
substance of what was said H.H. and according to my promise
I will communicate it to you.
100087
Confidential
David Barbaugh
