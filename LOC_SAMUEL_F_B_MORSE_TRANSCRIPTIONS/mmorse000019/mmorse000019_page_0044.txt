Paris Nov 12. 1844
My dear Sir
I should write to you occasionally did
not the newspapers furnish promptly and in detail
all news from this side of the Atlantic indeed from
every corner of the world. I now write lest by
chance a matter which concerns yourself, and the
United States too, should escape your observation.
I saw in the Journal des Debats of yesterday that
the Minister of the Interior has appointed a
Commission to report on Electrical Telegraphy
telegraphie electrique: I think your invention
ought on this occasion to be duly presented to
the notice of Europe. Independently of the discovery
of this application of electric galvanism, I am
persuaded your apparatus for transmitting it,
etc., is much superior to any employed here;
unless perhaps some great improvements may have
been recently introduced of which I am ignorant.

London: -- You may remember I saw yours at
Washington in the Capitol before any time was laid
down, and I have seen subsequently in the
American newspapers notices of it working between
Washington and Baltimore. One account of it
