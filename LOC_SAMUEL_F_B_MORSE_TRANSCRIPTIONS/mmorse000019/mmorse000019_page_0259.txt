Three obstacles in the way he said the petition ought to be printed
and he did not know who should pay for it -- He said the printing
would cost $150 -- We cannot charge that to proof of Honor -- very
well I replied I will pay that and remove that objection if you
will get the signatures -- In the mean time Mrs Louisa Sisson did
did -- On my return from M.S.B. I called this morning on my
Merchant for the list of petitioners to my great vexation
and notification I found the gentleman Mr C. who accorded
me in this manner, "Your fellow sum to be very dilatory
in forwarding that petition" and took from his desk the
draft of a petition so badly drawn up as in my opinion to
render it objectionable. It had 3 names on it at the head of which
stood Loring C. Chapin a large letter. After some remarks he
(Mr Godling) urged the necessity of my taking the paper to the Mayor
to get his signature first -- Having removed the names from
the first of the paper I supplied him with a obtainable letter
of introduction I went to the Mayor who recognized this paper
which had been presented by the said Chapin on (Bulley)
before. He said Mr Haspin said he had no objection to
put his name to the paper but he would not have it -- So you see I have written all this mass of detail
that you may know how the matter has been conducted ab initio. It has been an utter failure from
being put into bad hands -- Chapin has not brains enough
to do his own business -- Godling complains he has so much
business that he has no time to give it -- Notwithstanding all
he said to you he says a different song when you are away
I shall go to see your brother in the morning and take
conseel as to the best course to be pursued.
