4. goodness has allowed me but 200 pounds, (and I wish you again
to receive my sincere thanks for this allowance, for my
sole endeavor be to spend all this, to the utmost advantage,
to keep as closely within the bounds of that allowance
as possible, and would not economy in this instance consist
in rigidly keeping up to this rule; if this is a true statement
of the case, then have I been perfectly economical,
for I have not yet overspun my allowance, and I think
I shall be able to return home without having exceeded it, a
single shilling, if I have done this, and still continue
to do it, why, in every letter I receive from home, is the
injunction repeated of being economical; it makes me exceedingly
unhappy, especially when I am conscious of having
used my utmost endeavors, ever since I have been in England
to be rigidly so. — As to Industry, which means
I am falling off, I gave you an account in my last
letter (by Mr. Kalston) of the method which I use in parcelling
out my time, since writing that letter, the spring and summer
is approaching fast and the days increasing, of course
I can employ more of the time than in the winter, Mr. Leslie
and myself, rise at 5 o'clock in the morning, and walk about
a mile and a half to Burlington House, where are the
famous Elgin marbles, the works of Phidias and Praxiteles
brought by Lord Elgin from Athens, from these we draw
3 hours every morning, wet or dry, before breakfast,
and return home just as the bustle begins in London
for they are late visitors in London, when we go out of a
morning we meet no one but the watchman, who goes
his rounds for an hour and a half after we are up.
