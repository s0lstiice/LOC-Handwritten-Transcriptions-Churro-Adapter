New York January 29th 1814.
My dear Sir Copied
There seems to be a fatalilty attending our correspondence.
At least your letters to me constantly miscarry; mine to you I
hope are more fortunate. Your favour of November 29th 1815
I did not receive till the 12th inst and while I was making
arrangements to send you a draft for the amount, I received
a letter from Capt Hull mentioning that he had paid you
and drawn on me. As he observed it was at your request
I am afraid you thought me remiss, but this letter will
explain to you the cause of my not having written you
sooner. I feel under many obligations for your kindness
in putting yourself to so much trouble on my account,
and I would not be so ungrateful as to neglect for a mo-
ment any account you should present. Be so good in
future as to direct to me, merely at New York, as I am
persuaded your letters have failed of immediately being re-
ceived on account of the name "Bloomington" which is probably
not known beyond the bounds of this City.
