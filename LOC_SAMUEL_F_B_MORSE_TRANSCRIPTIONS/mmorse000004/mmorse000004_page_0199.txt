London Decr. 22. 1814.
My dear Parents,
I arrived yesterday from Bristol
where I have been for several months, fast endeavors
to make a little in the way of my profession,
but have completely failed owing to several causes, find
the total want of anything like partiality for the time act
in that place, the people there are but a remove from
brutes; a Bristol hog, is as proverbial in this country
as a Charleston man of the same species is in
Boston; their whole minds are absorbed in trade, cattle
and gain, and interest, is all they understand, if
I could have painted them a picture for half a
quintal by which they could have made twenty, whilst
I starved; I could have starved; secondly, the vulnerea
of national prejudice, which rages now with brutal
acrimony, they no longer desire, they hate the Americans,
the battle on Champlain, and before Plattsburgh, has de-
cided the business, the means and bewailings for this
disgrace are really to an American quite comforting;
after their arrogant boasting of reducing us to uncondi-
tional submission, is it strange that I should feel
a little, the effects of this universal hatred? I have
told it, and I have left Bristol after six months
perfect neglect, after having been invested there with
