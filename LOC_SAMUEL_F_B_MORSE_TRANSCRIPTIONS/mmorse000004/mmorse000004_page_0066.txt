My dear Brother
Mr George Jack goes to England in a
day or two in the Russian ship Joseph, & intends to de-
liver our letters in person. He will be able to give you ans-
wers to your inquiries about us & the country. He has also
been furnished by the Editor of the Centinel with a good file
of that paper, of which perhaps you could procure a sight.
He intends to visit France & Holland & other countries of Eu-
rope; when he returns, is uncertain, & perhaps, as he is an En-
glishman, he will not, at all. I wrote you about a month since
giving you an account of my jaunt in N.York State. I don't know
by what vessel it went but Mr Jack can tell you, if you have
received it. I should have been glad to have stayed there
during the summer, especially as what I anticipated has come
to pass, & I have nothing to do at home. I was cautioned indeed
not to be satisfied with the idea of staying at home, but
to look for some occupation. But it requires some force of mind
to resolve that one's present situation is not the best & to
exchange it for one untried. Whatever step I have taken hitherto,
I have taken reluctantly. Thus much of myself. You shall hear
if I change my situation. The ladies in Charlestown are
very social. Almost every evening they meet in company with the gentlemen
