I need not add, what I trust you know full well already,
that the sooner you take possession of it the more will
you enhance our pleasure. - It is not impossible indeed
that I may see you in Boston in the course of a
couple of weeks, as I promised my sister Mrs Hull to
pay her a visit this autumn; and though I have hitherto
been prevented from fulfilling it I have not laid
aside my intention. - A clergyman however is so con-
fined, that he cannot wander at will, and should it
so happen that I shall be disappointed, I hope you will
not let the winter pass by without paying us a visit.
Upon casting my eyes upon the date of your letter when
I had before overlooked, I find to my great surprise
that it bears date Octob. 19th. - Where it has been all
this time I cannot positively divine, but it fills me
with regret to think that you have been a fortnight
arrived without receiving my congratulations, and
what is still worse that I had seemed so negligent
and cold hearted. - Do my dear Sir, let me hear from
you by the next mail. - I have a thousand questions
to ask you.
