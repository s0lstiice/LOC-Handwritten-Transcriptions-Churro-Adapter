You are perfectly right in supposing
that the plan you propose will be
agreeable to me & I assume you
candidly that your letter gave me
very great pleasure. If you
have at any time drawn other
inferences than what I have stated
above, from my words, I am most
heartily sorry, -- You blame me
rest on my imperfect manner of
expressing myself, for I am sure
you cannot be more anxious
than I to maintain the friendship
that has hitherto subsisted between
us. We ought to know
each other pretty well by this time
of day, & I feel confident that as
I have any inclination to
respect good qualities, I can never
intentionally abuse the feelings of
excellent a heart as yours.
