your poor parents are doing for you how good your Dear Brother are to be
satisfied with so little done for them in comparison with what we are doing for
you I let the thought stimulate you to more economy & Industry as I greatly
fear you have been falling off in both since the elicit you received for your
first performances it has always been a failing of yours that as soon as you find
you could excel in what you undertook to be timed off and not humble yourself any
further about it I was in hopes that you had got over this fickleness in thin in
your letter in which you ask leave to stay longer in Europe you mention a large
picture that you were going to paint for the exhibition in Paris in hopes of obtaining
the prize; is this plan given up? and if so why is it? I think if you could get
one or two hundred pounds in Guineas it would while trying for it when you leave
France your papa wishes you to purchase a very handsome good combo black cloth
for a suit of clothes for himself & a cloak & also a suit for each of your Brothers
it will take I presume 15 or 16 yards if you should get it let it be perfectly neat
put up so as to keep it from being injured in coming home do not forget it and
look over your parents letters before you return and endeavour to comply with
their requests made at different times to you for Books &c &c bringing home pictures
paintings & include those at Liverpool and come with a good repellent
behave by self next if possible you must not expect to paint any
thing in this country for which you will receive any money to support
you but portraits therefore do every thing in your power to qualify you
for painting & taking them in the best style this is all your hope here is to
be very obliging & condescending to those who are disposed to employ
you our circumstances are altered much for the worse since you left
us we cannot keep but half the help we Used to do and have sold some of
our furniture to purchase Bills for you I have offered more for sale for the
same purpose so you see our deprivation are not small but we do it
with pleasure when we have reason to believe that you are exerting yourself
to relieve us from the great burden which we are labouring under and are
taking it on yourself I think young fester is a very estimable young man
to be as I am told he is supporting himself & assisting his widowed mother
by his Industry this is considerately does him honour I trust your feelings are
equally good if you had suffered it as necessary as it really is at present
to relieve us I must now conclude my Dear Son with wishing you every
happiness that our heavenly Father has best for you in this life & everlasting
happiness in the life that is to come all had a great deal of love to you from
your loving father
Brother Hill
