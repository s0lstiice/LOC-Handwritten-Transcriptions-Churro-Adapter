of the most despicible Sovereigns in Europe. With such men there can
be no union; I mean no permanent union.— The repulsion of British
Invasion is the duty I will be the pride of every American; but while I
stand in defence of his much wronged country against a
arrogant & in some instances a cruel foe,
he cannot be said to be the unprincipled conduct of the internal
enemies, & such he must conceive the present ruling party to be.—
But enough of politics.— Mrs. Jarvis desiring to be particularly re-
membered to you. I know not whether I mentioned in my last that
she has made me the father of a fine Son who was 9 months old
the 5th inst. & is the picture of health, spirit & good nature.
Now that you are in France you will I hope have more frequent
opportunity of writing; and I beg you will believe that to
