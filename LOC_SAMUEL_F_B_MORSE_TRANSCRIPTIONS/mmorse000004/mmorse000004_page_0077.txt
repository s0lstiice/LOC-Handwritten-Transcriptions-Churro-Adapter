But we rejoice with trembling, while the destiny of
our own country hangs in awful suspense. But
we do hope, that the time is not far distant when
we shall be freed from anxiety by the return of an
honorable & lasting peace. There is not time to dwell
on this interesting subject.
I have spent my time since I graduated principal-
ly at New-Haven. For about a year I have
been sick and unable to make any progress in
my studies. But I have not forgotten that
my life has been spent, and that I have the prospect
of soon enjoying my usual state of health. Then
have been considerable alterations at N.H. since
I have been there. Several large buildings have been
erected -- two of the Meeting Houses, the Brick and Fair
