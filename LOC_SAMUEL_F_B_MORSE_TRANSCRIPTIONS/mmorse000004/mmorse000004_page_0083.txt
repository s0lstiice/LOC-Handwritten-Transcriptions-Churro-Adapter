they have have in all their battles with their enemies.
The fireworks and illuminations, and fêtes, in conse-
quence of Peace, and of the Royal Visitors, have exceeded
every thing of the kind before in England they have indeed
been very splendid, but as you will doubtless see the most
interesting, detailed in the American papers, I will not repeat
them.
You will also have heard of the entry of Louis 18th into
London after his call to the throne of France, I had an ex-
cellent view of the procession and of him; on the day of his
entrance I stood 5 or 6 hours, before the Pallmey hotel, where
the Emperor Alexander has staid during his residence in
London, and where the Duchess of Oldenburgh his sister, resi-
ded before him; — as the procession was to pass this spot, I
concluded it would be the most interesting place to see it, and
I was not disappointed; for all the Royal family, or
rather the female part of the Royal Family, of England, soon
appeared on the balcony, and I had a good view of them
for 2 or 3 hours. The procession was splendid, and Louis was
cheered as he passed, with loud acclamations, the crowd
