I could get out of it, and so good fortune would have it I
blundered directly before the hotel where he put up; he was
called by the immense crowd of people to show himself at
the window, when I heard this I rushed forward, and with
some little difficulty got directly under the window & just
in time to meet him it at, for he came to it and stood for
nearly a quarter of an hour, while the air rang with acclama-
tions of "Vive le roi! Vive Louis!!" he is a very compul-
sive man, and much afflicted with the gout, he had
at the time a very interesting expression, his eyes were
suffused with tears, and his mouth seemed expressive of
the greatest gratitude, he often lifted his hands, as if to bless
the people.
I am sorry I am under the necessity of breaking
off here, but Dr. Hayward who will take this letter, is
now waiting while I finish it, he goes in the morning.
Please make my apology for not writing your good sister
Mr. Jarvis & also to Mr. Jarvis, I intended writing by
this opportunity but have been prevented for want of
time. Please remember me kindly to your family
and believe me as ever
Your very sincere friend
Jam. T. B. Mosby
Miss Jennette M. Hasty
