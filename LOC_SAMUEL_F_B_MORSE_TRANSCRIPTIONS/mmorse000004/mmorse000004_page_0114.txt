When I see such unity in a whole nation, for the purpose
of subjugation and revenge, such expectations of a division of our union, and such encouragement given
for these expectations. Posterity will curse the man
who first proposed such a measure. I will give
no opinion I see much wrong; but I must feel
My country disgraced, or honored, is too important
an alternative for me to be indifferent to it.
This letter goes by private opportunity to Amelia Island, I therefore cannot enlarge beyond this
sheet on account of double postage. In writing to me
I think letters may as well be directed as formerly, Mr.
Bromfield will forward them to me wherever he
Thanks my Mother & Safford for their good
letters I wish I had time to answer them; all my business is taken up in studying French. – there are scarcely any artists left in England they have all gone to Paris to study. – Mr. and Mrs. A. N. are well, and in good spirits they are with me in Bristol. It will cost me about 5 pounds to go to Paris. I think with 200 pounds the next year, I shall get along very well.
Mama's request about the pictures, as soon as practicable shall be complied with. I bought some books for my friend Rev. Mr. Jarvis of New York, to the amount of 4 or 5 & 20 $s. in my next I will give an order on him for the amo: I do not now know the exact sum and must write to London to ascertain.
96838 Love to all friends. In much haste, sincerely
J. D. Mason
