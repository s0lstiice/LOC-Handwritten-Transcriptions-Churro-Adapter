Mr. Editor,
Since my residence in England I have been accus-
tomed to take in your paper, from a conviction that it
was conducted in the most impartial manner, and
was free from that illiberality which is characteristic
of many of the other journals of the metropolis; this
opinion has daily increased, and I have admired
the spirit and feeling, which your
remarks uniformly conducted. In your paper however,
of Wednesday, (13.) I met with an exception to what I
have said, in it you give an intercepted letter from
a private friend of Mr. Bartlett in New-York to Mr.
Bartlett in Paris; this letter I have read over many
times, and really cannot conceive in what
way you could draw such inferences as you have;
the letter mentioning a request from the academy of
fine arts for a whole length portrait of the Emperor
Prunaparte, from this among other things you infer
the devotedness of the greater part of the Americans, to
the tyrant of Europe; just read over discover your inference,
again sir, and your good sense will be the peculiarity of it,
are there not many portraits of this man in England, is
he not painted by English artists, the the the celebrated
Williams the murderer, are they on that account friends
