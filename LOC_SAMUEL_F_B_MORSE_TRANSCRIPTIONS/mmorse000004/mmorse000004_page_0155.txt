My dear Brother,
We have received your packet of letters
containing an account of the crowded heads in London
I am much obliged to you for the pleasure they afforded me. I
paid extracts from them to Louis I sent them to the Daily Advertiser. We all enjoy you
the happiness of having been in London at such an
extraordinary scenes. I am looking out for some account from you of the Duke of Wellington which will
wanted to complete the catalogue. I hope you have
seen him. Your proposal of going to Russia struck us
with surprise. I suppose they have no very distinguished
artists there. I suppose you expect to be more conspicuous
there. Believe me I could not help fancying you as going to be the first, to be at the head of the Art in
that barbarous & uncivilized Nation. However your
task I think, would be no very easy one, for according
to Dr. Clarke they have a mean sort of state for
Paintings & other collections making the same use of
them as of money to get conveniences & necessaries
of life &c.
I have nothing very interesting to tell you of your friends here. Edw. is gone to Litchfield & I am going next week
to Andover so that the family will again be separated
You must not expect of courts that I am going to be a
Minister; I am admitted (at all) on favor. It year
perhaps, or at farther two or three will determine the
result of this experiment. I am no better now than when
you knew me but mama says, a great deal worse. The
fact is, I am in want of some pursuit if any one should
tell me now that I should adopt this, I should think there
was no truth in the old saying "That every man is
born to some particular pursuit," for there are twenty
that I might be brought up to as easily as to that of a Her
junman. If this plan is adopted, so; if not I must
seek some other employment. But enough of myself.
