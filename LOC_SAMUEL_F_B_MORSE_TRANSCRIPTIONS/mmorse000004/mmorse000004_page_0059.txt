I have heard that your friends in Boston expect to see you this
Autumn. If you should not come so soon I will avail myself
of your kindness to obtain some other works of importance, and
which I cannot get here. – But, my dear Sir I should much
rather see you sooner though perhaps in the expression of such
a desire I consult the gratification of my own feelings rather
than the promotion of your good. – Your friends expect much
from you, and it is important that no opportunities for im-
provement be lost. – In case of your return next Autumn
I trust you will not bid a final Adieu to Europe.
Mr. Fairbanks will not have his portrait taken, for she refuses
herself she says to become a subject for your pencil.
She & I sometimes have doubtfully this morning praised your
letters which I sent yesterday. – I forgot whether I
mentioned to you in the letter which accompanies this
that I have purchased a house in the City (No. 489
Broadway) in which I shall hereafter reside. I
