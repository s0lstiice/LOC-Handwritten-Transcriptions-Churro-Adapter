different we have not got your picture from Mr. Bolstone yet but hope
we shall soon, the little picture you mention that was exhibiting at
the British Gallery we shall be glad to receive I wish you not to ask
us to remit the expence of a frame of picture that you may see proper
to send us at any time but only let us know what the actual cost of any
picture or frame has been to you, leaving it to us, to do what we think proper.
I believe you will be safe in doing thus. I excuse this on the ground of your having
been mortified by the ungentlemanly treatment that you met with by some
of the Bristol men had it not been for this I am sure you would not have
thought of saying anything about the expence of frame of that magnitude to
your parents. I am very sorry for the disappointment which befel you
at Bristol in respect to your business, but any sort of it will be a lesson to you,
which may do you good in the end, it will show you how little dependance
is to be placed in promising professions of worldly men, and keep you
from placing too much confidence in them, if we are faithful to ourselves
We may extract good from every thing, troubles & miseries -- I feel very
tenderly for Mr Alston, in the loss he has sustained, of his beloved wife I
hope he is all concerned, will improve this affliction as to be made
wise, and better, more weaned from this world, and your heart more
placed on things above, and be daily placing your Affections where Christ
sitteth at the right hand of God. -- Your Brother Richard is we trust choosing
that good part that shall never be taken away from him and
which we (and all our Friends of the Clergy that visit us and which are
numerous at this time) pray daily may be the happy portion of your
other Brother. Indeed my Child you have many prayers daily offered
to God for you which we hope will get bear wind in your Salvation. --
