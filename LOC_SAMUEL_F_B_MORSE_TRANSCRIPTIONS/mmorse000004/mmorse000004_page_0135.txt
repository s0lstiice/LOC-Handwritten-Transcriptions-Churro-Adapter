My dear Sir,
We wrote you about a month ago by the
Packet ship which brought the Dutch Minister who was to leave at
some English port. In that letter I informed you that I wish
to have 80000 for this year, that was all I could do for
you, this is done with great exertion on the part of your friends
for the purpose of giving you all the advantages & encour-
agement in our power -- you must henceforth depend on
your own exertions -- I do hope you will, with proper in-
telligence & economy, find no insuperable difficulties in doing
it. We wish much to see you as soon as it will be for your
advantage to return which will not probably be the case till
the return of peace, & this is still a very doubtful event
however earnestly desired. Our country is on the verge of ruin
never at any former period in so gloomy a state.
Our legislature are now in session, (extraordinary) & are about
adopting measures to call a Convention of the N. England
States first, then a Convention of all the States, to here
organize the Govt. -- or rather to alter the Constitution so
as to secure a just equal representation of the States.
Nothing but a speedy peace or a separation of the States, can
save M. Engd. & the U.S. indeed from bankruptcy & ruin.
This state of things, deeply affects all my property & income,
& your mother & me are sometimes seriously apprehensive
that we shall, in our advanced age, & if we be separated, be de-
pendent on our children from support. It. this be the case
& they shall be blessed with the means, we can have no doubt of
the dispositions of all of them -- I mention this, only to
prompt you to your utmost exertions to study the strictest
economy in all your expenditures. I have nothing to write
