Brussels July 5. 1814.
The President of the United States
I received your last
in the morning, and should have answered
your first letter out for two reasons:
1st, that I had nothing to say, which I think metaphysicians
allow to be the most natural, as well as the most powerful, cause
of silence; 2d, that if I had had anything to say, that the early
expectation which I entertained of seeing you allowed no confidence
to the hope that you would hear what I had to say, I should have
said it. I thank you for your solicitude, and am as sure your
thick both with myself are in every respect, better than when we left London
