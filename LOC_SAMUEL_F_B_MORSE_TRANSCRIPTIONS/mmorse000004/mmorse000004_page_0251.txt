they were exceedingly kind and hospitable to me, but I

I at the other end of Bristol, the way to them was very
dreadful and considered not altogether safe for a single person
at night; night was the only time when, from the nature
of my studies I could visit them, it being winter and on
little daylight, beside the dreadful state of the weather
in the winter season, if I had visited Bristol in the
summer I should have availed myself of every oppor-
tunity to call on them for they were a charming
family, and I feel very grateful for their kind atten-
tions to me; as to the portrait which I painted of Mr
Russell, I am sorry you mentioned it to Miss Russell, as
I particularly requested you would not, because in case
of failure it would be a disappointment to her, but
as you have told her, I must now explain, in the first
place then it is not a picture which will do me any
credit, I was unfortunate in the light which I chose to
paint him in, I wished to make it my best picture
and so made it my worst, for I worked too timidly
on it, it is a likeness; indeed, a very strong likeness,
but the family were not pleased with it, I had not flat-
tered him, and made him too old, so I determined I
would not send it, indeed, I promised them I would not
send it, but notwithstanding, as I know Miss Russell
