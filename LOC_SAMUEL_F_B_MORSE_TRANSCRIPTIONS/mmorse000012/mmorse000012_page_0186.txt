Philadelphia January the 25th 1859
McKinnon
My dear Sir
At the request of my son I take the liberty of addressing to
you this few lines to apologize for his apparent remissness in not sooner
writing to you, which I believe is not only an obligation due to your kindness
and attention to him, but estimes it also his highest privilege. A few
weeks before he left the post he had the misfortune to steam the waters
of the injury, and he had returned home and recommenced his
professions. He wished to complete one portion and leave what
engagement he could remain before he wrote to you, he finished one
when he began to experience the same oppression, but in a less degree
in the other eye, and his physician thought it necessary for him to
restent. He supposed until his eyes could be entirely restored. Since
the last week the source appears to be yielding to the influence of
medicine and we now hope that in a few months he may with
recovery resume his employment. The painting gave such general
instruction that he has as many portraits engraved as will keep him
employed for months at a time. I am sure he will be much
better than any he has yet painted, and puts it a great trial to be
so suddenly attacked in his advancement. I am still unwilling to con
clude without expressing my satisfaction at his improvement, and
returning my grateful acknowledgements to you for your care and
instruction. I am the present his best respects to you and thanks to
remember him to Mr. Slayn and the other gentlemen of his acquaintance
in the University.
Very respectfully
Frank E. Boyle
