Boston July 15, 1836.
Dear Sir
I know nothing particulars of Henninger more than you have told. He came hither about a year since, from Pittsburg; I addressed myself first to Dr Follen, whether with letter I do not know. Dr F. sent him with a bustle to Dr Best; the latter sent him with a note to me. As my engagements did not permit me to look at his manuscript, -- & as I knew that the Rev. Mr Cozzell had taken an interest in the publication of "Six months in a convent", I think the liberty of affording Henninger to hear, with the note which has been published.
Henninger was several times at my house; he addressed intelligent & well informed sentiments on all subjects except the subject of liberty; -- Poor, I must say, he appeared to us all to labour under a very strong hallucination of mind, amounting for half to monomania. He had come hither direct from Pittsburg, he said, rather than from any of New York, because the Society were on the watch for him in N.Y. would defeat the publication of his book. I mentioned your name to him as the author of Breton; I was sure at the point of giving him a note began; but did not, because he said he had letters for New York; -- & there were in him here a something of a Jesuitism with which I hesitated to hinder you.
