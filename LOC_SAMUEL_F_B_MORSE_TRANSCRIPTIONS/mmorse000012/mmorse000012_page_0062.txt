Professor Morse.
Dear Sir,
I thank you for your kind letter of the 8th inst
which I received only three days ago, on my return from a journey
to the West, to this place which I have made my summer residence.
It was about ten days ago, at Pittsburgh, that I received the
sad information of the death of young Claving in whom you
have taken so generous an interest. My acquaintance with
him began last summer when he called upon me at
Watertown, near Boston. He told me his story, and handed
to me part of a manuscript work on the Jesuits which I
read with great interest and returned to him with some criticisms
which he thought just. I then endeavored to find a publisher
for him, but did not succeed. He left Boston in order to
return to Pittsburgh, as he told me, and since that time I
did not learn anything with regard to him, until my recent
visit to that town.
I know of no particulars of his history except those which
he undoubtedly made known to you, and which are summarily
mentioned in the newspaper which you sent me. His residence
at and near Pittsburgh I do not see mentioned in this account.
