Post. S.F. B. Morse,
New York Feb. 10. 1838
Dr. Six,
Yours of the 4th came to hand
yesterday. A compliance with your proposal would be
exceedingly agreeable to me & my family, & since the re-
ception of your request we have talked it over & stuck
to make provision for it. On the whole, however,
we regret to say, it will not be convenient for us to
accommodate your daughter as a boarder. We
already have a young lady with us who rooms with
Harriet, & whom we have engaged to keep till the 1st
of May. If we could dispense of her, we would gladly
take Susan in her place, you are only sorry we can
not do so. But, as it is, we have only one spare
room & subject, as we are, to company which we
must accommodate, we think it our duty to deny
ourselves the pleasure of having your daughter with
us, as a boarder, which would be very agreeable to
us all. It is with reluctance on your account, as well
as our own that we decline, as it would afford us great
pleasure to do you the kindness which you request.
In account of the present of the times, we expect to
change our quarters on the 1st of May. After that, should
you desire the accommodation proposed, it may be
in our power to grant it. In the mean time, should
your daughter return to New York, we shall be happy
to see her often at our house, to cherish the intimacy
which already exists between her & our own daughter,
