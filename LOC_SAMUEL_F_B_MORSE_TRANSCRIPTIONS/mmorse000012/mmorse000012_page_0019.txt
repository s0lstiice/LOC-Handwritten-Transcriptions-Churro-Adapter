On those who hastily condemn
works of art that have stood the test of ages,
Boileau has some excellent remarks on
the judgment to be pronounced on the writings
of the ancients equally applicable to works
of art. He says [Reflex. 7. sur Longinus]
"When writers have been admired for a great
many ages and despised only by some persons of
a capricious taste, (for there will be always some
or other of a bad taste,) it is not only rashness, but
folly to question their merit. For though you do
not discover their beauties, you must not therefore con-
clude that they have none, but that you are blind,
and have not a taste for them. The generality of
man kind in a long course of time is never mistaken
in the judgment they pass upon works of genius.
There now is no question whether Homer,
Plato, Jully, and Virgil were wonderful men.
