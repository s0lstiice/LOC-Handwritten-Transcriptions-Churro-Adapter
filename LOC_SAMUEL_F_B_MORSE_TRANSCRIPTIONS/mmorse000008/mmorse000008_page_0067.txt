this will be the case, but am not yet able to say with cer-

Monday, and can then go out where I please without danger.
I cushioned my hand and arm up to day and walked round
the gardens, where, you perceive, I can now do little good.
Wenvy set out yesterday for Oxford.— I shall be about again
I hope next week, perhaps be able to paint.— I am in
excellent health as are all the rest of the family.—
I have been thus particular in describing all the
minutiae of this accident, that mother may not have
the least chance for her imagination to supply any
irrealties; I have told you length and breadth, bound-
daries, situation, and extent, and No. of Sq. miles of
the whole affair, and hope you will consider it all as
past, and cause of great thankfulness with me that it
was not worse; I have felt I believe entire submis-
sion; knowing that I deserved far worse, that it was
ordered by a wise and good being, who orders all things
right, and will accordingly to his promise make it work
for his own glory and my eternal good.— We have rec'd
3 letters from you since you left, 2 from N.york, and 1 from
Albany, we were glad to hear of your safety thus far; we rec'd
the order for 300 you sent from N.york. and the books, you are, this
evening safe? Some of the family will write you more particularly
soon on business affairs, which as you may suppose, I am in no situation to know
much about.— all send love.—
Yr Aff. Son Finley.
