In the house in which is my room there is a family of 7
small children the oldest not 10 years old, the youngest cries so
much like Charles that I have waited in the night once or twice
thinking it was him, but when wide awake I am soon undelivered
he is now crying which brings it to my mind.
One thing that tends to make me feel more low-spirited
perhaps than I otherwise should is that I have had a constant
cold in my throat ever since I have been here, it still troubles
me and I think is a little worse this evening; I was perhaps
impudent in conforming to the fashion here in New York in having
my whiskers shaved off at this season, my neck has felt cold
ever since, and though I rub it with camphor & wrap it in flannel
it gives it only temporary relief.
I long to see you all again, but all in good time,
Give my love to Father & Mother & Sister Susan, and Nancy
and keep my dear Susan & Charles for me many times; I hope
they will be great comfort to their mother in the absence of their
Father. — Excuse this incoherent speech, dear Wife, as
it comes from your affectionate husband
Finley.
