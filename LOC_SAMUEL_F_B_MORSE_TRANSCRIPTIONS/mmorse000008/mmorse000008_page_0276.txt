Albany, Aug. 21st 1853.
Dear Mrs. White
Your dear letter of the 17th inst. is before me, it gives me great pleasure to hear of dear little Charles' convalescence. I hope he will continue to grow better until he gets entirely well. I was sorry to hear that Father was so sick. I hope it is only a temporary indisposition, and that by rest and the approach of cooler weather he will grow stronger.
I am in the prospect of something to do, I have one Portrait besides the portraits in progress, and a promise of money for 3 months. I calculate that this will pay all my expenses here, so that the two I am now painting well clear me in that respect, all that comes after will be gain, I am therefore, easier in my mind as to risk, the Portrait painting is Judge Moody Kent the brother of the Chancellor, who says I shall paint the Chancellor when he returns to Albany, and his notice, from these particulars you may infer that I shall be here for some little time longer, just as long as my good prospects continue; but should they fail I am pretty much determined to try N-York city, and sit down there in my profession permanently, I believe I have now attained sufficient proficiency to venture there, and though my progress may be slow at first, yet I believe it will be sure, I do not like the idea of going South, and I have given up the idea of New Orleans or any Southern city at least for the present, circumstances may vary this determination, but I think the prospects of a settlement (in my opinion) in New York is more favorable now than ever before.
