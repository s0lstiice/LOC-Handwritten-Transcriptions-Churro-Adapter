In considering my future prospects I have been a good
deal perplexed to know how we shall manage to live with each
other at all; if I should have enough to do here I cannot leave
it without disadvantage, and if I should not I cannot live in
N. Haven. In either case the result is the same in keeping me se-
parate from my dear family; the idea is a very melancholy
one to me I suppose you. There seems to be only one way, and
since I have been here, it seems to have fewer difficulties
attending it than any other, I mean that of removing all together
to this city, for some years; with the view (Providence per-
mitting) of returning permanently to New-Haven after I
shall have acquired sufficient to live there without depend-
ing on my profession for support, if that shall ever be; There
are many things in favor which I could better tell you than
write you, but I will mention some; first & may I not say
chiefly with me, is the comfort of my dear family, in whose
society I might lose the remembrance of the cares & toils of
the day, and to whom I might impart that instruction which is
due from a parent & the head of a family; another reason is
that I should be in New-York while our children are very young
and should probably leave it before they are old enough to be
exposed to the temptations of the city.— The expenses of the
