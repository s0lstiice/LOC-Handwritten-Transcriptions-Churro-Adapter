New York July 15. 1823.
Dear Brother,
I am sorry that Henry returns
to me without bringing to you what you anticipated when I saw you. However, your pictures have been praised by judges & amateurs, but yet this is not they only thing an artist thinks desirable. The want of success with the multitude is, that the figures are small, whereas they do not boast of taste, must be pleased with something that can be comprehended at once, with figures (course I badly proportioned if you please) larger than life, & should man
aspaint Hercules strangling serpents, the form as large as a steple, & the latter as immense as the size of the sea serpent, he would please New York. But even in the latter case, the owner of a lion, bear or monkey would realize more money, & receive quite applause. This summer has been famous for exhibitions of all kinds in the City. Fine or superior art exhibitions have all failed, with one exception. There have been and are many strange sights of Elephants, Hippos, potamuses, Monkeys, &c. &c., &c., &c., &c., &c., &c., &c., &c., &c.
