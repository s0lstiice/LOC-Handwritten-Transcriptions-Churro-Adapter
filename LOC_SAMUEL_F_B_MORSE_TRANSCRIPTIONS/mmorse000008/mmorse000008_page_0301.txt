New York Dec. 8th 1823
My Dear Wife,
You will probably feel anxious to know
what I am doing, the answer is very simple. Nothing.
I am waiting for applications but none offer. The
Chancellor's Picture & Mr. Breese's have been finished about
a week, and as far as painting is concerned I am almost
completely idle, and of course a little less spirited. I have
been active in calling on my friends and inviting them
to my room, they have promised but as yet few have
called; as far as human foresight can perceive my prospects
have been gloomy indeed, the only gleam of hope (and I
cannot underrate it) is from confidence in God; when I
look upward, it calms my fears, and apprehensions for the
future, and I seem to hear my Savior say "if I clothe the
lilies of the field, shall I not also clothe you I ye of little
faith," here is my strong confidence, and I will patiently
wait for the direction of Providence. I have seen many
of the artists, indeed most of them, they all agree that little
is doing in New York; This city seems given wholly to commerce
every man is driving at one object the making of money, not the
spending of it; New York is a very miserable place to live in
and I feel that even if my family were here, I never could
feel at home; New Haven of all places on earth is more
like home than any other. I have seen Mrs Kingsley
at Mr. Perits she is well and says she left you in good
health & spirits. What is Mr. August doing with the Machine?
