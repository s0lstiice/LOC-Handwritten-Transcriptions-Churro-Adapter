My dear Sir,
I beg you to accept my best
thanks for your letter, and for your kind
attention to my commission. which I am
worrying however that, circumstanced as I am,
it is wholly out of my power to enter
at present into the engagement you
recommend. The situation of the proposed
house &c are every way what I could
wish, and I should not hesitate did
I feel certain of being able to fulfill
the engagement. But indeed the future
is, for one so slack in purpose as I am, very
uncertain ground to build on; and though
I may allow myself to build air castles
on it, I should not feel if I were
acting either prudently or honestly to
tempt another to erect any thing more
substantial for me on so precarious a
foundation. In short, though I still adhere
to my plan (much as talked over with
you) and apprehend no cause of change,
it is yet so dependent on contingencies
that I can neither naturally do more
now than arm me with patience or indulge
myself in wishes. I have been obliged to leave
Bilsharzer ever since you were here, in order
to raise the means where with to proceed with it.
