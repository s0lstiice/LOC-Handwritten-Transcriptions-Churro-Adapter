return. — Do write often, my dear Lucrce, for next to seeing
you, hearing from you is my greatest comfort. I hope Susan
is a good girl and continues to improve; tell her that Papa
expects she will be a comfort to Mama & GrandPapa & Grand-
Mama while he is gone, and that she must get a great many
trickets to show him when he comes home; give her Papa's kiss
whenever she behaves well; Kiss dear little Charles too. I hope
he is better, write me full particulars about them all.
I hope Father & Mother are well and that their dark
days are becoming brighter, I shall do all in my power to com-
fort them in the decline of life; When you write again let me
know from Father what I shall do about the sale of Steele &c.
which he sent me, they said the charge of $4..00 was wrong
and ought to be $3..50. I hate any dealings with book-sellers
they are a class of men I have very little respect for, they
seem to be as a class a most impertinent race of beings, the
more I see of them, the more I am convinced of this, there are many
honorable exceptions but they only prove general rule.
How does Mr. Agar progress with the machine? tell me all about
it when you write. — Give my love to Father, Mother, Edwards &c
Nancy Shepard & Henry, and remember me to the rest of the
family and all N. Haven friends. — I see by the Papers that Uncle
Stout of Belleville is dead. I keep a little corner of this open
as I find the mail will not go till Tuesday morning.
Monday morning Aug. 18th I have this moment been notified by
receiving your dear letter of the 13th. note it has been delayed 2 days
Sam Grier & Smith has the care of Charles, as I believe he understands more of him than
any other, your letter serves all my yearnings for home, but God time is the proper time. I have nothing
to do but wait for the mail to return to Providence. I am your affectionate husband
& ever
Samuel B. Mason
