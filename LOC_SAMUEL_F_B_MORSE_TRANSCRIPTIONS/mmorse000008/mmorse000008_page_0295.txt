New-York. Nov. 25th 1823.
Tuesday evening.
My Dearest Wife,
Your letter of 17th inst. is very your letter
are a great comfort to your exiled husband, write him
often. I began to feel a little settled, and have begun to
paint two portraits, Chancellor Hunt and Mr. Irving Denver
the latter a small portrait; I feel encouraged in thinking
I shall do very well here with patience & industry, and I
have determined on both. The bed clothes & screen came safe
to hand, and I am as comfortable as I can well be without
my dear wife and family. I have been obliged to go into some
expence to get established, there are a great many necessary
little articles which are indispensable in beginning an establishment
such as a stove, coal, tin pitcher, basin, soap, wood,
shovel & tongs, heart brush, and some Bed's and which run away
with the cash; my greatest expence has been for a green baize
carpet which I found absolutely necessary to make my room
comfortable, it is a very excellent stout piece of green baize
contains 40 square yards & cost me 30 doll. although this seems
a large expenditure at the present time yet on mature consideration
I do not regret it; I have borrowed 50 doll. of Mr.
Cobit which I hope to pay him by a Portrait but dont know
how that will be; I board at friend Boolidges for $2.25 per Week
and live very well, it is more than half a mile from my room
so that I am obliged to walk more than 3 miles every day
this is good exercise for me & I feel the better for it; I sleep
in my room on the floor, and put my bed & out of sight
