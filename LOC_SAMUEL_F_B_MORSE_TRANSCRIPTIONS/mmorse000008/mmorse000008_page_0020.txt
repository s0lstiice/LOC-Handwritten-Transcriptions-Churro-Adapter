Will not Mr. Pettleton now visit us? are there none of
his zeal and experience in revivals, that will come here
to the help of the Lord against the mighty if he will
come I will pay his expenses to Charleston, and I will
engage that he shall be at no loss in pecuniary affairs,
indeed, that cannot happen, five times the amount of his
expenses could be raised here in half an hour. I have
endeavored to be faithful to my dear cousin, but of what
need have I to apply the promise "in due time ye shall
reap if ye faint not," I have as yet discovered no
signs of good in them, gay, and frivolous, Volatile, and
now they almost wish (I judge from their conduct) that I
would not visit them if I took so much religion to
them; yet I know they love me, but they hate the
truth, pray for them, dear ducece, and for me.
As to my Profession, I am now fully employed
from morning till night, and shall be kept here prob-
ably longer than I expected, as this is the last season
in Charleston, I may as well make the most of it, Par-
ming other portraits, I am painting Bishop Bowen, as a
present from some of his people to him, which is also
to be engraved, it is as large as the one of Gen. Thos Pinckney
