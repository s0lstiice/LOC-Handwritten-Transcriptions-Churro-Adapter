Mr. Morse
Dear Sir
Your last favor Prevailed yesterday of a
Gentleman from New Haven. I was very sorry to learn of the accident
you met with by drawing a bottle of wine. I hope you soon recover
so as to have the entire use of your hand. I this morning
received a letter of Mr. Wright which informed me that he
would leave Charleston on Monday last. so he may be effectual every day. the Machines I received by Mr. Morse and
I am much obliged to you for them they will be of considerable
service to me in real engraving. the Bishop you have
nearly finished. I am pleased hear that. you have some doubt
respecting the painting Small one I am very to hear that. I
don't know who we can get to paint it as well or as quick
as you could do it. as you have become acquainted with
the features by the painting the large one. we certainly
cannot do with out a small painting. & Mr. Wright & myself
would prefer you to do it. you will when you next
write if you can do it or not that we may know what to
do respecting it. in your first letter you took of
the engraving of the portrait. when the large one was in Charleston
I did not that we should finish it in new-york but to commence on the common place. and have the likeness till next winter in to finish in Charleston. in haste address
your obedient servant
William D. Smith
