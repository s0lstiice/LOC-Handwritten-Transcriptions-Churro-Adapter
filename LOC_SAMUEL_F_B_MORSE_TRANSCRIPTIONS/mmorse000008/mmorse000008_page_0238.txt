Salem April 26th 1823
My Dear Sir.
I hardly know, what sort of a letter I ought to
write you under existing circumstances, if I should tell you
my feelings, it would not probably be any advantage to you or me
I shall therefore merely state a few facts, and if they have any effect
on your spirits, you may conclude that mine are likely to be
affected in the same manner.
Thursday of this week was pleasant Receipts $5
Friday Fair but cold 125
Saturday (today) Pleasant 225
making the Receipts for this week $1412.05
I shall carry about some bills on Monday morning, post last week,
and hope I shall see more visitors, than I have this week; if I dont,
I shall not be able to pay all my expenses, and I think that I
had better be in the states of Vermont, and the Picture in your
Painting Room, than continue the Exhibition, if it will not pay
its expenses. However there are a number who talk of
coming to bring their Friends, and I hope that I shall not have to go
away in debt Dr Bartons and Young Gen. Dearborn
were here today and spoke in the highest terms of your Picture.
I will send you a list of my expenses in my next letter if you
desire it. I feel anxious to hear from you have seen but one
