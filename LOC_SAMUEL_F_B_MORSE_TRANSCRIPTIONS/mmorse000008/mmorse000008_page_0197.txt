1823.
Boston March 6th
My Dear Sir
I have just rec'd your letter, I am glad to hear
that you are safe home again, I also rec'd this morning a letter
from my Father, they are all well. The weather has been very
unfavorable most of the time since you left me, so that the
receipts have been small, I think nearly as good in proportion
to the weather as formerly. My Books stand thus. Receipts for
Monday 11.50 Tuesday 8.50 Wednesday 13.63
Reys 60 Pots 4 do Pots 4 do Pots 4
Mon. Blues. very wet and blustering. Wet, dull and heavy
Today, thus, it has rained all day & now continues to rain fast
with considerable wind -- The streets are running Brooks, with
glass ice beneath
I have paid your Note at Mass. Bank, and destroyed it,
also $250 for distributing small Hand Bills, also 3, for board
you probably recollect Daniel Lemonnier has established himself
in Charleston and is said to be an excellent workman -- As I think
it necessary to get a new R of Bret I will let him make
things he said that he had no leather good enough to make them,
so I'd him in advance $5: that being his price for the Brets --
I hope you will not disapprove of this measure, as I cannot
wear my present Boots more than 1 or 2 days at a time, on account
of tightness -- As to lighting the Fiction in the East
as you propose -- it will be attended with some evidence & trouble
but perhaps may be profitable -- with your leave however I should
not think it best to do it until the walking is a little better
