are under the hospitable roof of Uncle and Aunt Batt
lett, and Aunt Livemore, we can easily believe that
you receive every kindness and attention from them;
give Lucretia and my most sincere love and thanks
to them for their kindness to us and to you.
Little Susan is in fine health and sends many kisses
to dear Grandpa, and Grandma; she continually asks
why don't they come home?
I hope your next letter will relieve our anxiety
about Father's health, and that he will be able to reach
home soon.
Our money for family expenses is running
out, as Father has been gone so much longer than he
expected if you have 10 dollars to spare, it would relieve us,
if not I will try and borrow it.
My Picture proceeds rapidly to a close. I shall
complete it in all fancy if I have my health.
Nancy feels much concerned for Father and sends
a great deal of love to you, she says she will come
on immediately if you should wish it.
