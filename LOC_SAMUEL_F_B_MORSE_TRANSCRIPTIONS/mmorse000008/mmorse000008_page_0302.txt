Is he still danguine? I should be glad to hear from him.
8 o’Clock P.M. I have just seen Fatcher letter
and am glad to hear that little Charles has got a tooth
through, I hope the dear little fellow will be carried safe through
all his troubles both in infancy and manhood. I may have some
thing of an interesting nature to communicate to you in a
few days which concerns our future prospects in life but as
it is only in agitation now I shall leave you to your guesses
what it is; the only clue I will at present give you is, that it
may cause me to return to New Haven in a few weeks for a short
time. I have written Mr. Doolittle and advised him from
his obligations to finish the contract, as he has lost all his
time and he can afford to lose it less even than I can; that
rather suffer the burden of the failure of the picture alone.
The order I gave Fatcher on him is good against me, and neither
may put it down against me. The due bill of 7 dollars of
Mr. Doolittle’s he will pay.
If Fatcher has not sent to Auburn the books which
were to be given by me (Boccaccius,) I wish he would not send
them, if any excuse or apology is necessary to the gentlemen
there, it will be sufficient to say that the Institution intended
giving them to was that over which Dr. Rice presided,
and by some mistake I had thought it was Auburn, which
is all true; you know I never wished to send them there
and the gentlemen in that region have behaved so shabbily
to the Editors of the Observer that I shall regret ever sending
them anything, I hope they are not gone.
