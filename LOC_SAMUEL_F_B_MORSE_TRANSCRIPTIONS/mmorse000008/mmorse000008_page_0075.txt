My Dear Parents,
I have been waiting some time for
your promised letter from Utica, we rec'd your
note from there, saying you would write again
to-morrow; In expectation of this letter I have
delayed longer than I otherwise should have done
as you will probably feel anxious to hear how
my hand is; I suffered a great deal of pain
from it at the commencement; for it was very
badly mangled, but it is now free from pain and
the wounds are all healing fast and properly;
I think I shall escape altogether a stiff joint,
all the inconvenience will probably be a large
unsightly scar between my thumb & forefinger;
perhaps another on my fourth finger; I have great
reason to be thankful that all has proceeded so
favorable; it was a fortnight yesterday since I
hurt it; and I left off the poultice only last
Friday, I have not been able toaint or draw at
all since; but hope to, in a week or two. — I
have thought it advisable to take this opportunity
of sending you a copy of the paper which I
have been writing for the last few days. It is
not a very important one, but I think it may
interest you. I am, dear Sirs, your most
affectionate son,
Samuel
