to me, and all our dear family -- I have not heard from Concord
since I received the letter informing me of my Mothers death.
I wrote Susan a long letter last evening I think it probable
Charles will return to N.York this winter -- I feel for
Susan, a great weight of care and responsibility has devolved
upon her -- but I hope she will be able to pay us the long
expected visit next Spring -- but every thing here seems is so
uncertain, that I make no future calculations of enjoyment.
I shall send this letter by Mr Edwards, whom you will
probably often see -- Tatchell expects to go on soon, but this
will be determined on the Presidents answer to your note --
I will send your Correspondent full of tea and the Camas, about a
lb. Father -- who has spoken to Mr Turner to procure the
Elms -- Mr Hillhouse is absent for the winter at Savannah
or somewher in that region -- James is here on a visit --
he and Mary were here yesterday they requested to be kindly
remembered to you, as do all our neighbors --
Little Sue was much pleased with Papa's pretty letter she talks
about you when she sits up in the morning and wants
to hear a story about good Papa's her hair growsaster --
and she has a sweet color in the cold weather -- She is more
fond of reading than of reading but knows seven letters --
whilst I write she has got a piece of charcoal, with
which she is making eyes, and nose on her baby's face --
I feel very glad to hear you are furnishing your studies under
circumstances so favorable, but do not like to hear of your deafness
grateful and sincere appreciation of it -- as it you will get upon them by such means
a severe application to study -- I shall feel anxious about you, dear Kinley --
