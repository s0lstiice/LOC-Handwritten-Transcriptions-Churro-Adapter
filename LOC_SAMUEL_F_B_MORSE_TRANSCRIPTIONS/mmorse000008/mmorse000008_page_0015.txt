Charleston Feb 15th 1821.
My Dearest Wife,
I beg to feel a little anxious to hear from you
now, it is a fortnight since I heard by letter from you,
but the mails still continue so irregular that I attri-
bate the delay entirely to them. I am quite tired of
seeing before the Post Office door the mail north of Raleigh.
Raleigh seems to be the sticking place. I sent yester-
day, to father, the (Courier) Feb 17th in which you
will see the notice of our Academy of Arts, the mule
were drawn up by myself, it looks prosperous now
but I am not sanguine as to its success, still I mean
to act as if I was sure, whilst I am here, which
will be but a short time, my plan of a school of
painting in N-Haven strikes me as eligible the more I
think of it. Providence I think is indicating me a
removal from Charleston for the present, I find no increase
of settlers I complete the last settings of my last sister
to-morrow, I wait with submission the direction of Pro-
vidence, I know he will direct to the best course, if it is
his will that I stay longer, it will be shown me in the
proper time; if it is to return home, that also will be
shown in the proper time, I feel perfectly easy on
either event, affection strongly pulls one way, interest
