from being downright low-spirited; I am too happy in
the Society of my dear wife & family, and it is right that
I should be made to feel that they are not pleasures, on
which I must fix my affections too strongly; there is
nothing permanent but God, and to have our pleasures
permanent they must centre in him; then wherever we
go, we find the object of our enjoyment always with us.
I feel anxious to hear from you, and to know how
dear little Charles gets along, I can't but hope he will
outgrow his present feebleness. Don't let Susan want
your superintending care, till her Papa expects she
will be a very good girl, and not give you any time
or any reason to write that she has not been got.
I wait with some anxiety the result of Mr. Steele's
experiments, I look forward with some hope that that
invention may enable me to remain at home; but
I would not take thought for the morrow, God's
ways and time are the best.
Tell Father I delivered his notes, Mr. Steele
objected to the charge of 4 dollars & said it ought
to be 3.50 I knew nothing about it, and therefore, could
not rectify it, I hope Father & Mother are well, give
my love to them & Edw. & the rest of the family; Kiss
the dear children for me & believe me as ever my dear
Lucyce. Your Aff. husband
S.J.B. Morse.
