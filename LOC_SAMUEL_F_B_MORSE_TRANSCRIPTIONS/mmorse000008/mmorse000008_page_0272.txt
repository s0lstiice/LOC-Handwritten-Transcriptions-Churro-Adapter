Albany August 16th 1823 in
My Dearest Wife,
It is now nearly two weeks since I left home
and I have not received a single word of intelligence
from you. I must say I was disapponted in not receiving
a letter from you this morning, as I wrote you more
than a week ago: but it is possible you may have missed
the mail. I feel anxious to hear from you, especially
as I left Charles in such feeble health; do write me at
least once a week whilst I am here. I have written
twice before this, this letter being the third, you will also
hear verbally by M. & M. Silliman. I find it more
and more trying to leave home; I do long for the time
when I shall no longer be under the necessity of having
it. I am as yet uncertain whether I shall do anything
here; I am painting the Patron (who is the way will be in
N. Haven at Commencement to enter another son) but, as you
have rec'd no application for a portrait, many tell me
I have come at the wrong time; the same time that has
been sung in my ears for so long a time; I hope the right
time will come by & by; the winter it is said is the proper season,
but as it is better at the South in that season, it will
be more profitable to me there than here. I shall give
Albany a thorough trial and do my best leaving all events
in the hands of him who always orders all things for the
best. I have been introduced into a pleasant family
of the name of Lovett, she was a Mrs. Sillman of Windisch
