I shall be near you, and home, in cases of emergency, and
in the summer and sickly season can visit you at N.Haven while
you can do the same to me in N.York, until it may be advisable to
live again at N.Haven altogether. I have out of this calculation the
event of the Machine for Sculpture; if that should entirely succeed
my plans would be materially varied, but I speak of my present
plan, as if that had failed.— It is necessary for me to be either
at home, or so near, as to be at home at short notice, and I am
completely tired of roaming abroad.
I see by the Papers there is one case of Yellow fever
in N-York which seems to have excited some alarm, I think
needlessly, Richard G. Charles, however, I hope will be in their
quart.— We had a very sudden death here yesterday, Mr. John Cook,
the man who sneezed so singularly, Mr. Tilliman knows him,
he left the reading-room, he died of bilious colic, or in eating
imprudently. The city is very healthy, I hope N.Haven continues
to be so too; I read with much surprise in a N.York paper
the death of Lucius Monson in the West Indies, I recollect
warning him very seriously never to stay through a summer at
the South, but money tempted him probably to his death.
I hope Mr. August will not be discouraged by the little
minutia of the machine, but carry it through, I should like to have
a letter from him on the subject, putting down a list of questions
respecting marble and marble cutting which he wishes me to ask to
stone cutters, as there are some here I can put a good many letters
I approve of Mr. Whitney's idea and am glad Father
has put me down for my share in the Schoolhouse.—
