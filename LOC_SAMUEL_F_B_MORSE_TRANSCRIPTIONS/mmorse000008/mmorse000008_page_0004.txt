Jan 24th 1821. In
full hope he was frequently visited by pious people and seemed at times
to be anxious for his soul, which their prayers and thought
if he should live he never should do as he had done, at other
times he thought he had been his own worst enemy, having
forward come and injured nobody but himself. Jackson and others
with great plainness he told his friends well in time by his
habit of intemperance that it seemed to have no effect how careful he
might be of such an one I thank my dear Aunt and cousin many
times most sincerely for their kind remembrance of me I intend
to have devoted so whole page to their service but have had so
much to say to you that it has been crowded out if I
have said but little about them it has not been that I have
thought but little about them I frequently talk of them
to see them again and remember my visit to Charleston made so
pleasant by the unvaried, kind attentions of my dear Uncle and
his family with the liveliest gratitude and pleasure – All the dear
things had more of my heart than any one in Charleston, you of course
know I should love to make one of their circle with Mary and one side a
corner on the other holding a hand of each and to do it
to be acquainted with cousin Ruth and Joseph and my love
to them all, not forgetting little Edward – to Aunt quite a great

Charleston
