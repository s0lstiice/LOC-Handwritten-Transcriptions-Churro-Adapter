Washington March 24th 1847
Friend Mosse your Tel despatch of the 19th &
letter of 16th both reached me late last week while
at Richmond and consequently I could not reply to
them until my return. he have had an anxious time
of it at Richmond, and a glorious result. we have defeated a man who is feared not esteemed by the people
of Virginia. and he is Monroe Robinson, a bosom
friend of Mr H Rogers. Mr Robinson object was
to pass a bill through the Va Legislature compelling
us to sell the Tel. right to him for a 1/4 proportion or
the deny us the right to put it up any where East
of the Blue Ridge. The injustice of such an act
became palpable to the members of the Legislature
through papers drawn up & printed by Mr Kendall
with which the members were furnished, The consequence
was, our substitute, for the Robinson bill (which had
already passed the house) was agreed to by the Senate
13 to 7 and the House by 44 to 27. Thus
has justice triumphed over despotism & inveterate
meanness. The line will be immediately built.
I have this morning taken a hasty inspection of
Thurber's machine, and see no advantage which
we may derive from its application to the Telegraph.
To work it would require some fixtures similar to
letter printing Telegraphs and a great deal of other
machinery. I at present think it is of no account.
Enclosed you will find a Check on the Bank
of America, New York, signed Corcoran & Riggs
for two hundred & ten dollars. — drawn in your
name. Please pay Mr Pynch the amount
stated as due to him in a previous letter to you
& which I think is 1137. — To Mr Kent the
