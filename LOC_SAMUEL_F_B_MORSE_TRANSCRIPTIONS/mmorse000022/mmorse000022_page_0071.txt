Copy Washington June 22, 1847
W. C. Bryant, Etc.
Dear Sir
I have this moment received a line from
Mr. Tenney, Associate Editor of Evening Post, in reply to
a note addressed to you, in which I asked your attention
to the statement which I believe and still believe
to be a gross exaggeration in the sense in which it is
generally understood; It was stated in relation to
Mr. Hoadley's telegraph that "an experienced person has
practiced with this apparatus at the rate of one hundred
and eighty letters per minute." In his letter Mr. Tenney not only
endorse this assertion but says "he is convinced that it is demonstrable"
he further says (see A)
"Now in the pledge to print 400 Roman letters in one
minute, Mr. Hoadley simply means that he can make his
type wheel revolve 16 times in a minute (which is right
alphabetical order in which I have previously stated it can be
done, of what use is it and what does it prove
that even 50 letters can be executed by this wheel in a minute
when used in a practical way to send a communication? If
then in the way he means to redeem this pledge, and the Evening
Post should announce that Hoadley had actually printed 400
letters in a minute, would not a deception be practiced on the
public in fact, it is not a fact, while it might be literally
true? I need only state this danger to you to have you agree with
it and appreciate it as an act of selfishness, should this be the
intentions of some of my operators, if you will, I have observed in many of the papers
