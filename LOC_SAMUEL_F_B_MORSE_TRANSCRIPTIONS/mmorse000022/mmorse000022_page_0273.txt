you reply. I shall this, a hearty welcome. I have no
time now to write to you about it, as I am necessarily ex-
pecting to take the boat for Newbery to visit my son, who
is quite ill, I hope not seriously. I am rejoicing to
hear of the success of the new link in the chain, & who
have been right glad to have been present at its first
speech, & to have made the acquaintance of the high
minister Virginians around you. I hope that you are
agreeably situated. You say "you hope I shall not forget
that we have spent many hours together" You might have
added "happy hours" -- I have tried you dear Vail, as
a friend I think I know you as a Zealots & honest one.
You cannot tell how pained I am at being compelled
to change my opinion of R. If feelings correspond
entirely with my own. I was hoping to do something
gratifying to him & his family, & soon & I have done
it, if he will permit it, but no! The most of friendship
covered a deep selfishness that seemed not to
sacrifice a real friendship, to a short sighted & over-
reachig ambition. Let him go -- I wish to befriend
him this, & would have done so, from the heart, but
as he cannot trust me, I have enough who can & do.
I have nothing special to tell you, in the way of
new things -- Reid has invented a very ingenious
instrument "to turn the lightning out of doors" & if
it is worthy of patronage, if it accomplishes what
he says it does, & I have no reason to doubt him,
the instrument wants a little more actual testing
perhaps -- Reid is an ingenious honest, good fellow --
& as ever I shall be
S. H. Mosse
5th 13 Mosse
