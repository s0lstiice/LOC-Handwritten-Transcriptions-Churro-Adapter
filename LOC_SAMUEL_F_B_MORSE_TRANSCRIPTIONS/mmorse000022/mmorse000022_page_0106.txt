The Commercial Exchange of New Orleans
January 30th 1847
Professor Samuel P. More.
I have made an earnest effort, and used every exertion to establish in
this most important City a Company to carry
out Your great and truly immortal invention
of The Magnetic Telegraph.
At present, all my efforts have not been attended
with any success, though I have succeeded
in getting the support of the Chamber of Commerce, which is one of the
most important subjects which it is to
the City, and the Country generally of such
immense National advantage; I have
negotiated with you, and with any Corporation
which is likely to have the least interest on the
subject.
I have been most fortunate from my very
intimate connection, and acquaintance with
the gentlemen of the press of this City,
and the Senators of the United States, to get
the principal Senators to advocate the
establishing of a line of Magnetic Telegraph
connecting the North, East, Middle States
with the West and constantly advancing
West and South; which will soon unite the
two great Natural arteries of the West and
West-Profession Country, the St Lawrence River
and the Mississippi River.
It is my earnest wish that the
Government would take the
lead in establishing this
great line of telegraphy.

Government would take the
"lead" in establishing this
