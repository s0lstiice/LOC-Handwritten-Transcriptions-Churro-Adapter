considered in your Guagama Society, as indelicate in a young

lady, I have a higher opinion of their moral sense, while I deduce from that very fact, that the Theatre is not the place where
any lady of true delicacy should ever be seen. I could write
much on this subject, but it is unnecessary to you, for you have
judged correctly, and I am glad to know that you have had given
you by grace, firmness to resist temptation in this instance, and
I pray God that for Christ's sake he will always protect you, &
enable you to preserve your principles in every hour of trial.
Always, ask of him counsel and guidance in any hour of need
and he will be sure to give it you.
I have received from your Aunt Louisa seven dollars,
which I have used as you requested, I put your name down
for the Sailor's Magazine for one year, and paid subscription One
dollar, ordering it to be sent to you through Messr. DeForest & Co.
The remainder 6 dollars I entered on their books as a donation
from you. I at the same time added a donation of my own of
10 dollars. -- Charles writes you by this same opportunity and
presume by the vessel that takes this you will receive a lot
of letters and papers. He has now gone up to Newburg. Finley
I presume will join him this summer. If you return this
summer I shall be rejoiced to see you all, and we will
continue to be together as much as possible. I do not yet
