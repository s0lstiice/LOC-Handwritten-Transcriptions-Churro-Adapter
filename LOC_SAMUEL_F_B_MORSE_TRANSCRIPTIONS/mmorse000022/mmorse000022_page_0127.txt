New York Feb 24th 1847.
Dear Sidney, I have this moment received yours by the
Cambria, and am rejoiced to learn that you are all so
well in health. You do not say a word about your return in
the probable time we may expect you. I hope you will in
your next that we may know whether to write you or not.
In regard to Telegraph improvements, I would say that I
have intended a caveat for two methods of recording, one of
them by printing from ordinary type, but although simpler
and more efficient than any other plan for the same object it
cannot compete with my present plan, I shall proceed to
make it for curiosity sake, and to defeat these insinuations
on their own ground. But I have no apprehensions whatever
from House or any other. They are mere bubbles which
will burst in their proper time.
I have a carried time of it with the Telegraph, adverse
and successful results, I have just received my first divi-
idend on the N.Y. Alb. and Buffalo line, 3 P.C.'s for 5 months,
$1393,,00. This is a good beginning. We shall make a
dividend on the N.Y. Wash. line soon, we have had extra
expenses on this line but our receipts for several
weeks have averaged $200,,000 per day! Boston line
is managed by Smith, I need not say to you what the
result is except that it is so badly managed that it is
odious everywhere, and there is no doubt that it is in-
tentional on his part that he may get the stock at
a depreciated value, and so obtain the control and
use it as a monopoly for speculation. A more perfect
specimen of satanic possession, I do not believe
exists. I have to keep appearances with him; but
