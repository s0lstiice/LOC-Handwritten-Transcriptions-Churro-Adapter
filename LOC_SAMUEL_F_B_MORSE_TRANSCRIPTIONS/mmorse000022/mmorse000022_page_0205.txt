Had such being the facts -- I have to request
that you will allow me to publish your letter to
me. I have shown it to Mr. Eddy, father of
the gentleman before mentioned, & one of the most respectable citizens. He saw all Jackson's letters to your
& yours to him & particularly the last which I
asked him what he should do with, & when
Mr. Eddy advised him to hide it in his book-
case! His opinion is that it will do you no harm & even
causes much good to publish it in a paper I am
trusting up. If, also, you will allow some truly private
to occupy the entire correspondence, I will cheerfully
refuse the appeal. They can next Summer, I will
transmit such documents to Europe & particu-
