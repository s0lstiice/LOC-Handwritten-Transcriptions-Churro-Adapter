I used paper, moved by clock machinery, upon
which I caused the lever moved by the magnet to
imprint the letters and words of any required despatch,
having also invented and adapted a new and
peculiar Alphabetic Character for that purpose, a
conventional Alphabet easily recognised, and easily
made and read by the operator
It is obvious at once from a simple statement of
these facts that the system of Messrs. Wheatstone and
Cooke, and my system were wholly unlike each other.
As I have just observed, there was nothing in common
in the two systems, but the use of electricity upon
metallic conductors, for which no one could obtain
an exclusive privilege.
The various steps required by law, were taken by me
to procure a patent for my mode, and the fees were
paid at the Clerks Office June 22nd, and at Home
Department June 25th 1838*, and I had reached that
part of the process which required the sanction of the
Attorney General. At this point I met the opposition of
Messrs Cooke and Wheatstone, and also of a Mr. Darcy,
and a hearing was ordered before the Attorney General
at John Campbell's on July 12th 1838. I attended at the
Attorney General's residence on the morning of that
day, carrying with me my Telegraphic apparatus for
the purpose of explaining to him the difference between
my system and those of my opponents. But contrary
to my expectations the similarity or dissimilarity
of my mode of operation was not considered by
the Attorney General. He neither examined my
instrument, which I had brought for that purpose,
nor did he ask any question bearing upon it.
