In consequence of my request for this letter I was
allowed a second hearing. I attended accordingly
but as my charge, the Attorney General had not
himself even read the letter. He carelessly took it up,
and turned over the leaves without reading it, and then
asked me if I had not taken measures for a patent in
my own country, and upon my reply, on the affirmative,
he remarked that "America was a large country, and I
ought to be satisfied with a patent there" I replied
that, with all due deference, I did not consider that
as a point submitted for the Hon. Attorney General's
decision. That the question submitted was, whether
there were any legal obstacles on the way of my obtain-
ing Letters Patent for my Telegraph on England?
He observed he considered my invention as having
been published, and that he must therefore forbid
me to proceed.
Francis O.J. Smith Esq. was present at the latter part
of this interview and can testify that the ground of
refusal taken by the Attorney General was "that my
invention had been published"
Thus forbidden to proceed by an authority from
which there was no appeal, as I learned, but to
Parliament, and this at great cost of time and money
I immediately left England for France, where I found no
difficulty in securing a Patent. My invention there not only
