Copy to Thomas Davenport, Brandon Vt. in answer to his
letter of 15th Jan.
Dear Sir,
Yours of the 15th inst. has just been received
from New York. In reply to your inquiries I would say that in 1826 my attention was first drawn to
the subject of Electric Magnetism, in the Lectures of my personal friend & colleague Professor
Freeman Dana at the New York Athenaeum. Professor Dana is now Professor of Chemistry in the University of
Washington. When in 1832 I conceived the plan of my Magnetic Telegraph, the information received from
Prof. Dana in relation to the Electric magnet, furnished me with the ready conception of applying its force to printing my signs
in letters. The more important of my experiments however, in applying the magnet, were not made until the latter part of
1835. At the New York City University, the essential parts of my invention were then proved, and during the year 1836, were
occasionally modified as I had leisure from other duties to make experiments. My object was not to obtain any great power, but any
power however small with the magnet, a magnetism, at a distance, that I had obtained for short distances of a few hundred feet, but I had
no funds at that time and was compelled to wait for the accumulation of funds before I could try its use a half a
mile, and I had not obtained half a mile of wire, but it was not till the latter part of 1837 that I obtained ten miles of insulated wire
with which my experiments were performed publicly at the University and afterwards in Washington. I recollect contrasting the operation
