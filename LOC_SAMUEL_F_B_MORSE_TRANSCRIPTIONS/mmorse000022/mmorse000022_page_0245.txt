At that time I had with me the drawing of an
instrument called the Receiving-Magnet, connected
with a Relay or local Battery, a provision against the
reduction of the magnetic power of the main Battery, as
the length of the conductors increased. By means of a
fresh Battery and magnet, any quantity of power could
be obtained, according to the size of the said magnet
or local Battery. This instrument has never to my knowledge
been described up to this date (1847) in any public
Journal either in America or in Europe. The receiving
magnet was a provision devised for an exigency,
which, at that time, I conceived only to be possible,
but was to be used and would be effectual in case
such exigency occurred. This instrument was unnecessary
in any of the then attempted systems of Electric Telegraphs,
nor was it then necessary, nor is it now necessary, in
the exhibition of my main instrument called the
Register, which is confined to a few miles of conductors.
But it is essential to the efficiency of my whole system
when a circuit connects two points at a great distance
from each other. Although devised sometime in the
year 1835 while contemplating this possible exigency
it was not necessary to bring it into use till the first
line of conductors for my Telegraph was prepared.
