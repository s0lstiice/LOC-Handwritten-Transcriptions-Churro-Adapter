Mr Kent Can testing to my having bought an assortment of chemicals
salt be, previous to November 30th 1846, an assortment
which he selected at my request, on account of their quality of being
easily decomposed by electricity, he understood from me that I desired them
to ascertain the best substance for applying my original plan of making
my character by electrical decomposition of the chemical which
was using, among them was a bottle unlabelled which he tested in my
room and found it to be chloride of potash, which some other bottles and
was anxious to find some iodide of potassium, but did not find any
I had other salts, and when I left the room he walked down Beekman
street with him, to me to his office, and in the course of that walk first
proposed to enter into an arrangement with him to buy apparatus in
make & dispose of my Telegraphic apparatus to companies and
others. -- This agreement was subsequently entered into & signed
on the 30 Nov. 1846. -- But on that day he remembered
that I dined with him at Clarke & Brown's, and returned to his
laboratory, to select the necessary salts & other Chemicals for the
experiments above mentioned. He recollects my describing an
drawing or paper that portion of the apparatus by which the
electrical decomposition was to be effected, this apparatus consisted of any
point which rested on a small cylinder of metal, between which
the paper, cloth, or other fabric moisture in the salt was to pass, so
that when the electricity passed from the wire to the cylinder
through the profane fabric the decomposition was effected and
color produced. -- Another mode described was the passing of the
fabric between two metallic points, connected with the two
points of the battery.
