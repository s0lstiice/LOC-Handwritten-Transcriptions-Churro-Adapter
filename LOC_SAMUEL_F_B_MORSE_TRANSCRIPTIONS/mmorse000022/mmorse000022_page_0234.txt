think is dead, I believe it has been held up by speculators to drive a better
bargain with me, thinking to scan me, but they don't find me so easily fixed
on it. — In Virginia I had to oppose a most bigoted narrow liberal clique
in a Rail road company, who had the address to get a bill through the House
of Delegates, giving them actually the monopoly of Telegraphs, and attempting
to hallo before they were out of the woods, Mckindell went post haste to Richmond,
met the bill and its supporters before the Committee of the Senate, and after
a sharp contest, procured its rejection in the Senate and the adoption by a vote of 13 to 7 of a substitute granting me right
of way and corporate powers, which bill after violent opposition in the House
was finally passed 44 to 27. So a mean intrigue was defeated
most dismally, and I came off triumphant.
I had also as you know some difficulties at the West; there
is a prospect of a fair adjustment of them.
We are at this moment in a good deal of excitement
with the rumours from the Rio Grande. Santa Anna
has an overwhelming force of some 30,000 men, while
Taylor has but about 6,000, yet it is evident that
Santa Anna has been discomfited. Authentic accounts may
arrive to give you details by this Steamer. If they arrive after
my letter closes, I will send you the papers. — A dreadful
gale, worthy of March, has temporarily stopped the Telegraphs
in a most critical juncture of affairs, but a day or two will
put all right again.
