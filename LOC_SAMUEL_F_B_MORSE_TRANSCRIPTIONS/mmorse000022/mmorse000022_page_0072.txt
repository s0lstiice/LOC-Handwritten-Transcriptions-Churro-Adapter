2 Mr. Tenney
In his letter he not only endorses this objection, but says he is convinced
that it is indefensible. I write now to you as a personal friend having
the pleasure of personally knowing Mr. Tenney, to request your attention to this
matters. What I speak plainly? There is utter ignorance on the part of Mr. Tenney
of the subject made of the matter of patent printing. I have been attempting to secure
the capabilities of Mr. Tenney for printing his books or else there has been an attempt to secure
it in some other understanding. I am sure from the word ignorance I do not mean it
to be offensive sense, for those who have not well studied the matter, there
is an ignorance which renders them very liable to be deceived. I might without
carrying out the work of some who may be with me, give you a definite
description of the relations between us. I cannot see the importance of
deceiving the public by my mistakes. I have been in the habit of
having my mistakes mistaken (and I am sure I am not alone in this)
of an observant character to notice your errors in the subject.
