Washington Janv. 28. 1847
Dear Sidney,
My letter will be short for my time is Straitened having
just heard that to day is the last day from here for the Steamer, and I have
but just got the intelligence from you by her. I am resolved to hear of your
continued health and that of Catherine and the rest of you. I shall be greatly
delighted to welcome you home again. Telegraph matters are becoming
more & more interesting, the people of the country every where desirous of availing
themselves of its facilities and the lines are being extended in all directions. As might be expected then, I have my plans interlaced with
the telegraph project, which I have been trying to put up real telegraphs
and contest my patent. I am ready for them. We have had to apply
for an injunction on the Phil. d Pittsburg line, the case is an approximate
one and will be decided on Monday or Tuesday at Phila in C. Court
of U. States. I have no anxiety at the result, there are more
F. Als than one, yet not one quite so bad. I think amid all the
scramble, I shall probably have enough come to my share, and
it does not matter by what means but Heavenly Father chooses
to control my receipts, for I shall have just what he pleases,
none can hinder it, and more I do not want.
We have just received sad news from the West. Mr Ellsworth,
had a fall which broke his leg badly, and is terminiated fatally
on the 14th inst. The loss of his mother is a sad blow to poor
Annie, and must greatly shock Henry in Stockholm when he
hears the melancholy tidings.
Richard will doubtless write you by Steamer from N.York
and give you all news from that quarter. I have just got a letter
from Susan at Syracuse, all well and in good spirit, and sends
much love to you and all. Charles is well and passing the winter
at Newburg, &c. is Finley at Bladewack.
