I give you an arrangement of the wires which will enable you at once at Boston to try the same experiment.
This arrangement as you will know is this
A.P.P.D. is one ground circuit, and B.P.P.C. the other, while
A.B.C.D. is a complete metallic circuit. C is the battery and battery which will enable you at Boston to try the same experiment.
Now connect this metallic circuit by a wire represented by the dotted lines without disconnecting the ground at P. disconnecting B.F. from the
positive other pole opposite P. The electricity has now a choice between the ground circuit & the metallic. If upon closing and breaking at A.F. the instruments A & D work and not B & C, then the electricity has chosen the ground in preference to the wire, and but if B & C, as well as A & D, work then it has chosen the metallic circuit. This I think is the experimentum crucis and will decide the point.
Please give me the result as you have facilities for the experiment which at present I have not. We found that fewer cups were necessary with the ground circuit than with the entire metallic, and I therefore infer that the electricity in the experiment suggested, will choose the ground rather than the wire.
