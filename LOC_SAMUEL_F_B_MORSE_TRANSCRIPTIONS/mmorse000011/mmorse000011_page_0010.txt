New York City Aug 30th 1832.
My dear Morsell,
In order that you may receive without delay the sum of money you kindly loaned me in Paris, I have handed it to your brother, supposing that a letter from him may find you, with more certainty and rapidity than one from me. Having forwarded it in this way, I feel as if a hundred were removed from my mind; the in no degree is a sense of your kindness diminished thereby.
On my arrival in this city last week, I found that the Cholera had already appeared here, and after having committed considerable ravages, was greatly on the decline. This circumstance has dissuaded me from firing myself in New York at least at the present time; tho' I surely do not and cannot regret the declaration of the cholera. Immediately arrival here, two letters have come to hand from my brother in Salisbury, North Carolina, in search of me, whereas I should be, and requesting me with great earnestness to return with him. This I shall do in 12 or 15 days.
In the mean time I am impatienting the publication of my pamphlet, which I found in print on my arrival. It will afford much to later and is intended for the benefit of the medical men, and not for popular use. I shall risk it however,
