Copy April 10th 1833
Entered July 1st 1833. enclosed to Mr. Canes
My dear Madame Trainways,
You favor of the 6th February is
this moment received and I regret to learn that
you have not received the letter which I transmitted to you in the latter part of last year. I find
mention made also of a letter sent to me, I have received but one from you to which that was an
answer. One from you to me and one from me to you
are somewhere in France on the way between
Penn and Washington. It may be that you have received
the last one this last that should not be the case, I
will give you the substance of it, especially in relation to the sale of your collection of paintings to the Government of the United States. – I took the
first opportunity after my recovery from a long illness which confined me several weeks, to converse with my friend Mr. Yerplanck, member of Congress, and
who as Chairman of the Committee of Ways and Means, would not only know the views of the Government on the subject but would have great influence in securing
the Government to accept any measures for the improvement of the arts which he might think advisable and expedient.
His answer was in the highest degree discouraging not only to the prospect of present, but of future encouragement by the Government, he said he had received my letter from
Paris, but so entirely out of the question was any such proposal to the Congress, and so difficult was it in public duties that he did
not answer it supposing he should soon see me and explain at personally, there were his remarks, and I wrote to
you that there was not the slightest probability, scarcely a possibility that the Government will in my life time
