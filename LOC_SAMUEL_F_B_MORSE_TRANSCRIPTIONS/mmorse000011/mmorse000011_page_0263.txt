Mr. P. Van Rensselaer, Esq.
My dear Sir,
In looking over some old sketchbooks I
yesterday, accidentally found the enclosed slight outline
of M. P. V. R., which I hope Roger, and think, made at
Cooperstown, (I think in 1839 just before I left the country for
my late visit to Europe.) Your brother Stephen and himself
well remember the time. Thinking it might be pathetical to
you to have it, imperfect as it is, and perhaps possessing no
resemblance (of which you are of course the better judge,) I
have cut the leaf out of my book, and ask your acceptance
of it.—
Since I heard but a week or two since of your
severe affliction, and affliction, my dear Sir, in which I
know how to sympathize with you from the bottom of my
heart, I was upon the point of sitting down to write you
having experienced in my own case the influence of the kind
sympathy of friends in mitigating the danger of a trouble the latter
part of which must be felt in order to be understood. Yet you will
feel as I felt that the sympathy of those most dear
to us can only apply a healing touch to all the wounds which our
much beloved sends through all the affections of the heart. The heart
knows its own bitterness and alone who makes the heart can
real the wounds, which he in righteousness and in mercy too, has fit to inflict.
