My dear sir
West Point Jan 25 1832
In the absence of the 21st in reply to mine of the 28th ultimes.
You may imagine I am in a little difficulty in writing this letter from the fact that I have not received your letter of the 28th ultimes. I am deeply regret your determination as I had expected the case that the Academy would, sooner or later, leave the benefit of your distinguished talents and would have felt no ordinary pride or satisfaction in having you as an associate fellow labourer.
It was only necessary for the Government to know that you must accept in order that it should confer the appointment. It was of course no room to doubt.
