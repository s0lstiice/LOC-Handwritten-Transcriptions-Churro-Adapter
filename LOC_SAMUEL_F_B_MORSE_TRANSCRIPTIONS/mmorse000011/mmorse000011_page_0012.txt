Several gentlemen made particular inquiries concerning
you, among whom is Mr Gallandet. At New Haven I
saw only Mr Day and Mr Kingsley -- it was vacation and
the others were absent: --
Nullification, which now awfully means disunion, has
assumed an awful importance -- it threatens with a cer-
tainty that appears to me inevitable, at least, civil
war. The nullifiers in S. C. form a large majority,
and already have committed disgraceful outrages on
the property of the unionmen. They must in contest
of physical strength, for the nullifiers, will doubtless
attempt to expel from the State, and at no very dis-
tant period, those who refuse to abjure the general
point and to give their allegiance to
that of the State -- They are likewise
getting many adherents in North Carolina and Ge-
orgia. Many here affect to underrate the importance
of nullification and say, what can S. C. do? -- They
can create a civil war by attempting to expel the
unionists. -- The I do not in the least fear the general
result, this country seems to me to have arrived
at an awful & tremendous crisis. Our country
needs men of efficient intellect, could not Jefferson
Docher do us an immense service? I think so. --
Should the Whigian spread over this country and
destroy half the population, it would in any
station be a blessing, compared with the consequences
that may flow from nullification. --
With haste Your true & much obliged friend
Abel Smith.
