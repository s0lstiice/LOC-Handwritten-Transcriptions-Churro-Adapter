2 first met Prof. Morse in the Louvre, copying Renouard's
great picture of the Angel of the Louvre, with small copies
of the choicest pictures of the collection on eight. When the
cholera broke out, being unwilling to run away, and find
that Prof. Morse intended to remain in the house in the Rue
de Turenne, where he then was, and not liking that he
should remain alone there, for poor Americans, I
not speaking the language, I ventured to move to
the same house. He agreed & I did so. By my room,
near higher & more exposed than his, we made my two
rooms, one bedroom, and lost him as one setting
room, & studios. We lived together, & generally said
the same company. Our conversation, always turned upon subjects of importance to me, and I have
never forgotten the substance of many of them. He
told me of his experiments on light, & shade, in painting
of his studies in England with Leslie Washington
at Stony Washington Irving, & others under Berry West.
of his visits to Italy, and the political information
which he obtained there (afterwards published in book
from some the columns of the New York Observer) &c &c
