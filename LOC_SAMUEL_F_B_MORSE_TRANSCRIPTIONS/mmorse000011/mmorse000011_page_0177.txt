Dear Sir,
Cambridge May 24th 1834
Mr Allston left with me last week, a
folio Vol. to be forwarded to you, one of Carwill, N.Y.
It went by Jno P. Haven, Bookseller, N.Y. in a box of
books belonging to him. Haven left Boston about a
week since. & I not proper to drop you a line, lest
the work might not have reached you, & this, to be
sincere, was Mr Allston's duty; but you probably know
something of his honor of letterwriting. As much as
he loved his friends, I very believe that the first
thought which comes into his head upon receiving
a letter from one of them is, "Well! well, I shall
have to answer this." He is intolerable health,
His picture for Mr P on Phillips is not yet finished,
but very, I trust, very near being so. My brother was
extremely ill all winter. Is now tolerably well again.
I liked to have made Mr Robt Sedgwick a visit; but
instead of it, have sent my daughter. When I still see
N.Y. I know not. I should be glad to take a peek at
your Babel, for I have several friends in it
S.F.B. Morse, Esq
New York
with great regard
Will H Dana
