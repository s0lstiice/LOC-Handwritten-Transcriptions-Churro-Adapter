My dear Mose, Phil April 22d. 1833.
Write to consult you upon the
subject of our valued friend Allston's interest. I have
just heard that very recently the scheme of
having the four remaining pictures for the Capitol
has again been revived at Washington -- that is, so far
as talking it over has gone.
Can the Artists as a
body do any thing to further so desirable an event
as a commission from Congress to Allston for an
Historical picture; turn it over in your mind,
see Mr Verplanck on the subject, and if you
form a plan in which I can be useful, do
gratify me by imparting it.
The only means -- as well as I can
judge -- to relieve Allston from his present embarrassments,
would be such an order; the price
a large one, and paid in advance. Such
means, would enable him to put the last hand
to his great historical pictures so long deferred;
would be honorable to the Country, and
save it from a lasting reproach of this
duty to neglected.
Sincerely Your Friend
The Truly
L. Mosby Esq
98214
