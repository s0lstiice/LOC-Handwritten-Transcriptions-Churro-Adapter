Albany Augt. 15. 1834.
My dear sir,
Receiving your letter with its valuable
enclosure in due season, and have made use of the General's
letter as you will see in my opinion, a copy of which I have
with me from the letter which shall return, separately to your
directions, till I have the pleasure of seeing you or your letter
again. I should send it done immediately, but from the fear
that some evil might possibly befall it.
I am happy to say that your picture has
received a universal and magnificient appreciation. It is the
common remark of those who see it that it can not be more
perfect. It is to be put into the hands of Demand in the most
probable form next week for an engraving. May well you are taken
an eye on the work in its progress, and let him understand
that unless he does his very best in the engraving the picture will
come away with all the glory.
In the hope following your letter
I am very sincerely yours,
B. Ray remains your brother from
Philadelphia Robinson's only
draft, and I am sure that it will
do as well as any other in further
conveying the State of the Union to the people of this country. David Knowlton Knowlton. - W. M.
