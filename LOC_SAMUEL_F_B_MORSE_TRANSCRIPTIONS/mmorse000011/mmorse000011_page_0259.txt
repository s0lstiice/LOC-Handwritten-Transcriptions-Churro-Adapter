New York May 20th 1835.
A. S. Willing Jr. Esq.
My dear Sir,
Your acceptable letter in answer to mine of the 13th April was
duey rec'd and should have been replied to by me before this time but for
the unusual pressure of duties connected with the Academy, which is
always greatest at the season for our Annual Exhibition.
With regard to the matter on which I felt that I ought to
address you, as the article in the Courier was editorial, as your
name alone is published at the head of the paper as editor, and
as I had presented a copy of my work to you as a friend and
not as editor, you can easily believe that my surprise was
great at the manner in which not only the matter of the book
was noticed, but at the use which was made of presenting to you
a copy with my respects. I was obliged to consider you as
the writer and yet the very first observation I made to my brother
when I had read it was "Mr. Willington never wrote that article,
I know him too well to believe that he would treat any one who
had made a compliment to him with so uncourteous a manner
yet it appeared on the face of it as if I could therefore be
no less than attribute it to him until I had written you an
explanation. Your letter my dear Sir, is entirely satisfactory
so far as regards yourself, and I am not disposed to cavil
at the editorial amende that I call it? I might with
justice, perhaps, ask Mr. Yeadon why he would have
said that a copy had been presented to him with the author's
respects, when the author had not the pleasure of his acquaintance
