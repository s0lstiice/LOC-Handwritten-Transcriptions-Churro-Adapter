Mr. Morse,
Sir
Having had the charge of the
Trumbull Gallery, from a short period after it was first
opened, I wrote you about a year ago, on the subject of
Prof. Silliman's portrait, which is now in the Gallery with
other paintings of yours.
Being a stranger to you I retained the letter in
the hope that I could have a personal interview.
When your Picture was at the Franklin Institute
I called to see it and on going out told Mr. Stoner
that I wished to see you. He replied that you was
in N. York.
The portrait alluded to is not approved of by
the acquaintances of Prof. S. generally; indeed
in most instances it is condemned. I think for
myself, that it is so unlike him that I could
with it were removed from the Gallery -- though
it might have been a good one when taken.
The question is often asked Who painted it? I
am obliged to tell them -- at the same time I
alludes to your other ones, as being excellent.
