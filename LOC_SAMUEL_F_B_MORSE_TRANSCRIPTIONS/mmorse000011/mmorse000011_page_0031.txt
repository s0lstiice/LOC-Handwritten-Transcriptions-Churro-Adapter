Albany Nov 29 1832
My dear Sir
Accept my congratulations for your safe return, our
news on safety of Mr. Heath being in New York about the time of your
arrival. I should have said my respects to you personally, but
when I heard you were there it was too late for me as I was under
the necessity of leaving for the same afternoon.
You have heard of my seven bereavements,
my dear Margaret was taken from me, just as we thought we were
entering in life, we look around us I saw every thing to make
life desirable, and thought amusing safe, but how soon did I find
that we were mortal, my hope, my anchor, my every thing was
broken from me and I am now alone, no, not alone, the loss
of two dear children, now my only comfort. You will sympathise
with me, for to you she was known. You know her
worth, how amiable, how gentle and lovely she was, but
she is gone & I have nothing to bring her features to my mind.
I have flattered myself that I am might be able to retain me
that you would be able to delineate her features I know it is
difficult, & this is why I wish it, but at times her features are more
vivid than at other, may I hope that you retain so vivid a
recollection of her as to be able to furnish me with a portrait.
I do not expect it to be exact, but some time when her from
some circumstance her features may be best, more forcibly
to your mind, I hope you will be able to embody them I do
not make any apology for making the request. You know
