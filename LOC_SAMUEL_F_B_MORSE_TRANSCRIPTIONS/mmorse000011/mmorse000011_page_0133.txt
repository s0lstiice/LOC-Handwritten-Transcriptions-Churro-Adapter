New York July 16th, 1833
9 1/2 o'clock.-
J. F. Cooper Esqr.
My dear Sir,
The packet will go in a few minutes so I must be brief.-
I recd. an impudent sort of a letter from Gould a day or two ago enclosing
me a paper the N. York Traveller, (as yet of limited circulation,) in
which was an article which he pretends to have come accidentally
yet it bears too strong internal evidence of his own pen for me to doubt
who is the author, he says he has sent a copy to you. He is too low in
every respect for your notice. I could wish that he might be treated with
the most persevering silence, for every breath of notoriety exalts him
if it does not detract from you.- I saw Stone yesterday he is going to
explain today; as I said before in a former letter, there is no malice in
his attack, and he regrets having wounded. You know Stone, he is not al-
ways most prudent, but he is at heart on your side and well disposed. We
will see, however, what he says. As for King he has overshot his mark.
He is hollow hearted and shallow, and it requires all the charity I pos-
sess to resist my propensity to despise him.- I have no time to speculate
at present. I wish you were at home. You would soon set matters right.
I spent the 4th inst. at Mr. Jay's at Rye in the old mansion. He in-
sists that you have the mass of the public on your side, and that you
are wrong in being so sensitive to these petty attacks.- I do think myself
that the character of those who delight to sting if they can demands that
they should be treated with silent contempt.-
I long to see you all back again. N. York is remarkably healthy,
and my own health was never better.-
The picture will be finished this week.
Yrs in great haste but with sincere esteem and respect
S. F. B. Morse.
