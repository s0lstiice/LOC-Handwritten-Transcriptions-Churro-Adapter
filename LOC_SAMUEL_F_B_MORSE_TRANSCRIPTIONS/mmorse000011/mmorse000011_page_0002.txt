Paris July 18th 1832.
At length after nearly 4 months delay I
have received a letter from you. I had become completely
discouraged in writing to you, and had determined to write you
no more except to say when I left Paris that I had gone to
England and should sail for home at such a time. Your long
silence has prevented your receiving many letters which I
felt in the mood to write, had not the reflection been forced
upon me that you either did not receive them or did not care
to receive them, for after so many reiterated invitations to
write me, to have ship after ship among from your very
door to the number of 13 without giving me the least intelligence
from you, I could not but feel strongly your neglect, and when
ever I thought to write you, I had no encouragement to take
up my pen. You say you thought I must have left Paris
yet if you had still written to Havre to Mottignac &c. I should
have got your letters wherever in Europe I might have been. But
what is past can't be helped, I am glad exceedingly glad to hear
of your prosperity and hope it may be continued to you.
I am diligently occupied every moment of my time at the
Louvre finishing the great latter which I have there undertaken
to finish I mean that part of it which can only
be completed there, viz. the copies of the pictures, all the rest I hope
to do at home in NYork, such as the frames of the pictures, the figures,
&c. It is a great labor but it will be a splendid and valuable
work, it excites a great deal of attention from strangers and the
French artists, I have many compliments upon it. I am sure it is
the most correct of its kind ever painted, for every one says I have
caught the style of each of the masters. Cooper is delighted with it
