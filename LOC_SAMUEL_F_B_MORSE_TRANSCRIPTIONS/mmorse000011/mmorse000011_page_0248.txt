Boston Feb. 20th 1835
Dear Sir.
The distress and oppression by
you in Mr Allston's situation gives you the
best claim to learn what we have accomplished.
Mr Dutton & Mr Doster entailed with me into the
labour, unprofitable as it was, of attempting his
release. We first learnt his debts, & then obtained
from him, the necessary text, permission to detain
prisoners to pay them. We have been successful,
fully, perfectly successful, and now his debts
are in payment to entire payment, we have
all that they require, & something more to
make him free from previous embarrass-
ment for a quarter more to come. One gentleman
advanced $1000, then other gentlemen
together advanced $1750, fine others together
$250 making a total of $3250. One note
of $2000 secured by Mortgage remains to be
paid. This is to be paid out of the first
financial position. Two are weekly finished, three
John & Macbetts three engaged at
$1500, and $333, only have been advanced on
credit. So that $2334 will remain which of
the $1200 on paid will leave us a $1134
clear. Now he is without all, enough
remains of the advanced sums to support
