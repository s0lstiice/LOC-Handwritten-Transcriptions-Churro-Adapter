New-York March 5th 1833.
My Dear General,
I have the pleasure of sending you a copy
of the New York Commercial Advertiser, of this date, in which
you will find three documents of some interest, The
President's Inaugural Address, The Explanation in
the Senate, and, may I say, the article headed "Our Legation
"to France." Of the latter article, I am the author. I could
not but feel indignant, when I learned that Mr. Harris
was nominated Charge des Affaires to France, knowing as I
do the course of that gentleman when I was in Paris, and
I felt it to be a duty I owed the country to lay the whole night before
them. I hope you will find my statement correct, and
yet, not having the documents before me, I was obliged to
depend altogether on my memory, and I may have erred in
some important points. I am utterly at a loss to con-
ceive how this appointment could have been made with
Mr. Rives in the Senate, unless he supported it; and
from the often expressed opinion of Mr. Harris by Mr. Rives
I cannot conceive it possible that he should not have
opposed it. This appointment connected with events
that occurred in Paris, such as the mention of Mr. Rives
as authority against your Mr. Power's Statements by Mons. Power
