Grove Hall. Oct. 13th 1834.
My dear Father,
Your kind letters and present, around
safety. You do not know how surprised I was
when I opened the bundle, to find a nice
fur cape. I do not know how I shall ever repay
you for your kindness, dear father I mean to
try and please you in every thing, if it is in
my power, and I know I can, by being attentive
to my studies, and obedient to my teachers.
The fur cape is very comfortable this weather,
and will keep me warm this winter.
When do you intend going to Concord, father?
I wish I could go with you, and I know
it would do me good to take a journey I
remained stationary so long, besides I should
feel new energy to commence my studies for
the winter. I leave it to you dear father to
think about. If you should give your consent
I should be very glad, but if you do not I
know you would do it for my good.
I have four new boarders this term, Miss Pratt,
from Brooklyn, Miss Van Richten, from Albany, Miss
Shepherd, from Georgia, (Cruin to miss Mary Hillhouse)
