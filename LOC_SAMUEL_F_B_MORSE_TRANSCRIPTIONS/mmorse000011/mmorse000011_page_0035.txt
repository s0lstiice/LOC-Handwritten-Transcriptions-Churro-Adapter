I am sorry that Mr. Bostons amiable family
should have been wounded by fictiously
criticism. May oppositors who have been before
the public have had webs of that kind &
in general it is better not to urge them
any further than to correct any thing wrong
which an ill-instructed critic will of course
expose - provided he has sense enough to
see it. You may have heard that we
have erected a picture gallery in our old
stone building with basement for
offices, &c - affords two rooms each 30
feet square in the clear & 24 high sky
lighted connected by a sliding door. --
but Trumbulls pictures occupy one room.
They are a legacy to the college to take
effect after his death in 1840. An annuity is paid him & the rooms are
now in the course of exhibition.--
In the second room (the first as we enter)
are the college paintings which he had
before & then made to our friends will
favor us by giving or loaning such
paintings as they spirits of art as will add
to the interest of the exhibition the avail
of which after Mr. Trumbulls death are
devoted forever to the support of poor students
with my kind regards to your best
friend & a renewed of my welcome I remain very
dear sir very truly yours J. H. Bostman
Mr. Morris
Bostman
