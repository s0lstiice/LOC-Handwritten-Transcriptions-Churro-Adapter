New York May 28th 1847.
Dear Sidney, I am obliged to write you a more hurried
letter than I intended, as I am unexpectedly called to
Washington this afternoon on business, and so shall not
have time to write the last days of the month and I
cannot let our first splendid steamer the Washington
on the 1st June go without taking you a letter. I expect you will receive
this on the 12th of June, let me know how near I am to
the mark. We are looking with a good deal of gratification
to the latter part of July in the hope of seeing you all safe
home again. Susan and Edward Hope will also be here
with dear little Charley, and then we shall have such a lot
of little cousins, the whole city will not be able to contain us.
My Telegraph affairs present both prosperous and adverse
phases. South & North they look well, but in the Valley
and West from Phila I have dishonest men to deal with,
and shall have much trouble to put things right. But I
am not distressed about it. The same wise hand still
directs and I know all will work well if the heart
only reposes on him with unshaken faith.
I shall have a somewhat romantic story to tell
you when you arrive respecting my foreign Telegraph
prospects, at present I must be mum. It may result
in advantage to me. I am still writing you from the
old office in your buildings, where you left me, and
I lodge in Bank St. at Richards. When you return
