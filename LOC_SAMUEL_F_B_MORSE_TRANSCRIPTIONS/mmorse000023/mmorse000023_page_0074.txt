June 23, 1847.
Dear Sir,
Your of 21st. is just received and finds me
overwhelmed with cares. I yesterday completed a resolution
among other changes in my relations with my associates, & H. Riley,
by which half my interest in the Southern line is
transferred to Mr. Kendall, and if I shall not be
present at the meeting of Stockholders, Mr. Kendall
will be my proxy. I shall not make myself a fact
in the conflicting claims of candidates for the Presidency of the Company, or for any other office. The
financial business of the Company, to which all these offices
relate, is wholly foreign to my habits, and I prefer
to leave them in other hands. I have confidence
in the Stockholders that they will elect to the office
of trust in the Company those who will conduct
their business to the best advantage, and I have
no design personally to interfere on any arrangements which they may think to will subserve
their interests. The examination of candidates
for the office of President of the Southern
Company will be made by the Board of Directors
and the directors of the Company will elect the President.
I was quite shocked at the death of Collier.
It might have been very sudden.
Kind regards to Mrs. Rogers, and Thomas
with his family
