1845 in Paris the conversation at one moment turned upon the
claim set up in the year 1839 by Dr. Charles T. Jackson of Boston, to
be the inventor of my Telegraph. The claim was made in a letter of Dr. Jackson
published in the Compte rendu of the Academy in March 1839
and in this claim I explained the nature of this claim and afterwards
added a note which I intended should be published in the
Compte rendu & counteract the injurious statement. I also promised to
transmit to you a copy of the correspondence with Dr. Jackson for the information of the
Academy, that the truth in the matter might be perfectly ascertained.
I have just been informed through a letter from another
Academy in London of an article in the Review Scientific for May 4 June 1845
in which my claim to priority of invention of the Electric magnetic
Telegraph is called in question and a most injurious use is made
of the note of Dr. Jackson to throw a doubt over my claims.
In this state of the case, I have not hesitated to ask your indulgence
while I give such a history of this matter as I think will not fail to set
the truth clearly before the world. I have written to the Academy
and to the United States on the 7th of March 1839, only
March (three days before my application to the Comptoir
to M. Belin de Beaumont was communicated to the Comptoir
and not published in the Comptoir, which was the first step in
having been communicated to the Comptoir, which was therefore
its existence not only authorized but established for Europe
sailing for Europe my again in the autumn of that year.
