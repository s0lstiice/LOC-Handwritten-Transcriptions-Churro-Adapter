Since Mr. Bennett's return, there is also a return to some features quite objectionable.
But a kind office requires no justice a kind return, and I therefore, give him the
publication of the Telegraph Map. He will speak also of Cerography (the
old Cerography of course,) I employed Galway to plan it for me.
All was well in New York, I left the city on Thursday evening
to see you and shall welcome your home, not here, but in Europe.
My affairs at the West are in an unsettled state as yet. Mr. Kendall
is there, (at Louisville,) and writes enviously. Mr. O'Reilly and his associates
have conducted in a most outrageous manner, and have created a widespread
misconception by their machinations which it will take time and labor to dispel,
but the tide is turned I learn, and I hope soon that a step will
be put to their injurious proceedings.
As to the House affair, the rival lines to Phila have
got their posts up, but as yet they are under 'here poles.'
no wire upon them. I am preparing to contest the matter
as soon as any demonstration really injurious shall take place.
I doubt whether I shall be compelled to try the case, for as you say of the
English edition of it, it has not life enough in it to oppose against us.
The more I think of your discovery the more I admire it. I see that
it must revolutionize printing & engraving.
Good bye. Truly as ever your affectionate brother
Finley.
Sidney E. Morse, Esq.
Please continue sending me books, papers &c. on Telegraphy
which you think may be of service. I sent you a history of my case with
the Attorney General by a former Steamer. I hope you got it.
