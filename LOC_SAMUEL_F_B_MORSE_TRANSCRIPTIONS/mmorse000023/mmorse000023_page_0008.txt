New York May 20. 1847
Dear Sir
Yours of 17th I have received. I am not
surprized at the proceeding of Mr. Robinson, after
his attempts to annoy and embarrass the operation
of the Telegraph, in Richmond last winter, but
I have no belief in his ability, much as he may
vault it, to seriously, to injure , the public convenience,
add to his own selfishness, and not the public good,
I regret to see, seems to be his impelling motive.
I have too much confidence in an enlightened
public opinion especially in the chivalrous
South and among the principle-loving Virginians,
to believe for a moment that his schemes
will succeed, even should he temporarily deceive
them into the idea that he had found some other
system quite as good as mine. I have no fear,
of that, when I have accounts from Europe by
way arrival that my system is supplanting
all the European systems of Telegraphy.
