Stedding Jan 14 1842
Dear Sir
Yours I received some time since and
what you designated in its pages I freely acknowledge. Although
the plan of two cylinders was new to me at the time. I was perfectly
aware that House's or any other Telegraph machine must be
operated by exact motions dependent on each other at each
Station. Although, if the apparatus I have devised (not calling
the uniform cylinder motion a new improvement) had been
what you most desired, I would willingly part with it
(which I hold is more simple and complete than any other
which has been invented) if it would place the original
inventor beyond the reach of competition of those (House to)
who would willingly, yet, gainingly blot his name from the
world and reap for themselves the benefit of what has probably
cost yourself many sleepless nights (like when your experiments
were defeated on Sommons-Island). I write not thus because
I despise or have the least grudge against House, for I know
him not. not even his residence. But I know men inventors of useful inventions, that would undoubtedly have been
made independent thereof from had it not been for like compulsion. Although you are probably reaping rich rewards
from your invention, it matters not the man or his circumstances. Of course I have some object and pride in
