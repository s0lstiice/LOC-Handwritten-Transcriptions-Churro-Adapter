Baltimore June 17th 1847.
Doctor Morse
Dear Sir:
Your favor of 15th is now before me,
and I am not surprised to hear of the opposi-
tion made to my being brought forwards as
a candidate for the Presidency of the Magnetic
Telegraph Company, after the misrepresentation,
and false accusations made when I was not
present to refute them. In justice to myself
and friends I am determined to have these
things cleared up. I am now satisfied why
so little estimate was placed upon the service
I was willingly rendering others, when their
aim has been to lessen me in the estimation
of my fellow citizens who hold stock in the
M. Telegraph Co. I have however the proud
satisfaction of knowing, that instead of depreciating me, it has had the contrary effect
of elevating, and strengthening their confidence
in me. Let my chance be but upon the
hazard of a die, and I will risk it, and
neither yielding to Mr. Trimble nor Mr Fuller,
the last gentleman, I feel assured by letters
from Capt. Black will support me, and Mr
Trimble has already admitted that I am
competent to fill the Station. I learn today
