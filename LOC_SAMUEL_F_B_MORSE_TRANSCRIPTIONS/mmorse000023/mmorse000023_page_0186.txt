New York, October 1st 1849
Dear Sir: Having learned at Irving House that you
had left for Philadelphia I take the liberty of re-
introducing the subject of commutation hell with you
during our interval of a few evenings since. Your
note of the evening day was forwarded to the Governor & I have just received a reply of which the
following is an extract.
"Washington, Sept. 25th 1849
I was aware at that time that the
business of contracting to supply materials, &c. &c.,
was foreign to the vocation of Prof. Bringe; but knowing him to be the inventor of the Telegraph, & deemed it nothing more than proper that he should receive a bonus (to which I agree with him he is entitled) I thought it would be better, if his invention should be used, that he, with his great experience in this business should be approached, & if agreeable to, have the contract for building said telegraph, he charging so much per mile, bonus included, & provide everything, even operators, &c., &c., &c.
If Prof. Bringe should be inclined to take charge of this affair, he might greatly facilitate our views, & at the same time secure that remuneration to which his invention undoubtedly entitled him --
