Preamble.
When I, in October of the year eighteen hundred, I thirtysix years
old, was on board the packet ship Sally on her voyage from France
to New-York, I conceived the idea of marking the telegraphic
signs, I had invented, (being dots, spaces to signify
numerals, by electrical decomposition of certain salts
and chemical compounds; and whereas the application
of the proper means for producing a successful result
of this thought, was soon after superseded in my mind
by another method, at the same time conceived of
marking the said signs, to wit, by magnetism produced
by electricity, which in the successful method was
in use, and having recently returned to my original
thought of applying decomposition by electricity through
a single circuit of Conductors, and discovered a means
of successfully applying the same, as then conceived
to the marking of the French signs for numerals and
letters, and of any desired characters, I will here describe
the nature of my invention and the method by
which I obtained my results.
The nature of my invention consists,
First, in the application of the decomposing effects of
Electricity, produced from any known generator
of Electricity to the marking Alphabetic or other signs
for numerals a letters, a words or sentences, though a simple
circuit of Electric conductors, by which intelligence may
be communicated, marked on cloth, metals or other
convenient material.
Secondly, in the mode of applying this decomposition,
and the machinery for that purpose.
Thirdly, in the application of the bleaching qualities
of electricity to the printing of any desired
characters.
