United States Legation
Stockholm July 15. 1847
My dear Friend
I have just returned from Hamburg
to which place I accompanied my dear wife and child
on their way to the United States, and where they left
me for London nearly three weeks since
It was in lieu of remain sometime after their
departure, in consequence of repeated attacks of the fever
and ague, with which complaint I have suffered since
last fall, at rather frequent interval, proving that Sweden
needs an accommodation in itself, as well as peace and quiet
as any nation of the United States, and that the climate
of our Western State, are found all over the world, even
in latitudes as high as this.
It would be quite a surprise to the
mother of those who assume that their and our relations
exclusive, to certain conditions and situations, like to be
that I brought the seed of the discord with me from
my former residence, for it happens in fact, that
almost every other person in Stockholm, has been similarly
affected, during the past six months.
While at Hamburg, I made the acquaintance
of Messrs. William & C. B. Robinson, who give encouragement
to introduce your excellent telegraph into Germany, and
I think with Considerable prospect of success. For the purpose
of aiding its future introduction into Sweden, I give the
form of a short telegraphic despatch, in my Majesty's
king of Sweden and Norway, which I have this day,
sent for his inspection, addressing the following communication
to the Minister of Their Affairs.
Coppy
