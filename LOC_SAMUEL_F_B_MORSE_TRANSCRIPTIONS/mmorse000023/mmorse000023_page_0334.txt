Washington Jan. 28th 1868
Dear Sir,
I enclose you a short article for your
journal deemed necessary by the passage referred to in it,
and to which mine is a reply.
I have been with much regret the tone of your journal
in regard to the unpleasant controversy which, though
no seeking of mine, has grown out of a contract made
in 1865 by my agents with Mr. O'Kelly. I regret it
the more because I have been long led to entertain
admiration for your talents, and a high personal regard
for your character as represented to me by friends who know
you. If these representations are correct, you cannot know-
ingly give countenance to such desperate attempts to injure
me, as have been made by certain parties, not merely in
my legal rights, in denouncing my talent as an odious
monopoly, but through every kind of obstruction from the
merits of my inventions, thereby distorting upon it in such
a way as to rob me of the fruits of years of arduous toil, at
the moment when these seemed to be a prospect of my
reaping some reward. I believe you have been impressed
upon by wicked men, who have not hesitated to use false
blood and money to compass their selfish ends. These are
strong things but they are weighed and carefully selected.
