Utica Nov. 10, 1837.
Dear Sir-
On the day before yesterday the gentlemen for
whom I made the application for the loan of
$1000, informed me that Mr. Matteson whom he
offered as endorser refused to do so from some dis-
satisfaction in relation to the matter, and that he should
thence be obliged to abandon it. I have delayed
writing in hopes that they would bring it about.
In the meantime I shall receive interest on the
amount from the 3d instant. I have spoken
to two other gentlemen to take the money, and
have had one answer as early as Friday
who will be equally satisfactory, and I think
the offer of $1000 will cause it to take place. I desire until
you are satisfied as to the amount of the interest I will
not take it. I will write to you on Friday as soon as I see them.
I am required to leave the Cincinnati
on Monday morning. I am satisfied of the bankruptcy of
the House patent and seriously hope that
you will have no more trouble about it.
I speak to Mr. Willm about the Wall
street notes and he will take it to tomorrow.
Next day when I will depart the contract
to you in credit.
I will defer executing the contract about
the hours as you suggest until after the
15th January. The heavy rain now being com-
pleted. Our friend Calhoun is in Albany fully
