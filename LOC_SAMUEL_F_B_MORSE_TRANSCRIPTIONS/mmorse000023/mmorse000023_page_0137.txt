take your fancy, coincided so exactly with ours, that we conceived it
as a presidential intimation to go forward. I think I have not
moved in this matter without asking counsel of the great Counsellor,
and the way seems to have been made so plain, that I have no mis-
givings in the matter. In regard to the farm part for Charles Finley
I have wished their funds, in your hands, to rest for them in part pay-
ment, and for their sake I wish you were here at this time, as Charles
does not know exactly what the condition of his affairs is with you.
I think as you have the kedge anchors well fixed on this side, we
shall see you warping yourself up to this side of the Atlantic
soon. Do come soon, but come in a Steamer, a Burard Steam
so as not to be forever on the voyage.—
I forgot to say that Susan, and the Overmanns were
delighted with the place.
I have much more to say but must close to be in time
for the mail to Boston & the Steamer.—
In haste but affectionately as ever
Yr Brother
Sam Finer morden
