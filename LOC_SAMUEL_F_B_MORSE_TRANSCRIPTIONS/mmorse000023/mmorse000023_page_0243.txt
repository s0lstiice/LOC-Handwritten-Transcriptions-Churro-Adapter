Copy sent to J. D. Reid Phila. Dec. 21 1847
My dear Sir
You may recollect when I met you in Phila. on the unpleasant
bussiness of attending in a Court to arbitrate the contest of two parties for their
rights, you informed me of the Disturbing condition of O'Kelly's family. At that
moment I was led to believe from consultation with the Counsel for the
Patients that the case would undoubtedly go in their (the Patients) favor. Your
Statement touched me, and I could not bear to think that an innocent
wife, and offensive children should suffer, even from the wrong doing
of their proper protector, Should this prove to be the case. You re-
member, I authorized you to draw on me for Twenty dollars to be
remitted to M. O'Kelly's family, and to keep the source from them it was
desired secret. My object in writing is to ask if this was done, and in
case it was to request you to draw on me for that amount.
I see by the papers your admirable skill and tact with
your associate operator in transmitting the late Message of the
President. The performance reflects the highest credit on you
and them.
Truly wishing you all prosperity
remain Your friend
Sams S. B. Morse
