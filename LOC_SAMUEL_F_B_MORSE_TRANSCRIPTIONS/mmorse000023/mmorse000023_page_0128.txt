Washington July 28th 1847
Prof. S. F. B. Morse
My Dear Sir
Mr. A. Reebe has sent me what
purports to be a copy of a letter from you
to him dated the 3d inst. in which you propose
the boundary arrangement of 10 per cent upon the cost with him in case
he will get up a company to build a line
of your telegraph in Texas. This he calls a
contract and from our refusal of a
connecting line from the Sabine to the Mississippe.
This proposal seems to be based on the assumption
that Texas is to be considered in relation to the
telegraph a foreign country. I do not understand
it to be so in point of fact. Upon the annexation
of a new state to the Union, I take it that the exclusive
rights of all patents are ipso facto extended
over its territory in the same manner and to
the same extent as they previously existed within
the former limits of the United States. If I am right
in this, Texas need not and should not be separated
from the rest of the Union in our telegraphic
operations. It may raise troublesome questions between
youself and corporations in the patents, besides
which (though I should make no difficulty about
it) I consider Texas as brought by annexation
within the scope of my contract with you as
well as of your patent.
