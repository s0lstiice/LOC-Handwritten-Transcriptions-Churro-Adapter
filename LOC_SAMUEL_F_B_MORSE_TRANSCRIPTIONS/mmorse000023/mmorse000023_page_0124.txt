Washington July 25th 1847 9. P.M.
My Dear Sir.
Your of yesterday is just as true. Some
& others are hostile to Hart (and not without
some reason) and he to them. It is natural
that they should look on any disagreement in an
unmitigated opposition.
He was absolutely furnished and by Mr. Hart
here appeared to be a volume unnumbered for
of about $100,000, the volume I called his attention
to him produced his statement in detail, upon
a slight examination of which, I was satisfied
that his statement, hastily made out, was erroneous. A committee was appointed to examine
this amounts who, I hoped, would report to the
Board before adjournment, but being myself
not, I heard no more about it.
I do not think the meeting at Philadelphia
illegal, though I am not much of a lawyer.
As soon as Mr. Trent returns, I will get him
to inquire into it. If any vote, if we are
not a corporation, we are still a Joint Stock
Association.
I had sufficient reasons to turn Hart out; but
it was not necessary and I thought not prudent.
It might have united the Philadelphia stockholders with Rogers and given us some annoyance.
The position of the latter to the last, was in effect
"if you will pay me, I will be your friend, I
