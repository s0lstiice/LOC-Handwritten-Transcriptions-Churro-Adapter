Dear Father,
Your very kind letter of the 25th inst.
has just come to hand, though I regret that I can
not be in Africa on the day I have so long wished, yet
I am very happy to say that my disappointment is very early
done, when by remaining here I oblige my best of fathers.
I would not have you, my dear father, think you are at
all on my account, I should think that I should have
been passed much more pleasantly by you in the City.
Everything is going on very well here, the two Patricks, for
themselves are engaged in getting out the rocks that include
the flough in the lower lot bordering on the James' bluff
and I think the time I have profitably employed Charles
hers himself employed. To day I have engaged a carpenter
to fix his (Charles) house at 100s for doing he will do it
all in one day, I am to get the materials. Our stable
floor wants repairing which I will let remain till you
come. I have several letters here for you, one of which is
from Sirs. We have indeed much to be thankful for, we
have at least for I am that among many in having
such a kind and affectionate father as you have always
been to me.
Ever your affectionate son
Charles
W. H. Perkins
U.S. Navy Yard
Portsmouth Va
25th January 1847
Tuesday Morning
