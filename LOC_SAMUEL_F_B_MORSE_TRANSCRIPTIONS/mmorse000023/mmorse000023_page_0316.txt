Washington Jan. 21. 1848
Dear Sir,
Your of the 9th inst. forwarded from St. Louis
I have this moment received. It gives me real pain to learn
that there is any prospect of physical collision between the
Reilly party and ours, and I trust that this may arrive in time
to prevent any movement of those friendly to me which shall
provoke so sad a result. I emphatically say that if the law cannot
protect me in your region, I shall never sanction the appeal to
force to sustain myself, however, conscious of being in the right.
I infinitely prefer to suffer still more from the proscriptions
of these unprincipled men, than to gain my rights by a single
illegal step. Law and justice move slower than violence, but
their triumphs are more enduring. And I must have success through them
and my right.
I have great confidence in the high sense of right contained
in your region, despite the misinformations of those who would deceive them, and when the truth returns
to them their demands will be strong for the right, and I can trust them.
I hope you will do all in your power to prevent collision. If
the parties meet in putting up posts and wires, let them have their way
and opponents of my party will have no patent for putting up posts or wires, the men are not to blame, let no
feeling be entertained between them no rivalry, let friends, feeling as between
them be cherished, and teach them to refer all disputes to the principals.
