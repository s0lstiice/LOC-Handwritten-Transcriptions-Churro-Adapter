Sharon Springs 6th September 1847
Dear Brother,
I received your letter of 29 ult. and the Telegraph
despatch announcing it, and was highly gratified with its contents.
I should have answered sooner, but have been occupied as I will proceed to state.
I came here with Sidney, leaving his mother & the younger children at Saratoga
to return to NYork this week, in company with Rev. Wm. Nathan Hanisly, Elizabeth
& Charlotte went ten days ago to Hadley, I am to go by way of Albany
& NYork. I remain here till Monday, 13th inst. when I must hasten home,
having been absent about 7 weeks. Sid's sore head is not easily cured. His general
health requires attention & I think of nothing less than taking him from
school for the present, hoping by care in his diet & exercise that he may
soon resume his studies.
In rejoice in your prosperity. I congratulate you on Hall, Mr Lind
Husman, Charles & Sidney & little Charles, & especially, that you are
all met under your own roof. I am above all pleased with your acknowledgment
of God's hand in all his dealings with you. If your readiness to bless them
whether be gives or takes away, You say well that you expect trials they will always
come, I be as great perhaps in prosperity as at any time. One source of
trial for you, indeed, will be the unwavering attempt to detract from your reputation
& your income. But you have already been not to be sensitive in these
maters. I believe your knowledge of human nature from too correct a source
to expect to gather grapes from thorns, a figs from thistles. As an offset to
this evil, you have the sympathy of the good who are glad of your success.
Dr. Bronson, Dr. Arthur Bronson & not a few others have strongly expressed their
selves to me.
I have much to say when we meet again, and Ianticipate
your reply.
