Washington June 24th. 1844
Dear Sir;
Your of the 25th inst. is received and in reply I would say that I have not from the
part with the Government, without leave of the Department; I shall be in NewYork in a week or two
probably and can perhaps span you the quantity you want from some of my own which is stored there.—
In regard to my opinion of the failure of the first trial lead pipe I think the failure due to the
injury the insulation received in putting it into the pipe, such was the injury that not a single coil that I tried was
free from the fatal defect of having the electricity cross from wire to wire and to the pipe, so that my pipe has turned out
a dead loss, and has been sold for simple lead. It is its being tested before it left your premises; if it is true, it was my
misfortune to have trusted this testing to an unfaithful assistant, who passed it all as sound, leaving it to me to discover
when too late to remedy the evil, the fatal defect I have mentioned.
I congratulate you on your safe return, and thank you for the information respecting the extent of the English
Telegraph; The extent in pipe is or was greater than you mention. From London to Dayton on the G. Western R. way 13 miles
was laid down two or three years since. I am very respectfully
Benj: Jatham junr. NewYork. Spotsuit. S.F.B. More
