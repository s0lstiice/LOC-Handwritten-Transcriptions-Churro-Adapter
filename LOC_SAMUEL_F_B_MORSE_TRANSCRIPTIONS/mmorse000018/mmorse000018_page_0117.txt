Philadelphia July 1, 1844
Dear Sir,
As soon as you hear from your partner
I would be obliged to you to let me have answer
to the queries contained in my brother's letter, as well as
to those I made verbally when I saw you.
From what I have done since I returned I am sanguine of success in establishing
a telegraphic line between here and New York, but
I cannot consent to put the means I have for the
purpose into operation without some guarantee that
I shall not waste money and time in the attempt.
Within thirty or forty days I think with the peculiar
means in my power I can doubtless obtain the
Capital, provided it is not a greater sum than
that you mentioned to me. Therefore I would be
obliged to you if you would give me in legal form
the right to purchase if I choose to do so within
say forty days, for the sum you and your partners
agree upon an electro magnetic telegraph to be
erected between this city and New York upon the
line of the rail road, and the patent right to the
exclusive use of the telegraph to erected by you
and to any number of additional wires or additional telegraphs hereafter erected on the same
