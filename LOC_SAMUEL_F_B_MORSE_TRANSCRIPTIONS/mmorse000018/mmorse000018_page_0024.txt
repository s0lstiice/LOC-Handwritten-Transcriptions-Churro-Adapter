Copy June 11, 1844 Tuesday. Wash. 6 o'clock
D'Vail Have but a moment to say that your faith as a Christian
will be tried as well as my own, in the blow of which has taken place, I
so before the Committee this morning. Mr. S. (Smith) has written me a
Passionate letter accusing my friend W Ellsworth in the most offensive
terms. Depend upon it such feeling cannot cover any but the darkest
designs upon us all. I am sick at heart, and can only find relief
in that refuge, which in the hour of seeming hopelessness, has appeared
to turn sorrow into joy. Let them be your hope I trust, and all will come out right.
I have no fear for the result.
no time to add Truly yours
J.R.B.Morse
Wash. June 3. 1844 (Extract) (Pamphlet)
D. Vail,
The pamphlet of which you speak needs
a great deal of care in the preparation, and ought to be a joint
concern of yours & mine, since we alone have the materials and
documents for its compilation. I have laid up materials for
such a work for many years embracing a description of the above
time attempts of the first plans abroad. There ought to be plates.
It should not be a hasty or mere cates penny affair.
In haste
Yours
Alfred Vail, Esq.
Sam. B. Morse
