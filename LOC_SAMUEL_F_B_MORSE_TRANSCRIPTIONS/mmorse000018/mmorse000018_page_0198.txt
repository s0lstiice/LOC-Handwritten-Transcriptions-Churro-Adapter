Baltimore July 19th 1844.
Dear Sir
I have not the pleasure of seeing from
you a line since you left us here, — In relation
to the refusal of your half which you gave me, I
find the enquiry made can you not content more
of your desires therefore if Mr Gale will do so,
that he will, or if you please give me a refusal
for the purchase of his 18th also — I have not heard
from Mr Smith yet, but should suppose him
willing enough to do as you have. Mr Cornell
has not yet made his appearance — How will it do for
me to employ help to take up the lead? by having
Mr Avery with me a day or two, so as to have it
done as it should be could it not be done to
your will? In such case I would give it all the
personal attendance possible, and in my absence
would have one equally as good on the spot, as
to play let my play be left to Cornell, say 150,
or any way that you might deem best. All is if
you think my position requiring, which command him
as I presume you are anxious to have it so.
Again if in the construction of a line of telegraph you
may be desired to represent it — and prefer to continue
as to the basis & connecting part of it, would it be agreeable
