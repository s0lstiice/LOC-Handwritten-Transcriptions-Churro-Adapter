New York July 17th 1844.
Dear Sir:
A letter from Mr. Fry of Phila. in
disfavor to the proposals, which you sent, I have
just received. I wish much to see you, as I can
not move in this matter until I know your views.
I am here for about a fortnight, and wish
some arrangements made by which our business
can be transacted without the necessity of so much
waiting and so much writing. I have an excellent
office here which might be used for an Agency
for the Telegraph; but we must settle terms for
such an Agent first. When will you be here?
How are matters in relation to the New York &
Boston line? -- Mr. Fry objects to our reservation
of the way lines, and finds it an objection
in the minds of Capitalists to whom he has
applied. He also makes this proposition, that
money shall be raised to defray the cost of the
construction, and then this sum shall be doubled
as the amount of stock, this sum shall be doubled of which stock
we shall take in payment of our right. --
