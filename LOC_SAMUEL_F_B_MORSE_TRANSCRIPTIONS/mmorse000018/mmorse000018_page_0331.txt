Baltimore October 21st 1844.
Dear Sir.
Inclosed is a letter which I received, will
be obliged if you will hand it to Mr Vaid.
On calling at the Telegraph Office this fore noon
I learned from Mr Rogers that Mr V. resigned, who was
on the instrument up stairs? Mr R. replaced Mr B. this
Son - Mr Strong this brother. To which Mr V. orders perfectly,
"take it down & box it up" -
I thought I would explain to you, so far as I
know, and for my own part, I had an inclination to learn
if you were willing - And as for my son, he did accompany
me occasion ally but not with a view to learn neither is he
qualified for it. but no one else has attended with me. &
I was about to have a key & fasten myself in when I practised
Mr Strong does not wish, he says to learn, only to read
as there was an opportunity, he would be able to answer
when alone, or rather to signify that the attendant was absent.
Mr Rogers & I presume did it by the permission of his brother
and there can be no doubt of the best of situation or the fact
of either. being a young man, clock in the Mechanics Bank, and
of the first respectable and I learn from his brother that character
he has no particular desire, other than curiosity, as he has a very
good situation when he will be. If however you have
objections to my speaking, & such as you did not think of when you con
sented, I will say no more, but if you are willing, I would be
pleased to continue. Yours respectfully Truly
David Barback.
