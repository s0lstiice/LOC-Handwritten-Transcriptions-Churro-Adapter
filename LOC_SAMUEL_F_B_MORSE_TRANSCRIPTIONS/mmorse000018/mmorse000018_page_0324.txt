Mr R called his telep. The American Telegraph, he was
not aware perhaps that he has adopted my name for
mine. Show him in the first report to Congress, when
Smith was Chairman of Com. of Commerce, at the
bottom of the 7th page this name which I have taken, to
my Telegraph. He has given the same to his, inadvertently
doubtless, but it is (as he will see) producing confusion &
causing unjust inferences. I presume he will see it
properly corrected. How are you getting on? Write me
In haste but as ever Truly Your
P F B Morse.
New York July 26. 1844.
Alfred Rail.
Dear Sir
Since of the 24th I have just rec'd, I am glad to learn
that Mr Rogers & himself have got so as to Correspond between
Washington & Baltimore. I wish you to direct yr attention now to
the quantity of matter you can send in a given time, say in me
hour, I am glad to know that Mr Cornell is at work,
taking up the wires. Tell him he need not fear for want of
Employment, for by the time he has accomplished laying up
pipe & wires, I think it likely he will be wanted in a
way more to his satisfaction in Constructing other lines.
This will be the case if our arrangements here shd be as
satisfactory Completed, as they now promise —
