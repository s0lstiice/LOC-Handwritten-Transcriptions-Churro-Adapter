Washington
June 10. 1844
Dear Sir,
The bill you drew, I find is liable to
the following objections, and therefore a different
bill had better be drawn, and I am willing that
Mr. Choate or any other Honorable Member should
draft a bill to be presented, against which I
will state no objections.
I am advised by my Counsel at Baltimore
that each Proprietor becomes liable under the
contract you have drawn as a Partner, and
therefore, as I am not willing to assume the
such an obligations I am opposed to signing the
Contract.
I am willing to sell the whole right of the
Telegraph, or I am willing to sell the right
to construct and use it for a given distance
at a certain rate per mile and as the Govt. are not
likely in your opinion now to pay us the full
value this last may be the best.
1. It compels the Govt. to contract with the
Proprietors of Telegraphs for the Construction
of the Telegraph, as well as for the use of
the right (of the proprietors) -
2. It makes one Superintendent, while he is
also Contractor, and owns one half of the Telegraph.
3. No sum is definitly appropriated from the
hearing, but the P.M. General is authorized
to take money ad libitum for the object.
4. No sum is fixed for the right to use the
Telegraph.
5. The proprietors may sell to companies & individuals
