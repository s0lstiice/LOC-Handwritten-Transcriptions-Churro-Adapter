If the Telegraph is calculated
to enrich itself upon the perquisites of the
P. O. Department, and accomplish
in the great productive lines of business
about even private expenses
cannot be the damage of the P. O.
system; surely the government
should have the control of it, and
not suffer it to pass into hands that
it control it only for purposes of
speculation, & monopoly the means
of instantaneous intercourse between
the principal points of political &
commercial news.
The proprietors have no other
alternative but to seek their reward
in the enthusiasm of the Government, or in
that of individuals.
If Congress shall elect to make
the necessary appropriation at the
present session, the Government will retain
its control over the Telegraph.
If Congress is either undisposed
to do this, or refuses it to be omitted,
the proprietors will have discharged
their obligations in making the offer
they have, and must be considered as
at liberty to accomplish such their
damages they desire in the enterprise
of private individuals & capitalists.
Washington June 14. 1844.
