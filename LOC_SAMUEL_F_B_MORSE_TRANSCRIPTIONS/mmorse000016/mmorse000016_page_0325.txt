I would say in relation to this difficulty that it
will require some little time to examine into its nature
and determine on whom blame, if on any, should fall
I have had this pipe safely stored free of charge to the
Government and by economical expenditure this winter
hope to apply a remedy which will recover what has been
lost at least in it final result on the great experiment
I must deeply regret any delay, any partial failure, but
delays and partial failures are incident to all new un
der takings, and contractors must bargain with the
knowledge that it is in the nature of the case to
have such disappointments. But I am confident
that no other consequence will follow these delays
than the postponement of the completion of the Telegraph
till next spring. I have every reason to believe
that a great economy will be the result of the present
delay and that the Telegraph will be established at
much less cost than originally supposed to be possible.
With great respect
Yrs Mottain
Sam H B Morse
Superintendent of the War Telegraphs
To Hon John Spencer
Secretary of War
