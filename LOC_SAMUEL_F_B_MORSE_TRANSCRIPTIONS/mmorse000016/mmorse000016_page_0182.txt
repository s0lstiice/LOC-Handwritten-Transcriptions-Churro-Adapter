New York Dec. 9th 1843.
Prof Morse, Superintendent of U.S. Telegraphs
Sir I have the honor to report to you that
Mr. Falkam here made & delivered on board ship (39) thirty
nine casks of Lead pipe with the wires enclosed in them
which after faithfully proving, according to your instructions,
I have accepted & approved, the net weight of which is 57,534 lbs
including the wires. They have also made about 12,600 lbs
including the wires, of which about half has been proved & accepted
but is not yet packed & delivered, but will be in the course of the
day. The proving & accepting, packing & delivery will be completed
in the first part of the week. According to your instructions I will
have the wire not yet enclosed amounting to about 525 lbs, weighed
packed & prepared for shipping to Washington I will deposit it here
subject to your order. As soon as we can complete all that is now
here on hand I weigh the spools & I will transmit to you a schedule
containing on exact account of every reel of wire & pipe with
the weight of each & the times of shipment & also on account of
all that remains on hand. I am very sorry to hear from De Gole that
Schell's pipe is so very worthless but it is no more than I was
afraid would be the case. He is all well, but has left in hourly
expectation of her confinement. I shall hurry the business here
as rapidly as is consistent with perfection so that if my come on
at the earliest possible moment I will write to you some week or two
weeks since but not having heard from you of the receipt of the letter I
suppose it has misrendered, terms of payment would be paid to receive a
