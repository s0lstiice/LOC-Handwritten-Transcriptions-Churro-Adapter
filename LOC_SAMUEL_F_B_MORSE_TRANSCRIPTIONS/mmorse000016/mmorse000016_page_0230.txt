New York Dec 22nd 1843
Prof Samuel F.B. Morse Superintendent E. M. Telegraph
NY
I had the honor to write to you this morning immediately
on the receipt of your letter (of the 2nd of December enclosing drafts
for Messrs Fathmans, of the 1st Inst finding fault with me in rather an impertinent
manner for not answering a letter I had not received, for not writing to you of
the progress of the work, when you very little sending the draft in answer to mine
requesting it to be sent shows that you had received a letter from me on that subject I
when one was then on its way to you, the receipt of which is acknowledged, in another
letter also just received by me (written the next day) enclosing the letter with the
receipts of the Messrs Fathmans the schedule of the five trains of the bills of Lading
for the same, in an envelope to the Hon H.L. Cloworth, Commissioner of Patents, On
returning to my residence I find a letter from you to me written on the 10th inst.
discharging me from my situation as Assistant, Had you taken the precaution to
direct your former letters with so much particularity, it would probably have
saved you from much irritation & me from the necessity of writing to you with so much
plainness as I am now in self defence obliged to do, I am not going to ask you to
reinstate me, I would not remain a day or an hour after the insulting paragraph
in the conclusion of the letter to which I am now replying, my sole object is to
state the truth & if possible by showing you how unjustly you have treated me
prevent you by the remembrance of it from giving way to your constitutional
instability & treating others in the same manner. It will be necessary to go back to the
commencements; after giving my time & labor to your existence when the proprietor
of the Telegraph would not come forward adding my utmost to forward your interests
by procuring an exposition by a committee of the Institute, in various other
ways, I assisted you at the first did everything in my power to produce a favorable
public sentiment towards your splendid invention, from a strong personal regard for you
