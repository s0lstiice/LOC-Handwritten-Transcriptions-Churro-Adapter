Washington Dec. 26th 1843.
Gentlemen,
I have received by Dr. Gale your account of
lead pipe "as per statement handed to Prof. Fisher," all
of which I presume to be correct as to quantity.
But most unexpectedly and to my inexpresstible
disappointment, I find report putting the pipe into use,
that it is wholly unavailling to the purpose of it, in
consequence of the extensive charring to which your
process of manufacturing the pipe has subjected the
wire. In fact, out of a great number of the coils
thus attempted to be used we have not found one that
is free of this very serious injury, and the consequence
is that the whole operation of trenching is necessarily
suspended until the wire shall have been taken out
of the pipe, repaired, and reinserted if practicable
a larger pipe obtained that will admit the inser-
tion of the wires.
Under these circumstances it is proper that
some mutual understanding should be devised in
relation to the loss thus unfortunately occasioned. I
do not feel it to be just that I should be exposed
to bear it alone, and I submit to your consider-
ation what would be proper and equitable in
relation to it.
When my contract was made with you, it will
be recollected that you declined making it unless
the time for its fulfilment could be so extended
as to enable you to make the pipe in your old
and usual process, and it was understood that
ten miles, at all events would be so made, and by
your order that quantity of wire was put on board
a canal boat to be transported to your factory in
Phil.
