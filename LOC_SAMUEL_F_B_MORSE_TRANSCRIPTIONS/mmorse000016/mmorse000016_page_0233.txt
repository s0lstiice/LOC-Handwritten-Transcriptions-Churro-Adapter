many have written under feelings of invitation, which however is not my wish
but to state nothing but the truth & that which may be proved by the testimony
of others. From I have a small bill against the telegraph for some articles
purchased at Philadelphia for proving the fire & wins & I have also a charge for the
portage of 3 letters on business in they means are now being limited & I have only
the $30.50 due me to support my family upon which I can get into some business
to support them, unless you should send on the money for my services & do not wishing to
present claim all that is justly my right I shall therefore enclose with this
letter a despicable bill accepted for the above articles & the portage, & a duplicate
receipt for $52.50 being the bill from Philadelphia $30.50 still due me since the
conclusion of the manufacture of the fire at Philadelphia & the I have been
engaged while writing for letters from you in investigating the cause of the difficulty
& have found out the remedy which is a perfectly simple & easy one. Should
you think this of any service to you I will cheerfully explain the whole
difficulty & put you in possession of the remedy which I think you will
find to be a certain & sure one. I hope & trust that full success will yet
crown your experiments & that you will reap such a reward as will console
you for all the disappointments you have met with thus far. Though I shall
have no further share in it. Please remember me to my Ill-will & family & to
my beloved & faithful, when you are home.
I remain respectfully yours
James Fisher.
from Lieut. Col. Fisher
in his dismission as
Assistant Sec. of War
Philadelphia
Sept. 22, 1863.
