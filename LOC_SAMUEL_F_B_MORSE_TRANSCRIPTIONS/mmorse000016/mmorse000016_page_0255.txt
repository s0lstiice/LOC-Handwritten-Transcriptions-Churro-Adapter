Under these circumstances, it was quite evident, that if I left, this
establishment, would go by the board, if I it it should leave, since Mr
B. knows comparatively nothing about the Manufacture any farther
than to buy, sell the product.
I next took legal advice fearing, that should we fail I should
be also liable and heard that, for any, lack to pay the
debts my furniture and all I had must go to satisfy claims
against the concern.
Now, my Dear Sir, I have told you in the grasp the state
of my affairs in New York; and the question is asked, what
is my duty? What ought I to do in this critical juncture?
Aught I to return to Washington or should I remain here and
prevent the wreck of what little I have left?
I have carefully sought wisdom from the only source whence
it is to be obtained, and I look upon this as such of God
for some good purpose, but what that purpose is, is audibly
concealed from my view. It seems to be my duty from
the light I have to remain and take care of my lead works
in New York and to resign my place in the Telegraph, and have
made up my mind to do, but I will not do it until I hear
from you. The whole of this state of affairs has fallen upon me
like the thunderbolt for shock of an earthquake.
