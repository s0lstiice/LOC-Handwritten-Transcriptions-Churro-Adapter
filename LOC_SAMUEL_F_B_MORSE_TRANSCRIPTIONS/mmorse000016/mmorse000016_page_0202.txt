Washington D.C. Dec. 16th 1843.
Dear Sidney,
I write a hearty line just to say that
after the payment of $5.00 each to the wives of Batting
and Taylor, up to this date, you need pay no more
as I have discharged both from pay & service after date,
in consequence of my preparing to go into winter quarters.
The season is against all my operations and I expect to
resume in the spring.
I have difficulties and trouble in my work
but none of a nature as yet to discourage, they arise
from neglect and unfaithfulness, (alter has) on the part
of Fisher, whom I shall probably dismiss, although on
many accounts I shall do it reluctantly. — I shall give
him an opportunity to excuse himself, if he ever gets here.
I have been expecting both him and Gale for 3 weeks, and neither
but without bringing either of them. They may have a good excuse
we shall see.
I am uneasy respecting 2 drafts sent to Fisher
to pay Jathams on their order, amounting $273.20. One
drawn by Bank of Wash. on National Bank New York
for $273.20 and one by myself on Bk of America for
$400.00. The package was directed to Fisher, and painted
by Mr Ellsworth at Patent Office on 2 Dec. 1843.—

respecting them, although I may find letters before this
reach you which may relieve my mind. I have received
