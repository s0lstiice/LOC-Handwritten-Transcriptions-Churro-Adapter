New York Oct 7th 1848
Prof Morse,
Chief Superintendent of Telegraphs
Sir,
Yours of the 27th ult also yours of the 5th inst
were duly received. The first gave me great joy that
I had thus far met your approbation. Though in an
exceedingly debilitated state I was so much elated that it
seemed I was able to start off on the next passage of
The boat of Cox but my told me it was a piece of pre-
sumption that would very likely lay me in my grave
I was forced, though much against my will, to yield
I am now rapidly improving and hope to be able to leave
here on Monday 11th for the line of work -- God has been
exceedingly merciful in raising me from the gates of the grave
My bones pricked through my shirt in almost every direction
But it is most true to close the mail and I am
nearly to quit from the office --
Cock F. I think will close up his work tomorrow
and then of course will leave immediately for B. order
