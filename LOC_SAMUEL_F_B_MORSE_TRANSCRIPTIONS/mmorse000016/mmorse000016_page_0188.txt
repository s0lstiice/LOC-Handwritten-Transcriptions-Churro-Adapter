Rev. Eliz. Harriet Smith
My Dear Sir,
From the excellent family of our dear friends here, I learn with
great regret the deep severe and varied trials to which you and your beloved ones
have lately been subjected. Dark though they are in themselves, yet the light of
respective circumstances gives them, God's continuance in their afflictions of the righteous but
He and delivereth him out of them all. Mysterious though are these evidences
of legitimacy, yet without abasing myself, I have the evidence of being brother to
you here, as I learn you will be in a few days.
My dear friend, I have imagined from your distress and your
cures and anxieties that you may possibly be relieved from some of the letter by the
triple enclosed. If any feeling of deliverance on your part should oppose itself to your acceptance
of it, remember that I but remain in obligation. You may have forgotten the kind tender of your
aid at a time when I was tried, but I never shall. Make my kind respects to Mrs Smith
and hoping soon to see you both, with little Willis restored to health. I am with
Sincere and affectionate regard
Your friend and
E.H.B.M.
