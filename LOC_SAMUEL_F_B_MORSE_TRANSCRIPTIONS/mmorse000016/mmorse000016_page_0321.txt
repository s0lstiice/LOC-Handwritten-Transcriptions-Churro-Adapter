Brannock Mill, Jan 7th 1840
Dear Sir,
Although personally unknown to you, I shall venture to introduce myself on the strength of my acquaintance for several years with your much respected Parent, while living in Charleston.
I have been much interested in your telegraphic discoveries and experiments, although I have seen only one brief newspaper notice. – I have seen nothing published by yourself; nor do I know how for you have given any public notice of your experiments, or how for it may be consistent for you to give such notice.
However, on knowing my object in addressing you at this time, you will instantly be able to determine what sort of reply it may be proper for me to give.
I shall shortly commence my annual course of chemical lectures, during which I devote a considerable time to Electro-magnetism. It has occurred to me, that a brief notice of your Telegraph, such as would be suitable in a lecture, would at this present time, be peculiarly interesting to my class.
You are, I believe, acquainted with our President Woods, who is now in New York
With much esteem
Your friend
Barry Clavelous
