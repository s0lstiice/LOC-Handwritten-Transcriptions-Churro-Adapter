When neither Mr Bailnor nor Mr Smith could I would go on with you to
Washington I left my family & went on with you to render you all the assistance in my power
to procure an appropriation for the full trial of the invention, I made no stipulation for any
remuneration other than that any expenses should be paid & that if the appropriation was
obtained I should be appointed your first assistant in order to save you from any expense
on my account, which I knew you could afford to pay with the money received from Mr Pick
while in Washington. I used every endeavour to interest the members of Congress in favor
of you & the Telegraph, & had it not been for the interest which I succeeded in inspiring
in my personal friends in the matter I am sure with perfect certainty that the appropriation
would not have been obtained, you repeatedly promised me then that you
would repay me the money saved from my board etc. & on the faith of those promises
I honoured money to send to my family. The appropriation was obtained & on your
return from Washington you fulfilled part of your promises by appointing me one
of your assistants, I have held the situation since that time to the present I have
endeavoured faithfully to fulfill its duties I had some cause of complaint for the
manoeuvres in which you treated me in the summer. De Gale could neglect the duties
of his situation & attend to his own private business as much as he pleased for the
whole or greater part of the time & no fault found but I to whom you owed so
full as much if not more your success, could not repay a short visit to a brother
where I had not seen for a long time without being threatened with being discharged
I overlooked it then attributing it to your constitutional insubility, when only
things went cross, which however a Christian should strive to refuse & not permit
to grow upon him, I have endeavored from that time to this to mend even the very
inconvenience of neglect of business going on for as not to attend the funeral of a beloved
friend residing near the city without first asking your permission, I have endeavored
as faithfully as I knew how to fulfill all your instructions & to promote as much as was in
my power your interest, not being one of the proprietors of the Telegraph I could only
