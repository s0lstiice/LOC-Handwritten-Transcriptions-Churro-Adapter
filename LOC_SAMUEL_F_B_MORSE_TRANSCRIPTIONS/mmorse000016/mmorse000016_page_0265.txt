Mr. Levi Woodbury
My Dear Sir, Wash. 1843
You accept my sincere
thanks for the copy of your
learned and valuable Lecture
on the Uncertainties of History,
which you have had the kindness
to send me. I have read it with
great interest and pleasure, and
have expressed to all who
have read it my acknowledgements
for the flattering manner in which
you have treated
me in your letter. I am
affiliated
to
the
United States
Government
and should
have
the bill in relation to it
which is now before the Senate of
which I am a member. It is a measure
which will be opposed by
the abolitionists of the
North. It is gratifying to me to find that the greatest
intellects, the highest scientific
minds of the world, those who
can prophesy most largely the
future, are precisely those who
have manifested the most enthusiasm in
the Electo Magnetic Telegraph.
