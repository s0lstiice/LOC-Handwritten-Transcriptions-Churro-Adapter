New York Dec 29 1848
To S.S. Morell Esq Superintendent Elec. Mag. Telegraphs
My Dear Sir
After taking leave of you on Sunday Eve
I returned to my lodgings and at 6 the following morning left
Washington for New York, where I arrived on Tuesday morning,
at 9 O'clock day after Christmas. Found Mrs G. confined to her bed from
acute inflammation of the liver, ascertained that Mr Smith my father-in
law, who was my foreman in the Lead Factory had left the works
in consequence of repeated attacks of hemorrhage of the lungs, and
from the advice of his physician concludes not to return to the
business.
On turning these facts I immediately went to see Dr Fisher to engage
him as I mentioned to you to take charge of my business at least until
a foreman could be instructed but to my miserable surprise
and disappointment I found he was already engaged in other business
and thus my hopes and expectations from that quarter were entirely
cut off. I went home and spent a considerable portion of the day
and night in prayer meditation and fasting, that God would
give me wisdom to know my duty in this trying situation of my affairs.
After this I went and had a private interview with Mr Brown my
partner and learned from him the financial condition of the
Establishment, that we are in debt about $10,000. and that
we have on hand about 10 tons of lead in the unfinished
