In the diagram the shortest distance between the two poles
of the battery P. N. is through from X through F to G and to
N. and though the water being one half to
circuit, but the water is many thousand times inferior to
wire in conductibility, so that if I can offer a shorter con
ductor within a shorter distance of water and this
electricity will take the wire and wire in preference. This
arrangement I make, by causing F through F to i on
the opposite bank of the river, more than double the
distance from G to F. The electric fluid in passing
from G to F and from i to h, travels a shorter distance
through a bad conductor, than in passing from G
to h, and will therefore cross the river.
If this solution is correct, however, plates G
to h may be made so large, that the column of water
between them shall be of equal conducting power
with the wire, and then the electricity should
not cross the river, but should pass directly from G to N.
have not yet tested this by experiment.
Paris Nov. 10th 1845.
