Exeter, Monday May 6th 1845
Dear Sir,
Mr S. W. Chick, a manufacturer of Philosophical
instruments in this village, has had his attention turned
particularly to the phenomena of Electric Magnetism and
among other curiosities of the science, has constructed the
Telegraph. He is in the habit occasionally of giving lectures
and experiments in a humble way in the same village
around me, when sometimes the exhibition of the Telegraph
is called for. He is unwilling to do anything destructive
or the proprietor of that ingenious instrument should think
a truce upon ground rights, and no desire to put it to
any other use than as agent in duty. As such it would
in this neighborhood increase the popularity of his
institutions. I have examined the paper on the subject and
in your answer while you expressed an individual
opinion, he received no official assurance that others
would be satisfied. It would do him a favor could you
afford to drop from a line telling him whether he may
or may not exercise his discretion with an assurance
on his part, that he will never intentionally invade
these rights to which he justly belongs to the inventor of
that wonderful machine. As a man and a minister
