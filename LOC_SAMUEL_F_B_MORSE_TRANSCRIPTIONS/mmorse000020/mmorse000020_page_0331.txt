Mr. Lind has sailed for Porto Rico; he learned just
after we arrived that his Manager on the estate was dead, and
he felt very desirous of reaching home as soon as possible. Susan
is in New York for the winter & little Charley, she is in better
health. Charley is well and I am glad to think doing well.
Our political horizon looks somewhat dark, but I still
hope that war may be avoided, I do not think it depends
on us. If war should take place, England will find it a
very difficult affair from the Chinese war, and notwithstanding
all the lamentations that "we are not prepared," we are better pre-
pared than they have any idea of.
I have so many interruptions, and so many letters to
write that I am compelled to cut short my letter or run
the risk of missing the Steam. Therefore, I must say
good bye after saying give my love to Katherine & Eliza-
beth and kiss the 'babies' for me. Write me how you
are doing. I will write you longer & better soon.
Affectionately as ever
Your brother
Finley
Sidney E. Morse Esq
