enclose to you a table of Telegraphic signals which the telegraphic signals now used on the lines of France may be readily translated into the characters of my alphabet and will advantages, which allow me to enumerate.
The exceedingly ingenious instrument which I examined at the Telegraph terminus of the Paris & Rouen rail road a few days since, you observed could send about 12 signals in a minute, Each telegraph has 8 positions, and in moving to each position must hesitate a moment at each intermediate one before it stops at the one desired, so that the distinct steps in arrangement of the two arms amount to 16; but as in each change of position 16 steps are not always required, the average number, you supposed might be 8 for each signal. The number of signals capable of being made by the combined movement of the two arms, 2 circuits are required. In the table which I submit, I have taken all the letters in my alphabet which are comprised within 4 points, amounting in number to 11; the combinations of these letters in pairs amount to 132, and the single letters 11. There are therefore 100280 different combinations of two letters, which sufficient
to represent any message of 100 words, and which will enable me to send to you a message of 100 words in 1000 words of telegraphic signals.
