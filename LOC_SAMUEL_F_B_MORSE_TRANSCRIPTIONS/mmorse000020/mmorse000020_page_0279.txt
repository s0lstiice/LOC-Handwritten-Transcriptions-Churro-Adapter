He shows on a dial plate by arms like the common
Telegraph the same signals as are now in use. He
requires 2 wires, and can give but 12 signs in a
minute!! At once divine one. He uses the magnet
(my basis) as Wheatstone uses it to move a watchwheel.
I have had a long interview with M. Arago, he is the
same affable and polite man as in 1839, he is a
warm friend of mine, and contends for priority in my
favor, and is also partial to my Telegraphic system as the
best. He is President of the Commission, and is going to
writ e e the History of Electric Telegraphy, I shall give
him the facts concerning mine. The day after tomorrow
I exhibit my Telegraphic system again to the Academy of
Sciences, and am in the midst of preparations for
a day important to me. I have strong hopes that
mine will be the system adopted, but there may
be obstacles, I do not see. Wheatstone at any
rate is not in favor here. He may crow on his
own dunghill, but the French cock crows here.
