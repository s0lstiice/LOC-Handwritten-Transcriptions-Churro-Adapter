20. J. Smith Esq
Portland Me.
On my return on Saturday from Washington I found
your note containing proposals for purchasing my portion
of the Patent right of the Telegraph. I have taken them into
consideration, and would say in reply that the only terms
at the present time that would tempt me to part with my
right is a realization in cash or notes at 3 & 6 months
for $100,000, for 8/16th of the patent right. Should such an
offer be made within, 10 days from date I think I would part
with this much, the Telegraph stock as I have stated
as I find myself practically doubling that amount as my share, and the only
inducement, therefore, that I could have, is the sure realization
of a certain sum, free from all contingencies.
If you cannot renew the offer of your 7/16th of the Patent
right in all countries out of the U. States, for the sum you men-
tioned which was the sum you had extended in writing Europe amounting to about $6,000, and give me the refusal of it till 1 Dec.
I should like it otherwise next, I do not dare to have it. Such an offer on your part would
induce me to make exertions to procure you that sum, in the
hope that I might make something myself, but as it is more
than doubtful whether I shall make anything, I have no particu-
lar inducement to enter into the enterprise. What I do
in Europe at the present time is to effect favorably our enterprise at home
for the purpose of preventing the Printing Office into words
you may have no fear of it. It is mathematically demonstrable that it is
of no consequence with mine.
Respectfully.
J.B. Morse
