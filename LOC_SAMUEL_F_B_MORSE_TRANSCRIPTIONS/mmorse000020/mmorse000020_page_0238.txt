Thursday morning. Oct. 2d, 1845
Hamburg.
Called by appointment on Mr Arneumann
at the R. R. Depot, in Altona; found
that the plan of Wheatstone was not much
in favor although the King of Denmark saw
it in operation and was pleased, as one naturally
would be even at the most indifferent kind of Telegraph on the Electric principle.
They consider it too expensive & not efficient. Four wires run from the Depot down to the river, by means of which information was given from river to Depot a distance of less than
a fourth of a mile. I went into the room where the instrument had been, (it was now removed and is not used,) the woman who occupied the room on being asked where it was said "God be thanked it was taken away," What the precise cause of the good woman's joy was, I could not say.
I was recommended to ascertain from Copenhagen, from the Director Bluhme; & Herr Conferenz Rath Garlieb
and Francke at Copenhagen, General Zoll Kammer.
If anything is done in the way of Telegraphs, I think them in little doubt mine will be adopted, for I learned for the first time that Wheatstone's cannot be multiplied on a route, while mine may be multiplied every foot of the way. — I mean to examine this critically. I have to Mr. J. Brun,
Kammerjunker 42 Nørgade Copenhagen,
