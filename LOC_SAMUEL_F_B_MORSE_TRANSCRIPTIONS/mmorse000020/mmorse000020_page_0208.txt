New York City March 1845
Professor Samuel F. B. Morse
Dear Sir, It may be satisfactory to you prior
to your departure for Europe to learn what progress I have made as your agent in arrangements
to bring Morse electric Magnetic Telegraph into
general use.
My agreement with you was closed on the 1st of
March last. Within a few days thereafter a
contract was formed for building a line from
New Orleans to Mobile; but I never had much
confidence in its performance and am not likely
to be agreeably disappointed.
Having failed in an attempt at New York to
run means to commissary operations on a large
scale, your co-proprietor Mr Smith and myself,
after overcoming many difficulties succeeded in
organizing a joint stock company at Washington
to build a line from that place to New York.
This began with a subscription of $15,000 required
to be sufficient to build a line of two
rivers from New York to Philadelphia. In consequence
of the exertant demands of the New
York and Camden & Atlantic Railroad companies
for the right of way and their control over
the Susquehaha and other improvements on the
direct line, much time has been occupied
in discovering some other practicable route.
These difficulties, I trust, are on the point
of being overcome, and it is expected that the
two great cities will be connected by Telegraph
in October next.
