Washington April 19, 1845.
To Hon. Cave Johnson
P. M. G.
I have the honor to state that in
of the Department, respecting
the telegraphic regulations, of the Telegraph established between Washington & Baltimore, the provision is made for free communications, except in the case of
the Railroad Company. It would seem to be necessary that there should be done such provision. There is often intelligence of a public character such for example as news by the European Packet Ships, which it may be very
important should be communicated at once to the various Departments, and of such arrivals at Baltimore, and is forwarded to the Department, the charge of postage should be free. Orders transmitted from either
Washington or Baltimore to the various Agents of the Government who have the franking privilege, should also be free.
The correspondence between the officers of the Telegraph relating to the operation of the Telegraph instruments and other should obviously be free.
Very Respectfully
Yours,
Observe
S.G.B.Morse.
Superin't. Elec. Mag.
Telegraphs of U. States.
