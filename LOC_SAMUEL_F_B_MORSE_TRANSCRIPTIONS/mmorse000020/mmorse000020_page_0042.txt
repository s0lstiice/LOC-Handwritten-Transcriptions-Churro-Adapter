Samuel P. B. Morse Esq:
Dear Sir,
I do not think I could purchase a final
purchase for your interest in the Telegraph in the terms men-
tioned in your letter of the 17th inst. Men may be found who will
risk $30,000 as the matter stands before the public; but if made
aware that Professor Morse himself declined taking a lien on
the whole interest sold as security for the balance of the purchase
money, they would be likely to lose all confidence in the invention.
The first of June next is too soon for the advance payment;
it allows too little time for making arrangements a finding a
purchase. It should not be sooner than the first of July.
It should interest commence until at least a reason-
able time has been given to make the enterprise productive, say the
first of January next.
It is understood of course, that all the risks of the
enterprise are to fall on the purchaser while the patent is
to be guaranteed by you, and the purchaser is to have
the benefit of all discoveries and improvements hereafter
made by you pertaining to the subject matter.

to be and the heavy advance required, diminishes both my ability
and inclination to become a purchaser; but I would like to
