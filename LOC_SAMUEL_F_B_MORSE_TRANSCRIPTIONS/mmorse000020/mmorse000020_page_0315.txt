My Dear Sir,
From of the 12th inst was duly
received and content noted.
The first addition which you propose to
our agreement I willingly accede to, and heartily
wish you may be so fortunate as to sell the exclusive
right of your valuable Invention to Government.
Should you succeed, I trust you will use your
influence in recommending me as worthy of its
full patronage. I assure you, I shall use my
best exertions to merit such a recommendation.
I make no objection to the second addition,
but it is quite evident that I shall never be able
to sell to those companies already established,
for if they can get their instruments made
wherever they like, they can be supplied cheaper
than I can do it, for the manufacturer does
not pay you a portion of the profits. And
if new companies find that the old ones
can buy cheaper than they can from me, they
also will buy of other makers. A mechanic
who makes an instrument only as he receives an
order for it, has the rent only of a workshop
to pay, and no division of Profits, has an advantage
over one who keeps the instrument constantly on
hand, has high rents, clerks, loss of Interest
