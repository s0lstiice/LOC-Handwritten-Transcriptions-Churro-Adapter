Articles of agreement made and concluded this ninth day of
April A.D. 1845, by and between David Burkank of the City of
Baltimore, in the State of Maryland, Merchant of the one
part; And Samuel F. B. Morse, now resident at Washington D.C.,
for, and in behalf of the United States of America, Equire, of the
other part, Witnessed:
That the said Burkank for himself, his heirs, Executors
and Administration, does him by contract agree with the said
Samuel F. B. Morse Equire of or resident, to furnish and deliver to the
party of the second part of said a sufficient number of Chimney
posts, such as shall be straight, sound, thirty five feet in length, eight
inches at the butt, and up than five inches at the top, the back to be putted
off, the posts to be painted twice; and the same to be erected, to be
placed in the side walk in range with the places of usual lamp
posts in the same – and to be placed at a distance of not less than
one hundred and fifty feet, nor exceeding two hundred and fifty feet apart.

Viz: To commence at the Southern Westerly corner of Chandler
Street, to extend to Fayette Street, and from thence to the Post Office
building – Said Burkank is to be furnished by said Morse
with enough of the Telegraph Wire now belonging to the United States,
and to extend & continue the line of "Morse Telegraph" from the corner
of Chandler Street of or said by the route of Fayette to the Post Office of or said,
on the line of said posts, insulating the wire in the manner prescribed
by said Morse according to the model furnished – stipulating
that when said in Crossing Baltimore, Calvert & Martin Streets,
the wires are to be placed on the tops of buildings instead of the posts,
provided said Burkank can obtain permission for that
