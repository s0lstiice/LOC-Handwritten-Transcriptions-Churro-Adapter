Prof. J. P. B. Morse.
Washington.
Dear Sir.
Prof. Baker of this city wishes to procure a Correspondent
for the benefit of his Chemistry class, as also does a gentleman from Boston, connected
as I was given to understand, with one of the Universities there. I agreed with them
to write you to secure your consent to their correspondence either here, or wherever you may direct.
While writing to you, I cannot help supposing as much that you
would give the making of your instruments to Mr Clark, who has done himself much
Credit by the correct workmanship on the one he made for me.
When I returned from the line, a week after putting one of your
new instruments up, I found the standard covered with brass jingles, and the wheels
making a noise like a circular saw. Whether it was from trial treatment or original
defect I am not positive, but I feel certain that Mr Clark would execute your orders to
much better purpose, and with satisfaction to all interested. The line home to Mr. Clark is stopped.
Where we shall resume I know not.
Very respectfully yours
James H. Dick.
