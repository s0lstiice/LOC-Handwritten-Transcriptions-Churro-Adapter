Philadelphia May 19th 1845.
Ref Samuel F. B. Morse
Sir
At sufficient time has now elapsed,
for any feelings of invitation arising from the conduct of either of us,
to have subsided, I have taken the liberty of calling your attention to a
subject in which I am somewhat interested, I have written to you before
on the subject but no answer has been received, I suppose that my
letters have either not been received, or you have from some cause or other
been prevented from replying, when I went on to Washington with
you in December 1842. there was an understanding between us that
you were to bear all my expenses, in going, staying, returning therefrom.
The second week after our arrival at Washington, as you will short of funds
it was uncertain how long you might have to remain in order to save you
from paying out money for me I accepted the invitation of a relative I resided
with him during my stay in Washington, you told me then, respecting the
assurance to me afterwards, that if the bill passed for the trial of the Telegraph
you would pay to me the money which you would otherwise have had to
pay for my board, Confiding in this assurance, & feeling sure of the passage
of the bill, I borrowed money of a friend which has since been repaid to him,
I am now, very much in want of money for the support of my family &
would feel much obliged to you, if you would remit to me in accordance
with your promise, the amount ($51.60 two years interest being added).
which you were thus relieved from paying at that time. I am glad for your
