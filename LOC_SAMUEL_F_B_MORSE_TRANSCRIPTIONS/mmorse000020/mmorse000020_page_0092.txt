Mr Rogue
Dear Sir.
I have not written to
you before this, but have been so busy that I had
no time to gather up anything with writing.
Hearing some news yesterday, I visited the
Telegraphic office, where I remained in conversation
with the director during the afternoon. The Telegraph
(Wheatstone's) is really as pretty a failure as ever in
law. Potentially it requires an hour for them to transmit a sentence which you could do with speed in
five minutes. The Telegraph works by the deflection of two needles, and they deflect so slowly, that they
can scarcely read the letters at all. They use five wires, each wire being composed of three smaller wires twisted together. Has I cannot see what is gained by this? In my opinion there is a lot. I gave
the man that fiddling you gave me -- also a bill of
your alphabet, and honest report; but I gave him no information in respect to the manner in which yours was made. You manifest a
great wish that your Telegraph would transmit as rapidly as we could talk -- which the poem I gave
you proved -- and still more wish that it would do it, for their does not exist. In a word, I consider Wheatstone's Telegraph without any comparison with Morse's; and you may congratulate
yourself upon having got far the best Telegraph in the world. The English are a bloating
mind, people, puffed up with an idea of their own importance, and depreciating the value of all other
people -- particularly the Americans, of whom they are jealous enough. The agent of the Telegraph
