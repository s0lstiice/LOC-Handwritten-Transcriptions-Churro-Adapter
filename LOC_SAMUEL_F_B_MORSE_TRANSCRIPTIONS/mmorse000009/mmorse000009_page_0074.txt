certainity which hangs over my future movements, my greatest
and I may almost say my only support & consolation is the
casting of all my care upon him who cares for us, our
family motto "in de fide", is continually present to my
mind, were it not for this confidence in God, & sure
persuasion that he has directed all my movements
thus far, and will direct them; I should have long since
have given up my plans, and sought out other ways of suppor-
ting my family. I feel that I am running many risques
both of health & property, but I know who can preserve
both, and it alleviates every painful feeling the moment
I can commit myself and all that is dear to me on earth
into his hands unreservedly.
5 o'clock. P.M. I have just rec'd father's letter
under cover to Gen. Van Rensselaer; I have not yet restrained
how much I shall probably want, nor do I know definitely
when we shall sail, I have been waiting all day to see the
Sec'y of Legation but have not yet seen him, he lives 6 miles
from me, and I rode out to see him this morning. Did not find
it at home, left my card and a request that he would call and see
me today but I have waited in vain, I hope to see him soon.
Mr. Dix called in today but had not seen Mr. Mason. he refuses
to be remembered to you, he was admitted to the bar yesterday.
97776 I forgot to state another singular circumstance to
me by Mr. Poinsett, which is, that the Mexicans tolerate all colors
but black, they call a man in black, a priest or a freemason
he advised me not to take a suit of black; I have thought if you
could get Mr. Tournend to make me a blue coat instead of the
one I have I will send the black one home, and you send me the
blue one to N. Orleans, let me know in your letter whether this can
be done.
