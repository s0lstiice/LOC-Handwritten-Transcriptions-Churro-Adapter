Dear Sir
I called last evening at your house to
consult you on the expediency of addressing a line to Mr Heston
for the purpose of fixing him if possible, in our part of the
town, if he should remove here. The house which you
formerly occupied, together with the painting room, would
form, I should suppose, a very complete establishment for
him. The house, as you know, is as large and genteel
as the custom of the place requires, and the situation pleasant;
far preferable for a man of his taste and habits
in one nearer the scene of business. The painting room, which
I suppose you would be willing to rent or sell, would form
an important part of the establishment. If he with a
large garden than has hitherto belonged to the place,
I would willingly add to it the lot in front of Mr
Silliman thus extending the garden through to the Avenue.
