Washington April 19th 1824
Monday.
My Dear Lucretia
I am at this moment put into a very
embarrassing state of suspense, by a political occa-
sion which has caused a great excitement here, and
will excite considerable interest no doubt throughout
the country; this morning a remonstrance was read
in the House of R. from Hon. Ninian Edwards against
Mr. Crawford which contains such charges, of so
serious a nature, as has led to the appointment of
a select committee with powers to send for Person
and papers in order to a full investigation, and I am
told by many members of Congress that Mr. Ed-
wards will undoubtedly be sent for and occasion
of course a great delay in his journey to Mexico,
if not cause a suspension of his going until the next
season; you can easily conceive in what a state of
anxiety I am placed, by this occurrence; I shall know
in the course of a day or two what will be the issue of
this, and will then write you a fuller account of myself. I am
obliged to close in consequence of the closing of the Mail
