Portland (Me.) Aug. 10th 1824.
Dear Parents,
I arrived here on Saturday from Portsmouth bringing letters from gentlemen in Portsmouth to Gen. Wingate, Judge Mellen and Mr. Asa Clapp, the first and last I have delivered, and find that Gen. W.
and his lady leave town to-morrow for Boston, Judge Mellen and family are absent on a tour, and I find only Mr. Clapp, who has no more taste than a cow, and who thinks I shall do nothing in Portland, my reception by him I must leave relating till I see you.
Portland is very sickly, there were three funerals on Saturday; on Sunday there were 12 notes read from Mr. Payton's pulpit, 10 of which were on account of deaths: four funerals this afternoon, and in one quarter of the town it is very dangerous to pass; the disease is malignant bilious fever, which some say was introduced here by a vessel from the W. Indies.— I am in great doubt what to do, but at present think it will be best to return to Portsmouth and paint the Portrait of God.
