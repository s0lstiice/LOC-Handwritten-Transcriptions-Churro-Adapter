Washington April 22d 1824.
Thursday
My Dearest Wife,
I hardly know what to say, or think, or do;
I went to the House this morning to hear the Committee's Report.
They made a few statements to the house, in which it was
served that it was necessary to a full investigation to have
Mr. Edwards present, (Mr. Randolph informed the house that
a warrant was already issued to detain him, and that a messenger
was on the way to serve it; thus am I placed in a
most embarrassing state, one that no human foresight could
predict or provide against; I have no misgivings, however, I
know in whom I have believed, and although my way at
present seems completely hedged up, yet I know assuredly
that all will eventually in good, I have been told thus far
by an Almighty and wise and merciful God, and I will not,
cannot doubt but all things are ordered right; I, not only, do
not feel responding, but I can truly say I rejoice in this
trial, the way will be made plain, I will keep you informed
of every movement. Some say I shall be detained more than
a month and advise me to go home to wait, others advise me
to give it up altogether till next fall, and others to go on
without the legation among the latter is Mr. Poinsett; I
