Mr. Poinsett,
Dear Sir, I had several
conversations with you, when I had the pleasure
of seeing you in Albany; I have not aban-
doned the idea of going there, if the way
should be plain. I see by the Pres. Mts.
that it is in contemplation to send a Minis-
ter thither soon; Might I presume to ask
your friendly aid in procuring me some
situation in the Legation? I do not seek a
high one, I think I should prefer one
in the gift of the Minister, rather than
of the Pres. of U.S. Should you not
be appointed yourself to that situation,
which I could wish might be the case, yet
your intimate acquaintance with that
country will doubtless make you intimate intimate
with the minister who might be ap-
pointed, and a friendly word in my behalf
would confer a lasting obligation upon
me. It is a melancholy time for artists
in our country, All are poor & discour-
aged, some are leaving the profession, others
