My dear Son,
I write with difficulty, being almost
lost amidst the ruin which surrounds me – within one
short month I had been called upon to follow three of
our relatives to the silent grave & had scarcely received
from the shock when the appalling news of Lucius's
lamented death plunged me into the deepest sorrow.
Since that melancholy event I have scarcely known what
to do. My mind is constantly agitated with most melancholy
and pained emotions – Soon after you left us
I set about arranging my affairs here with intention
to visit you next spring & to remain a considerable
time; But the afflicting dispensation of Divine Providence
has dissipated all my plans & reduced me to the
lowest state of despondency – Our Loss is great & griefs
to be borne. She was a pleasant & a favorite child
with whom I entertained the fond expectation of spending
the residue of days, But the Almighty in his
Providence has seen fit to take her from us – and
we are left desolate to mourn her loss. We must bow
with submission to his holy will with a firm reliance on
his mercy & goodness. I feel for you, I feel for the dear
little children & I feel for myself – You are all near & dear
to me & whatever may happen in the course of Providence you
will almost find me an infidel in spirit
Chas Walker
Mr. Sam. J. B. Walker
City of Louisville
