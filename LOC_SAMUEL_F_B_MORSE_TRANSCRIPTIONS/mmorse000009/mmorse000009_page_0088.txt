go there in her, and leave the location to follow & proceed by
myself to Mexico; this latter, however, will require consideration.
I shall take the best advice, and act according to the best of my
judgment; tell Mother she must not suffer any uneasy feelings
on this subject or on my account; leave it all to be directed by
him who has ordered all things well thus far, and will continue so
to do; although I see no way as yet that is not beset with difficulties
although to go back seems ruin, and forward hazard, yet I am certain
that I shall be led by an unweaving guide into the right way in his
own proper time, to him I leave it; to him let us all leave it.
Dec? your affectionate letter of the 16.th it gave me mingled
emotions of pleasure & pain, I cannot tell you all I feel in seeing
the handwriting of her I so dearly love, in reading her expression
of tender regard, and at the same time thinking how long a separation
we may be led to endure; it is a subject on which I dare not trust myself to think too minutely; I can only leave
you in the hands of our heavenly Father and look forward with
hope to the time of our meeting again when it shall please him
to grant it.
With my dear children for me, many, many times, I give
you love to Father, Mother, brothers & all neighbors & friends and
the family. I have rec'd fathers letters of 9.th 16.th & 18.th and
mothers for which I thank them, I will answer them more
fully in a short time the moment I can know what my future
course will be. As even dearest wife & Aff. husband
Finley.
