My Dear Senator
I received your long kind and affectionate letter on yesterday, and I would have answered before now but my nerves were on full
loom, loss of sleep, that I was unable to do. I do return him many thanks for it, and for his kind offer of doing
any thing that lies in his power for himself and my father's children. In time it is as he has said, that
he has lost the best of husbands and they the best of fathers. How mysterious are the ways of Providence
Had Uncle a father should be taken from children just entering into life one so well qualified to understand them in things both spiritual and
temporal. I feel he lost a great deal more with regard to my children than myself do. He is all human probability I shall soon follow him.
Great indeed must have been the shock to you and my dear Lucietta after leaving him to well on Saturday. The Monday
before you left as he was bent for to go to James Island to Mr Wilson after ten o'clock at night
and bid to go part of the way by water and to ride three miles by land. It is generally supposed that in riding through the dew he took
the country fever. The Monday night you left us upon his coming to bed I observed that he had the fever but did nothing as
he did not complain, until the next morning when I told him I had observed that he had the fever, he got up and went down stairs, but
