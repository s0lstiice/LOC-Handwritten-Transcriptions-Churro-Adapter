Georgetown Jan 6th 1819
Sir,
My Son Thomas will have the honor to present you this letter
& I will be very much obliged to you to finish the face of his small
Portrait as soon as you can conveniently, but with regard to the
other parts of it I would prefer you taking a long time. – Let his
hair be beautifully disposed of & give with perfect fidelity a fair
expression to his animated eye – Having said so much to you about
his portrait I will close this part of my letter by again praying
you to let the rays of your genius converge on this little picture
& to charge a proportionate price I offer for it my eternal
gratitude. – My Son William will also
attend to your commands – I beg the favor of you to take
a small portrait of the same size as these two last orders of
my Sons, of my friend Mr Benj: T. Dunkin, if you will I make
known my request to him he will sit for it immediately; I am too
lazy to write to him but he will not refuse me any thing –
Paint it if you please with hands – I have a sister Van
extremely desirous that you should take a full size Portrait
of her with hands – She resides in this place & has at length
yielded to my solicitations for it – She will go to Charleston for
the sole purpose if you will consent to take it, but cannot
remain but a few days – Could you not take a sitting
morning & afternoon & finish the face in a short time, the
rest you might finish at your leisure – I am Sir,
with great consideration
Your humble serv
John C. Alston
