My dear Son,
Your Mother & I are pretty well, & inten-
table spirits— Your brothers are well Rich. is going to St. George's
on horse back, to return on Monday & conclude
his engagement there. Lexington has had something of a fever
—& has been rather bad. I am very well, having sweet baby & promises
to make an admirable wife for Miss P— & I am sure I am
consenting to her proposal. Give our love to Mr & Mrs P.
Elizabeths will be here (D.V.) week after next, probably with
your Mother— As to the matter of proposing a referendum
to members of A.B.C. (Mr. Boffeyson, a fellow in the proposal
has been rejected & things are to remain as provided for
in our contract — The Parity owe me more than
$1300— besides interest. Other matters angle $300 more perhaps.
have called on them to pay before I go my promise is that
they will do. I don't know they are in trouble out
the business. I am managing all my affairs for
removal next summer.
I send 2 vol. of Tracts to Mr. S. Dwight— I
have no time to look over my map of Kentucky find
reports of what societies your trip to Lexington
the Recorder of Transcripts furnish all that is requested.
I wish the Vol. returned Mr. Edwards may examine
them with a view to select any while will date reprint.
We have just heard that Mr. Huntington
died at Galion this morning! What does Calhoun
intend in regard to the orthodox church in this region?
To give them up a prey to the adversaries
of the truth or to appear in his glory & power to
rescue them from the brink of ruin & desolation or
what their now signs? They, with other troubles, brings a great
weight upon my spirit. I shall seek, I hope find relief in prayer.
97251 All you in love to you— Your ever affectionate father
97251 Your loving son
97251 J. Mosse
