My dear Friend
I received your letter this evening, and have had a long and full
conversation with my father on the subject of it; and I think I shall be
able now to give you precisely the state of their feelings.
The course which my father would prefer before all others would
be this: that you should come and be the minister of Charleston, and
connexion with his people for sometime to come as colleague pastor
for some time to come. – Discouraging his wish would be that his connexion
might be as nearly nominal as possible – the responsibility and direction
of affairs to belong to you. – The advantages of this course he thinks are
the following.
1. He believes it is the only course which will secure the union
of the church and society. His reasons for this belief are as follows:
2. You would wish to revise the discipline of the church, and to
introduce a change in this practice which would be in the interest of Baptists.
My father thinks that he could not sustain the change without remaining
connected with the people he could assist you in their plans, and render the
revolution less hazardous.
3. With respect to himself personally, it would be desirable that the law relating to Overseers of Harvard College will
be repealed – and then the proposed plan will give two orthodox overseers
from Charleston.
4. – On the proposed plan my father retains his connexion with
the Anderson Institution, the Prudential Committee, and the various
donations to which he belongs.
5. With respect to himself personally it would be grateful to
his feelings – because it would give him an opportunity to wind up his
affairs, and gradually to deliver the church and remove this region.
It would give the occasion for the admission of an enemy to triumph. He
now feels that the public impression will be that he is driven away.
