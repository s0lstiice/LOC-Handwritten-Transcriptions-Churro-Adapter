and we both think that it is best to choose the former
of these two evils; I have found this journey much more
expensive than I had imagined; I am under the necessity of
borrowing 10 doll. from Mr. Silliman which will leave
something over. I presume at the end of the journey.— I
set out to-morrow morning at 5 o'clock ride all day and the
night, the next day I next night and reach Charleston prob:
ably on Tuesday morning, at 10 o'clock, when I shall be
glad to rest in good earnest.—
I have written four letters to Lucretia on the
way and requested her to inform you as I proceeded; I
thought this was better than to write 2 letters each time
one to you & one to her each cost me 25 cents, when by writing
to her yours would cost but 10 cents, you see I understand
economy! "Have at the Spicket and let out at the Burg," I think
I hear you say, among other proverbs; Well I can't help
it; I hope soon to begin to fill the barrell at the burg, as
soon as I can get to Charleston.—
I shall write you again when I reach Charleston
in the mean time I must bid you adieu! give my love
to Edie? and the family Nancy, Betsey, & Sally if with you
and believe me as ever, your affectionate Son
Sam. F. P. Morie.
