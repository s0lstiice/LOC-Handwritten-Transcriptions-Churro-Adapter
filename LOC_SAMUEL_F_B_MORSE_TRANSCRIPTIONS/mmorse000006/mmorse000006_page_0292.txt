My beloved Husband,
It is with no small degree of pleasure that I sit
down at this time to write to my dear Tully; not however without feeling
a little something like compunction for not having written him one line in
nearby, a fortnight and for which offence I deserve a hearty pardon in his
next letter; but in excuse I plead a constant inability to write in the
day and night too, during a month past, and the days have been so
respectively cold, and short that one could scarcely look around, and perform
the ordinary duties of the day, before it was night. I received your dear
letter informing me of your safe arrival at Charleston, after the terrible
encounters on the way, and seeing the goodness of the Lord, which we
have experienced in such remarkable degree, make a suitable impression
upon our hearts, and lives, and may we with greater earnestness enquire
God what with them have us to do, and put in the way of his commandments
with enlarged hearts I look forward, dearest, with anticipation
of the most pleasing kind to your return, after a few short months
shall have passed away, how earnestly I long to see you, Tully, you
can never know how public expressions cannot give you an idea of my
love, nor of the continually increasing attachment I feel for my dear
