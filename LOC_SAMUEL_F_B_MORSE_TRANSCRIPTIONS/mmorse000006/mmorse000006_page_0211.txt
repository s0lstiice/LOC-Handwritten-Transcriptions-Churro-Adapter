Charleston S. Carolina. Jan. 12th 1820.
My Dearest Ladies,
Through the blessing of a kind and indulgent Providence I have been kept from all harm and have
at length arrived safely in Charleston; I left Fayetteville the morning I mentioned in my last, it was a very disagreeable
day, rainy & foggy, the coaches were watched beyond description most ingeniously contrived for inconvenience, we were as
much exposed as if out doors, the seats were wet, and the top leaked through every crack, so that in a short time
we were wet through; as night approached, the stages grew worse, and the weather more unfavorable; no moon, and
the darkness so great as to prevent our seeing our white horses, we proceeded without accident till about midnight, when our
leaders took fright at something and started out of the road, and in a moment the stage struck against a tree and stopped
with such violence as to throw us from the backseat to the front seat, we found on examination that the swingletree
was lost and the tongue broken, this detained us half an hour in a hard rain; we had a sleepless night, a miserable break-
fast at a log hovel for which we had to pay 75 cents, all the next day was rainy till towards night, it then cleared off fine
we got to Georgetown at 5 o'clock and set out immediately again, rode all night, very cold, but clear & pleasant, and
arrived without accident or sickness safely in Charleston.
