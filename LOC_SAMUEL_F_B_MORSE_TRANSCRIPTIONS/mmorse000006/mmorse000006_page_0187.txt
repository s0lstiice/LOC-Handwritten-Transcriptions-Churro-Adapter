I find contrary to my expectation a very fine hotel in
Richmond, Kept by Mr. Butler of Haven, where we are
very comfortably situated; we shall set out to-morrow
for Petersburg at 11 o'clock 25 miles, where I shall finish
my letter.— By careful nursing and precaution I have succeeded in throwing off my cold, which was a very severe
one, and I am now entirely well. I have now and then
a twinge of the defective tooth which gives me a gentle hint
that I shall soon have to throw that off also.—
I find it a great comfort having a companion with
me, especially such a man as Mr. Silliman, an intelligent
prious man, and one whose feelings and views are congenial
with my own; we are also similarly situated, both having
left wives whom we ardently love and sigh after, and children that make us long for home; he has the advantage however of one in Britain to my one; he sends his
compliments to you and says he hopes to see you one of these
days. 10 o'clock P.M.
I called and sat a little while this evening with
Mr. Turner & his wife & Mrs. Burd.
Petersburg Virginia, Jan'y. 4. 1820.
Thanks to a kind Providence, I have safely
advanced another step on my journey, and am comfortably housed

we are agreeably disappointed again; we rest here till to-morrow
morning, when, at 3 o'clock in the morning, we set out again on the
