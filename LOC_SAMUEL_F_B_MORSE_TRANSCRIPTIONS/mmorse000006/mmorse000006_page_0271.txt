Charleston S.C. Jan. 17th 1820-
Dear Mother,
I recd your good letter of 19th of 22nd ult. I
thank you for it; I wish I had time to give you a nar-
tative of my journey as you will but, you know "time is
money," and we must "make hay while the sun shines," and
"a penny saved is a penny got," and "least said soonest
maided," &c. &c. and a good many other wise sayings, which
would be quite fat, but I can't think of them; the fact is
I have scarcely time to say or write a word, I am busily
employed in getting the cash or else Meds almanac for
Marchill foretell falsely; I am doing well, although the
city literally swarms with painters I am the only one
that has as much as he can do; all the rest are complaining.
I wish I could divide with some of them, very clever
men who have families to support, and can get nothing
to do. -- I recd a letter from Father at Washington today,
but I suppose it contains no news to you, he had seen the
Pres. who spoke well of my picture of him &c. --
Richard came from Johns Island 2 days or two
since I spent 2 or 3 days in town, I was very glad to
see him and find him contented in good spirits.
he went back again yesterday.
