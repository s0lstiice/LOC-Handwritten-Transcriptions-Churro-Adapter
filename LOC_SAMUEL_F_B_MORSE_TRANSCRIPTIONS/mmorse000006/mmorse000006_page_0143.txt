My Dear Father,
I improve a moment to write you in answer to
your letter we have received from you, we are all well and send
you many love, Lucretia is fast recovering, and dear little Susan
is healthy and thriving. As to the concerns of the old Parish I
shall devote the greater part of this letter to that subject; and I
have proceeded to the statement of circumstances without preamble,
the project of friends which you were so much interested in,
will not, (in all probability) succeed, the project was intended to
appear immediately, (how we know not,) and we have reason
to believe that we have taken efficient measures to counteract it. I never
could feel satisfied myself as to the propriety of this measure,
it looks, under hand, I hope, you will excuse me if I say, I wish
you had not continued it, the motive was unquestionably
good, but the end does not justify the means, your friends
and family all know how to account for these things in you, but
does it not, my dear father, give occasion for your enemies, and
those that have been warning, to ventilate the cry of "a plotting
deserving man," and will not the constant failure of schemes in
this business at length induce the few remaining friends, to say
that you are desperate, and will cater at any thing to support
you and will tread so near to the line of honorable, of course necessary
that enemies will easily mistake, and friends can scarcely dis-
luciate you: Excuse the freedom I take in being so plain, my dear
