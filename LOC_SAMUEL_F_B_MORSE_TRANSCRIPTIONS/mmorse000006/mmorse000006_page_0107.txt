Washington August 13th. 1819.
Dear Sir.
Received your letter on my arrival
at this place, which has deferred far beyond the period
that I had anticipated, when I had the pleasure of
meeting you at Charleston.
The President will leave Washington
in a day or two for his farm in Virginia. He is much
engaged at the present moment, and it is impossible
to say with certainty when he may return to the city.
In these reasons, his family have requested me to
inform you, that in their opinion, it would be better
to defer the execution of the portrait, until a more
convenient opportunity may occur. During the winter
he will be constantly at this place, and as the seat
of government offers much more inducement for
