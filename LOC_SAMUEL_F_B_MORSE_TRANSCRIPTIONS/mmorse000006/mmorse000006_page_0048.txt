Charleston S.C. Mar. 26. 1819.
My Dear Parents,
Two of your letters have been lately
reached, detailing the state of the parish & church; I
cannot say I was surprised for it might be expected from our people.— Before saying anything on this
subject, however, I will just say that Lucretia is just
recovering from a severe bilious fever, which has confined her to her bed for several days, she has, however, by the blessing of God on the means used for her recovery, safely passed through it, not however without considerable alarm on my part and indeed on the part of Uncle Finley and our friends generally, in consequence of her delicate situation, she is now comfortable and recovering fast, she exhibited a pattern of patience through all her sickness, making no complaint, and expressing no unreasoning; as to returning home in the way I mentioned Mama need not be at all uneasy on that score; it is necessary I should visit Washington, as the Pres' will stay so short a time here that I cannot complete the head unless I due him in Wash. (by the bye the City council have passed a unanimous vote to request me to paint it for the City Hall and have agreed to give me more than Dashed, 750 dollars,) I therefore, propose leaving here for Baltimore by water, leave Lucretia in the case of Nancy at B. and go on to Wash. to complete my business there, then proceed to B. and soon to Phil.
