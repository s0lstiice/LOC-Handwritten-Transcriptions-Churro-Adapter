Richmond, Virginia Jan 2. 1820.
My Dearest Wife
I wrote you a letter from Fredericksburg a
day or two since, and according to my promise I sit down
to inform you of my safe arrival in this city, we left Fredericksburg
yesterday morning at 3 o'clock, a most bitter cold
morning, but clear, with a bright moon. With all my precautions of
leggings, gloves, & mocassins, I suffered a great deal from the severity of the
cold; we had scarcely rode a mile before our hats, the bows of our
cravats, our hair, whiskers, eyebrows and eyelashes were covered with frost and icicles.
I actually found upon shutting my eyes for a little time that
my eyelashes were frozen to my cheek, we ascertain upon our arrival that the thermometer indicated the temperature at 4 degrees below 0, this is the coldest weather ever known in Virginia, (so we are told) it
has been a very fine day to-day, and although very cold has moderated some. We attended church to-day I heard Mr. Turner, an excellent sermon on the New Year, Mr. J. and his wife were at our house last summer if you recollect, I spoke to him after church and called upon him. Mr. James Dwight we found in this city also, endeavoring to establish himself in business here, he has closed his concern in Charleston: he was very glad to see me, and inquired particularly after you a gentleman by the name of Proctor, I also found here who travelled with us in the Steamboat from N.
