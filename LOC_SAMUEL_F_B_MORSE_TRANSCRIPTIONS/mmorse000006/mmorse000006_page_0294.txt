I am glad to hear, dear, you are accommodated with such excellent rooms but
the rent you give is a comfortable price, however every thing in Charleston
is dear
I hope to receive a good long letter from your dear husband
this evening, you must write me after love, my greatest earthly comfort
is to receive good tidings from my husband
I received a long letter from Philip State last
month he took a journey to Lexington during the Christmas holidays
and did not write for some time which caused us much anxiety
Mr Everett wrote a letter to Charles informing him he had some business
with a gentleman of L. who he was about to settle in the western style
that is to fight a duel, but added as Charles was a friend to both
parties perhaps his interference might produce an amicable adjustment
accordingly Charles took his leave, and proceeded seventy five long miles
in that uncomfortable season, to use his emotions with him sufficient
to prevent what all men of opinion would term him in blood
Everett but is a pretty choleric young man and often gets
into such a state of mind that he returns to Louisville. There has been
nothing new to say Mr. Charleston had been a widower almost one
whole year and to allay his inconstant sorrow for his first love, has
taken to himself another Uncle Joseph Walker is published and will
be married in the course of a few weeks, we always considered him
an inveterate old bachelor but Mr. Stegney's weighty charms have
melted his heart -- Mr. Chadbourne has a little daughter a few drops
of milk like some other he is very fond of -- you would think
he is married a few days before us -- Mrs. M. Garland has one
about the same age born September, baptized in the afternoon
Mary Green sends her love to you now together with my Parents, brothers
and sisters -- baby Susan sends Dear Papa a sweet kiss -- my health
has been very poor this even weather a constant feverish head ache all
the time, Mr. Bartlett encourages me in the hope that I had entirely got
rid of the feverish habit but I am very subject to such I think
I have not drank tea out been out a single evening since you left me
but good bye my precious husband your most affectionately
Evartt
