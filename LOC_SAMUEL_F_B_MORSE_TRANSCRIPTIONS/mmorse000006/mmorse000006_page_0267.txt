Washington Jan 28th 1820
my dear wife;
Several days have elapsed since I have heard from you & I fear that I shall have
no more letters till I reach Phila as you will suppose that I shall have left this city in this
time but my business will keep me here probably the greater part perhaps all this week It is
going on as fast & with as much success as I could reasonably expect I trust will have
in all respects a favorable issue The President (with whom I dined in a large company,
on Friday) & the Secretary of War (with whom I dined the day before) are very kind, about
me more as a friend than as a man of business. Mr. Adams, too is very attentive & obliging,
as indeed are all with whom I have to transact my business. Mr. Mc Kinney, the Agent of
Indian Affairs, I have from & am to spend to morrow, the following night at his house in
Georgetown, 3 or 4 miles from this for the purpose of giving us the official talk over deliberately
& fully all our affairs I shall get obtain information of Indians to bring home with me which will facilitate my getting much more
trust in my contemplated tour I have nothing new to add to what I expect wrote on the subject of
my Commission. I intended to have written you
