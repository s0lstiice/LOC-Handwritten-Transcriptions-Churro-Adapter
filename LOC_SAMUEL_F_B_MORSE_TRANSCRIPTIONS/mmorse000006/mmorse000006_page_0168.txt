him, I should have been curious to have seen him.
I am painting, Dr. Sewall, he will pay by note
for a year, in consequence of his friends not being at
command at present, as he is removing to settle in
this city with his family; he is a man with whom I
have become intimate, a pious man, eminent in his pro-
fession, and moving in the first rank in society; I have
no other portrait as yet engaged but presume when
the Poets and Dr. Sewalls become known I shall have
as many as I want, to employ me till the middle or
perhaps end of next week. I am doubtful how
