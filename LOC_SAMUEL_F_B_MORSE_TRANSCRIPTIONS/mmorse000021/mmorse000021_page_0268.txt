it lifts, the fraction of an inch, the current of electricity is connected
with the Telegraph. This method allows me at any distance whatever
to make a magnet lift a weight of some hundred pounds.
I thought to say that I made this telegraph by myself & that
it has not yet left the house; indeed it is not completed, other cares
having prevented. And I shall not make it public, until I hear
from you. Your system has certainly great advantages: but so
has mine. In mine, the characters can be seen, can be transferred
immediately to the Dial Telegraph. Force versa. Then too the great
facility of making corrections as fast as intelligence is transmitted, and
the very important advantage of being able to interrupt one despatch
for another more praising. It is a real conversation, for the characters
are always seen, just as words are heard. With yours, which
I admire very much however, you must wait till all is passed,
to see what has been said. But, I repeat it, this detracts
nothing of ingenuity or utility from yours. I believe different
systems are necessary according to the use to be made of them.
You will ask me for designs & drawings, and details of what we can do. I should like nothing better than
to give them, but my letter is already so long that I will
defer this to another opportunity, not distant, for I am
desirous to please you.
Mrs. Brequet charges me to remember her
to you. Receive, I beg you, the assurances of my consideration
Your Servant
