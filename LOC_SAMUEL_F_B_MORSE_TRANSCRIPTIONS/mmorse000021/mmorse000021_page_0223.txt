Washington July 12th 1846
My Dear Sir,
You rightly understood the Postmaster
General yesterday, he does not intend to carry
on the Telegraph to Baltimore much longer
as a government concern or pay the salaries
as heretofore. He seems to have been well in
formed as to the claims set up by some of
our Company at Baltimore and inquired
what the Company contemplated. I informed
him how the matter stands. He then said
he proposed to sell the line and informed
that we would give for it. I told him
I was not prepared to say, but would confer
with our Directors on the subject.
In connection with the matter of salaries,
he spoke of the scheme of yourself and
Mr. Nail. I hope it will be convenient
for both of you to return; but I shall
not attempt the negotiation for the
the line.
Most sincerely
Your friend
Amos Kendall
Post. S. F. R. Morel
P.S. When can Mr. Stockell furnish us with
two additional instruments? We want them
130 immediately.
