New York, May 23rd, 1846,
Prof. S. F. B. Morse.
Dear Sir,
About 2 years ago I
made an invention for protecting the wires of
your Telegraph from injury from the passing
of discharges of the electric fluid from the
atmosphere, along the wire, to the earth, at
the points where the wire passes through or
near the post; or from the earth upward,
through the same channel, to the atmos-
phere. Probably the discharges go would
mostly be from the atmosphere downwards,
My plan is to have a suitable point, coat-
ed with Platinum, attached to the Tel-
egraph wire near the post (at many posts
as may be found necessary probably one
in each quarter of a mile) & another point
of the same kind, attached to a lightning rod
which I propose should be erected on the
post for the purpose of forming a channel
for the fluid between the Telegraph wire
& the earth. The point on the Telegraph
wire, & that on the lightning rod attached
to the post, must be placed at such dis-
tance from each other as to allow the fluid
when your wire is operating to continue along the Telegraph
wire from end to end; but when the wire
receiving a charge from the atmosphere, the
