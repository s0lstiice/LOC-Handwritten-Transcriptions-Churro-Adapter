Dear Vail
I am here at Newark at the Court House. There
was a strong defection of the militia last night about
10 o'clock; but this morning the arms must be broken
somewhere, as Bailey & Coulson have been waiting all
day without effect. Mr. Joel went about 11 o'clock in
a buggy towards Louisville to look after the difficulty.
It is an anxious time, & I have had so much of anxiety
from various causes, that I am almost sick. But I
shall rally I hope, after to-morrow. Keep up battery in
good order. Tell me if you tried the little magnet, &
with what effect. I have no time to add, it is so
dark, & I must go. I hope all will be right for the
news by Steamer. Efforts will be made to get it here
to transmit, to you, but we have great opposition all
round, the Government the press, & certain Speculators.
It will be almost a miracle if we succeed. But
if the wires are whole, we will try. I put up two letters.
You must be very anxious what you say & do about
C. You must restrain your feelings. It is impossible
to get along just at present, without him, & the
violence of your opposition will only create sympathy for
him, she can do us a great deal of injury. I have
no more favorable opinion of his proceedings than
I ever have, but we must wait a proper opportunity, for
freeing ourselves from him. I cannot write more now
but had hoped to converse with you by telegraph. Goodby
till Monday. In instant haste - S.B. Morley.
