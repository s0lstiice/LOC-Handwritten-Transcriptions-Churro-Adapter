Washington Thursday Morning Sept 9th
1846.
Dear Morse
I have just tried an experiment
upon un soldered joints of Copper wire. I cut
up into a 1000 pieces copper wire of the kind we use, and
then put them all together myself that I might
be confident of them being done well. Every joint
was made by twisting the end of one wire around
the body of the other with the pincher and the
contact perfect and clean. These I have hung up
at the ceiling running back & forth over my head
in length about 150 feet and this morning I tested
it by putting the wire in the circuit from here to
Baltimore, and also put in the circuit our old
Galvanometer, and what do you think was the
result? Give a guess before I tell you. The galvama-
ter stood the same with the 1000 joints in the
circuit, as it did when out of the circuit, I am
much surprised at it but so it is -- The wire is bright
at the joints and I presume the difference should
not have been much, but still I thought there would
be some. I dont think it worth while to mention
this to any Contractors or other for there is no much
neglect in putting up the telegraphic wires that
it is necessary to insist upon their doing more to get
done even what is absolutely necessary. I desire to
make the same experiment with iron wire and with
you could get for that purpose 1000 pieces 4 1/2 inches
long & bring them on with you. I should prefer
annealed wire of the same number as this. Will
you also bring with you 10 or 15 pounds of the
fine shot we used for testing the strength of magnet
I want it for the same purpose.
155
