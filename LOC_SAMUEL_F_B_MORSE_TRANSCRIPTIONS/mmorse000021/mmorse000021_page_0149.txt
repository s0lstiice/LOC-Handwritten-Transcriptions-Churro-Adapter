Middlebury Apr. 6th 1846 Monday
Prof. S. F. B. Morse
Dear Sir
I warmly appreciate both the readiness with which you have given attention to my humble speculations on the friendly frankness with which you have pointed out the points of failure you conceive to be in my way. I also acknowledge the favor of Mr Vails Pamphlet which was duly received. It is true that at the time of sending my letter I was unacquainted with the methods to which you have referred me. My eye had caught, it is true, the figures of the portolute method, in Billman's journal, I believe, and elsewhere, but considering it an method now abandoned, I had never possessed myself of it. Perceiving, as I thought, that the method I sketched, in my last two letters, would operate admirably I presumed on its merits. I find some apology for leaving both thus unknowingly, in due my distance from the sources of accurate information. However I see reason to believe that I have not put myself or you to trouble wholly in vain, as I shall now attempt to show.
I do not see how it is possible, by the portolute method, to operate much faster than a printer can set types, while your present method (which from to my best previous information was competent, as I supposed, to no more than 30 characters a minute) I find by your last is at least double raised to what the printers tell me they can execute. Moreover if your apprehensions as to the rapidity with which keys may be touched are well founded it would seem to me to follow certainly that you have reduced the Telegraph already to one wire, one operator in the use of the present Alphabet, to this topic be laid aside for a moment.
