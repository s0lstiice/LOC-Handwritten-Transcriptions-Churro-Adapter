New Haven March 14th 1846.
My dear Sir.
Your favor of the 12th just came to
hand last evening. I think my letter led you to infer
that I considered the plan I proposed for communicating
by Telegraph as entitled to more originality than I claimed.
The principal features of my plan which I thought now
was, to have a book such as proposed, for general circulation
whence I had supposed that the words of the kind herefore used
were on a comparatively small scale, & designed only for
the Telegraph offices or for private use. The Kind Report limited Report
I had not seen; I borrowed it of a friend today, & find, much in
it that is new & interesting.
The plan of having a book of numbered phrases & for gen-
eral use, appears to me more important as I contemplate
it, (among other reasons, besides those already suggested,) it affords
so great facilities for private or secret correspondence. It will
only be necessary for persons to agree previously upon certain letters
of the alphabet to represent the digits, after the manner in which
merchants mark their goods, & no persons should should where hands the cor-
respondence might pass, would be any the wiser for it. Something of the
kind is suggested on one of the pages of the report to which you re-
fere, though not exactly the same as the plan I have in my mind.
