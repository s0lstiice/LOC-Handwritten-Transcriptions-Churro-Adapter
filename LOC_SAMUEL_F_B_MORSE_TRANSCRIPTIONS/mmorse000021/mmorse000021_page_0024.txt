Washington Jan. 12th 1840
My dear Sir
The enclosed letter from Mr. Tipton will
explain itself.
I have referred him to you in the hope
that you may have some of the small mag
nets made by the time he may want them.
It is very desirable to have a quantity of them
done as soon as possible.
I see in the Herald and Express a puff upon
Mr. Cornell's important improvements and have
a letter from Mr. O'Reilly proposing to refute
it. I have advised him against it, not because
these things do us no harm, but because
they would do us more if ridiculed. As to Mr.
Cornell himself, we shall soon be done with him,
and without a general hope.
These articles mention some experiments from
Karlsruhe across the river on Friday last, said to
be unsuccessful. These I take it were by Mr. Cor
nell without your agency. But I hope you will
as soon as possible satisfy yourself as to the
capacity of this mode of washing the river, particu
larly whether one line can be made avail
able, and if so whether a second can, by a similar
installation, be safely laid alongside of it.
