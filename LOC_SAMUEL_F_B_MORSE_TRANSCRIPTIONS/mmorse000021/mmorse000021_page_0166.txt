My explanations of the Telegraph have been
well patronized -- the first night receipts in New
Haven were $88.00. Every Professor except two, I
believe, in Yale College were present. -- Many of
them flattered me by personal commendations.
My success in the sale of this work
has not not been as great as I could be think, principally to the fact, that nearly my
whole time has been occupied with the painting.
I trust it will be different in a few days,
when I arrive in Boston.
In all my future operations with
the Telegraph, I would pursue just that course
which you desire. I would not represent myself
other than I am. Suppose I adopt the course I
purposed in New York -- "Mr. Colton, by the permission
of Prof. Morse" -- This will give me, apparently,
some authority -- and yet will not make you, or
any one else, my backer.
The "Annexed" -- I have prepared in such a
manner as I think will meet your wishes, and at
the same time, will not be a direct confession
to the public that I have used unwarranted authority.
It will have its effect upon those interested.
I should be pleased to know your feelings upon the
subject. I enclose $20.00 (which is more than
I have received) for Mr. Bail. Yours humbled Friend. G.R. Colton
(Professor Morse)
