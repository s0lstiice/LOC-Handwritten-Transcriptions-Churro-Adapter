Washington, Oct 8th 1876.
Dear Sir:
I have received your letter of yesterday's date, and
passed its contents with the greatest pain, because I am
sure that the grossest misrepresentations, in every respect,
have been made to you respecting my conduct. I have
never received any message from you to the effect that
it was "your request that I should be reinstated in
my office", and, that, it is declined on my part! Your
name has never been mentioned to me, in any way
by Mr Bail, since his return!
I should deeply regret if any impression,
calculated to change your good opinion of me, could, for
a single moment, find a resting place within your
breast; and I have, therefore, to request that you will
suspend your judgment until you should hear further
from me. My brother, to whom I have shown your
letter, is much pained at its content; and requests me to
say, that he would not, on any account, have me to
accept of a situation from the Company, with the present
unkind impressions towards me, and my family resting upon
your mind, conveyed, doubtless, for the purpose of injuring
