Washington April 20th, 1846.
My dear Sir. Your favor of the 12th March by the "Unicorn" is
received. You could not have been more surprised than
myself, to learn that Mr. Green & Co. had no order to pay
your bill. I sent a letter of credit from one of our first
Bankers by the same Steamer of the 1st inst. which took from
letter to you. The letter of credit was dated 29th Decr. 15, upon receipt
of London, which letter has long since been acknowledged by the London
banker, but as I have received no acknowledgement from Mr. Green, my
letter to him has must have been carried. I have written them
by the Steamer which carries this, apprising them that the money waits
only this draft, and I hope there will be no further delay in paying
your bill. You were correct in supposing your letter to Mr. Green had probably not reached him.
I hope it will not be long before your country and mine will be more immediately connected by steam packets dependent on England for communication.
I know not if you get information through the Times, papers of the progress of my telegraph. I have every reason to be confident with its success. A few weeks more, and Boston, N.York, Phil.
Balt. & Washington will be connected 428 miles, and also these main lines extend 861 miles.
N.York, Albany, & Buffalo 433 miles; there are besides these main lines many branch lines of 30 or 40 miles each.
I have not much to communicate of importance in relation to the action of Telegraph, I have always preferred to understate rather than overstate its efficiency.
I have already shown before the Academy of Sciences, a Telegraphic communication in which 60 characters or digits were given in one minute.
I enclose you one written in the same time of 85 characters; and at this rate I have several operators who perform at that rate.
