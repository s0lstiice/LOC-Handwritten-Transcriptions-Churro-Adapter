Washington March 28th 1826
Dear Sidney. I am just reminded that this is the last day
for writing by the Steamer, and I therefore, take a moment to write
you a hasty line. I was much gratified at receiving a letter from
you by the last arrival. I thank you for your evidence on the fact
of inventing the mode of making one circuit operate more than one
pen. Since I wrote, Prof. Tannin has explained more at large his plan of
writing and it is only a modification of my keyed instrument offering no
advantage but on the contrary having more disadvantages. It will not amount
to anything. If you see the American paper as I suppose you do, you
will see how the Telegraph is going ahead. In a few weeks we
shall hear direct from Washington to Boston 430 miles! We have
some difficulties with the New York & Philadelphia line on account of
the North River, but we shall surmount them soon. In regard to
the Rotundo Picture, I learn that my friends are quite zealous, and
it is not improbable that it may be given me to execute. If so what
should you say to seeing me in Paris? However, this is but Castlebuilding.
I am quite indifferent as to the result, except that in case it is given me, I shall
be restored to my position as an artist by the same power that prostrated me, and
then shall I not more than ever have cause to explain, "Surely your heart led
me in a way which I know not." -- I have already, in looking back
seen enough of the dealings of Providence with me, to excite
my wonder, and gratitude. How singularly has my way been hedged
up in my profession, at the very moment when human appearance
