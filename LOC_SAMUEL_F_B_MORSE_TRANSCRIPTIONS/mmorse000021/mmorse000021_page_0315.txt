Dear Sidney, I don't know where this will find place
but as the Steam Baledonia goes in a day or two I did
not write you by the last Steam I thought I would
only a few moments (not exactly of leisure) to write
you. Richard received your letter by the California from Fort
Fort in the Maine and we were all rejoiced to hear of your safe
continued health. It is an anxious time here to know where you will
be the coming winter whether in Paris or London. We should
have been glad to have seen you at home this season, but as
you will excuse the water you may as well improve your
advantage here to do with all that these advantages give you.
I have just had a conversation with Munson on Telegraphic
matters. There is only one part which is uncertain, and which
discourage him and that is the battery up. All the rest is certain
and I doubt if this battery part he complains that with his utmost
preparation, sometimes this force will go on well, and sometimes
very badly to the ruin of many day's work. I encourage him by
assuring him that there is no cause without an effort and that cause can
be made to serve for the extent that a good result under certain
circumstances can be obtained makes it clear that when that
now hidden difficulty is ascertained (as I doubt will be) there is
a double advantage gained for it will be a barrier to others in
the attempt to emulate you. He feels most anxious on your
account, and sometimes doubts whether he is wise in employing
the hands at work which is spoiled afterwards by his processes. I
hardly know how to advise him, but think on the whole he has better
keep them in your service, than run the risk of losing them and perhaps
of revealing the process.
