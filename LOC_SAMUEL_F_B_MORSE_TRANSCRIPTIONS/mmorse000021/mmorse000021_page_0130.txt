I have since learned that he denies that the smallest current (spark was the word used) will exist at all for any length of time even on wires connected, insulated. I am sure he is wrong, but perhaps you can explain the apparent incompleteness of his opinions with what you have ascertained. If this is the case, I must think by saying that I cannot doubt that, at least the matter of the wire, known in my papers as the hands of Dr. Boche and Messrs. Tracy & Gould, will be offered as a subject for the complete water circuit -- even if your present methods are not. I hope my plans will be thoroughly weighed.
Finally I request you to consider whether the plan does not allow you at once to substitute iron wire in your ordinary circuits instead of copper. This is a question of great importance to your invention, and deserving from generals to the small scale of duty it is also one, in the circumstances, of more or less value.
I may think it advisable to discuss again that matter of the applicability of your water circuit. The practical disaffection has surprised me not a little. Possibly I am greatly in the dark as to some fact in the case and I am certain I am not well informed, at this distance, to supply such a deficiency if existing.
In haste (as usual)
Your affectionate friend & Servt
When you reply, of your whereabouts.
Abt C. S. Irving
