New York Jan 30th 1846.
Dear Sidney,
I find I have but a single
half hour in which to write you, and I must
therefore be short. I thank you for your
letter and advice respecting Jackson, although
I think there will be no necessity for it.
He is very quiet, and makes light of the
whole matter as one of no consequence. He
will not trouble me any more.
Do you remember some little time
before we left for Europe, I was conversing
with you, (it was in June I think,) on a method
of using different batteries & magnets, both of
different strength in order to multiply the means
of writing with more rapidity, for example
a battery of 3 cups will not give magnetism
enough to operate a magnet and pen, when
the magnet has few coils, and is small, but
6 cups will move that magnet, and so on. This
method you will remember I think, was
suggested in order to provide against any supposed
improvement on my mode of writing by Hesse
I have drawn out a plan, and prepared a
caveat for that mode, on that principle
