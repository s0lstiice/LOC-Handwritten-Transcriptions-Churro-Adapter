I was anxious to sell these works, -- both on
my own account, and on Mr. Rail's and Yours.
I had the Agency of them -- and in some slight
sense I might call myself (as I believed) the
( humble) Agent of the U.S. Telegraphic Company.
Under this belief, that I was acting for the interest
of the Company, (although as much, as more for
my own interest) I pursued the course you
see. I was aware of the fact that the success
of my exhibitions -- as well as the sale of these
works, depended very much upon the authority
by which I came before the public. I think
I was actuated as much by me, as the other.
It is true, it seemed rather a stretch
of propriety to call myself an "Agent of the
U.S. Telegraphic Company" or to allow an editor
to do so, simply because I had these works for
sale. -- but in doing so, I did not claim that
I was transcending any privilege you had given
me, -- or that I was claiming an authority which
you would not sanction. Believe me, Sir, this
is true.
I have felt proud and humbly grateful for
the attention and kindness you have shown me;
and I hope most sincerely that I shall so
manage that, you shall not regret the Certifi-
cate you gave me.
