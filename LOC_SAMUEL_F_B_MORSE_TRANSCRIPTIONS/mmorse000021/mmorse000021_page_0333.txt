Washington D C
November 18th 1846
Dear Sir
Mr Kendall has handed
to me a parcel containing a proposed Contract
prepared in triplicate & signed by Messrs Smith
Well & Irvington, accompanied by objections (in
pencil) by yourself, which I suppose were submitted
to the signers. I should think your objections
valid and conclusive -- Were the basis of this
contract satisfactory to you, I should think the
consideration should be promised & guaranteed in
a less equivocal form, as to the time & manner of
payment & in fact, as to the actual amount itself
& I receive nothing from myself on either
of the Signers in relation to the Matter probably
I am only expected to hold the papers till your
arrival here
My stepson, Mr Dickinson, in Montreal, writes
me on 10th Nov. as follows "I have just seen our
"Attorney Genl. who tells me, that by our Patent Laws, it
"is quite impossible to procure a Patent here, on an
"Invention or an invention discovered in the States by any
"one -- that the laws were made at a particular time, to
"meet a particular Case. The Attorney Genl. says they should
"not be revalued, but thinks the best way for us is
"to get a Special Bill passed at the next Session
"which is not likely to take place before March. In order
"to do this a Notice in the Gazette by authority is necessary to"
"this is about as I supposed -- He says further
"that my notice had not been published as sent to him
"but he waits my orders to give a formal & legal notice
