copy to P. O. J. Smith, Esq., enclosing a note of his for $149.00
Washington April 3. 1860
Dear Sir,
In looking over Bob an old account book I found within
the leaves the enclosed note of yours for $149.00 dollars. My memory
in such matters is very treacherous, and I have been puzzled to know
whether it has been paid or not. I have an indistinct recollection of some money
leaving about the date of the note, and a vague impression that the
note has been paid, but why it in my possession rests is the mystery. If
it has been paid, you ought to have it. If not you can probably solve
the mystery by reference to your own accounts, and will let me know
I am the more inclined to think it has been paid, from the fact that I
had not charged my memory with it. But if not you will make it all
right.
I regard to the line from Boston to Norfolk I should
like to hear from you its condition. When all the way through
should delight to have you in the Exchange in Boston,
while I shall be here, and hold the first telegraphic inter-
course from Washington to Boston. We ought to have some
plan of operation concerted against the completion of the entire
line. If instruments shall be ready at intermediate stations
say at Worcester, Springfield, Hartford, New Haven, Philadelphia,
Philadelphia, Wilmington, and Baltimore, you can conceive
the effect when it is announced that each of these places
received the same intelligence at the same moment. Let
me hear from you on this subject.
There are already indications of attempts to impress
the enemy with the idea that we are making preparations for
an attack upon their communications.
Ever affy
By
George W. Mason
Secretary of War
Washington
April 3, 1860
