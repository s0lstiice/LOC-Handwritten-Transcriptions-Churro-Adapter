is bad, and has been badly put up in some parts of the line. Who
is to blame? I do not pretend to say. But much more is
made of these difficulties by some persons, principal to the whole
enterprise than is necessary. Just. Perhaps stock-jobsitions
are at the bottom of the difficulties. All difficulties will be surmounted in
good time, as all scientific difficulties have already been.
The lines agree with you, ought to have been more
substantially built. I hope your line from Atlanta to Atlanta
will be built. It cannot do was to urge the necessity of this course but cheaper
circuits prevailed. In regard to intermediate Stations I believe
I gave you a slight sketch of the mode of
arranging them. These arrangements will depend
much on the quantity of business required at each
station. If with which you enter into such have little
business, a single instrument truly will be necessary at the
intermediate Station, if you wish to communicate each way
to and from an intermediate at the same time, then two sets will be
required. I will write you again on this subject.
Our line here is in perfect order, and is kept so with no
little trouble; there was but one day during all the last
year in which it was not in a condition to communicate at
any moment, and that was from an accident by lightning.
My own wish, from the beginning ever since the experiment of
this line proved successful, was to have stout posts
from 15 or 16 inches at the bottom to 1 foot at top, and capable
of holding 30 or 40 wires if necessary.
