Washington, 22 August, 1840.
My Dear Sir:
Some few days since, I received a letter
from Mr Wood, who is now stationed at Buffalo,
informing me that the line of Telegraph would
soon be completed through to New-York, and that
the Company would like to secure the services
of a competent person to take charge of the New
York terminus. He wrote at the request of the
President of the Company, Mr Inston, who wished
to ascertain if I would be willing to leave my
present station and assume the duties of that
post.
I replied to Mr Wood immediately, and re-
fired the President of the Company to you; as I
understood from him that you were expected at
Buffalo, stating that I should be pleased to go
to New-York, provided the compensation would justify
me in going there.
You know that my present compen-
sation is very small, and much less that the
Company's operations receive at this terminus. My
request is that you will give me an opportunity of
expressing to the President of the Company
my willingness to accept the position of
Superintendent of the New-York terminus.
I am, Sir, your obedient Servant,
George W. McClellan.
