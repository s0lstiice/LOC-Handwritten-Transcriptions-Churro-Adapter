Washington Oct. 9th 1846
Prof. S. F. B. Morse.
My dear Sir, urgent business called me
home; but had I been apprized of the extreme
danger division of the Board of the N.Y. M.L.
R.R. Co. I should have gone over from Phila-
delphia.
It is strange ground in this country that those
interested shall not decide important questions for
themselves and the directors will not be sustained.
We cannot in justice to ourselves or the country
agrimence in this division. It is a fatal injury to
a large mass of our property and denying to us
in part the consideration of our contract. But
worse than that -- it is the breaking down of
the system we had projected to give the bene-
fits of the Telegraph to every village and prevent
the Telegraph to the country as practically on unit,
upon whose wires messages would travel from
one end of the country to the other immediately.
I propose, however, before resorting to other remedies
to propose selling to the company our interest
in all the side lines and take payment in stock.
I have no doubt I can bring the company
to a connection or to a proprietor.
When shall we see you?
Most truly yours
Amos Kendall
New York. 175
