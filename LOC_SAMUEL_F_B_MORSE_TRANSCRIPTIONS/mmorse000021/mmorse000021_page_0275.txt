Washington Sept 13th 1844
Dear Morre
I have permission of the department
to spend a few days at the North, for which I
shall leave here on Wednesday morning, and proceed
directly for your city, provided Mrs Bail is there,
and if so I should be glad to see you on Thursday
morning at the Observer Buildings, when I will
tell you more about the experiment of the joints.
I shall spend only a day or so in the City, &
return soon to Washington. I heard a powerful
funeral sermon from Mr Spole today on the
occasion of Mr Speir, a member of his Church
who died after a short illness in the hope of a
blessed immortality. I had the pleasure of Mr
Speir's acquaintance, who was a member of our
Lodge. The character of Mr Speir and his happy
death exhibited a striking, though silent contrast
with the melancholy death of Mr Connell, and
must have struck every hearer, though Mr Spole
did not attend to him (Mr Connell). I presume
you have learned of Mr Stone's death. Dr Pages
opinion: There have been many sudden deaths
all around us, and many are still sick. I
have however been well, for which I am very
thankful. I hope you are in the enjoyment of
perfect health, and will long remain so. I
learn by Tel from Phila that Mr Kendall is
better and has been out today. Hoping to see
you soon I remain yours friend as ever
Alfred Bail
All well here
If Mrs V is not in N.Y. I shall go to Morristown but visit the city the next
day
