Washington April 20th 1846.
Dear Sir, Your of the 15th inst. from Hartford I have
just received with its enclosure from M. Vail of 20 dollars
I have no disposition to charge you with intentional errors, in this matter, but I feel strongly displeased that my name should be inconvenientably used to give currency, which, they who doubt me
the paper knew to be false, and with which they reproach me as the author. I cannot conceive how you could have supported yourself an agent of a U.S. Telegraph Company, merely because M. Vail one of the proprietors of the Telegraphs had requested you to be his Agent for selling some of his books. The other Proprietors have nothing to do with M. Vails books, and surely you were not "Agent by any appointment" temporarily at least
You have done wrong, and have injured me, in the estimation of those whose good opinion I value, by your thoughtless speech. The manner in which you have used my name. I do not like the style of your conduct a character that conflicts with all my notions of high and noble men, they are of the most brilliant intellect, but have dignity and belong to the scientific talent
to say anything on this matter of taste
