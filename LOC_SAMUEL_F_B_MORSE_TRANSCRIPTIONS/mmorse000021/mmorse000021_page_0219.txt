Batt. June 13th 1846.
Sir
Having the honor to submit a sketch
of a machine I have devised for the purpose of cut-
ting into strips the paper used for the Telegraph, I think
it will cut fast and lose no paper in the operation.
The mode of operation is this, A Roll of paper is put
in its place as at R, the end is then carried through the
rollers C, C, & after passing the knives K an inch or two
(they being previously adjusted) they are let down upon it,
it is then drawn as far as the farther set of spokes, the
end is then cut & the strips attached in due order to the
spokes of the naked shaft, if it is desirable to have but a small
hole in the roll.
The machine is then ready for operation and may be
moved by a crank attached to either set of spokes. I
judged it best to place the spokes alternately one beyond the
other that the strips may not interfere with each other
& that each roll may be easier regulated should they get
loose, or wind crooked or any other accident befall them.
I think this machine will cut the paper smooth &
without waste, and wind it tight and even.
I know not what experience you possess for effecting this
object but should the above meet your wishes better
I trust I may not to forgetter in its application.
Yours respectfully
J. F. B. Post Exp.
Saml Martin
17 Youth Entrench
Batt.
