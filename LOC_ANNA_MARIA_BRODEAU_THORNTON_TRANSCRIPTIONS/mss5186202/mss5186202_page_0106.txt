There is a man bringing over Col. Burt who makes
a great project, to buy his ships about this
time at $12 or $15 a head, he keeps them
up, takes profits, stores them in the harbor
& again on the opening of their takes them to
market & sells them at 70 or 75 for prime
meat thus paying the prime cost of food by
the mail & which he sells to the public
& buys it in worth & a dollar is formed
& my letter was too late for the post.
Sunday 12th 1861 about 9 o'clock and
under Col. Burt's command I went to
the office of Governor Davis & to the
office of Judge Duffey, Mr. Henderson & Campbell
and Lee. — a strong part of our military
town stood up & was very warm.
Monday 5th very warm but a fine air —
12 o'clock I left the office of Judge Duffey his
room & leave who arrived last night & sat
there after breakfasting to visit the wall — 12 miles
round — Read worked slept played
at chess — from 10 to 12
the gentlemen did not return till 10 o'clock
Tuesday 9th — I left Judge Duffey's office went to
town & returned to dinner also to Judge
Duffey — I left Judge Duffey's office to visit
the gentlemen & go down to the neighborhood —
Mr. Duffey & Col. Burt said I should think it more
or less complete & Anderson came in the evening
& played at chess & Mr. Anderson went to my room
Wednesday 10th Mr. Duffey's office went to dinner with
me & the gentlemen — I bought some better clothing
at Mr. Campbell's store to present to his family
& Mr. Duffey's children. — Bought my suit of
clothes with me & the evening returned for the gentlemen
to Mr. Duffey's office & I have brought the ladies
of Washington into the country, where they people
highly respect his memory, & many have
subscribed for the work — 20 in the little town
of Charlotte (including 8 Women).
— a guest & 8 women from Grinnell
where we had been treated with much kindness
& went to sleep at Mr. Duffey's — Saw one of
this trip Duffey.
Thursday 11th — The carriage was ready for us
by 4 o'clock but it was too damp & ready to go
so soon & did not set off till after 5 o'clock.
