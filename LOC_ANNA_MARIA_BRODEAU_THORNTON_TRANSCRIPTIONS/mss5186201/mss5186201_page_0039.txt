The eclipses of this year are all noted in
the Calendar, against the days on which they
will occur: the only one, that of the sun, visible
at Philadelphia, will happen thirty-six minutes
before setting, on the memorable 4th of July,
about the time when the Declaration of Independence
will be read. The greatest obscuration will be 1 1/2
degrees on the sun's fourth limb.—Such an occurrence on a
feastive day, in the dark ages of superstition,
would have occasioned no final inquisition; but
at this critical period, the people of Philadelphia
contemplating the regular return of a natural
event.—It may indeed suggest a wish; and it is
not unworthy the Christian and patriot to cherish
such a wish: "That the independence, liberty and
liberality of the United States may not be
scoured; but continue to shine, until solar and
lunar eclipses, and all the vicissitudes of time
shall be no more."
