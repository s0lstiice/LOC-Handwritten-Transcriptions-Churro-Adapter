John Brodcar in Latin (Brodens, a peculiar
name, on which Lepers, Balzac, Groutus
and all the learned have bestowed great
curiosum) was descended from a noble
family in France, & born at Tours in 1804
He was liberally educated, and placed under
that to study the civil law, but soon
found himself that, he gave himself up entirely
to the study of languages & the better letters. He
travelled into Italy, where he became acquainted
with Schubert, Mendelssohn, &c. other
famous artists, and here (says Thurneys) he
applied himself to the study of mathematics
& philosophy, &c. The various languages in
which he made no small proficiency, then
attaining to his own country, he has a talent
that not in idle lips as his many learned
commentaries abundantly testify. He was even
free from all ambition of princely dignity
offered his works to be published rather
under the sanction & authority of others
than under his own. The chief works are
1. a commentary on the Rhetoricon. 2. Ten
books of Miscellanies. 3. Notes on Spenser's
prose. 4. The end in 1863 aged 60.
