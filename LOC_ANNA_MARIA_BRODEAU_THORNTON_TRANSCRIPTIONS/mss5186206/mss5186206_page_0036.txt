(38)
On my lady Isabella playing on the Lute.
Even moving winds, from which a careless touch
Abandonment herself, & we so much!
What art is this, that with so little point
Transported thus by our spirits reigns?
The trembling strings about her fingers round,
And tell their joy for every note aloud.
Small force there needs to make them tremble so;
Touch'd by their hand, who would not tremble too?
Then love takes stand, and while she charms the ear,
Emphasizes power on the listening door:
Music is oftenness in discern the mind,
That not an arrow does resistance find.
Sins the fair spirit relieves the prize,
And sets herself the triumph of mercy;
For innocence with harp in hand, survey'
His flaming flame, & as it burned he played.
Walter
(39)
On a Lady's presenting a sprig of Myrtle to a
Gentleman.
What hopes what desires does thy gift create?
Amidst various problems of uncertain fate!
Our Myrtle, ensign of supreme command
(Conquered by Venus to Matlifons hand)
Not less capricious than a reigning fair
Of seasons, oft rejects the lover's care.
In Myrtle's heart of change the happy warrior
In Myrtle's charles desiring plaints complain
That Myrtle's heart the happy love breaks
And the happy lover quenches Myrtle's spreads
Oh! then the meaning of thy gift impart
And cure the throbbings of an anxious heart:
Thou must this bough, as you shall fix his doom
From Philistines' hand or grace, his Lord
Johnson.
