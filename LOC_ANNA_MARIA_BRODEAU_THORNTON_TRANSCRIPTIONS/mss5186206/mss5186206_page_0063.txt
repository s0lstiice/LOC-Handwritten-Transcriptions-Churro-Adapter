Montpelier
The seat of James Madison, (now Secretary of State), is situated in Orange
County Virginia, 5 miles from Orange Court House on
one of the ridges forming a part of the chain called the
Fourth Neck mountains -- it is in a wild and romantic
country, very generally wooded with flourishing trees,
such as Walnut, Ashnut, Mulberry, Pine, & - (though)
(though) owing to the hard soil, it has been in use for
many years -- one gentleman held, 10000 acres --
The house originally built by Mr Madison's father, but enlarged
& improved by himself, is situated upwards of 20 feet
high with a handsome portico of the Tuscan order, composed
of four large columns, the whole forming a plain
but grand appearance, rendered more pleasing by displaying
a view not often to be met with in such remote situations.
The house is on a height commanding an extensive view
of the blue ridge, thus from the house & appearance alone
to enircle the place -- by the contrast variation of the
clouds, and consequently of the shapes and appearances
of the mountains, sometimes appearing very near, sometimes
separated and distant, sometimes very distant & distant
lines, and when the fog arises between them, like rolling
waves, the view is rendered very varied and interesting.

Monticello
The seat of Thomas Jefferson (President of the United States)
is situated in Albemarle County in Virginia. The House
stands planted in the summit of a conical mountain, on a
numerous level, formed by art, commands a very grand, numerous
& extensive view. -- The small town of
Charlottesville, and a little winding river called the
James River with a view of the Blue Ridge & even more distant
mountains, form a beautiful ground on the north side
of the house -- On the West there is high mountain country
with woods, and on the other sides a varied appearance of
mountains and distant country. -- The house is of brick,
but in an unfinished state, the commenced 27 years
ago; the ground plan is a very good one, & the elevation
very
interesting.
