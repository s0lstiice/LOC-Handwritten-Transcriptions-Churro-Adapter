Extrait of a Letter from Mr. E. Montagu to
Mr. E. Carter - 1701
Our young Merchant (George B.) gives us
the fairest hopes of a happy rise. There is a becoming
V. dispute in his character that cannot be expected
at his years, modesty & firmness mixed, religious sentiments
& a moral conduct unblemished, application
to business, affability to every one; no bias to any
party or faction, sound & serious judgment
in his conversation, & an elevation of thought
& tenderness of sentiment. There hardly passes a day
in which one does not hear of something he has said
or done, which proves one's opinion of his understanding
& heart: "I am sure you will be
pleased with what the King said upon hearing a
flattering sermon from Dr. Wilson, one of the Court
Chaplains: This Majesty desired the Chaplains might
be told that he came to Church to hear God praise
not himself?"
High compliment to the English Character
