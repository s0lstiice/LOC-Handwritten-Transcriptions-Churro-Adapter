Bangal in a Storm
Written on board the Ship India, off the Cape
of Your Hope in May 1803 by Gen. L. G. Gipps.
Blow, loudly blow, ye hoisting Storms,
Ye firing elements combine,
Spume your most terrific forms,
Afflail the firmest hearts, yet mine,
Shed dread and dreadful by its roar,
Can mock your rage ye tempests blow!
Rise threatening, rise, ye whirling waves,
In liquid mountains dark and deep,
That fill with foam, and with foam-encircled graves,
And fight with fate the happy throng,
But with your feet the living ships
I turn your power, ye billows now!
Dart, rapid dart, ye murdering fires,
Invade the heaven in bound light,
Thrust death your winged blade inspired,
And nations shudder at your flight,
To furious flames indeed, my heart,
Defies your force, ye lightnings dart!

Blow winds, let thunders peal around,
And clouds rumbling with the roar;
My breast, attentive to the sound,
Is with the, through sympathy, to ease;
Turn by despairing griefs, my soul
Seeks relief, ye tempests how?
Sometime to St. Peter
Written at the death where he was finishing the
miniature of a Lady, by G. B. Gray.
If from oblivion's gulph to save,
The pledge to sacred friendship dear,
And enrich the remembrance from the grave,
Of those long and lifed to cheer.
If to the anxious Lord's eyes,
And to the pangè of absence given,
So well the object of his sight,
The soul's best hope, his earthly kenon!
If to preserve against shocks of time,
(That latest gift of many years)
The patriot in his task sublime;
The hero, amidst his native fire!
If under like thren can raise the artist's name,
With red desire, each stroke attests thy fame.
Pauline
