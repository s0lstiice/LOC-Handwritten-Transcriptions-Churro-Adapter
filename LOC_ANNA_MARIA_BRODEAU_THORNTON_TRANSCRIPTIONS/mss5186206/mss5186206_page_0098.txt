The Homes of England
Where's the Tower that would not dare
To fight for such a land?
The Stately Homes of England
How beautiful they stand!
And their tall ancestral trees
Over all the pleasant land.
The Dwarfs of their preeminent homes
The Shade of sunny Glenon
And the Swans glides past them with the Sound
Of some rippling Stream.
The Merry Homes of England!
Around their hearths they light,
What gladsome looks of homesick love
Meet in the kindly light!
There women's Voice flows forth in song,
Or children's tale is told.
Or Lights, more typically along
Some glorious page of Life.

The Blessed Homes of England!
Now safely on their towers
Is laid the holy quietness
That breathes from Sabbath-hours!
Solemn yet sweet the church-bells chime
Thatals the hour sounds at noon;
All other sounds in that still time,
Of breeze & leaf are done.
The Cottage Homes of England!
By thousands on her plains
They are smiling over the silver brooks,
And round the humble-faces
The glowing Orchard's fruit they press
Each from its work of leaves
And scatters there the lovely sheaf,
As the bird beneath their trees.
The free fair Homes of England!
Long, long in hut & hall,
May hearts of native race be heard
To guard each faithful Wall!
And bright the flowing brook
Where first the child's first heart loves
Its country & its God!
— Felician Homans
