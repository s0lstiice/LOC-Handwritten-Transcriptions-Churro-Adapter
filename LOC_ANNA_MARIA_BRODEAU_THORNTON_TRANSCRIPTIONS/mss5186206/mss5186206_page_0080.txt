"Such was the theme as it was seen from shore
Nothing more was ever heard of the Indian Ship's
port, nor were any of those on board ever after seen
The Sentinels who watched near Pâto, a tower on the
sea banks of the Marommah, found, on the following
day that the waves had washed on shore, some of
the wreck of a vessel -- this fitted up a few Islands
& a broken mast, around which, tangled with some
gits and ropes, was a white sail hawserchief, such
a one as had bound the trusses of cattle in the
night that she had embarked, & in its knot were
a few golden hairs.
She was never heard of more -- even her name finished
the sleep in the gray cavern of the Ocean; the sea waved
as tangled with her shining hair; & the spirits of
the deep wondered that the earth had united on
such a creature to the barren bosom of the sea,
such, as an evil ship-matter, deceives & belongs all
committed to her care.
Earth felt no change when she died; & men forgot
her; yet a toucher spirit never ceased to breathe
or has a toucher form ever distanced amidst the
masses of the ships' fleet, & end less years might well have
been shed at her life, yet for her none will save
the fathers ships, which departed the night before they
had themselves committed -- none mentioned except the
seabirds, that clapped their few wings above the
ocean banks whereon the cry -- "The mourning thunder"
always told her passing bill at the quarter's life,
which for her has been replaced with change & sorrow.
Valparaiso by McShellys
(daughter of Governor)
"Oh woman woman! the silvert & the most
engaging instrument in the hand of a wise Providence,
for accomplishing purpose of mercy --
when I, or any of those who were on this memorable
day returned from torment & death by this interpreter
fought thee, or cease to pray for thy happiness here
& hereafter, may we again be condemned & sufferings
such as we then endured."
Narrative of the Rev. Denzil Allen Battle from
the Irvingburg Magazine
