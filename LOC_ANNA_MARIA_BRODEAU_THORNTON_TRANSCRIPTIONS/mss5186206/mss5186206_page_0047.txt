(60)
Mr Egbert Irvine's address to his Wife.
Land of my life! - memorial of my praise!
The bright reward of all my toil, and days!
After unnumbered sleeps in Paris' streets,
The part in which my shipwrecked hopes were laid,
Whence my youth and pleasant's wound injoyed,
Home to my auring soul, fill'd the void:
To thee alone feeling heart & judgement shall
give thee of France's services to speak;
So that I declare these remonstrating days,
That noble they smiles beyond a monarch's eyes!
For on our lips the imperial quiet shines,
And still the sun which lit it, seems to shine:
Embrass'd is our union with a smiling boy,
And then still united one sovereign eye.
The shades of love's bitterness what we feel -
To modern couples vain were the appeal!

The human joys are gone on the wing,
Though small the scope of life's enchant'd ring;
The time advances with a courser pace,
And still must rob the of some charm or grace;
No lightest ungrateful ear wills out,
The use no optics but what love supplies!
Who lies in this believing a perfect side,
Still each to each, the bridegroom & the bride.
This Oldam looked when from the garden drive
And thus disquiet orders sent from Heaven,
Like him I go, but yet to go am I.
Like him I go for angels drew me there
That was his fate, but mine still more unknown
The one soul with him but mine stays behind.
