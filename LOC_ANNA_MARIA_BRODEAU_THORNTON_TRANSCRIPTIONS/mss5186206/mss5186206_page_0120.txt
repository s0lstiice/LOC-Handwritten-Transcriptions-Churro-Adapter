Lines by Robert Southey
The days of infancy are all a dream,
Those fair, but oh! how short they seem
For Life's sweet opening spring;
The days of youth and man
The morning sun to the distant plains
The bidding clouds they bring,
It is Life's burning Summer time.
Many hard, maligned in it, without fault
Reasons of learning, respect, parentage
Succeed, as Autumn follows Summer's prime
And thus is that calm year by
And what remains the languid bye
The failing flame, the soul direct,
The Winter's gathering, withering blast
Lives its final season—for it is the last.—

From a very old Manuscript or
a gentleman walking in the snow
I saw fair Chloës walk alone
When Feather'd rain came softly down
And Jenni descended from his Tower
To court her in a place shown
The wanderer's snows flew to her breast
Like little birds with their nest
And overcome with solitude there
For grief it thaws into a tear
These falling on her garments humble
To deck her figure with a German
