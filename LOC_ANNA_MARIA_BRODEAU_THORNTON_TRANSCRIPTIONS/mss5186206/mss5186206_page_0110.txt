Monument to four Great Men
In a small private chapel in Bristol, there is a
marble tablet, on which there is the following inscript-
ions, to the memory of four of the greatest friends of
humanity that perhaps ever lived. It was written by
a late worthy individual, John Birtle, on hearing of
the Nelson's victory off Trafalgar.
John Hawes — Jenni Hawes
John Fothergill — Richard Reynolds
"Not enter us, O Lord! but with thy name be
the glory.
"Beneath some ample hallowed Dome,
The Virgin's knees are laid,
And blazed on the Patriotic Tomb,
His martial Deeds displayed;
Beneath a humble roof we place
This monumental Tomb
To name it from shall our lips
And charity shall own
Is often human foe their care
So feel its light to aid its prayer;
Their work in earth not to destroy
And their reward, their Master's joy."

"In China the mourning is white, in Egypt
yellow, in Ethiopia brown, in Turkey blue and white;
with us it is black, white being the principal
color. Indeed all these colors appear to be alter-
native; for black being a principle of light or purity
the darkness of the grave, white denotes purity, it
is typical of the souls being freed from the bonds of
the body; thus the color of the sky, dignifies the dead;
perhaps we hope the deceased enjoys in heaven;
yellow alludes to the leaves of the trees, which become
of that color when they fall to the ground; brown
denotes the earth, whether the dead return, and
purple expresses both sorrow & joy; being a mixture
of black & blue."
Lips & G. L. Munroge
