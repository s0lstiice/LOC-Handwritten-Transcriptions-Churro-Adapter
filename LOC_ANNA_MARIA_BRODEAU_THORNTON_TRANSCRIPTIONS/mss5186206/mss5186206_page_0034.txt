(35) The following verses were hung on the boughs of a
venerable Walnut Tree which overshadows the burial
ground of the celebrated poet Walter in Bannock-
field Churchyard
by M. Stuart.
Stronger, of virtue, or of verse, be dear,
With pious caution pay thy debt here,
Planted by him, whose sacred dust has twin'd
Twice of thy presence underthine my shrine,
Protector of the hollow'd spot I Island,
In guard this vault from each unhallowed hand,
Save then each union that can fix the tomb,
A part of Walter feeds my resident bloom,
Oh! spare each leaf that bows the pious grave,
For in each leaf a part of him you save,
And on the fruits which clustering round me grow,
A more than mighty destiny observes:
Taste, but with reverence kneeling at the shrine,
Remember thou eat, as Walter's in thine
A second Tree of Knowledge may I be,
The philosopher's Wisdom mine in thee.
