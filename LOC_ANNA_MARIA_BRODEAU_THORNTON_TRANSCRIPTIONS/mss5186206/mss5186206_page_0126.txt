Extracts from Mad Roland's Appeal to
Impartial Justice published in 1793.
Napoleon Bonaparte
"It cannot be denied that the Catholic Religion though
little suited to a sound judgment and an enlightened
mind, that subjects its faith to the sole influence of
its priests, is well calculated to captivate the imagination, which is
always delighted by means of the grand & terrific, while at
the same time it occupies the mind of its votaries with
romances, alternately soothing & melancholy. Eternity,
always present to the mind of its sectaries, calls them
to contemplation. It renders them scrupulous approva-
lators of good & evil, while its daily practices of useful
rites serve both to keep up the attention & offer the easy
means of advancing towards the end proposed. Women
are wonderful adopters in giving a grace to those prac-
tices, & in accompanying rites with whatever can add
to their charms & splendor -- an art in which they
particularily excel."
(P. 46.)
"The first thing that shocked me in that religion, which I
professed with the firmness of a child, is its consistent
use of the universal damnation of all those by whom it is
denied, or to whom it has remained unknown. When, in-
structed by history, I had well considered the extent of the
earth, the succession of ages, the progress of empires, the
virtues & the errors of so many nations, I perceived weakness,
absurdity & impurity, on the idea of the creator who intends
to eternal torment those immemorial beings, the frail
works of his hands, cast on the earth in the midst of so
many perils, & lost in a night of ignorance, from which
they have already had so much to suffer. I am deceived
in this article. From my reading, it is evident: and I regret
my error in some other! Let me examine: -- From the
moment a Catholic has arrived at this stage of reasoning
he is lost forever to the Church. I easily conceive
why priests require a blind submission & preach up so
threnously that religion faith which admits without
