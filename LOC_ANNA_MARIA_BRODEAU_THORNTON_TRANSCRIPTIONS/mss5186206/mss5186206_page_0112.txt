Lines
by the Countess of Blessington
Though grief may sometimes leave the weary breast
And grant a respite of a moment's rest,
The but returns with renewed power
And springs each moment abreast with an hour
What causes does memory call to mind?
Dark'd in a trembling or a sombre hue!
Now gay as when life's current fills the breast,
But yet we know what grief it is to part,
Now sad as when death struck the fatal blow,
And bow'd us to the very earth in sore
The fondly loved, the lost, the wretched appear
Affection turns still thither upon the ear,
And seem to ask "Can't their forget, I should,
While in the grave we daily sleep the whole?"
Ah me! the grief may leave the weary breast,
And grant the respite of an moment's rest,
The but returns with renewed power
And pays each moment's absence with an hour!—

The days of thy mourning shall be ended
Oh! respect not for the joys that have
Like evening lights away;
To hopes that like the stars decayed
Thou left thy mortal eye;
To clouds of sorrow will depart
And brilliant stars to give;
And the in earth the tear may drink,
Yet bless await the holy heart
Around the bowers of Heaven!
Oh! respect not for the friends that pass
Into the lovesome Grave
As beeses sweep the withered grass
Along the pathless way;
To the thy pleasures may depart
And darkness lays to give;
And lovely the in earth them out
Yet bless awaits the holy hearts
When friends' rejoin in Heaven.
