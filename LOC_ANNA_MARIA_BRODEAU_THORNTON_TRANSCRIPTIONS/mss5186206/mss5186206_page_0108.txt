Old Portly's points are on the rack;
And see you rocks, how wide their flight
They emulate the gliding kite,
Or seem precipitate to fall
As if they felt the pinning ball;
Now restless are the moving swine
The busy fly disturbs the time
Low over the grass the swarming wings,
The crickets on how loud the night
Bugs on the hearth, with quiet sounds
Its ripening ear her whistled guns;
That surely vain, I see with sound
Our joint tumult be put off tomorrow.

Varies written by Mrs Delany in 1784, when
she was eighty four years of age.
"The time is come I can no more
The vegetable world explain;
No more with capture call each flower
That points the need of time's the bearer;
No more with admiration see
The perfect form & symmetry;
No more attempt with hope relate
The lovely lines to imitate.
For well to all those active powers
That blest my solitary hours.
Atlas! furnish but the shell I know
As one who is of hope falter'd?
Come help spirit on thy way
The ended consolation bring;
Teach me to contemplate the grace
That hath so long sustained my race
That various blessings still bestow
And pains in labour to all my worst.
Teach me submission to resign
When summoned by thy will divine"
