Epitaph on a Lady, who died of a Consumption at Bristol Wells. — by her Husband
Whose love, like mine, with trembling anguish brings
This heart to white banners to four Bristol springs,
Whither like me, to woe, silence and pain,
I shall pour three valiant springs in vain;
Condemn like me, to hear the faint reply,
To mark the fading check the winging eye
From the chilled bosom to cope the drops of Death,
And watch in dumb despair the startling Death,
If chance directs him to this earth of Love!
Let the sad mourner know his anguish remains,
Endeared to love the partner of my breast,
Whose virtue warned me, & whose beauty electrified every sin, that unite the soul, to prove,
Her duly friendship, and her friendship love —
But yet remembering that the parting sigh,
Appoints the path to Chamber, not to Heaven
The startling law I beheld, I held the Rose
And not to Earth resign her — but to God.
