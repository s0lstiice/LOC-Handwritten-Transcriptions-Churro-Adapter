The following was written by Ibn Alharni, a Syrian
by birth; he died in the year of the Regime 253.
"The first verse or passage on Idra, afterwards
borrowed by Lord Byron, in his poem 'Jane the Empress'
But the Arabic is the finer of the two. --
In a Lady's writing
When I beheld thy blue eye shine
Through the bright hair that pily drew
I saw beneath thee tower of thine
A blue eyed violet bethed in dew.
The violet ever scents the Gale,
Its hues adorn the fairest wreath,
But sweetest th' eye's dewy veil
Its colors glow, its hues breathe.
And thus thy charms in brightness shine,
When wit & pleasure round thee play,
When with its smiling in thine eyes,
Who but admires their sprightly ray?
But when th' eye's flood thy glance
Who but must love th' honest beam?
American 2d Edition 1829

Saw W.T. Valor wandering together over the
Emerald Isles, everywhere discover the fatal.
Says Valor See
They spring for me,
Those lovely graces of morning!
Says love Rose,
For me they grow,
My fragrant path adorning!
But W.T. perceives
The triple leaves
And cries he is not sever,
A type that bleeds
