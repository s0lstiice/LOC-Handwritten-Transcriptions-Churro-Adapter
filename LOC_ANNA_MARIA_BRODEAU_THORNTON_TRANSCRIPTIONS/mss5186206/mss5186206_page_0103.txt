Burial Vault at Kalorama
where Susan Smith is temporarily deposited
by Mrs. J. Harrison Smith - Fortune March 1832
Ye graves beneath your leafy shade
Where many flowers grow
Four lovely ones by Death are laid
Again in heaven to blow
Winter may wither, frost destroy
The flowers so frail, so fair
But spring returns with breath of joy
They ravage to repair
Shall hope & joy again return
And clothe the fields & trees?
Then active hearts no longer mourn
Life shall return to those.
A life no wintery wind can blast
No cancer can infect
Not long this gloomy scene can last
Look upward & be blest.
(175)
Yet strangely has the course of time
Brought here these lovely ones
From distant homes & different climes
To lie beneath their stones
Strangers in life, yet here they meet
As children of one birth
And lay in their last dreamless sleep
Cradled in mother earth.
Mary springs fresh & fairest flowers
Adorned thy natual bay;
Mary! thy early childhood's hours
Beamed bright in fortune's day.
The beautiful the angel bloom
How rapidly it passed!
Yet sooner did misfortune's gloom
Thy happy youth der cast.
Bereft of fortune, friends & home
Died night remain for thee
Oh yes the quiet of this tomb
And bright eternity.
* Miss Mary Scott
