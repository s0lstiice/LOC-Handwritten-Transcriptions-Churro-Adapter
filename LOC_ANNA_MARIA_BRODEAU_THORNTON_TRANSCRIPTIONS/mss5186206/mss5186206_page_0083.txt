"Not a Drum was heard upon the Death of Sir John Moore (in Spain)
attacked by Lord Byron, was written by the Rev. Charles Wolffe F.B.-- who died in
1823 -- in the 32nd year of his age of Consumption."
"Not a Drum was heard nor a funeral Note
As his brave soul to the rampart no hurried
Not a soldier discharged a farewell Shot
Over the grave, where our hero we buried.
We buried him darkly, at dead of night,
The vox with our bayonets turning
By the struggling green branc's misty light
And the lantern dimly burning.
Neckless coffin encased his breast
Not in steel nor in bronze we bound him,
But he lay like a Warrior taking his rest
With his martial Cloak around him.
But half of our heavy Task was done
When the Clock till the hour striking,
(135)
And we heard the distant & random Gun
That the foe was Suddenly firing --
Slowly & sadly we laid him down,
From the fields of his fame fresh and green,
We could not a line, we raised not a Stone,
But we left him alone with his Glory."
Oh, who could bear Life's stormy storm
Did not thy wing of love
Come lightly wafting thro' the gloom
Our peace-branched from above?
Then Sirens, touched by their, green's bright,
With more than rapture's ray;
As darkness shows us worlds of light
We never saw by day."
Moore
