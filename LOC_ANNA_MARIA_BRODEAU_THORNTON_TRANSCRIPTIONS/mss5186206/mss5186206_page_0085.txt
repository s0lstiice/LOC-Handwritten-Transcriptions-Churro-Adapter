Thus star by star declines
Till all are passed away;
As morning high & higher shines
To pure & perfect day;
Or sink those Stars in empty night,
They hide themselves in heaven's own light.
James Montgomery
— vain beast! another day may not be given
This Song may be my last; for I have reached
That stupery descent, when man looks back
With melancholy joy on all he has lost;
Seeming with love unsatisfied, on all his loosing;
Forwards with hope that triumphs while it turns
To the dim point where all our knowledge ends.
I am but one among the living; one
Among the Dead I soon shall be; I am
Among innumerable Millions yet unborn;
The sum of Adam's mortal Progeny,
From Nature's birthday day to her dissolution,
— Lost in infinitude, my atom-life
Seems but a sparkles of the smaller star
Around the sun's illuminations of ten thousand
Thrinking and uniformly; no ray returning
To shine a second moment, where it shone
Once, & no more forever; so I pass.
The world grows darker, fainter & more silent,
As I go down into the vale of years;
In the graves shadowy lengthen in advance,
And the grave's loneliness appeals my spirit,
And the grave's silence sinks into my heart,
Till I forget existence in the thought
Of co-existence, buried for a while
In the still sepulchre of my mind;
Thus imperishable: Ah! that soon,
Like the Archangel's trumpet, wakes me up
To death's help resurrection: Heaven & earth
Shall pass away, but that which stands within me
Must think for ever; that which feels must feel:
I am, and I can never cease to be.
