Four Songs of the Congo Indians — translated from
their language into English, at Washington Jan 1806
by D. Samuel Mitchell of New-York.
a Song on Friendship occasioned by the Visit made
in Decr 1805 by the Deputies of their Nation to the
President of the United States, at the seat of Govern-
ment. My Connoisseur brave if friends of note!
you hither came from lands remote,
To see your grand exalted Sire
and his sagacious words admire.
The Master of your life & breath, &
diverse accidents and Death,
That you might such a sight behold,
In spite of hunger, fear and cold.
Ye Red Men! since ye here have been
Your great White Father you have seen
Who cheered his Children with his voice
And made their beating hearts rejoice.

Then Chief Osage I fear not to come,
and leave while they explain home;
The path me passed is clear & free
and wider & smoother grows for thee
When I desire to march, then feelist inclined,
Will form a lengthening file behind —
And dauntless step from our Fathers walk
To hear our Great white Father's talk.
*The aboriginal name for the Supreme Being.
A Song on War, being one of those employed
to rende the Spirit of the Nation to hostile Deeds.
Say Warriors, why when arms are sung,
And sounds from every martial tongue,
Do thoughts of death intrude?
Why sweep the common lot of all?
Why fancy you yourselves many fall
Rushing or pursued?
