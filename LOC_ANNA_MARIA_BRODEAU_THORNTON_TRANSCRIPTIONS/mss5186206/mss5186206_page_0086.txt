Oh then that prudent! take this parable
Home to thy bosom; think as I have thought;
And feel as I have felt, through all the changes,
Which time, life, death, the world's great actor
wrought in,
While centuries swept like morning dreams before me
And then shall find this moral to my song;
— Their art, & then cannot ever cease to be;
What then are time, life, death, the world to thee?
I may not answer; ask Eternity.

Oh the emptiness of a bed chamber from which your
friend has just departed! The pillow still bears my help
of the bed which had rested on it so recently; the curtain
scattered on the floor, which you had gathered in a despair
hour, to give fragrance to the new vacant wardrobe; the
back of the letter interwined with a name that now ships your
utterance; & the thousand other trifles, light as air, that
take affection by its price, & make me melted this
very fibre of the frame!
Blueocking Hall
In vain may art the couch of piety, tend
Or friendship sigh, or sympathy implor
Or love all sanguine for the suffering band
The mortal sunk — & even hope is o'er!
These brooding thoughts in purple, panger expire
More soothing sounds let struggling nature hear
Catch from religious shame an holy fire
And awake to duty from her trance, sleep.
Yet still there remains, on the death bed stand
Still born as thorn could it the healthiest clay
Still bring thy flowers, & clothe with leaves round
And wish behind the tree in their way;
And give the stone, inscribe the record kind
And all thy hearts vain tenderness reveal
And guard the dust, careful hope resigned
And bow to heaven, that found thee thus to feel.
