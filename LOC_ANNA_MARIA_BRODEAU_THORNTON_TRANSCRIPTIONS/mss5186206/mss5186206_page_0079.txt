Philipps the Irish Barister — from
"The Boy — a prince with rules"
An Ordnance maker of fine character —
a sort of Bastard eloquence that reaches,
But never reaches beyond the doubtful line,
Which gentlemen do not quite mean to call ortho
and dealing diversifies, furnishes a home —
a garden picture, planted by displays,
But Smith's Court, from its colour marks,
Jumbles without figures to light or shades.
In search of metaphors, he finds him self
To summarize all about or beneath the Sky,
But like a lumberman, in his wild career
Beyond this visible diurnal sphere,
Thickens the earth and flack with a meteoric breath,
Or heartlong plunge into the earth's deep —
Then in an earthly thope, opens from
To dazzle & confuses the whirling train;
And after all this frenzied wild sport there
To vulgar down both out of time & place,
Still a mere model — painting for applause
And losing sight of nothing — but his Cause!
Lord Chancellor Eldon is also very fond of stitched
With just views judgment strong opinion
And charming temperament. Yet profane
Strumming on it takes its mighty course
Displays its secret treasures to their Source,
Then calmly rests with winding current down,
Like some vast late illuminated by the Sun
Where thru the winds of waves distant debris,
The hidden wonders of its depths appear!
Old age is honorable. The spirit remains
Arbiter on its flight to brighter worlds,
And that strange change which even in small decay
Is renovated life. The feeble voice
With which the soul attempts to speak its meanings,
Is like the Sky looks in mute, and faintest when
The wing seems highest; & those heavy signs,
These white & reverend locks, which move the steam
Of thought like so pibalds seem to me like clouds
Upon an Alpine summit, — only proving
How near it is to heaven. — Henry Neville
