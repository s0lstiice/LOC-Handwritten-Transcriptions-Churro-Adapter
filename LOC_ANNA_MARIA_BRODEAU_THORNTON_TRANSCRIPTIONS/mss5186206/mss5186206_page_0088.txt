Extracts from the Grave. — By Blair
The new-made Widow for five sometimes seven,
Eight nights! Slow moving over the restless Brain
Lists by the cracks along in hateful black,
While twits of sorrow gush from either eye
Fast falling down her now pale face, check
Proud on the lovely grave of the dear Man
She drops; while busy meditating mewing
In barbarous succession, ministers up
The past orderments of their Sister hour's
Tenacious of its Home. Still still she thinks
She sees him Vividizing, the fine thought
Clings yet more closely to the torridose turf
For hears the passenger who looks that way —
Indifferent Grave — has met them, and in sorrow
When love has knelt, & sympathy made one!
A bye one shudders far than nature's band. —
Abroad! to think to ever reach the Grave
And from the wreck of names, to resume ours!

The best concerted Schemes men lay for fame
Die fast away — only themselves die faster.
— Spirit is a serious thing to die my soul!
What a transformation must it be, when near
This journey's end then hast the gulf in view!
That awful gulph of no mortal ear refused
To tell what lies in the other side!
Nature runs back & shrinks in at the light
And every life-stirring blood at thoughts full of parting!
In part they must, body & soul must part;
Tend'emple, look & more close than middest pain
This wing's its way to its Almighty Tower
The witches of its actions, now its judge,
That reprints the dark & narrow graves,
Sighs a drunken pitcher of no use.
But if there is an hereafter
And that there is concurrence in the thrown
And suff'd to speak not, tells every man
Then must it be an awful thing to Die!
