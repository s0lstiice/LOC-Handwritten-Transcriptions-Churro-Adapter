A Sonnet by the Rev. Richard Dale, delivered to his infant Daughter Maria.
"My dear Babe, thou smiles on the tear
That hangs upon thy mother's smiling cheek;
Eager as thine heart would, her voice to speak.
But her heart swells with grief too full to speak.
For thy brothers in the land are dead.
Their hopes. Our own the country storm hath past.
And thine another rests his little head.
Thy sad feelings, but they feel not the keen blast.
On thine pale face lies the restless mind's unrest,
Unconscious of the tempest they inspire;
There, indistinct, sweet moments they sleep,
From human passions free, from human woes.
Yes dear Maria, they, my babe, are free.
From ills that wait, perhaps in time for thee.
Lines accompanying a letter to the chief returning to young Lady.
"Go," her chief to thy mistress back again,
Pure and unvaried as her virgin breast;
That thorn where all the noble virtues reign,
That seal of each exterior charm possess'd
When no soft breezes agitate the air
And sultry suns exhale the balmy dew,
Then partake wider air her father's fire,
Her nimble cheeks, & lips of coral hue.
No official blush shall then displace,
For thine thy surface with cosmetic art,
Nature's own roses bloom upon her cheek,
And nature's genuine feeling warms her heart.
Or may that heart ever burn with painful light
Or may thy gentle service in employ
Be safe the tear from those tender eyes
Be cast the true of overflowing joy!
With thanks.
