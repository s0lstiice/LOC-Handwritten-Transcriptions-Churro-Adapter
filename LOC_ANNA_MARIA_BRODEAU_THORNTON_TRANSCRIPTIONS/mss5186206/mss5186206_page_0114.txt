Tribute to an Album by
Wm. H. Porter.
The night-mare came to my silent bed
In the stillest hour of night
When at rest was laid my weary head
And the indescribable gnate
I think of the horrible shape it wore?
It was not a demon grim
Not a demon with scales & tails or horns
Not a head without a limb
Not a mocking friend with a maddening laugh
Not the whirling scaly spasm
Not a cup of blood for the lip to quaff
On spite of the sickening will
Not a monstrous bird with a furious note
Not the black dog on my breast
Not the ghost of Ruin with its grip on my throat
That came to disturb my rest
But my Sister pale with a grey, poor, guilt
And an album light of sorrow!
Get up the crib, & a long page fill
In this book must go back tomorrow!

Was an unwholesome Dream
If dream it was — a night-mare rather first
A stifling perfume composed in my heart;
On my dull ears, with thick & unfill'd eyes,
Beneath many a sound of battle & slight
Of tumult & distinguished cries; my mind,
This word have been the loudest, was untried
And seemed to swell the chambers of my brain
With volume out of sound I could not utter
The screams of wounded horses & the crash
Of broken planks, & then the heavy plunge
Of bodies in the water they were loud
But yet the sound that was confined in me
Had it got utterance, would have drowned all
But still it grew & swelled, & thinned out
The bitter thickened in my heart; my blood
That had been flowing freely from my wounds
Tickled, then ebbed, & then flowed more
My horse upon the barrier of the bridge
Tumbled; I started; I was wide awake
Was an unpleasing dream
Philip Van Artevelde
