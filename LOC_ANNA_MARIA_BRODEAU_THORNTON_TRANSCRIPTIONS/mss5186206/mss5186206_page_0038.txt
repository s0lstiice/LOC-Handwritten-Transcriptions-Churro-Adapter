A song by Dr. John Walcott.
Oh! faltering spell! beneath thy wing,
When every care no longer tolls,
Where helpless love forgets its sting,
And even despair in silence smiles;
I sit let me say my senses break,
My languid eye in silence close,
And rest beneath its friendly shade
Seated from the world of from my woes.
Fill'd with the lengthened sleep, I lie
With secret joy the minute part,
Am glad to bid the world retire,
And wish the present hour the end,
But to my arms the nymphs divine
Would sleep in mighty nations give,
Placid, sin in dreams, to think her mine
For ages wild, I wish to live.
(43)
Extract from Trugley's Triumph of Temper.
Hydroponus who saw the gentle fair
Lean to that cupids with peculiar care,
And at the tender interest she displayed,
And spoke regretful of the prince's mind
"Will misery thus lead us this conjugal sphere
In immobility or servitude here."
"But, sir, think you attentive to the plain,
"And mark the varying wonders of her region,"
As thus he spoke, she found her wings rent
High lies a plain whirling every week,
Carried by patients all the flowers that bloom
With the delicious air with perfume;
And in the midst a verdant throne affixed,
On simplicity from her peaceful fancy ward,
And decked with flowers, yet such whose glimmering eyes
Stirred with the Army's red tint and dazzling power,
But here I'd hide that tempest's fiery roar,
And shun the Approaches of a dangerous air.
