The Persian tree (Tours Indicus), sometimes
called The Pagoda Tree -- as given in Bentham's Botany --
M. Coursley has described it both in the spirit of a
poet & naturalist.
There is a fair chance wherein this stands
A green & sunny glade amid the woods,
And in the midst an aged Persian tree,
Now an ageless light tree,
That venerable tree,
On in the lawn, capriciously spread
Fifty straight columns past its lofty head;
And many a long depending shoot
Facing its stalks, its root
Heart like a plummet, grew towards the ground
Some on the lower branches, round & round,
With many a ring of rails entwined round;
Some to the palming wind at times, with sway
Of gentle motion swayed,
Others of younger growth, now overhung
Like their drifts from the caverns fitted light
Beneath the arches of fair light
No weed, no thorns deform the natural flow,
And thus the leafy apex which bow'd to air

Signs of Rain
Addressed by Dr Jenner, in 1810 to a Lady who asked
him if he thought he would rain tomorrow --
The hallowed winds begin to blow,
The clouds look blank, the globe is blue,
The dust falls down, the family sleep,
And others from their couches except
Last night the sun went pale to bed
The morning thaws hid in head,
The coming shepherd hears a sigh
In due a rainbow spans the sky;
The walls are damp, the ditches smell
Clo'd is the pink of the pineweed;
The spindles stand at dusk more thin
Slowly crawling on the green;
Loud quack the ducks, the peacocks cry
The distant hills are looking night
