The last darling -- by Mrs Fitzgibbons
She was my Idol -- Night I stay to see
The fine expansion of her form -- and mark
The unfettered mind like vermin rose but start
To sudden beauty, was my chief delight --
To find her fancy foot steps following,
Her hand upon my garments, or her lip
Close seals to mine -- I, on the watch of night
The quiet breath of innocence to feel
Soft on my cheek, was such a full content
Of happiness, as none but mother knew --
Her voice was like some tingling that yields
To the slight finger's freeze -- I, as it felt
Brief converse with her will -- or kindly spirits
Her morningitten -- or with patient care
Conversed on the alphabet -- but most of all
Her tender caress, under running prayer
Thro'ill on the ear like some Thérésa true
Thames in sweet dreams. But now alone I sit,
Musing of her, I dear with numerous tears
The little pokes that once with women's pride
I wrought -- as if there were a need to deck
A thing formed so beautiful. I shut
Half fancying from her empty crib there comes
A restless sound, I breathe the accustomed words
"Rest, rest, Sonnet, Serenit" -- then I weep
As tho' it were a sin to speak to one
Whose home is with the Angels.
- Gone to God --
And yet I wish I had not seen the pangs
That wrung her features, over the ghastly white
Settling around her lips. I wish that Heaven
Had taken its own, like some transplanted flower
In all its bloom & freshness --
Gone to God
Be still my heart! What can a mother's prayer
In all the wildest exarray of hope
Ask for its darling -- like the bliss of Heaven?
