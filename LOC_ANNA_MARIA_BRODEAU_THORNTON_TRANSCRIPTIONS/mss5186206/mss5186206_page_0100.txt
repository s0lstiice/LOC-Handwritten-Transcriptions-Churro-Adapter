On the Recitation of Palestine
Passage Poem by Reginald Heber in the Theatre
at Oxford, on the 15th June 1809.
By Miss Sophia Jennings
"None who heard Reginald Heber recite his Palestine
will ever forget his appearance. His father was among
the number when he first entered the Theatre; and
in the thunder of applause so short his frame, so weak
by law, utterly that he never recovered, may be said to
have died of the joy devoted to a parent's heart.
He had been the busy man, now once, now second
to the vast concerns which the moment near
to deep, to holy silence breathed around,
And with attention for the listening ear.
When from the Theatre burst the ballad's strain
And Heber, breathing with poetic fire
Struck into the gaping, expectant train,
And spoke to eloquence his sacred lyre.
The youthful student with emphatic tone
(The latter subject on his mind impressed)
With grave and energy martial theme,
And round devotion in each thought left breast.

The song of Palestine—that holy land,
Where saints and martyrs, & the warrior brave,
The Cross in triumph planting on its throne,
Beneath its banners sought a glorious grave.
The drum of cavalry, of his father's drum,
Of the red mercies of returning love
When thus the crowd spontaneously planted its hands,
Breathing a satisfaction above
What means that step he set, that grown of joy?
Why fall those tears upon the funeral clock?
The aged father hears his darling boy,
And sees his tears alone his feelings speak.
From his full heart the tide ofaphrodisiac flow,
In vain to stem its rapid course he tries;
He hears the applauding thongs, the solemn clasp,
And sinking, from excess of joy, he dies!
