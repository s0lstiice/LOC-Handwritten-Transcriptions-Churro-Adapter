Verces written by Charles Wolfe (the King's) 112
to the popular Irish King of Armagh.
If I had thought them could't have died
I might not weep for them
But I forget when by the fire
That them could't mortal be,
If none there my mind had passed,
The time would ever be over
And I on thee should look my last
And then should I smile no more.
And still upon that face I look
And think I will, smile again!
And still the thought I will not look
That I must look in vain!
But when I speak - them will not say
What them never left at my side
And now I feel as well I may
Sweet many times not send!
(133)
If there would't stay, even as them art
All cold & all serene
I still might press thy silent heart
And where thy smiles have been
While I on thy chill black course I have
Then seemed still mine own
But there I lay thee in thy grave
And I am now alone.
I do not think where'er are them art
Then hast forgotten me;
And I, perhaps, may soothe this heart
In thinking too of thee,
Yet there was found thee such a reason
Of light never seen before
At fancy never could have drawn,
And never can restore!
The was asked whether he had any real incident
in view, or had whetted any consideration which prompted
these lines - He said he had not; but that he had sung
the air over & over till he burst into tears, in which
mode he composed the words.
