The Mother's Farewell to her widow'd Daughter.
Go, dearest one, my selfish love
Shall never part the cheek
Not even mother's feet for thee
While I on widow's speech
With human's on child'sness check
The humming tears that start
That then not turn from me to deth
Within another heart!
I think on earlier brighter days
When first my lips were first
Upon thy babe's brow, whilst thou
Lay helpless in my breast;
In fancy still I see those eyes
Upheld in one fire
I hear thy lapping tones I mark
With thy tiny childish grace.
Even then I knew it would be thus,
I thought then in that hour
Another world its perfume steal
When I had passed the grave,
And yet I will not breathe a sigh
Now can I dare refuse
The scream that thy mother feels
Was suffered once by mine
A Mother's love! oh! then knowest not
How much of feeling lies
In those sweet words, the hopes of years
The daily strengthening ties
It loves to see yet the infant drawns
Its earliest vital breath
And dies but when the mother's heart
Chills in the grasp of Death.
Will he in whose fond arms thy sick
Prune all of earthly things,
Be feel a love returning, deep,
And free from self as this!
Am no husband's tenderness
My gentle heart may prove?
But never, never with them meet
Again a mother's love!
My love for thee must ever be
Find as in years gone by
While to thy heart I shall be like
A dream of memory.
Dearest farewell, many angel hosts
Thy visits on the keep,
Now can I speak that painful word
Farewell, I yet not weep!
