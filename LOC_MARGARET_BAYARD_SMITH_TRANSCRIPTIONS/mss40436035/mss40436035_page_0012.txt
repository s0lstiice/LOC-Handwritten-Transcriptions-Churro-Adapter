Sidney May 11th 1836
Once more dear sister do I date my
letter from dear Sidney -- How much more like home
can it feel than the city of Sydney, the society is
now changing -- here, my friends they know a friend is killed,
a friend is dead fast & unchangeable, & being separated
with the severest recollections of many, many
years I feel united to them by the closest bonds of
sympathy, I have with them a companionship which
if I did not feel, I never should imagine exist between
the heart & inexorable nature -- But for the first
week I felt sad & lonely without Anna, whom I left
at Kalarama -- but because of her absence, but the occasion
of that absence -- I went for Julia & when she came was
saved from that childish & disloyal feeling -- Her situation
awakened a more than usual degree of tenderness --
indeed we both felt, we had never loved each other
so perfectly before -- She was so full of activity, so
crazy to spend me, that I could not make her sit
quiet, & Mrs. Drake who was with me, advised me
not to restrain her from her usual exercise -- The first
week she was so perfectly well as she was happy, but
one day when I let her ride into the city she went
about all the morning in the hot sun -- stuck with some
other independent foolish consequences which disapponted
my fond hopes of having a grand manner -- She was taken
ill the evening day -- but soon recovered -- I would not however
