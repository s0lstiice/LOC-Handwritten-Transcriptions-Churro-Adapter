Christmas day (on Monday) 1835
(2 o’clock)
The clouds may break — the winds may sigh
E’en, absent the sun is enlivening rays,
Yet light lie thy heart — and dimmit in thy eye,
And merry pass, thy Christmas day.
was my greeting this morning to Mrs Taylor, when I sent her
a bowl of Egg-nog, in return for the kind wishes a pretty caime
she sent to Anna Mania — lines, which being just dispatched to
her, ran quickly off my pen, when I took it up dear Victor
to write to you — but they came not from the heart warmly
& purely, as the wishes which arise when I think of you —
Oh no — I say not a merry Christmas — but a placid — happy
day — far more congenial & acceptable than moment to
either you or myself. The day is dull & stormy — but
Contentment that sunshine of the breast, illuminates our dear domestic
circle & defies the power of dark doubts or contrary winds toadden
us. I have just finished making up my Christmas-gifts & distributing
them to such as were present. The happy faces of my servants &
children are the most precious & acceptable of all presents to me.
— And now, since the weather will secure me from the
interruption of visitors, I will enjoy my holiday in writing to my
Wife — (my heart says, Mother) & my daughter — I had seated myself
last Thursday to write, calculating that my letter would reach
you on Christmas eve — but I had only arranged my notes &
paper, when Mr Bompford’s carriage drove to the door, with a
request that I would come to Kalaranna, as she & Henry (her
younger son) were very unwell — my Christmas letter has been
therefore deferred to this day, which I view as a holiday, & yet I hope
for the company of some of our friends this evening — though I fear
the elders from Kalaranna, not being able to come — It is an occasion
when we are in only friends — Miss Thornton & her family — Mr Van Vail — Mrs
Bompford’s family — Cousin Dayard, Mrs Wood, Mrs Thirgill & Mrs Shepley, none to have
formed our social party — How many will leave the weather, we know
not — but one pretty sure of the gentlemen — so that our former Egg-nog
will not be lost.— Poor Mr Wood, however will be an exception — he is
in wretched health — I do not believe you would know — So thin, pale,
hollow-eyed! Yet his countenance is uncharged — it still beams with
cheer & spirit — his voice, one gentle
lower-sudder,
