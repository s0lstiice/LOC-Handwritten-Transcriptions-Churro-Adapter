Anna is now better than she has been for
many months. The air is perfumed with the
scent of thousands of flowers in the unending foliage
of trees, & it seems to me most perfect a healthy
& balgamic quality, peculiarly calculated to benefit
the lungs. Still, she is very delicate & feeble, a little
too much excitement of pleasure or occupation or exercise
brings on fever & a sinking or fretfulness of strength.
The two dear sisters enter Maria require the same
watchful care, & attention to Elizabeth & I am continually
cautioning & restraining our invalids -- "Now, my
Mama, sit out of the air -- "do Anna, mama, sit
down, you have walked too long on the prairie -- "you
had better not eat that --" you had better not speak
that -- or do this -- or do that" -- are words in
pretty constant use between her & myself -- for both our
patients, are impatient of restraint, & have spirits
which carry them beyond their strength -- I had no
idea that Maria was so seriously indisposed. In her letter
she had spoken only of a thorn-like affection of the arm-
bat, that local complaint afflicts her in a slight affair, &c
referred to the cause whatever it is, that affects her
whole system -- the nervous system, it seems to me, is in
