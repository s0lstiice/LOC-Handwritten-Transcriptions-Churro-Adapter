He has with him would be the appearance of the most skillful & ingenious in gauging my opinions, & I will write to you, the beginning of this month, Anna remains so entirely free from any of her usual symptoms of distress, that I am convinced to hope that they have quite ceased. I had not before slept of her perfect restoration to health. But the circumstances, many of which have been continually laid before me, have convinced me, as to the necessity of her remaining at home, for some time. I had given my consent, to her urgent solicitations of her friend Cousin Bayard, that she should accompany her to Boston & prefer herself for this pleasant journey commenced -- but I am now so deeply affected at her recovery, that I cannot even contemplate her return to her home without sorrow. I had hoped to have obtained her consent to remain at home, but this last affection has been decided, I believe -- a regular improvement. I am now, however, quite sure that she will remain at home, and I am now resolved to give her consent to remain at home. I have heard of her being in great distress. She has now suffered from chills & fever -- has been confined in her bed -- has been incontinent of urine at present, but I doubt not that she will soon be free from this. Her physician prescribes travelling on the Continent for her, as a remedy, but I doubt not that she will not recover; in the meantime her lungs, are not strengthened -- but that the greatest caution is necessary to preserve them from any danger.
