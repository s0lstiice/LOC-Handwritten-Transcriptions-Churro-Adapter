The Cobbler Ahmed of Isfahan.
In the great city of Isfahan, lived Ahmed
the cobbler, an honest and industriious man,
whose wish was to pass through life quietly; and
he might have done so, had he not married a
handsome wife, who, though she had contem-
nated to accept of him as a husband, was far from
being contented with his humble sphere of life.
Iltitara, such was the name of Ahmed's wife,
was ever formerly foolish, whimisical, sullen and
grudging; and though Ahmed never encouraged
them, he was too fond of his husband to quarrel
with what gave him pleasure; an incredulous
smile or shake of the head, was his only answer
to her often told daydreams; and she continued
to persuade herself, that she was certainly destined
to great fortune.
"It happened one evening, while in this
temper of mind, that she went to the hammam
where she saw a lady returning dressed in a
magnificent robe, dressed with jewels, and
surrounded by slaves. This was the very condition
Iltitara had always longed for, and she eagerly
inquired the name of the happy person who
had so many attendants and such fine jewels.
The learned, it was the wife of the chief shoemaker to the king. With this information she returned home. Her husband met her at the door, but was received with a frown; no words
of complaint were exchanged, for several hours he continued silent, and in apparent misery; at length she said:
"Dear your husband, unless you are ready to give me a proof that you do really and sincerely love me."
"What proof of love, exclaimed poor Ahmed, can you desire, which I will not give!"
"Give over nothing; it is vile, low trade, and
never gives more than ten or twelve dinars a day.
Some enter the service; some fortune will be made, and I will have all I wish, and be happy."
Enterloger! and Ahmed, enterloger! were you
forgotten who I am -- a cobbler without any learning?
