The Race of Liberty
Dear Sirs, I know, then Summer Bird,
In sublime announcing voice is heard,
In existence wild and inspiring sweet,
Of Winter's stern approach the last!
Aray! for arms, damp and low,
The mountains round the mountains bow;
And Tempest clouds their narrow fold
Round the faithful sentinel pole!
Bring! away, the earth and sea,
This land is now no home for thee!
Fire and Death thy waiting wing,
And such deliverance the shades of Spring!
The wanderer now, with pennies spat,
For the brightest dream's has fled,
In cast the broken heart, nor proves,
In those dear graves where shades he leans,
Why should he grieve, the beam he loves
Thins on them still when in the arms,
And all those early friends are near,
Who made his humble home so dear?
Oh! damn not that the tie of blood
Endeavors us to this spot of earth;
For wherever our steps may roam,
If friends are near, that place is home!
To matter where our fate may guide us!
If those we love are still besides us!
- PRAYER -
Father! I should have blessed thy name
In pure and uncorrupted prayer,
But when my eyes were raised above,
And while such lips have asked thy love,
My foolish heart has worshipped here.
Father! while in thy house I pray,
Oh may that heart be wholly thine;
There will its faith, and hope and love,
Like grateful incense rise above,
And worship wholly at thy shrine.
S. F. B.
Washington, 14th March 1829
