The United House.
By Matthew
From whom they have heard,
Whence they have received with much
Reason is on the bridge formed,
If they will plant this pennant.
The shadow of departed hours
Hangs like a fence upon these early flowers;
Even in this sunshine comes to break
Another flame deep than solitude.
Far out there, I can see a trumpet gone,
Some one sweet home of that way!
My children's breath alone yet for me
Is too much to look on thee.
For much I fear all about thee spread,
I feel the pressure of the air,
And almost longer for the foot
That never more may step shall meet.
The lock, the switch, all must'd a man,
Tell me where they put thee;
The echo of kind household words
Do cast me amidst the dying birds.
Tell my heart dies, it dies away
For memories for what night, not day;
For how which we're returning my hand,
For all which went with "Just I doubt."
What now is left me, but to raise
From thee, leave part of my spirit gone,
To left, though I leave, thy smiling eye
Left to my Fisher's house on high.
Oh! many are the mansions there,
But not in one both such a thorn!
No haunting shade given things gone by,
They have destroyed the unchanging sky.
And they are there, those long loved scenes
In earthly home no more at seen;
