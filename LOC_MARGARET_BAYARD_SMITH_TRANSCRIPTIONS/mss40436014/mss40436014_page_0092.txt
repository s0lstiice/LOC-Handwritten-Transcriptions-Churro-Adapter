On
The buried vault of Reformation
He graves beneath the solemn leafy shade,
Where many noble flowers grow,
From lovely ones, by death are laid
Again in Heaven to flower.
Winter may wither Frost destroy
The red flowers of faith and love,
But spring returns with breath of joy,
The ravage to repair.
Shall life a joy, again return?
And shall the old fields and trees
Then acting, furnish an eternal,
Life that return to those.
A life no worthy wind can blast,
No canticle can infect,
Not long thus gloomy, soon shall end!
Look upwards and be blest.
Yet strange is that the course of time,
Brought here these lovely ones,
From distant home -- different climes,
To lie beneath these stones.
They passed on life -- yet here they meet
The children of one birth --
And lay on their last decamstage sleep
Trampled on mother earth.
May springs fresh and fragrant flowers
Blamed thy fatal day --
May I thy early childhood hours
Beaten & fraught in fortune's ray.
The beautiful, the vocal bloom,
How rapidly it dies!
Yet come -- dear and faithful is bloom
They happy, gentle seed!
Though of fortune, friends and home,
But night remain for thee.
