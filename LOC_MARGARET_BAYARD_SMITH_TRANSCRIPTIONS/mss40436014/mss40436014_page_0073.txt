This speech was here interrupted by a man
singer from the King, who, having heard of the
whole his fame, desired his attendance. Sir
Thomson now concluded that it was all over
with him, and followed the King's messenger,
promising to do that he would deliver him from
this peril. When he came into the King's presence he
bent his body to the ground, and wished
his majesty long life and prosperity. Tell me,
Thomson, said the King, who has often my
happiness?
"It was not one man; answered Thomson,
after some consideration; there were forty
thieves concealed in the other."
"Let well be with the King; but who were they?
and what have they done with my gold and
jewels?"
"These questions," said Thomson, "I cannot now
answer; but I hope to satisfy your majesty, if you
will grant me forty days to make my escape from
Scotland; grant you forty days," said the King, "but when
they are past, if my treasure is not found, your
life shall pay for the forfeit."
Sir Thomson returned to his house with a heart,
for he resolved to take advantage of the time
allowed him to fly from a city where his
fortune was likely to be his ruin.
"Well," Thomson said to his wife, as he entered,
"what news at court?"
"No news at all," said he, "except that I am to be
put to death at the end of forty days, unless I
find forty chests of gold and jewels, which
have been stolen from the royal treasury."
"But you will discover the thieves."
