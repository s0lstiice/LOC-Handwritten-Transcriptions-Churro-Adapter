The Death Tree
He watched her breathing through the night,
Her breathing soft and low,
In her bed, the house of life,
Kept hearing to and fro.
He silently or seemed to speak
So slowly, never aloud,
He gave her half one power,
To be then living out.
Our very hopes rested on hers,
Two years our hopes behind
We thought of her dying when she slept,
And sleeping when she died.
For when the moon came down and we,
Had chill with early showers,
The quiet outside closed the dead
Burial more than ours.
(Sydney, 1892)
