The noon of Life?—What is this
The brightest hour are lusted with and
Is with the soul a season of fire,
Of life and rapture youth can know!
The noon time leaves day to more brighte,
And more serene the evening time
But give me morning's budding light,
With beauty's gentle and sparkling sun!
I saw a lovely flower unfold
In gay leaves to the sunny ray!
In Eastern breeze low, sweet and cold,
And all its beauty passed away!
This was a fair young face that betha a
Beloved one in the flowering tide;
Or dream arose—the lightening scatters
Its foliage in the hour of spicar!
There stood an aged oak whose boughs do
Bear sheltered men from sun and rain,
Though many a summer day, but now
He would scarce remain in the plain
And such is man! a luminesce flower,
A capting or an aged tree;
In age, in youth, or childhood's hour,
Death passes by—"ana where is he?"
W.T.B.
