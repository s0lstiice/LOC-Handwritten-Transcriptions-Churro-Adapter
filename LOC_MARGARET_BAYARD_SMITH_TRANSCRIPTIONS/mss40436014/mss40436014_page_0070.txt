These prayers conveyed no joy to the poor
Miller, who returned home more than half
afraid for his preservation than satisfied by his
good fortune. The moment he entered the door,
his wife ran up to him and exclaimed,
"Will my dear alderman [that success!]
Thorn! and Thirned very poorly, there are
two hundred pieces of gold: I hope you will be
satisfied now, and will ask her again to hazard
my life, as I have done this morning. He
then related all that had passed. But the
rental made a very different impression
on the lady from what their unanimous had
made on Thirred. Sittera saw nothing but
the gold, which would enable her to live with
the chief alderman's wife at the turnamen.
Beware! she said, courage! any second instead.
This is only your first lesson in your new and
useful profession. Go on, and prosper; and we
shall become rich and happy."
In vain Thirred remonstrated, an represented
the danger; she bust into tears, and warned him
if not loving her, enduring with her awful
threat of visiting upon a devotee.
Thirred's heart melted, and he agreed to make
another trial. Accordingly, most unanswerable
talks with his stable, his tender regard
of the religion, and his eloquence, explaining,
as before, I am an alderman! I know the sun,
the moon, the stars, & the twelve signs of
the zodiac; I am a complete mathematician;
I am for all every thing that is to happen! I stand
again gathered round him; but it was not
with wonder, and not with ridicule; for the
king of the city had now gone abroad, and
the once of fame had converted the poor old
thirred into the oldest and most learned
alderman that was ever seen at Philadelphia.
While every body was gathering at home, a lady
passed by, built. She was too the wife of one of
the richest merchants in the city, and had
just been at the turnamen, where she had
lost a valuable necklace and ear-rings.
