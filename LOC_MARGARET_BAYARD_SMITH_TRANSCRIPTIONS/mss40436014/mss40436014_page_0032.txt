The death of the Republicans.
Sweet is the scene when Brutus dies,
When with a righteous soul he rests:
Now with a dying eye
Slowly leaves the aspiring head!
It fades a dimness about away:
It marks the gate where stars are in,
And gently shuts the eye of day,
And dies there, never along the shore.
Innumerable miles the water flows,
Farmed by some angel people amidst
O grave! Where is thy resting ground?
Inaudible Death! where is thy drug?
That thy quiet sleeps around;
A calm which nothing can destroy:
Nothing can disturb the peace profound,
Which thine unfettered soul enjoys.
Time well conflicting hopes and fears,
Where rights and shades alternate dwell!
But night unchanging sorrow appears!
But well, in constant watchfulness!
The duty done, as sinks the day,
Light from its load the spirit flies:
While heaven and earth continue to say,
"Sweet is the scene when Brutus dies."
February, May 17, 1829.
"To day is out, yesterday is past, and tomorrow
may never come! The past marks the change
of hours, every night brings deathlike sleep,
and tomorrow seems a resurrection; yet amidst
all changes I decay, I expect no alteration,
except to live, already to die, we lose the
present I seek the future, ask much for what
we have not, I thank Providence but little
for what we have!"
(Mary E. Montague)
Washington, 1829.
