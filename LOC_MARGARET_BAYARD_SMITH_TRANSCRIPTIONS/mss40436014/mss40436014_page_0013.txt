Written by the Rev. Leigh Buchanan in his 12th year.
In the morning
While the earth is clost in after gray,
And humming thro' partell the offices of day,
The knee that stands on the third post,
And early steps behind the wooden post,
In splendid majesty the morning star
Becomes known, on her course.
The dark, the early clouds of the moon,
These slenderudes lift gentle flames above,
Thus from the moon above all heaven is light,
And to the deer sublime the bend of the light.
Her pleasure under the ambient hills is sweet,
And day, or half the world commends its art,
The splendour from the celestial light appears,
And nature whispers away her day's tears.
—Ferney April, 1831—
The accepted choice.
"Why wouldst thou leave me, oh gentle child!
My home on the mountain top is cold and wild,
I have suffered when with love I dwell,
Now is a fair and a pleasant hill,
Where many an image of mother gleams
And the sunshine of picture for ever shines."
"Oh! green is the turf where my brothers play,
Through the long bright hours of the summer day,
They fond the red cup of wine where they drink,
And they leave the bee on the painted thorn,
And the rocks where the heath flower blooms they scan,
Today, kind lady, do let me go."
"Entire they, they in my home to dwell!
You are most innocent, which their love will;
Thine on the sun in the stilly noon,
Thine in which the wandering breeze blows,
And the silent words of many a bird,
These are our eyes wider in thy unknown love:
My mother sings, at the archbishops fall,
Of any of the hills for more sweet than all."
