Ann & I are much happier together than
we have been for more than a year past --
We have had a full explanation & acknowled-
ment of errors & sufferings, difficulties on both
sides & have promised to bear with each
other in infinities. -- She is so much gayer
than she has been for some months past, that
it almost reconciles me to my disaffection.
I have not seen one of her family
since I last wrote & imagine all intercourse
must be broken off -- I feel such a sincere
affection for Mrs P. -- that I deeply regret
This -- She is truly a good & amiable woman
& was always a kind & affectionate friend to
you Mary Ann -- I will unite to her
when he should move me; if I unite between
my letter with her which I have written to her
& hopes a letter from her would inspire
me. -- I have received seven letters to and
from Beecher since I wrote you an interesting one by
Capit. Thirley, which must not be long neglected
Anna Maria is a great admirer of your
manns, she had heard his narrative & is
delighted to know him -- They are very good
friends & she sits for hours in his lap
has spent a long evening with us last weekend
but Mr Th. did not see him as he was at
Mr Coldwell's.
I am sorry Mrs P. has to stay
at Trenton before he sees you -- He will
forget a thousand things & wish him to
let you -- God grant he may be restored
to you in safety --
Adieu dear, dear sister -- quite
sorry to find most affectionate
Yr aff
Wm H S
