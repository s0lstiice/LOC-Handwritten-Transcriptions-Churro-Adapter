Mrs Smith.
I have just received your letter
my dear Margaret. a letter so long desired but now different
from your last! It makes me quite sad I am so anxious to
have my picture finished and exhibited by your permission
that I feel the contrast the more. Did I say how much I feel
for you in all your sufferings who have so been utterly useless
to me in some of your most poignant inquiries. Yet we have
some consolation and they are truly encouraging. It is not only
the gratification of a selfish ambition. But neither of us have
sought or indulged but our sufferings have arisen from attempting
the sweetest duties of humanity and in the course of fourteen
years we have therefore occasion to expect the blessing of God
even in the midst of difficulties. Take courage in that for
you will reap an ample reward. I am truly sorry for the
unpleasant dilemma in which your time was placed. I admire
her resolution in entertaining herself and thinking as Lady
Married. I certainly commend both her wisdom and candour
in avoiding a connection which without a decided preference
to herself could only be productive of misery. It was natural
that her heart should be softened in the midst of such tender
and affecting scenes and it was hardly to be wondered at
