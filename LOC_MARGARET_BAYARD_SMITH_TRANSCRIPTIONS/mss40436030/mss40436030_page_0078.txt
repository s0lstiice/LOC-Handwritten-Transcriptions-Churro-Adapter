Sunday 28-1718
Mr Smith received your letter the same day
I sent mine. How little did I imagine my devoted
son's letters would be to me, that our poor Mary Ann was
so confined to her bed. I expect my letter would
have to be sent after you to Brunswick, as I felt almost certain you would by that time be more
settled at home. I hope I happily ameliorate your dear
family circle - I was so hoping for Mary's health,
that her recovery would have been rapid I feel
immense regret that it is not so - and even too she
is now sick, how little, did I think when I parted
with you, of the anxiety that awaited you, a
how contrary to all your expectations, to all
your hopes has been the result of this visit to
Boston - I only have been the gainer by it.
When I think of the exhilaration I expect to be
in hopes which gladdens our meetings, how closely
I control with our sad parting so long I
imagine you have since had - for more than two
months you have been confined to a sick chamber
watching night to day over objects so dear to
you, that I believe you have suffered more than
they have - I am impatient to hear of news of
my two dear daughters - and most anxiously await
by enquiry the moment Mrs Smith enters her
house, an letter from Phila? If it is possible
to intimate your wife of times - two nights
2 days more have been while Mary was at her
work - Her image was not a modest about from
my mind - the most rep. & funny idea caught me awake
& companions was my companion from the
watchers of the night. - I am so constantly & unvariously
occupied with the day, that I do not
think of much of you at all. How have
you been? I am a very great intereste by
the pleasure from hearing from you, as I am not
what a day may bring forth.
