March 8th 1917.
Does human life thus, afford no pleasure
without its attendant pain? Do all our joys flow from
the same source as our sorrows? Always one person
is lost to the other? Yes, it is so, we must
pay sometimes deadly pay for human happiness.
Dear Mary - She is gone - a never have
I passed with any inmate, with regret with
which I have parted from her. I am very useful
to write to you this morning, for not weeks
before, her Department has quite overt me.
It is impossible to be more amiable than
she has been - a ten so gay! The House
will seem very dull without her - She was the
very spirit of an evening circle - her manhood
had thinned, which I knew at the Company round
her - She has been very much admired, &
very much attended to - she was always surrounded
by more beauts than any other girl. I lost one night
one of her new admirers, and notice put in
2 second as if he could not leave her. This
was Mr Talbert from Kentucky a Senator who has
for the last month been deserving of an introduction.
The most serious impression she made was on
Ed. Johnson & his attendants have been such,
that every body has called him her lover &
even believed they were engaged - a St. Louis
thinker, if Mary had fancied him, this would
with your permission have been her case.
