[1877] Decr. 28, Sunday morning
at last my dearest sister, your long looked for
letter has arrived, I received it yesterday, but too
late to answer if by the next post -- I had desired
only to give you from the daily expectation of this
letter & at last became very uneasy at your
protracted silence. I have thought much very
much of you & Mary & Anna especially during
the very dangerous nights of the last week,
which I have passed watching over our precious
little Anna -- She has been constantly for
ten days past, but since Monday has been most
somewhat ill -- Her complaint, the doctor at
first supposed it to be the scarlet fever &
ultimatly one-throat, but it has not turned
out the scarlet fever, but one attendant
on the ulcerated throat 2 months. It is
the sixth day since her fever set in & this is
obligatory nimity, it has never gone off at any
request Dr May yesterday given her some panacels
& she have not been able to excite
the least perspiration. I trust these conditions,
The whole of Thursday & Thursday night, I had
occasly very hopes of her recovery; Her sounds
never varied & by she did not forget the long expected
Christmas eve a ill as she was, she said
"Mama, dont forget Lawrence & Mary Ann, put
up their stockings; during the night she talks
much on her Uncle -- this night as she said
was about the stockings -- I sat on the bed
all night long, holding her hand, & trying to console
her not knowing & what a sad night it was --
She was in the morning bad, had a chokc, succeeded
by a fever & lay in one bed in my room, mostly
to sleepless, Julia on the floor, to be ready to
send her assistance, & my little
