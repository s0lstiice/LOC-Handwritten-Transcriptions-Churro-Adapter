April 12 1847
I am indebted for letters to Manassas & Manassas
to my dear Sister, I have been vexing myself to
answer first, but as I think it probable Missin will
be able to tell you, 2 as Ann is so good in correspondence
of many, I will devote this money to you. I have intended
the moment breakfast was over to have returned to
my room, I should then have been able to write more
than one -- but finding myself tied to talk with
My father, I embraced the opportunity of a little confidential
talk with him -- I have been affraid from the remarks
I drop, I often saw, that he was living beyond his
income a spoke on the subject -- but he assures me this
is not the case, I told me that Mr S. had never mentioned
to me, that his salary was increased 150 dollars --
he begged him very earnestly to lay by the additional
money to confine himself to his 600$ per annum --
he said he would if he could -- he opens me
up in juristical in his payments to Mr Calomel --
He spoke largely of the uncommon affection & honor which
he is treated by Mrs & Mrs Calomel, & his
affection in that family. Some Mary, went, I seeme
earlier to me -- He is now the only relation
that I have excepting those beneath our house -- I
was in before he would never omit a meal
supper his Saturday evening, 2 Sundays with us -- he has
been out but twice since we came -- but now the
weather is more pleasant, I hope he will come often --
He said I shall not wish him to stay over night,
as Jonathan writes as word he will soon be here, I
have but one room, & Mary knows why I
should not wish them to lodge in the same room --
It leads to a degree of intimacy which, I shall endeavor
to prevent -- I must own I was in hopes he had given
up his intention of returning, for he never wrote a single
