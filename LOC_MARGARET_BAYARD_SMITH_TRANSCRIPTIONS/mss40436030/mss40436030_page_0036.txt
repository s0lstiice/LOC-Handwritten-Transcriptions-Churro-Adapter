Julia has no character of her own, she will always
be like those she associates with, she is careless, reckless
& gay -- but capable of serious application -- I trust
she will make an amiable & useful domestic character,
she will be in spite of fortune. I like Mrs
Lipton less -- a note the power of realizing a fortune, I
think not be surprised if in two years she is as poor
as ever.
I look forward with regret to the time that will take up
dear Mary from me. Her conduct to me is mere, has it
all that I could endure & attack. She has confided to me ever
since I have had some long & confidential conversations, in
which I have put her on my sympathies, sorrow, desire,
anxiety, which I could not cannot to paper, but
of which she will freely speak to you -- I converse with
her as a friend of the same age & almost fancy
it is with you I speak -- Mary will return to you, with
a heart as free from love as she brought with her, but unsuppressed
with sentiment of friendship which I hope will last as long as
it will with my father's words contain more. Adieu dear sister
Miller --
