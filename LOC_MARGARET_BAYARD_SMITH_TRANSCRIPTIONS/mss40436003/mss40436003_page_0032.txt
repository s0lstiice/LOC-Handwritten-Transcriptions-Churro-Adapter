Sunday 24th
They all went to church as usual, it was my turn to stay at
home. Mrs. Bedford, Miss Baldwin came in the
afternoon & stayed to tea - Miss
B. is quite pretty & very pleasing.
Monday
I was busy serving first of the
morning duties & then Susan
went to the City. I read aloud
in the afternoon. Captain and
James Dougkty spent the eve-
ning with us.
Tuesday
Susan & I in the morning went
over to see old Mrs Williams
& paid her quite a long visit.
De Bollins & one more clergyman
Mr Smith, Mr. Bernard & Mr
R. Smith spent the afternoon
& part of the evening with us.
Wednesday
A very fine day, I went in, to stay
a day or two with Helen -- In
the morning I went to see Julia
Seaton & G. Hindsley. In the
afternoon Helen & I went to see
the Randolphs we returned home
to tea. (Thursday 28th)
The City was in a great hustle
& confusion, occasioned by
the celebration of the late
French revolution. All the
military of the City & George Town
turned out -- there was a very
long procession -- in which a
great number of the citizens
heads of department, President,
Diplomatics & we joined --
Mr W. Jones delivered an Oration
from the Eastern Portico of the
Capitol -- there was a Ball in the
evening.
