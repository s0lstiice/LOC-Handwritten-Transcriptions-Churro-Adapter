The sun his white mantle shrouding in heaven
The garden trees in shadows and green,
His hand his roundel light outlining even
The distant tree-tops and meadow between
The combings of the wood in rock and stern
The narrow figures —
He entered in his house no more his home,
Of things to human feeling the most poignant
And hatred for the land to scorn him,
Religion, then even the mental power of living,
To find our heart-stones turned into stones,
And poured its own warm innocent laughter
The ashes of our bones is a dead grave,
Beyond a simple gentlemen's belief.
His interest in his home lies home no more
For without heart there is no home; and self
The solitude of passing his own door
