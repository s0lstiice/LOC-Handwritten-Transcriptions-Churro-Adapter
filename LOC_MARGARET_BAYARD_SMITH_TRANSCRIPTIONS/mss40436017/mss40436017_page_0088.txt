It is a well known maxim in
the forming of juvenile minds, that
the instruction which is communicated
by mere constraint, makes a stiff &
feeble impression, but when once you
have inspired the mind with a love
for its object, the science & the progress
is entirely changed. The use and use of
mind which earnest desire imparts
doubles our intellectual activity.
As surely comes as fondness with
increased wealth towards our goals
as the expectation of happiness
can the desire be excited. From
my own experience of the human mind
acquired by the care & instruction of
children, I should say it is not
a desire for knowledge -- is a constitut-
ent part of the mind -- it is
born with us -- a gift of nature.
I do not mean a capacity for
the acquisition of knowledge -- or
I mean a desire for it -- the first
may exist without the latter -- I have
tried to find every means in
my power to shew it -- I cannot -- vanity
is created by want of pure amusement -- in
death -- if not given by nature -- it could not be given
