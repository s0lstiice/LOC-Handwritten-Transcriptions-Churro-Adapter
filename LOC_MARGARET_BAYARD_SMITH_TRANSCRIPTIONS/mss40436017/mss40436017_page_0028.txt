When the engagement had nearly reached the
fortifications one of the brothers urged the military
to accept his counsel in one direction, which he would
attain the attention of the enemy, by destroying
them their advantage and endangering to destroy as many
of them as possible in an anticipated revenge for the
route which he considered as inevitable, but one of both
of us said he would not consent to sacrifice our people
if you will, I will be the victim, and may fortunately
receive a death blow in the conflict and thus escape
the danger of captivity. The Pennant rushed forth
amongst the Indians, shot one, and with his own
handes wounded several before he was dispatched.
This brother considering himself in the abstacted
attentions of the enemy affected his escape but the
spirit was turned to death. In this magnanimous
self-devotion the gallant brother exhibited an instance
of Christian heroism which would have immortalized
a human warrior.

Extracts from Gen Washington's letters
to
Sir John Sinclair.
The more I am acquainted with the agricultural
and affairs, the better I am pleased with
them; and much that I see no where
find so great satisfaction as in these improvements
and useful pursuits. In indulging these
feelings I am led to reflect how much more
helpful to an understanding mind is
the task of making improvements on the
earth, than all the vain glory which
can be acquired from ravaging it, by the
most uninterrupted course of conquests. The
desire of this observation is only to show
how much as a member of human society
I feel myself obliged, by your labour to
render respectable and advantageous an employment
which is more congenial to the natural
dispositions of mankind than any other.
