Sidney - Septr 18-1920-
I regret my dearest sister to hear from Sydney
2 letters that you are so much better - I have
indeed had a most tedious confinement but with prosper-
tionably enjoy your restoration to freedom & health -
I have been hoping for a longer answer to my very
long letter, than the sage you annexed to Mrs's letter
but this expectation has not kept me silent - a
very large family, few servants & oppressive hot
weather, has for a long while induced me for any
part of the most necessary employment. - The last
three weeks have been to me a period more
try to my mind than body, & I have at times felt
almost unable to meet up with the painless
spirit - I have seldom experienced weather so
overpowering & oppressive, the atmosphere was heavy
& humid & produced such extreme languor & debility
that one could scarcely move. After the heavy
rains in August the air became very unhealthy
most of our acquaintances left it - Mrs & Mrs
Calhoun & her children took Josephson of Dr Bradley
farm house, where they have been ever since -
Mrs Bamford & her sick children, with two other families
left Josephson of the vacant house at Charleston.
But the country otherwise he came richly to join
all parts me hear of the unhealthiness of the season -
All these circumstances added to Susan's apprehensions
of our climate, & her anxiety about Phila made her sick -
Her disease was more of the mind than body - her nervous
system irritable in the greatest degree - she could not
sleep or let other sleep - she would not
take pills or fever - but she had neither - I sent for a physician
to at once for an cure for fever & for the rest of nervous
irritability & treated her as a child - Her illness might
be considered as the sole cause of fever & gave her patients
These had the desired effect & procured her rest & cured
her imagined intermittent - But not her nervous complaints,

as the one you advised. The morning we visited Mrs
