Sidney, March 4th. 1820
This rainy day, is propitious to my wishes. It has
kept away a comfortable company & left me to that
quiet & retirement which I so value. The gloom of
these is apt to displace my thoughts; but when he
lies light as a Sunday day, is added to the stillness
of my own chamber, I can best think, best read &
best write. I have read & one read & read my dearest
friend your last sweet & precious letter – How perfect
it got to know one, better methods than I know my
self – You always told me something which I seemed to
have told you & most truly describe my feelings –
You, I am indeed better in mind & body for the
retirement in which I now live. Joy, strength & awful
decisive ambition – vanity – are dangerous companions,
& even in the high fulfilled tide of prosperity, and
often mutilated by disappointment, their gratification
is sweeter – and the magnification of such disappointments
has no counterpart, without gratification of necessity
is attended with much misery & alloy. Too often have
experienced the truth of both these opinions, & in
moments of sober reflection have acknowledged, that
rather the know, & pleasure of life could satisfy
the immortal soul – But still, they have a charm
which affords no end to pursuit, & at the constant
inapprehended in the pursuit – As for the more
measurements of high life & fashionable society, they have
never any attraction for me – but as it concerns
this world only, that I could meet with characters
of high celebrity, the most distinguished actions or
the lips of great heroes – found the most nicely and
a manner the most polished – It satisfied a charm
for me, which no other scenes can – If I knew
myself, it is he lip of this sort of society only, I suppose
that as you observe the more entwined I twisted from
the circle, the more entwined and into the
reclusion of domestic life, and I must acknowledge there
is a pleasure, a self-indulgence complacency which
accompanies the performance of duty, which is more
satisfactory, than any of the pleasures of which
profound love my dearest sister, when I have you, I
can feel more grateful to Providence, for having withdrawn me from the world, than if it had ravaged me
with the deepest distinctions. I know myself no well, as to
know that it is but a safety for me.
