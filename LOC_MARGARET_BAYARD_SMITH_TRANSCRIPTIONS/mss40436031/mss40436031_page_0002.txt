I have just dismissed my two little pupils, for I have
resumed my old business of school keeping 2 trust as I
have now good health & spirits a more experience, &
shall do better than formerly. I felt very sorry to take
Anna away from school, where she was making
rapid progress; but she is so apt, & so diligent that
she will not I hope lose what she has gained. I
never expect to send her again – Bayard I hope will
again go, when spring returns. It is here my dear
sister I shall most painfully feel our altered circumstances,
but if his father thinks as I do – no sacrifice at home
will be too great to in order to give him the ad-
vantage of a good education. Mr Smiths office closed
with the year & I do not think there is the least prospect
for four years at least of his having any other.
I have not to be sane yet made the experiment, but
I think one can be happy without society. The great
difficulty is not to resist its pleasures, but to refuse
the mind attentions of friends, & still greater to resist
inviting friends to ones house. Already this winter,
have many acquaintances & friends from other places
been here, & it was impossible, at least I found it
impossible not to ask them to be here – our square
chamber has been in pretty constant use for some
weeks past. Mr Hodge & Mr Smith from Philadelphia
have stayed & lodged with us. I saw Caroline Bayard
has arrived – he went immediately to see her, &
the girls & myself are very much pleased with –
The testifies great affection for me & interest in the
girls – Mrs McLean with whom she came on, is a charming
– in woman, & seems inclined to be as intimate as Caroline.
