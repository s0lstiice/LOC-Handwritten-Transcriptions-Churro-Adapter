January 1st 1826.
Another year has gone -- &c &c how like a dream it seems to appear! I have been reviewing its events -- I have been recalling the hopes & fears wishes & anxieties, many of which have been fulfilled, & many of which have been disappointed -- the expectations have failed yet I persevere, clearly percipient in all that has befallen me -- the goodness of my God -- the tender mercies of my Heavenly Father -- and so deep is the conviction that I require that his will be not mine, from every event of my life -- then, I am perfectly thankful for disappointment, believing that often our own wishes give us not our wish, & thinking it more than probable this would not have been my case -- Oh how often do I repent that sweet sacrifice I made of my religion, when all my mercies of my God, my saving soul surveys, transported into the new I am lost, I wonder how I came to be so -- All my life has been a life of misery -- and yet how little want, how cold, has been my cause to be so poor a divine person? What then, is shortening necessary to make us love a father? I would fervor answer in the negative, but when I see the effects of application on the human mind when I see that prosperity is a gladness, happiness the heart, that sorrow & suffering distresses it, I must believe the charitable Lord chastens whom he loves, because it is necessary for their spiritual well fare -- How strange it is my dear Sister that a heart of any generosity or tenderness should be insensible to goodness -- I will give to severity -- I have often condemned on this, & I loathed myself otherwise for my ingratitude & insensibility to the mercies of my Heavenly Father -- It is not that I forget him -- I have not -- it is my sole duty to feel his constant presence & to recognise his providence. It is delightful to feel that I am led by him to another life, of which this is but the infancy -- The commencement -- Yet when I compare these feelings with the deep & fervid elevations others feel as if under totally incompatible circumstances -- I am utterly disappointed, I have lost all my hope & all my joy & at such moments have experienced that he was more sufficient for heart & soul, than any earthly object of affection or of hope -- But a sanguine temperament a natural elasticity of spirits, soon triumph over disappointment & a restless disposition all sadness,
