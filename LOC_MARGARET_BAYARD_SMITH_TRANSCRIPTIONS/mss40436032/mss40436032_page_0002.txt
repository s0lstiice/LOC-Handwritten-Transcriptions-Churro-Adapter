Thursday night, 13th January 1825
You must indeed my dear sister have been surprised,
at my allowing such a letter as yours last to remain so long
unanswered, I ought to have suspected any thing sooner than
such neglect - The very week after I received, I fully un-
sounded it - & what has become of my epistle in more
than I can imagine, I shall be sorry if it has fallen
into the hands of a stranger - for I wrote from the office
of a fall heat, what was meant only for years. It is
more than four weeks ago - as it was sundays there was
no convenience to the City Post office, I sent it to Bayard
to put into the College Post-office - which he assures
me he certainly did. - Since then I have been anxiously
expecting a letter from you, until I was most
agreeably surprised by the appearance of Bayard
& Lyttleton - It is a very long while since we have
had a pleasure so dear to us. Our 2 old, rich, noble
friend Prescillia, agreed to the black children of Jackson's
Post office Saturday in the 18th, left us Sunday, a come
back on Monday afternoon - Caroline Crawford came
back on Tuesday according to previous agreement and I returned
with her on Tuesday & here I have been ever since.
On Tuesday every one went to Mr Adams drawing room
& I did most possible to make the scene agreeable to
the genial gentlemen, by introducing them to our acquaintance
— the next morning they came here & Mr Norris
& Mr Crawford went with them to the Cabinet
& Mr C asked them here in the evening, but they preferred
going to the President's drawing room, much I think
to their loss for me had a most charming select party
very gay, entertaining & agreeable that I asked Mr Vernon
& Mr Hinkbrandt Real, both of them peculiarly interesting to
me -- we had several & a number are to whom I
should have been pleased to have introduced our
gentleman here was asked this evening to a small
gent Lafayette & had my young
gentlemen been here should have gone
in order to take them
