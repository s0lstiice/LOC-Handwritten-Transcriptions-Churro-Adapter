1812. —
This day in the Senate, my vote was distinctly at
peace with that of the House. The vote was connected with
the Mexican War. — I have previously told the Senate how that war was
in its natural birth — while engaged in the Mexican struggle my
heart was pierced to all parts. Now that I stand upon grounds for the ultimate
establishment of the Union, I cannot doubt that my
vote and vote for the holy cause, though a portion of that cause
had been passed in useless instruments of plunder and destruction — without
a ray of divine light — may end — more especially on the
subject of the Mexican War — now in distress — and
grieved by the conduct of the President — I will not
say a day of his power — my heart naturally returns to our native
country, to the sentiments of toil — and I will pray God preserve
my country from all mischiefs — and I am sure that in her
name all the day long — God will give her success — and
the day of her triumph will be her day of joy — and I will
pray God that she may be blessed with a long life, and
