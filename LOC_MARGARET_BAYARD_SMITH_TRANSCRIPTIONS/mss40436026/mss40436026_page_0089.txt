To Mr. Nicholson
x x x When we had finished our dinner, we left
the table & Mrs Smith & myself walked to our house. We con-
tinued the road, which leads from this house, down a steep hill,
where some fine houses are built, but which are surrounded with
bushes & trees. We descended the hill, & on the right hand we
saw a row of houses, the two first of which are ours. They
were perfectly enveloped in large forest trees, & shrubs of
various kinds. The road or Jersey Avenue passes the front
of the houses which is to the east; on the opposite side of the
road, rises a hill cover'd with shrub oak & black berries,
behind the houses a fine wood extends to the river, which is
about 3/4 of a mile distant. Our lot will contain some fine
oaks & hickory, & plenty of black berries & other vines & briars.
We went through the houses, the office was to be finished suf-
ficiently for use last night, as for the rest of the buildings,
I do not expect they will be done, in less than three weeks.
One great advantage arises from this, we have the liberty of
dividing the house as we please, of having closets, windows, &c,
wherever we like them. Mr S, & I are to draw a plan & give it
to the builders. You will learn by this that the walls alone
are up & roofs on. In the cellar & kitchen, shrubs & black
berries grow very plentifully, interspersed with shavings &
bricks. We left the house & followed the foot paths which
through the wood. After going a qr, of a mile, we found what
at first we mistook for a stream of water; we ascended its high
banks, which were cover'd on each side with a variety of shrubs
& vines, among which the grape & the Magnolias are most profusely
grown.
The Houses were on Mrs. Smith's, donee in the Square South
East of the Capitol, in one National Intelligence &c
printed.
