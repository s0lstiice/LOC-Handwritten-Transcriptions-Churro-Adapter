Washington July 2d 1861
I never write with so much care in this I answer
a letter immediately after its receipt, as these feel
or sentiments of affection enlivened & all we write
from the heart. For 3 weeks in a month
the weather has been oppressive warm
in one part, I have felt indisposed to write or converse but from necessity I have
been very busy. At one time I was entirely
without help, I at all times with me had
help, that the comfort of the family depended
on my activity. One woman I dismissed
on account of her excessive interference in
the old woman, I took in her place in
such a cold that she is rated by all the
family; but her neatness & honesty re convinced
me to her & I was congratulatory myself on
her good conduct when last night on my
return home, I found her too, in a state
of irritation. But if ever not so badly off
as Mrs Galatine, she has still more needed
reproachable conduct about her. I am unconvinced
that she is a servant, there was nothing
that an asked she did not do. One
morning found her on her knees praying
to her father, "this is what I would not do for
any other person but," said she, "and I do not
believe I would, you would like to have
done it, had I asked you." Indeed you are mistaken
there is nothing of which I could ask which I would
not do
