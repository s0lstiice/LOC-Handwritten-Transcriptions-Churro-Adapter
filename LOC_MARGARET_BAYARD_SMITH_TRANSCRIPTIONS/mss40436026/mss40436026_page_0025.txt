I have never been alone, I have told over every thing

I am now hopping indeed that you have at last
or excellent a woman as Mrs. Loring; I have heard
over this about her, a fair management of the
children. How my dear sister you will be enabled
to leave home, without much anxiety, does
beg of you to even stop that all time to the
restraint of your health. That is the meaning
of the number you mention in your letter to Nancy.
I have always said & always thought that your
conclusion required less noise & less frequency
that without it your health will languish
or wash every day. he have already given her
walks each more than two miles in length
& I always feel better after them. I am now quite
well able & in fine spirits. My imagination
is not in the least troublesome; I have no improper
impressions prepared for any little alarm or accident
that may occur. I am more at rather times in
my nerves than in any other part of my body.
The fatigue of troubles I had on the other part
of my life will be compensated by her fidelity &
volubility. I am better pleased with her than
I should have been with any other. I have not
shown myself. -- I have not been with my sister
since Mrs. Jackson & I suppose will remain the whole
day. I feel quite unable at this hour to enter into
the conversation which I have received a visit from Coleridge. I expect
me of my return at nine o'clock.
