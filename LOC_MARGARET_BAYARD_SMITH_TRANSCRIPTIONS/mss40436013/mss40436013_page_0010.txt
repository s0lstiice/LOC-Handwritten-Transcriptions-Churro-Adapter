The enjoyment of democracy, upon a great scale, under
the conception, considering the fate which has hitherto
incessantly attended all systems of popular government -
a conception worthy of a mighty mind - with the philosopher
statesman, who, amidst all fulness of human experience
knows how this vast machine will continue to work, is
a matter of interesting speculation. Without the Americans
have enjoyed the advantage of occupying a country, where
the soil of an overflowing population have not been fertile
where every man is either a farmer, a trapperman, or a
merchant - where there are no idlers and more than
all where there are no poor; for vile must be the
American, who cannot in some capacity, earn an ample
maintenance. Then however, the means of carrying off
the superfluous population begin to fail, which at some
period must be the case - when the ratio ambition of
wealth gives place to the law of destruction and power,
and when the struggle for superiority between the young
and the old, gives rise to disturbances, symptoms of which
have already appeared, we may then expect to see the
