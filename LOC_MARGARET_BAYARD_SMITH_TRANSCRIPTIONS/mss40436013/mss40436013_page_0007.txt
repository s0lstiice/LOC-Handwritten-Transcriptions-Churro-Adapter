I am well convinced then, that my dear departed friend,
your two illustrious fathers are as far from having reason to
believe, that the state they now enjoy can alone with propriety
be called life. The soul, during her confinement within
this prison of the body, is deprived by fate to undergo a some
penance. In her native seat in heaven, and it is with
confidence that she is forced down from those celestial
tranquillities into these lower regions, where all is foreign and
injunctuous to her divine nature. But the Gods, I am persuaded,
have thus endowed discriminated immortal spirits
and clothed them with human bodies, that their might
be a race of intelligent creatures, not only to have dominion
over this our earth, but to continue, plate the host of heaven, and
institute in their moral conduct the same beautiful order
and uniformity, or consciences in those splendid orbs. This
opinion I am induced to embrace, not only as agreeable to the
best instincts of reason, but in just deference also to the
authority of the noblest and most distinguished philosophe
descending by Pythagoras and his followers (who were formerly
distinguished by the name of the Statile Sect,) firmly main-
tained, that the human soul is a detached spirit, or emanation,
from the great universal soul of the world. I am further
confirmed in my belief, of the soul's immortality, by the
discourse which Socrates, whom the oracle of Apollo pronounced
to be the wisest of men, held upon this subject, just before
