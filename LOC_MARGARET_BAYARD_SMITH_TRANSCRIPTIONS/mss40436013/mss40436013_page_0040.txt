to a child is the needless way to a mother's heart.
It was such a heart as mine, it was a short easy
way. No home was no longer disolute, no virtues
decreased by affection, my heart no longer felt
an act of God. The tenderest sympathies had filled
it. God's finger to my unfortunate son with
regret for the purity of his affection, which my own
imperfections prevented had given with him.
The conduct of the nurse was entirely consistent
with the noblest of human sympathies. She
intercepted my letters from her, and
interjected her opinions, which were often
contradictory, and often injurious to my son.
