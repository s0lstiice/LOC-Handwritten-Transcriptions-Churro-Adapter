Platonism.
The object towards which mankind have
always directed their aim, and in the acquisition
of which every system both of religion and philosophy
seeks prefer to effect their end, is the
permanent eternal, the perfect perpetual degree of
attainable happiness; but in what this chief
good consists has not been universally agreed
upon, and this variety of opinion constitutes
the essential difference between the ancient
sects of philosophy. Of all these sects there are
now three which have so developed and so flourished
as entitled to distinguish the mind from the
perfections of man, and the inferior aspects
of human sentiment, as that of the Platonists,
which, by demonstating the imperfection of every
external enjoyment, and every temporal blessing,
rose at length to the contemplation of the sublime
cause, and placed the ultimate pot, in a perfect
abstraction from the world, and an implicit
love of God.
