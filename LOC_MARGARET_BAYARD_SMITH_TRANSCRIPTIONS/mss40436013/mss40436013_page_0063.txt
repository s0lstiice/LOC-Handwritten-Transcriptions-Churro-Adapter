But why enter farther into these details?
An Englishman had quite as much to blush for
on this score, as an Italian. Even at the moment
that I wrote London & Dublin, Protestant & Catholic
in a spirit of worldly speculation, grasping chiefly
in the religious feelings of mankind, set up the
weakest & heaviest of every new church to the highest
ladder. The health of the body should not thus
be sacrificed to the dead, & this is one of the
few cases in which the legislature is interfering
called on to interfere between the individual
& his absurd inclinations.
The English burgher ground, (at Rome) or Protestant
station, another calumy & beautifully drawn by Piranesi
of Caracalla's Baths. The site was admirably chosen
astray can be more practically or religiously useful
than this most attractive spot. It is within
a thousand churches. No one can long stand there
without feeling it full descent upon his
soul the best influences of the grave.
The rich, red ruinous battlements of the
city broken only by the calm & solid equality of
the Carmelites, the clattering foliage beginning to
brown on the ancient towers of the Vatican,
the deaf still, time-song -- the fluttering of mine
leaves as they float around, as one tries to speak to the ground -- the trunks of the green mounds at my feet -- these &
a thousand other features fully fill at the
time, but afterwards they in reality, convey
precisely that physical reply of death which the
past & present have more than once
attempted to breathe over their most
charitable wishes, & which here remains an
omnination from every object which you
see or feel.
Conclusions of Death
What can be more delicately allegorized
than the nature of death under the image
of virtue broken? (which one found at a shrine)
heels resting beneath the foot of a diviner
& penitent praying, the calf of a sheep which
had just reached its port.
