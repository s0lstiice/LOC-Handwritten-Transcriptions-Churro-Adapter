She was a woman of a steady mind
tender & deep in her grasp of love.
But feelings mixed, pleased rather with the joy
of her own thoughts: by some essential cause
her temper had been formed as apt to mode
A Being -- who by adding love to peace
Might live on Earth, in bliss of happiness.
But we have known that there is often
A Heaven to which friends
In powerful thoughts & always might be found
the power to venture friendship.
More than this -- a common tale
A Tale of silent suffering, hardly clothed
In bodily form.
And with a voice that seemed to sound
to happy thralls,
"Life spent in indolence & therefore sad."
The divine is rude
Prebilete with superior & dispassion such
All hearts to sadness & none, more than mine!

Oh! that I could own ever my enlightened
mind to the parent & enjoyment of philosophical
meditations!
From the appropriate subject which we feel
when abandoned to fortune; from that icy hand with
which misfortune possesses us when destitute of faith
& sympathy & consolation, we are in some measure
recovered by those writings which bear
the breathing impressions of noble thoughts & noble
affectations. Such writings draw forth from every
relation of life; they convey the mind to general
considerations, which sweet our thoughts & preserve
our affections; they create a society for us & a common
union with all the dead & the living authors &
all those who concur in admiring the works
which we admire. In the contemplation of nature
