"I have been told by many of learning, that of
all evils one ought not only to choose the least, but
that even out of that least one ought to pick out
the good, if there is any in it. Thus I made the best
use of my leisure, nor did I suffer myself to
be bothered amidst my loneliness, which with me
is the effect first of choice but necessity.
But this license resembles not that of
affluence, nor is my loneliness to be compared
with her, for in the intervals of her glorious laughter
he sometimes indulged a leisure hour in reading
to solitude as to a harbour -- but my leisure
proceeds from want of anything more than from
the love of retirement."
(This is the only self-defeating statement
I have met with in Lewis -- generally he is full
of warm brotherly esteem, & quite a polite
man, though he does not always speak as
he might speak in all circumstances)
It was Superfluous Affluence who said, "I am now
less idle, when doing nothing I never left alone
there about myself."
Edinbath
August 17th
