The Setting Sun.
That setting sun—that setting sun!
That remains, since first its light began
To spread from its age hath seen,
Whence one, as they had never been.
That setting sun! full many a year
With all its wealth of fading years
With sweet according thought enblinded,
In every age and every climate!
Is it not to mark this setting sun
The scene's golden hour before
And when the evening night is done,
Is not this use, sweet setting sun!
When my faltering nerves to play
Seemingly upon my evening ray,
Is not again, death's solemn banner,
Glorious like the sun, greet setting sun!
He that lives according to reason will never
be poor—he that governs his life by opinion
will never be rich; human for nature is limited
but fancy is boundless. Science
The soul is never in the right place, That
feels to quit the body is
too on my bed my limits I lay
It hath not been my use to pay
with moving life, or blended nature,
But, reluctantly, by slow degrees
My spirit I to love compose
In humble trust my eyes hide close
With reverential resignation
Is with concealed—no thought let expressed
Only a sense of insufficiency
Is upon me all my soul embraces
That I am weak—yet, not without
Force in me, round me, every where
ternal strength & wisdom, one—
Coleridge
