& never me approached the road that
was to deliver the arms that never were
to receive them. At last we reached the
door, where stood three beloved parents, with
outstretched arms & smiling faces -- General Lee
brings me through the gate -- my father
white hands, with a states' figure -- my mother
gloomy face & mild, but eyes, a clasped hands
approached to embrace -- Then they stood as we
came up the avenue of old cherry trees --
the boy I sprang from the carriage & fell with
a fond father's arms, left myself pressed to
his breast & my cheeks wet with our mingled
tears -- the girl whose fond heart I press
for the love of some beloved object, protests
the transport of mine when I felt the
pressure of affection -- He only can judge,
who like me have languished & withered
in the absence of this noble warrior --
unable to draw me from my father's arms
my mother clasped her hands & with a
grain of shell in her -- and when released from
my father's protective embrace, she renounced
my child to leave, while she received me
once more to her emotional bosom --
a mother's bosom refuge from sorrow
