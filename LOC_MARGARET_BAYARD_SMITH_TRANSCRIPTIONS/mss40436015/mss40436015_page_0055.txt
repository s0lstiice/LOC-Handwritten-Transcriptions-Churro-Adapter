Extract
from a letter of Mrs Washington's
on the occasion of Genl. Wm. H. Harrison's Presidency
"It is coming to the handdrop of
our numerous friends in all quarters,
that my new & unwholesome situation
is not indeed a tribution to me.
When I was much younger, I should
probably have enjoyed the innocent
joys of life as much as most
persons of my age; but I had long
since placed all the prospects
& my future worldly happiness
in the still enjoyments of the fire-
side of Mount Vernon.
I little thought, when the
war was finished that any circumstances
could possibly happen, which
would call the General into
public life again. I had anticipated,
that from that moment
we should be suffered to grow old
together in solitude & tranquility.
This was the first & dearest wish
of my heart. I will not however contemplate
with too much regret
the appointments that have inevitably
followed his feelings & my own were
in perfect consonance with respect to
our private life. I cannot blame him for having
acted according to his ideas of duty
in obeying the voice of his country.
The consciousness of having attempted
to do all the good in his power
for the pleasure of finding his fellow
citizens so well satisfied with
the disinterestedness of his conduct,
will doubtless be some compensation
for the great sacrifices which Stewart
has made.
