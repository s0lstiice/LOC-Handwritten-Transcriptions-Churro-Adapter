If the structure of the constitution
of the mind were as perceptible
as is that of our bodies, the conviction
would be as strong that evil influences
& unhind feelings are as destructive of
health as sensual desire or preference
to extremities of heat or cold are to
the corporeal health. Since that
source of happiness, which is the impera-
table companion of man, would lead
him as strongly as epidemics to produce
every bad disposition as it now does to
indicate every symptom of disease.
Another consideration independent of
social & religious duty, would lead to the
same result & that is, the influence of
mind over matter & of the moral
on the physical condition of man.
A careful controlled temper is one of
the very best preservatives of health.
An invincible violent or irregular temper
disturbs the circulation & the absorption
digestion & excretion & as effectually
damages the system, as any merely
physical disease.
Exercise of the bodily
affections, & of the intellectual
faculties, and is remedial to the ailments
of the body, as exercise of
the muscles, sustenance and care
of bodily disease being managed by
actuities of the mind. Marmouth is
a striking example of this fact. The
Physicians had pronounced him to be
in the last stage of consumption &
he died he could not live above
three months. He had commenced
his great work, Beliarius, the
one on which he built his fame,
he was naturally very anxious to
complete it. He set himself to
the task, with the feeling that his
time was limited & there at the
most, thought he had three months
to live. This space which he devoted
to the accomplishment of any work.
So intense was he absorbed in this
object, that he thought not of him
self, or the service which was totally
detracting his life. At the close of his
work, the return of strong journalism
completed his work -- the fatal disease
had disafforested him.
Now was there anything
more cruel -- this the flood of blood
had been carried from his lungs to his
head --
