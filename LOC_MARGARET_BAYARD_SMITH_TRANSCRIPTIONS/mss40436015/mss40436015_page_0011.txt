"The most sorrowful condition of solitude
is that of the hypochondriac, whose mind
is only occupied by reflecting on his pains.
The most desponded man does not more
often spend his time in pursuing the fleeting
pleasure of the world, than a melancholy
praying mind, over what at the greatest chance
and under the most absolute despondency
from the rest of mankind."
The mind, when employed in the pursuit
of noble interesting objects, is steadied from
those impurities with which the habits of
solitude stain the vacant brain. The soul enjoys
freedom and tranquility, full of all its
energies with superior force, and displays an
extent of power which was before unknown.
He will therefore use his solitude for
as the faculties are capable of greater exertion
in the leisure it affords, as he enjoys
greater liberty, and tranquility, as our idea
becomes more clear, harmonious and extended.
We are led with greater certainty into the
consequences of things. The mind exacts much
more from its duty in solitude than in the world.
