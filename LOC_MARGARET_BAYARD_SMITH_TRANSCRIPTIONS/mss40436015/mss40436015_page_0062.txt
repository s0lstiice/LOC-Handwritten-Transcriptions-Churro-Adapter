Old Age
Many years have passed by me in
their silent march to eternity, but I re-
gret not their departure. If they have
taken with them beloved friends & mutual
feelings, the buoyancy of hope & the eager
desire of enjoyment, they have happily also
taken the violence of the passions. If they
have brought me perplexed & weakness,
& melancholy, & sometimes of a pitiful which
I would not have, they have also brought
me -- which is a blessing that annuls
the evil, -- a quietude of mind, a serenity
of feeling, which is the modest treasure
that man can possess.

Old age

when vain imaginations, troublesome thoughts,
and hopes & fears have had their course, & left
the intellect composed, the heart at rest
nor yet decay hath touched our mortal frames.
Style in writing

in language it is a rule that perspicuity ought
not to be sacrificed to any other beauty whatever;

a greater beauty it cannot be doubted that
the want of it is the greatest defect of writing
