but I conjecture it to have been the effect of arrest
& watching a broken rest -- would that I were by you
to share in all your cares -- in all your perplexity --
My dear Mrs. mother &c -- how is he this Christman
day? I shall pass the most of it, in a situation
that will naturally make me think of his side
bed a quiet chamber -- Poor Mrs Cutts! (a son
of Mrs Madison) is lying dangerously ill -- I have
visited (great part of the last two days, with her
last night) & desired her to rest -- I hoped to
see her two daughters (both grown up) & would
not hear this morning -- she is much worse &
Dr Smith has no hopes of her recovery -- I only wait
to finish this letter & then shall go to her &
if necessary remain till late at night -- Mrs
Randolph sat up with her the night before --
but to do so -- had to leave her own sick family --
Three of whom are confined to their beds --
I dare not venture sitting up at night -- my dear
sister will not hear it -- it is only during the last
week I have been able to sleep without artificial
aid -- Her daughters are growing & maria remains
and now the family are poor -- very poor -- at
least as they seem -- Mrs Cutts was removed from
office by Genl Jackson --
From Benbrook's young people I hope
you will be well --
Yours ever
Samuel
