Dear - 25-1830-
This is Christmas day -- but far from a
Merry Christmas is there I might say -- Therefore it
cannot be so to me -- I reflect since my marriage
on other melancholy circumstances -- my dear daughter
was so ill, that I had little hopes of her life -- But
a gracious Providence showed to me in a blessing that
she is a blessing more than I could expect her to
be in herself -- many times -- how many years more could
she have been -- These commemorative days! how many of the actions in our fortunes have finished their parts -- left
the theatre of life -- for them, what matter --
whether the part assigned them by the great manager of the drama was heard with high, or humble --
obscure -- whether it was light, or papaous, or macaronic
or sad! -- The only question with her, whether the
given part, was performed well or ill -- The only important point, the judgment, appreciation of the managed
part of the sheet alone awarded them little value --
and nothing afterwards on what a good part her own
dear daughter could act -- I felt most tenderly affected
by the news of her departure -- all her kindness to me
in my youth -- all her kindness in my hour of greatest
suffering -- a painful watching over my dear son, rushed
on my memory -- on my heart rather -- I felt that
I had indeed lost a friend -- and such friends, can never
be replaced -- What a useful, what an excellent, what
a kind woman she was! Such, debilitated and consequently inactive as she was during the last part of her life, yet
she maintained her influence over the mind and conduct
of her family, & gained the support and judgment, founded
to well regulated & most discriminated affection --
The reached a good old age & has now gone to
receive the reward promised to faithful servants
