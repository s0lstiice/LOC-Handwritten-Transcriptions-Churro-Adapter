The rest of the family are well. J. Bayard joined us yesterday
and went to church with us this morning. I again urged him to send the
trunk up to take possession of his room; but he says his business will
not as yet attend of it. he left evening to wait tea with Mrs Stanton
Mr Bradford was asked -- but I suppose he said never she must have heard
of her brother Judge Baldwin (whom she almost idolizes) prevented her. But
I doubt the present admission of his mind, will be but temporary.
Mr Smith had a very satisfactory letter from Anna Need, a few
days since, about our sister S. She is comfortable & I hope contented to
be at Aberdeen. Mrs. authorized Miss Stewart & Miss Need to do many things
neccessary for her comfort & to take care of her. I am sorry for her
that she will be quite happy, when they once more get settled in their
home, which event is to take place early in the spring. This arrangement
is a great relief to our minds.
My Anna wrote a long letter to dear cousin Mary Anne which
induced me to delay finishing this. I cannot tell you how much
her heart-ache has returned to her. Her basket has been most
wonderfully admired. It is now completely equipped --
only article that was wanting was a knife. I last night brought her a new
one she says she is afraid of embelting the basket to her friends, or
any one on seeing it, but I am afraid of embelting the basket to
her, something -- my sister is too full to add, what remains it is
unanswerable. I am sorry to say that I have not seen your letter
for some years --
