Dear. 20. 1831. - Sunday -
Last Friday I closed a long letter to you - yet after writing it during
it - I was sorry I had done so - I felt as if it would not accord with your
feelings & therefore would not be acceptable - I did not notice the contents
of your last. The tender & sad reminiscences it contained, could not be responsible
for the same page with such frivolities as I had detailed - for vain
fantasies on the highest objects of ambition which I received from her
eye of the grave - Since I closed that letter, the last page of which
as written in my chamber by the side of my dear Susan, I have
already been out of it - The weather was so severely cold, that
Simon advised her not leaving the room, until it modicated
to still continue too cold to try the atmosphere below stairs -
This confinement has given her all the feelings as well as
habits of a confirmed invalid - too languid to occupy herself
he found it more comfortable to lie in bed & has done so
at least half her time - Her feelings are so keenly susceptible - her
irreverent system so disordered - her views of religion so limited & discouraging -
Not these things added to the debilitating effects of disease
render it difficult - to cheer, support & amuse her - She moves
as my own motions spirits quite at times - begins & at times stops
up, & depression affects my body, as surely as anxiety does
my mind - Select me my dear sister how to perform a task,
to which you have seemed such a faithful apprentice - a
task for which affection fits & yet unfit as I cannot hesitate
with my views of religion, thinking of my duty to combat her
toward the suggestions of her timid & too sentimental conscience
& from her those appearances I myself feel of her future happiness -
Oh my dear sister, the risk often says - your paternity blinds you - you should
think much better of me than I deserve - My heart is full of painful feelings -
I have no fault - no saving fault - I do not love God - nor trust in
Christ to trust to the efficacy of Christ's atonement - I am your mother
unhappy creature - what shall I say my dear sister - Honourary as I am
the devotedness of her life to the duties of religion - the purity of her affection -
the tenderness of warm charity of her heart - My dear old country friend
Mrs. Belt, who loved her from her infancy - used to say to me, my beloved pen
must not set your heart on that child - she is not yours - she is God's
own child - he has set his seal on her & will not leave her long in this
world - He will soon take her to himself - so do not fix your heart on
her - indeed my dear sister - such instances of doubt & fear make me more
and more anxious to the glorying system of Calvinistic doctrines - Such the
model, in glad tidings of God's love & mercy & ought to encourage a cheer, not
intimidate a terror - fear, weak, sinful, finan - I am glad my mind is incapable
of adopting some of these amiable fancies which to me
are incompatible to the character of God, as they
are incompatible to man
