1830.
Sidney, April 27,
To Mrs Kirkpatrick,
Brunswick, N.J.
x x x But here we are, -& for myself, gratefully
do I acknowledge never was I more happy. Happy in being per-
fectly tranquil & content, living by the day, without regret
for the past, or anxiety for the future. "Give me my daily
bread", is all I ask or desire, & gladly leave all beyond in
the hands of the great disperser of all things, without ex-
pending even a thought or hope on what may be decreed, certain
that be it joy or sorrow, sickness or health, poverty or riches,
it will be that which is wisest, -best. Amid my doubts and dark-
nesses, never has my belief & trust, in an all directing &
special Providence, been weakened or clouded. God reigns! thy
kingdom come & thy will be done on earth, as it is in Heaven,
I can say with my whole heart & soul. Would that all the
other points of Faith, were as obvious & soul convincing.
In your last letter you say, "Poor Christianity, what has it
done to arouse so much enmity"? & I tell you my sister what
it does, (at least that system taught in our churches) it re-
quires us to sacrifice our reason, that best & distinguishing
& brightest gift of the creator to his creatures. It opposes
itself to that instinctive, moral sense of justice implanted by
the creator in the hearts of his creatures. Our natural
powers of intelligence & our natural feelings, (& is not nature
God's ordinance?) are opposed to such doctrines as the Trinity.
