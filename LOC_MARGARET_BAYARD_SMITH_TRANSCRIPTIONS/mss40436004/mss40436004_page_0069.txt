This little being in the most wretched situation, deprived of liberty, and exposed to every hardship, and danger, which the most hardened criminal, or the most vicious animal, could not inflict upon it. I was much pleased with the reflection of the woman's tale, and with the genuine tenderness she displayed. It on further acquaintance I find no doubt, I doubt nothing but in preference to others, I should be sure, about judging her children out, to arrive at finding the most obvious evidence of her affection for them. I would make a trial of her opinion. After I departed the woman walked awhile during time with Mrs. H. The air was clear & clean & the prospects around me seemed beautiful. In our walk I met at several places, made acquaintance with the Indians as my guide, as the other quarter of nature we never much observed, he conversed for an hour while with a woman who had a son who did not seem happy, he went thro' a garden of large extent, examined the principle of the manufacture, explained with familiarity with the persons employed, who seemed most intelligently to give me all the information they could. After dinner we went to George Town & found the tavern in disarray, returned to our apartment where a cheerful fire awaited me. While Charles was frequently tea, I sat beside my husband & read to him part of the Traveller's. Simplicity but tender conversation gave way to several hours when the table was removed & till my watch was read & dinner appeared & while still in this agreeable scene, we realized the joys of mutual love & conference we talked of, I believe, of the most intimate conversation which mankind can give, of many years eternal comfort & peace, as to poverty, the causes of the great, the most humble, as to personal resources, but we always remarked that family, in neglect could not define, as to happiness, within our own hearts we possessed an inexhaustible store of felicity, secure against all the storms of adversity. The honest & most animated kindness flowed in our hearts & filled our eyes with tears. Another day passed, and another day passed, bitter of experience, may I ever remember that then at the source of every blessing!
It is, in one word, peace.
