Greens from the Grave Yard
at
Monticello -
Washington. 1802.
The long wished for, the happy moment at last arrived,
the moment which was to give to my arms a little
daughter, already dear, a freedom to my heart. Often had
I heard of the ecstasy of that moment, I often in
imagination had enjoyed the repetition of possessing
a child to my bosom. Were these feelings separated by
others, or was I miserable? I did not experience those
devotions of regret and lively pleasure which I had anticipated;
my affection seemed for a while, destroyed by enchantment,
larger, a pain & I have indeed embeled the first
of my child. Some time afterwards, when I was aware
what remained & it was brought to me in the arms
of its father, I felt a grateful & tender sentiment;
gently caress my room, but I was tranquil. The next
morning I felt more sensible of my happiness &
received the sweet & heart-thrilling pleasure of possessing
my little one to my bosom, in keeping it in
little life; sleep, for had I then had rested I should
failing myself into a sweet slumber with my life held
to do & thus laying it within my breath. Henry &
Mary Smith were with me; their tender & unceasing
attentions enabled me patiently to endure for a few
hours. By good husband, could give me into few
moments in the day, -- indeed whether the paper
