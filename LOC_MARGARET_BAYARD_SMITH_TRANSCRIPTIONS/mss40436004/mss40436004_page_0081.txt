Washington - February 24-1861-
This day, I have undertaken an important & interesting task,
the instruction of a little boy of 11 years age. Since Charles
- the child of five parents & has been so entirely neglected
that he does not know his letters, some day said him pushing
chips before the door & on speaking to him, was so pleased
with his answers, the tone of his voice & his countenance
that I brought him in the house, his sensible & ready
answers to my questions, confirmed & delighted me. Perceiving
the superiority of his capacity over that of his age
I determined as far as could be without harm, domestic
allowance not permit my taking him in the house
to live, I have therefore deprived his parents & breed
him to me every morning, at present I shall only treat
him reading & writing. My mind is quiet in knowing
that a night & useful & my heart is equally tranquil
in fulfilling the duties; but the probability of my
debt problem or generally prevents my performing in any
but themes. In this case, I have endeavored to combine
the influence of feeling & to be entirely regulated by
principle; I have calmly reflected on the responsibilities
of my undertaking, which I promise to be many, as
I have no authority over the child, to have so many
various domestic operations that I may be perpetually
interrupted, but still the advantage I will be able
to the child & myself induce me at least to
make the confession.
An Elegy
on The death of a Child
From Heaven's high cave, where serenely mild
The eye of mercy, turns upon the dark
Look down on painted spirit of a painted breast
Yet rather bare from misery and woe
Than nearest offspring of carnal love
For let a mother's watchfulness behind
One moment dash thy happiness abroad
Of nature, then my aching breast calm
With peace of soul to act my trying part
Then who with magic art, will fill the harm
That twines a child so closely round the heart
Dear last Eliza! in thine infant years
When youthful sweetness frothed over its lips
The smile of thine words dissipate my fears
And fill my bosom with a thousand joys
They cheering softness and thine artless truth
The starting tear from misery has stole
I applie the buried husband of my youth
The first and last possession of my soul.
