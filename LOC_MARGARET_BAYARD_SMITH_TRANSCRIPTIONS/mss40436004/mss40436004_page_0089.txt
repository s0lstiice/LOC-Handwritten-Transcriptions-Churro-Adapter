that fancy, ardour, sentiment, had been banished by
sapience, shyness, and indifference. But no sooner did the
glow of health return, no sooner was I once again
encouraged by the works of nature, & far from the tumultuous
triumphs of men, than enthusiasm, fancy, poetry
all returned.-- In this humble dwelling, I passed
five delightful weeks; when Mr Smith was absent
I had no companion but Julia, in whom I found
a friend. -- But I felt no want of society, to me
the woods, the plains, the streams & rocks which
diversified the scenery around, were more attractive
to the most splendid habitations of men, -- I have
felt more joy, when away alone &
confined amidst the dark woods, or when seated
in my little room, with a book in hand, than
I have ever done in the most brilliant & crowded
assemblies. Happy moments, when the heart was
enlarged by the forest & tender affections,
when the mind was gathery ideas, when the
hands were ever busy, when the soul was elated
by the beauties of nature, & the songs of poetry!
What tho the storm & winds & hail & rain
walls of my dwelling, in my bosom I saw sunshine
& serenity.-- What tho my roof was tumbled, my
place was elevated what tho separated from society,
I was not alone, when I conversed with Thomson, Milton
& many other great men. -- Mr Smith in the City, was
a man of business, in this retreat he was the friend
& the only intimate friend, the beloved companion -- At
