So much perfume offends my nose,”
Exclaimed the vulgar weed.
“Well, too enough to make one chilly,
To see that pale consumptive lily;
Among those painted fells;
My Tulip, too, looks monstrous old,
This gaping like a dying wolf;
What a poor stick is golden weed!
And how the wild poppy!
“Not for the gayest tint that lingers
On leaves with his very finger,
Would Smith her exchange:
For this at least, is very clean,
Since they are there, and I am here,
Twisting a higher stem—
Enjoy a wider range.”
Alas! poor oxycyce weed! — for he,
This instant came the gardener’s line,
And lipped it from her sleeve;
But none lamented when she fell,
No protesting rephry sigh’d for well;
No friendly Bess would turn her knell;
No Irving, despite a tear.
While those sweet flowers of genuine worth,
Inclining towards the modest earth,
Adorn the water below;
Content to hide in styrian shells,
Their rosy buds and purple bells;
Though scarce a young rephry tells
The secret where they grow.
The last rude ray of evening bright
Is fading over the sea;
I will wake again in beauty bright,
I will wake again with morning light,
But, where, sweet Hope, for me?
Here I, Ah! too but the silent sprig
That dances on the waves;
The mountain mist that floats away;
A number small — a matter very
It only home — the grave!
