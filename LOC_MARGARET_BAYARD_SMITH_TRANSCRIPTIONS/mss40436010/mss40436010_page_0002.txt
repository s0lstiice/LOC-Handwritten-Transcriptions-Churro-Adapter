1812
S. B. Smith,
The following lines were suggested by a look of love
of the lamented Decatur to whom the last stanza
refers. They have not yet appeared in print.
Affection & Religion,
Take a look from the innocent heart,
Or it vanish forever from view;
From the eyes of the grave that have fled,
And a change of mortality too.
Take a look from the infant, new born,
Or it sinks into gentle away;
That the power of thy deeds may return,
And its weakness thy spirit display.
I take a look from the innocent maid,
Or she bends her kind gaze upon thee;
I take a charm in the beautiful, brave,
That will guard thee in danger and peril.
I take a look from the heart of the wise,
Or the light of their wisdom inspire,
I take a look from the pure,
Or their love agains in the grave,
I take a look from the courteous tomb,
The form of thy love one melancholy
blends with that look the perfume
of death, broken lilies and roses.
