The Philosopher's Psalm.—
In days of yore as goeth his fable tells,
When learning dimly gleamed from quited walls
When wild theology's distorted eye
Shone'd the fair field of true philosophy;
And wandering thro' the despatch of mental night,
Doth not dark predictions and the clouds of light—
When curious Whiggism, with puzzled brow,
Attempting things that Science laughs at now,
Being the useful purpose the sensible,
In vain chimeras and unknown monster—
In those young times there lived a reverend sage,
Whose wisdom shed its lustre on the age
I think he was imprisoned in cloistered walls,
Where now the wyd min crowning falls,
Was a profound reflection that he knew;
The noisy world distrusted not that refuge;
The flow of man-manning waters, day by day,
And whistling winds that send their deadly wing
That's reverend tree, of ages grown, that made
Around the holy pile a deep monastic shade;
