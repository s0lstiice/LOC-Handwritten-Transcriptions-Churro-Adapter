What is life? Not in a dream
Nor memory's own wild to the vision
Somelies new in cameras beam
Or reverbering in the storm's commotion
Or
Softly stealing smooth & clear
Through verdant meadows & flowing glades
Or roaring, rushing wild & drear
Under rugged rocks or flaming shades
But in that which I call
My very being -- I can make no doubt of it --
The highest Characteristics of my Genius, -- I, who, when
as ready to pardon the worst errors of mankind as
I have never been, am guilty of some himself, at the
same time as careless of committing a fault, as of the
most forgivable one, -- and in a noble hour, overwhelmed with
all occasions meet, not only enough strength, to be invincible
to our own private fortunes, which are truest love
to the enemy of the world, with tenderness, not capacity
for me who presume none but Heroicities -- for me
the great Philosopher uses to say, the noble holds
reel, hate mankind.
Immortalalty -- the natural heritage of man
Mankind differs in their notions of Philanthropy, but
in my opinion the only truly Philosopher is who leaves his
concurrent contributions of honest fame to the glories
of pure Deity made in the eyes of Patience, I suppose
if I had not in Paradise the reward of an immortal
reputation I should prefer a life of miserable
care & indolent retirement, to any other.
If they had had the power of giving me
to an immortality of existence -- how much
more happiness would the hour had, their
immortalities of fame bestowed --
& turned ever outside from another world!
Yours
Brant
