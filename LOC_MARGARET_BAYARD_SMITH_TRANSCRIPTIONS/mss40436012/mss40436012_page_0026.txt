There are few, if any instances of a friendship so perfect, so
unselfish as that which united these two great men. In
a letter to a friend Mr Madison gives the following account of
the commencement & growth of this friendship.
I was a stranger to Mr Jefferson till the year 1796 when
he took his seat in the first legislature under the constitution of
Virginia, then newly framed being myself at the time myself
a member of that body, & for the first time a member of any
public body. The acquaintance then made with him was very
slight, the distance between our ages being considerable &
other distances much more so. During part of the time whilst
he was governor of Virginia the State, I had a seat in the
council associated with him. Our acquaintance then became
intimatele & a friendship was formed, which was for life &
which was never interrupted in the slightest degree, for
a moment.
It is wonder then that Mr Jefferson looked forward with
satisfaction to the close of his political life, under believing
as he confidently did that Mr Madison would fill the place
he vacated, & would carry out those principles & measures
which he sincerely thought would best promote his country's
good.
In the morning of Mr Madison's inauguration, he asked
Mr Jefferson to ride in his carriage with him to the Capitol,
but thus he declined, & in answer to a friend who enquired
if from delay he had not accompanied his friend
he smiled & replied, "I wished not to divide with him the
honors of the day. I preferred one better to see them all bestowed on
him." A large procession of citizens, some in carriages, &c
