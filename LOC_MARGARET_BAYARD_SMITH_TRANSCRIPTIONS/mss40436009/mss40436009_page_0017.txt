Julia Strickler to Jenny Strickler
letter 2
My last letter my dear Jenny gave you some
part description of this place, which I shall repeat
it, an earthly paradise. I described to you an aspect
of nature with my friend -- a long interval has since
passed -- during which I have encountered some peculiar
scares at home -- But I did not get away to notice
of my self -- so no matter what happened -- I would
speculate on our mutual friend -- who is part Kentucky Old
countryst, from whose "brawn me travellers return."
I have some time ago to spend a day with her. When
I rose to leave her, she clasped my hands in awe --
her school -- bounded, agitated by some conflicting emotions,
at last demanded lodging in my room with an assurance
I have no words to describe -- I find that since
the commencement of our engagement -- I have not spoken
to her of fashion, yet I, with an eye of the present for
the future, must admit that often on matters of
the household -- I professed her hand to my heart -- speak
with I -- "this heart longs to give its sport" -- Again
she rushed her eyes to mine -- "India was fled -- Canada
respald -- I am now arrived in that unfrequented land -- Into
a faulting room she said -- "India is still in Africa -- I agree --
I do not know that land and opinion find me at home."
I felt shocked -- I could not for some moment reply
"Do you insult me?" she exclaimed -- a heart full of anguish
to her face -- "Oh no, I speak it, but I am unjust to you --
unjustified -- It will be long before I can forgive such
an affront from my heart -- You do not think so -- and then looking steadfastly at
me -- "I am one not deceived -- neither am I -- I say
with one thro -- It will not be managing affairs
you will be the bearer of a family proposal take
"and I beg -- and she clasping her hands, while her head
fell back on her forehead -- I too shall be married"
