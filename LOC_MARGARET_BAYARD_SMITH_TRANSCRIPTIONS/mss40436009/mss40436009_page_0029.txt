1817
Fanny Ostborn to Julia
In your letter my dear Julia, one interesting letter melancholy letter
written by the aid of a dying friend. written in the solitude
of a rich room, whose solitude is broken only by the
approach of a feeble & almost unanswerable voice. by an ever low
light & whispered answer a chamber from which the light
reflected is excluded & where a strong light
reigns -- But these are not the circumstances which make
your letter seem to me so melancholy. The have been
my dear Julia, a dying friend -- perhaps I should say neighbour --
since Julia is a recent acquaintance with whom I have
had little intercourse until lately & that intercourse
has been only that of fellow Christians -- But Julia
is what a contrast between the humble unlettered
Christian & our mutual refined & highly educated
friend -- And could you pass a few hours with her
from excellent her, you would confess that the contrast is in her favour. -- Here there is no melancholy -- The reason indeed, in darkness, may scarcely be
granted, for poverty has brought it to this -- I am
supposing comfort, which affirms the learned name of friend.
But there is no melancholy in the light of a candle which is a candle which
is the light of immortality, & I suppose the light of salvation in
the present -- I never knew the tranquillity of death achieved
to me or complete as since I have received your letter
& continued its power over the mind of this good woman
with the power of Heaven over the mind of any intelligent
friend. -- The new grand, new sublime -- how many there
are the virtues & faults, which contemplation may
