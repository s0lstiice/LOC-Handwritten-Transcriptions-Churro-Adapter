4 O they, the leaders of their race,
Whose graceful form in my sorrow's sleep,
These worth my soul's delight to have,
Whose wingless flight I cannot grasp,
Thro' which beneath the silent moon,
With none to hide to hear, to see,
Life can alone be known from
One whom death declares to live.
5 I leave a world that knows me not,
Whose command I with the dead;
But pay reverence to the spot
Where Fancy's softest dreams are shed,
From each shadow, all alone white,
From each spirit's melting sigh;
There to clothe their forms of gold,
And the fate morning with its rays
6 But over the last dim morn shall rise
The lamp of life burn feebly now,
When stronger hands shall close my eyes,
And smooth my old and weary brow,
And crown my head with white
As some unmonumental step,
Where his name lies ashes low,
Who sought for gold, and found it dust.
