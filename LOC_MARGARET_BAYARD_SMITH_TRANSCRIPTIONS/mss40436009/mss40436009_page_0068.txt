The next morning he went to the Counting-House
where he was returned through the street in which
(as Douglas writes) he could catch a glimpse
of Catherine, a stout woman, walking with her in
her coach, he returned comparative safety,
to the miserable suburbs of Rochester,
in which they lived.
Five days passed, in which they were
not cheered by a visit from Charles, whose
constant attention to undistinguished affection by
premises pay in many parts, furnished them
with checks notwithstanding the enforcemens
& labour to which she condemned herself. But
it was far otherwise with Mrs. Douglas, amongst
whom she was devoted to her health as her people
to en spite of the affectionate cares of her children
the enemy day grew worse in health & spirits.
She listened not to the cheering songs of
Mary, as she watched her bed, nor to the one
lament of her brother Henry. The severity of nature
to her children often displayed, but
whilst they talked of better times, she sat lost in
gloomy thought.
