— Bishop, reading
— Popul says, in one of his letters — I am growing fat
of Hope for a better world, of which the light of the sun
is but a shadow; for I doubt not that God's control
is here, and what comes nearest to his works there; a God
— a true religion of the brevities of nature is the most
easy preparation & gentle transition to an enjoyment
of those of Heaven.
I think so too — Joseph writes "God manifest in
his works" becomes as it moves, not only visible
to the mind, but clear to the heart — For in all
his glorious works we see not only divine infinitude
power & infinite wisdom, but likewise infinite
tenderness — The why such a proportion of that
delight, our every soul — The brilliant colours
which adorn creation — the harmony melody of
birds of numerous throats, & flowers, & the flavour
of fruits & mends & leaves & soft, the moisture, the sound
& polished surfaces so grateful to the touch —
These delights were not necessary to the
prolongation or preservation existence of
life — had nature been clothed in one dim
& unvarying hue, had her forms & streams been
silent — her fruits tasteless & her herbs scentless,
but oh how far less happy — God manifest
in his works, must be more beautiful as the creator
to forever & loved as a Father — to any
other manifestation necessary to humble
