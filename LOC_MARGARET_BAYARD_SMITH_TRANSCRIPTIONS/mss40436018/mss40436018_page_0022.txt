Greece.
Our own! whose every nation sends
Triumph in their Liberty, dies,
Whilst seen from far Colonies in flight,
Makes glad the heart that holds the light,
And lend to London's joy delight.
Those wildly dipping waves check
Reflects the bows of many a proud
Thought by the laughing Isles that line
These Islands of the eastern wave,
And if at times a transient breeze
Breath the blue sky till of the seat,
Or drops one clover from the trees,
Am welcome to each gentle air
That passes -- though the odious ship!
To them the tide on day or end,
Shuttles of the Nightingale,
The maid for whom his melody,
Or thousand angels on land in light,
Blinds flattering to her loved tale:
For from the winters of the wild,
His own brave & season's blast,
Retains the South, by Nature given
In bestial innocence back to Greece;
And grateful guides that smiling day
For Fairfield him & for England sigh.
The Yeoman.
London May 1833.
