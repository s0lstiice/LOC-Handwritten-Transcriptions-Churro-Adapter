The Silken Gards—Sir Musette de Nina
When the Silken bands that felled our youth
Are sounded by Fate’s infernal drums
And the fondest hopes of our young hearts burnt
Shall wither away as a leaf on the tree.
Is there any other place to desolate them?
The Majestic charm our eyes to restore?
Ashes! As an altar for the heart injured once
Maythrob till it breaks, but never fall more.
The wild rose may spring from a desolate root
And shed its blossoms in barrenness perfumes
The oak that has felt the lightnings shock
May stand, and the day around it will bloom
But when it is injured another may spring
Its tendrils will twine round the oak as before
But the heart injured once, like the trunk may remain
But the flowers of life will never bloom more
