For were these earth-born castles bare,
And looked they among a flower fairy,
For, from their shivered boughs display'd a
Tree in the unfathomsable glade,
All working with the dew-drop silver,
The briar rose fell in summer green
And creeping shrubs of thousand year
Stood on the west winds sun-warm sigh.
Brown matlure scattered pine and wild
Each plant or flower the mountain's side
Star cylantine embellished the air
Northern and hazel mingled there
The primrose pale and violet flower
Found in each cliff a narrow bower;
Fox-glove and night-shade seed to seed,
Emblems of punishment and pride
Grown in their dark hills with every stain
The weather beaten orange retreat
With boughs that gaped at every root,
Grey birch and aspen wisp beneath
Altoft, the ash and warrior oak
Cast anchor in the rifled rock;
And, higher yet, the pine-tree hung
His shattered trunk, and frequent flying,
Where seemed the cliffs to meet on high;
His boughs across the narrowed sky,
Highest of all, where white peaks flamed
Where glittering streamers waved and swam
The wanderer's eye could barely view
The summer heaven's delicious blue,
So wondrous wild the whole might seem,
The scenery of a fairy dream.
Onward, amid the copse 'gan peep
A narrow inlet still and deep,
Affording scarce such breadth of firm
As served the wild ducks breed to swim;
Lost for a space through thickets nesting
But broader when again appearing,
Tall rocks and turf'd beneath their feet
Could on the dark hillerner trace;
And farther as the hunter dragged
Still broader sweep its channels made
