The bloom'd in a partless flower;
gone from her hand and bosom; gone,
The regal brooch the jewel'd ring
That sin her titles of the spring;
Yet why, though fallen her brother's knee
Beneath De Bourg's battle stein,
Why yet in Leicester every plot is,
The friends survive the English sword,
Why linger she from time's knot,
In far on Gilemany's ship-marched coast;
Why wanderers she a hundred wild —
The love of pale St. Louis's child?
And fixed on empty space, why even
Her eyes with mourning mirths;
And therefore do they their return
From me their women's matches?
Siriswell's are her seven locks
In bonneted woman's name she calls
And oft swarms the lovely rocks
The rings over madrigals,
And in the fragrancy and the mops
Behold a parted warrior's crop!
That is the spot where ever since
The lady at her evening door
Enjoys that in communion sweet;
The living and the dead can meet,
For lot to love love's fantasy,
The love of her heart's night.
Bright as the bow that spans the stream,
In Time's yellow vesture clad,
A son of light, a lovely form,
The comes and makes her back:
Now on the grass green turf he sits,
The light-filled hair beneath his feet,
Now on the hills in chase he stirs,
The hunter and the deer a shelter!
Sweet moonness! there are shadowed veins,
That cast the twilight of the moon;
Yet she will tell you she is this,
Of Bonneville Man's tomb hospitable,
More richly than in Aphrodite's bowers,
When hands first praised her beauty's power,
And knowing pages offered up
The mortal in a golden cup.
