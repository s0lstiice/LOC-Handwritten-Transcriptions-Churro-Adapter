The Grave.
There is a calmer for those who sleep,
A rest for weary pilgrims found,
They softly lie and sweetly sleep
Low in the ground.
The storm that wrecks the winter sky
No more disturbs their deep repose,
Than summer evening's latest sigh
That shuts the roof.
I long to lay this painful head
And aching heart beneath its soil,
To slumber in that dreamless bed
From all my toil.
For Mercy stole me at my birth,
And cast me helpless on the wilds,
I perish,— O my mother Earth!
Take home thy Childs!
On thy dear lap these limbs reclined,
Shall gently moulder into thee;
Or leave one wretched trace behind
Remembering me.
Think!— a strange sound affrights mine ear;
My pulse— my brain rung wild— I know;
Who was dead then whose voice I hear?

"Let there be a Fretch, of hope forever,
The victim of commingling fire;
Is thy immortal innocence torn
By fall despair?
"Do feel models of former times
Bring with them round thy pretty breast!
And ghosts of enfranchised armies
Murder thy rest!
Lashed by the furies of the mind,
From which red vengeance would then flow?
Oh! think not, hope but Feel! to find
A friend in me.
"By all the terror of the tomb,
Beyond the power of tongue to tell!
By the dread secrets of my heart!
