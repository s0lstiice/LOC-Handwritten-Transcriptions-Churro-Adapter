Her familiar haunt fix'd his eye;
With cruel astonishment he smiled,
The Gypsy saw—and stole the child,
And cast on her embrac'd breast
Such a delighted babe to rest;
Convey'd him to her innocent groves
And told him with a mother's love,
Awakening from his rosy mope,
And gaily sporting on her lap
Her canton fingers on her lyre,
Thrilled like electric fire;
Quick and quicker as they flew
Sweet and sweeter tones they drew;
Now a bolder hand he flings
And dives among the distant strings,
Then forth the muse broke like thunder
Back he started wild with wonder!
The music of sorrow went for joy,
And clasped it and helped her chosen boy.
And then no more his rosy hours
Were passed on childhood's smiling bowers
Romantic hopes and fond desires
Sparks of the soul immortal fire
Flamented within his breast the rage
To break through every future age,
To slay the fleeting shade of fame,
To build an everlasting name,
Or leave the narrow vulgar chain
And live beyond the life of men!
Then Nature's charms to his heart reposed!
And Nature's glory filled his breast:
The sweet springing morning's infant rays,
Merciful Summer's youthful blaze,
Matured Autumn's evening mild,
And heavy Winter's midnight cold
Awoke his eye inspir'd his courage,
For every scene he loved, he sung,
