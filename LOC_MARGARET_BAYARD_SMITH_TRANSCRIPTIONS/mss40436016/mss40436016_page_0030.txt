And old age was a sword armed
Such with a luxuriant wig she had
Those obdying black to change might bring
The plumeage of the parent's wing;
And old age in a breast so fair
Martially a plain with modest care,
And never travel the fields combined
With a heart more pure and kind.
Her kindness and her worth repay
You and I but gaze at Helen's eye;
Yet Nature in her mirror thus
Gives back the thrifty looks more true,
Than every free born glance confess
The guileless movements of her breast,
With joy danced in her dark eyes,
Or with a pity stained a sigh;
Or filial love was glancing there,
Or with devotion poured a prayer,
Or a tale of injury called forth
The indignant spirit of the north,
Or only passion, unrevealed,
With maiden pride the sword concealed,
Yet not life purely felt the flame;
I need I tell that passion's name!
Impatient of the silent hour
Saw on the gale her voice was borne:-
"Father!" she cried, the rocks around
Loved to prolong the gentle sound.
While she paused, no answer came:-
"Malcolm, was thine the blast? the name
Life resolutely uttered fell,
The echoes could not catch the swell.
A stranger! the Plantagenet said,
Advancing from the hazel shade.
On his bold visage middle age
Had slightily painted itsignet sage,
Yet had not quenched the open truth,
And fiery intercourse of youth,
Toward and frolic glee were there,
The will to do, the soul to dare,
The sparkling glance, soon thrown to fire,
Of hasty love, or hasty sin.
