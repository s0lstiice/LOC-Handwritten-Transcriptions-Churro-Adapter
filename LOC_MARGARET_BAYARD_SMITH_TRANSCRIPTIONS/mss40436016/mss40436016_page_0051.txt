Steal from my breast the vital breath,
Beyond the silent grave I see there of
Thine still is left a hope for me.
Health, bleams eternal in the sky,
And if I can never, never die
Adversity, disease, and pain
How otherwise there can never obtain;
Those simple friends that never flee
There still is left a hope for me.
When business calls or friends invite me,
And I am forced abroad to roam,
To objects that I meet delight me,
Like those that I have left at home.
Though welcome'd with unforged carefore,
And, liking all, may loving some,
Much, much I fear my look enforces
That I am far afar from Home.
What tho' my friends the hours to weary
For me select the curious time
Of verse and prose I soon grow weary,
Yet Pope amuses when from Home
Upholding Hope! when life's last embers burn
With will to will, and dust to dust return!
Hearin' to thy charge reasens the awful hour!
Oh then thy kingdom comes! Imperishable Power!
That through each spark of earth born captive fly
The evening sun, pale check and slant eye!
Bright to the soul thy serpent hands convey
The morning dream of the eternal day!
Then thine the triumph and the cause begins!
And all the Freeman spirit burns within!
Lean, every joy, to glisten on my mind,
But leave—oh leave the light of hope behind!
That though my winged honor of life have
Left angel wings few and far between:
The musing mood shall every pang appease,
