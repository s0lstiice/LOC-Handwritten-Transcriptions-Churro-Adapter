What the stern winter frown on thee
Thy beauties never shall fade.
For now, with retrospective powers,
Tend fancy points each view,
In colours bright as opening flowers,
Impartial'd with morning dew.
Benignant power! I see thine art,
And greet the pictur'd scene,
When free from care— with lightened heart
I spotted on the green.
Flow oft beneath these aged trees,
Upon the tender blade,
When Nature's frolic fail'd to please,
My infant head I laid
There shelter'd from the noon tide heat,
Soft sleep mine eye-lids prest,
And swir'd around her poppies sweet
To give the worried rest.
Blest days of childhood! when each hour
Renews the gilded toy—
When yet reflection hath not power
To dampen the parent joy.
What undisturbed— what calm delighted content
My fearless breast then knew,
Twice tender cares and pastimes spent
My days untrifled flew.
Or let my muse withold the lay
To generous friends his due,
For often times since childhood's day
I have found repose with you.
Or well my heart, while memory here,
Enjoys its wretched sway,
Refuse to yield the tender tear
Which gratitude must pay.
And ere my friends depart from you,
Shall be my earliest care
While traversing here my fond adieu
To add an humble prayer.
May every blessing Heaven can spread
Be scattered where you go.
