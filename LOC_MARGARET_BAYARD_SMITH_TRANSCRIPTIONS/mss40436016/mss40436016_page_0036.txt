Oh Winter! ruler of the inverted year,
The winter'd hair with steel, like ashes, fills it,
They breath congealed upon they lips, they cheeks
Bring'd with a brand, made white with their snows
Than those of age; thy forehead wraps in clouds,
A boughs branch thy sceptre, and thy throne
Of melting ice, extended to no wheels,
But urged by storms along its slippery way,
I love thee, all unlovely as thou art not,
And crowded as thou art! Thou holdst the sun
A prisoner in the yet unvarnishing east,
Shortening his journey between noon and noon,
And hurrying him, impatient of his stay,
Toward the day's end; but kindly, still
Compensating his life with admid hours
Of mental converse, and instructive ease,
And gath'ring, at short notice, in one group
The favorite dispersed, and saving thought,
His lips dispered by day light and its cares,
I own thy king of brilliant delights,
Ten side enjoyments, home born inspirings,
And all the comforts that truly rest
If undisturbed retirement, and the hours
of long uninterrupted enjoyment know.
As rattling wheels stop short before these gates,
He poured a part sufficient in the art
Ofounding an alarm, signals three doors
With the fleet rings, the stationary steers

The silent circle fares themselves and number.
But here the middle piles its busy task,
The Poets or historians page, by one
Made vocal for the amusement of the rest;
The sprightly lyre, whose treasure of sweet sound
The touch, from many a trembling chord shake
And the clear voice sympathetic, yet distinct
And in the charming ship triumphant still,
Regule the night; and set a barrier edge
Or female industry; the thronged still
This swiftly, and, unsull'd, the task proceeds.
The volume closed, the customary rite
Of the last meal commence.
Discourse ensues, not trivial, yet not dull,
For such as with a favor, forbids the play
Of fancy, or prescribes the sound of music.
