Be hushed! my brave old American friend,
Is but the crowning of the eagle.
His blood runs in the eyes' of men,
Their bloody hands had trampled out;
The lot now rests on another hand—
And hark! again, that never silent
Brings faster on the murderers
Shout— spare him— Basil Desmond save!
In vain no voice the other trains;
Their weapons crush in their arms;
Arthur's sword has laid him low—
Arthur's and another's;
But every hand that dealt the blow—
Shall have it in a brother's!
For when his mornings died away,
His own hands had dug the clay;
And in his burial turf they lie,
And I behold— Oh God! It is God!
This life-blood oozing from the soil!
"Whine in his death-wounds spirit bleeds,
Alas! my country's spirit beats,
An ally now Allen-Elk lands
And sentiment with his grave—
Drifts to their hallowed mansion back,
How long in their calm grasp, I say,
I know not for my soul was slack,
And knew no change of night or day,
One night of horror round me grew,
As of fire or hell or heaven,
Twas but when time's grim waves
The enemy brothers of my care,
Found on each eye-ball's shining thick,
And check'd my bosom from its oak;
Or when my heart with sudden thorn,
But like a death-watch to my ear,
"But Heaven! at last my soul's eclipse
Did with a vision bright inspire
I woke and fell upon my lips
Of prophetic fire.
Throne in the earth a new dawn beat,
Threwed the Emperor's trumpet sounds,
And rang it out to the judgment seat
As quietly, trembling tickets round.
Bed in the Union and shield thy name
For a man De Bourget's sword and flame
