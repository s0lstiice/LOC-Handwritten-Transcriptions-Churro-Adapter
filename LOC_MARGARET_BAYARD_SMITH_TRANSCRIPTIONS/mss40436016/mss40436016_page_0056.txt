I would I were a Glyshtome Bird
Leaving midst I wantling praise
But No! The proud iron gear is heard
Low in the dust it lays
I would I were a Spirit free
With my Angel Plumeage on
Where love and Peace for ever give
Where Death can never come.
I would I were an Spirit bright
With Angel Plumeage on
Singing midst glorious forms of light
Or Death the Victory door.
August 1743.
O Connor's Child:
The Flower of love lies blushing
Oh! once the harp of Insufficient
Was thung full high to notes of gladness;
But yet it often told a tale
If more prevailing odours.
That was the note, and could it fall,
As winds that move at night sail
Along the isles of Iron Gates,
When for O'Connor's child to mourn,
The harpist told how long he ran
From any mansion, trembling star,
From any path of social men,
In voice but from the poor den,
The Lady in the desert dwelt,
And yet no wrongs, no fear she felt:
For why should well in place or well
The body rule O'Connor's child!
But lady she no more enquires
Queen Erin's heart with beauty's power
Do in the palace of her knee
