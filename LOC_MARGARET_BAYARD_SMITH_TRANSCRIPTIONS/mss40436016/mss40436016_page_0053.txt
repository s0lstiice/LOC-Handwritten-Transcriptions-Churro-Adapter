He! but a brighten with the last adieu,
Seals of imperishable memory are stuck to you
Thus not she says it, Nature's transient passion
Longing spirits part to meet again!
Think therefore, put o'neys of health and ease
And contemplation, heart consoling joy
And harmless pleasures, in the thronged abode
(Of milli-ture unknown! hail, mortal life!)
Those human souls well to the pursuit
Of honours, or sentiment, or fame,
I shall myself not add to such a choice
Thouart his attempts or envy his success.
Some must be great. Great offices will have
Great talents. And God gives to every man
So, virtue, temper, understanding, taste,
That lifts him into life, and lets him fall
Just in the niche he was ordained to fall.
Some evening, once again, season of peace!
Return, sweet evening, and continue long!
Methinks I am there in the sleepy west
With matron steps, slow moving, while the night
Trains on thy ever-spring train; one hand employed
In letters full the curtain of repose
On land and sea, the other charged for man
With sweet deliver of the cares of day:
Yet unconsciously altered, nor yielding aid
To the homely features Night, of clustering gone;
A star no less just working on thy brow
Ruffles thee: vain that the morn is thine
Is life than his, not even indeed on high
With celestial pageantry, but on
With model grandeur in thy purple zone,
Resplendent lips, but of an emptier round.
