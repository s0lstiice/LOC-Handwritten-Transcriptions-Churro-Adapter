For thee, who, mindfull of the unhonoured dead
Does on thine lines their artless tale relate;
Of chance, of lovely contemplation led,
Some kindred spirit shall inquire thy fate;
Stoopily severe, heavy-headed swain may say,
“I have no seat here at the post of dawn
Brushing with heavy ships the dew away
To meet the sun upon the upland lawn.
There at the foot of yonder nodding beech
That wreaths its old fantastic root so high
In its lifetime length at noonside would be sheek
And pore upon the book that babbles by.
Hard by your wood, now smiling as in scorn
Muttins his ungirded fancies he would roar,
Now drooping, angel worn like one fortune,
Or crushed with care, or crushed in hopeless time
One more I must’d him on the custumed hill,
Along the heath, and near the far-ride tree,
Another come; not yet beside the will,
Nor up the lawn, nor at the wood was he;
The next with derges due in sad array
Slow thro’ the church-ery path we saw him borne,
Approach and read for then cannot read the cry
Grav’d on the stone beneath you aged them.
