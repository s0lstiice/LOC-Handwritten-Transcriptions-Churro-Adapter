I charge thee, Love!— repent and pray;
In debt thou hast infancy disfigure;
There yet is mercy, go thy way,
And sin no more.
Art thou a Mourner?— Hast thou known
The joy of innocent delights?
Endearing days forever flume,
And tranquil nights?
Is love!— and deeply cherished still
The sweet remembrance of the past?
Rely on Heaven's unchanging will
For peace at last.
Art thou a Traitor?— hast thou known seen
Cruelthieving tempests driven thy bark?
A shipwrecked sufferer hast thou been
Misfortune's mark?
Though long of words and words the sport,
Command'd by watchfulness to roam,
Love!— thou shalt reach a sheltering port,
A quiet home.
To Friendship's aid didst thou trust thy fame,
And was thy friend a deadly foe?
Who crept into thy breast to turn
A cruel blow?
Love.— and expire not on his lips,
A love unworthy to be said:
Then hast unbroken virtue drops
For Friendship's gold.
Go seek that treasure, seldom found,
Of power the furriest grief to calm,
And soothe the broken, broken heart
With heavenly balm.
In Heaven hast thou placed thy ship,
And did the Fair One faultless prove?
That she betrayed thee with a kiss,
And sold thy love?
Love!— twice a fatal hurler of fire:
Too often love's insidious dart
Thrusts the fond soul with secret desire.
Blest kills the heart.
A nobler flame shall warm thy breast,
A brighter Maiden's virtuous charms!
Blest thou shalt be supremely blessed,
For Beauty's arms.
