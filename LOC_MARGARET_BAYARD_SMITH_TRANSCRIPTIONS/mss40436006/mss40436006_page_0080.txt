Confucius the most celebrated & renowned of the
Chinese philosopher was born in the kingdom of Lu
about 500 years before the christian era. Disciple
at the depraved state of his countrymen he made
many efforts to reform them by preaching a
morality refined, pure, & even sublime. For some
time his efforts apparently succeeded, but in his
last actings in the god years his age he acknowledged
to his disciples that he had been overpowered with
grief on beholding the disorders which prevailed in
the empire & he mournfully exclaimed, "Monroe
mountain (alluding to that fabric of perfection which
he had been endeavouring to rear) how art thou fallen!
The grand machine is demolished and the virtues
& virtues are no more! The religious principles of
Confucius appear to amount to pure and rational
Theism -- he teaches that the Tyan or Deity is the
most pure & perfect essence, principle & foundation
of all things, that he is independent & almighty &
watches over the government of the universe, &
that no event can happen but by his will & power,
that he is possessed of infinite knowledge, that no
most secret thoughts are not hid from him, that
he is without partiality of such goodness & justice
that he cannot possibly let virtue go unrewarded or
vice unpunished. With respect to the immortality
of the soul he has expressed no specific ideas
though the Chinese profess the greatest reverence
for the person & writings of Confucius for I think
except the literati take them for their guide in
religious matter. Their predominant system comprehends a mixture of the ancient pagan idolatry
with the fables & superstition of the Indian mythology
containing the worship of Goe which was introduced
into China within the first century of the christian era.
Though in China sacrifices have ever been paid to
to divine tutelary angels yet before the time of Confucius
they had never been idolators they adored only the
creator of the universe who they call Lien-ti, to
whom their family emperors erected a temple which
probably the first that was built to God they
poured Goe with great pomp & magnificence but
the same with a modest & humble behaviour saying
that all this external worship is no wise agreeable
to the Deity unless the soul be inwardly devoted
with purity & virtue. They pay great respect to their
parents -- to persons advanced in age in this state
the Chinese religion continued until the time of
when Le Tiao Tien declared their were many gods
Confucius soon allanced them but in the 650 years
the Christian era upon the introduction of Christ Jesus
from the Indies the nation sunk into superstition
& idolatry
