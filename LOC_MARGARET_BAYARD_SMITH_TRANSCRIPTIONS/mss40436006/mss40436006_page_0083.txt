Epictetus was born in a little town called Gangyettum
in the Albanian territory 342 years before the birth of Christ
he early shewed a love for philosophy and soon founded
impossible to anyone altogether with any teacher who had
preceded him he imbued his first principles of religion
at Samos where his mother carried him to worship
in the splendid temple of Zeus he then found
that piety & that purestest consecration for the gods
which continued with him through life he over
shared many gods though some of his maxims would
induce us to believe that an angel he recognised only as
perverse deity he taught that the sole of man's
mortal, & incapable of either happiness or misery after
death, that man is not obliged to honor, reverence, worship
God in respect of his benevolence or out of the hope of
any good, or fear of evil at his hands; but merely in
respect of the transcendent excellences of his nature,
immortality & beatitude. That the gods confecture
with their own supreme felicity both an interest in
the concerns of our earth -- Part that the mechanism
of the universe being perfectly constructed in the
beginning went on of itself like clock-work, without
the superintendence of any intelligent being -- his
supreme good was pleasure which according to
his counsels in perfect tranquility of mind & indolence
of body -- He had written upon the gate of his
delightful garden these words: "Savorage, it is now
then well find good entertainment, it is here that
pleasure is esteemed the ordinary good; the amateur is
always ready to receive them continually; best rule
motion of one thing, show must expect only a piece of cake
& they pile up wealth. Their hunger is not provoked but
entertained, thirst is not excited but quenched & that with
what cost little or nothing -- This was the pleasure of Epictetus he says "Thanks be to Jupiter & Nature
for having so continued it that these things that
are really necessary for us are every conceivable --
whereas, those that are not necessary are bound to
be found. What then support life? have bread & water,
of what then few riches? be hungry -- It for myself a
piece of cake & a draught of spring water make me
live with Jupiter himself with his mother I acknowledge not in the least envy him -- For these twenty years
life, since a journey a long has helped me -- If it be
the character of the gods to want nothing, it is the
character of a wise man to want very little --
he did not believe that the world was made of nothing but taught the eternity of the atom -- he was modest &
unambitious, a good citizen, a faithful friend, I shall
beloved by all Greece particularly by this fellow
who was all over his picture -- Death the 20th day of each
month in commemoration of him -- It was his intention
to be a pagan without light surrounded on all sides with
perfidible darkness -- That there is a God is an incontestable
truth but who can pretend to know that truth perfectly
but the Christian! It is he alone that is convinced of this
our true philosopher has doubted or even ignorant of -- Truth is
his master she lights him in the dark & makes him understand how monstrous it is to submit man to him, she
forces him to deliver himself up entirely to her guidance &
gives him that security of mind which philosophers &
gods but saint bishops of -- he died with great courtesy
