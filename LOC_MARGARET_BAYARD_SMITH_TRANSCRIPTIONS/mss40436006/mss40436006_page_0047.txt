But I, I gazed upon his fearful ship
And tried to weep; but I could not weep.
Yet he was pale and ghastly, nought was left
But that high intellectual forehead crowned
With a few dark gray hairs, his lips bright
Of all their bitter years, his eyes darkened
In minute, and all his glory shuddered and died.
In solitude and gloom even gathered round
Save that poor fragmentary and poor parade,
Which the dull Prometheus for gladiators made.
I turned away, my heart was sick, even now
This shade grieved me in my dreams! I knew
That he had evil in him, but to how
Of tyrants, but to father upon the foe
Of freedom, but to father up a son!
If aught, but man's most sacred interest. No!
Thus Byron never died, yet gladiators till
If ye have sword for cause of man so well.
I watched him when his light was like the gleaming
Of a gay triumphs lantern in the sea
I watched him when his mounted eyes were gleaming
In all their lustre from Thermopylae,
I would have then addressed him in almost despair
The one a remembered fact,
But of this grand temple that Greece has raised
To memory alone shadows now have reappeared.
Now there he died, fit grave! and there his form
Shall go off with faith, when in Portsmouth bend
Some gather from the Senate some awful stone
For shall he go in white or in red to tread
And breathing elegant words to cold and warm
The heroes' ghost, and for the patriot dead
Shall mount a hymn of liberty as when
The few thousand keep in firm by mortal men.
