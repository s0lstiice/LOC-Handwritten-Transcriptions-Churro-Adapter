I knew that we must part, day after day,
I saw the dead destroyer win his way;
That bitter anguish find every little knell
As on my ear the deathshot warning fell;
While I knew this one light footstep grew
By passing I knew put on death's deadly blow,
My thin feet found to move now slowly down,
Each sweet word might fall fainter from my tongue,
I knew that we must part, part forever, and soon
My quiet goodness from an early grave
That ever so dull though hard and gloomy they must
Leaving a mother's fondness to the last,
They kiss or pull that gentle parting which
This voice cries, these could not but try to speak—
Still told the door, I felt it in my heart
The strength had slackened, I knew that we must part.
And we have parted — — then all gone!
Here in these innocence, mercy, suffering one.
This weary spirit breathed itself to sleep
It peacefully it seemed a sin to die
In this fond embrace who around this stone
And fell upon them, that God sin then was gone.
Let me see that plunge through the clouds of grief
This eye one moment caught a glorious light,
Do I see in that deadened hour two eyes
To know or earth what faith believes of heaven,
Then like tired blessed dead, then sink to rest
Not one, one from the awful change escaped
Death stole in, passed in that lovely eye
And touched each feature with a new born grace,
On cheeks I know unutterable beauty lay
And told that lips, poor cares had passed away.
And on those parted eyes I saw
Those eyes, even I felt that we had parted,
But not forever in the distant tomb
Where those that live for hundred shall live more
With their to stay, Liberty's death's own decalogue might
With their love up, all the mourning light—
