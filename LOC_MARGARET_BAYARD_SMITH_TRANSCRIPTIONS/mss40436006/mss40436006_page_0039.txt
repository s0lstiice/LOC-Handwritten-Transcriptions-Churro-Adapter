Thou desolate and dying year!
Emblem of transitory man,
Whose treason and wild career;
It seems but as a little day
Since nature smiled upon thy birth,
And spring came forth in fair array
To dance upon the joyous earths
Tad alterations Now how gone
How verdureless in nature's breast,
Where ruin makes his empire known,
In Autumn's yellow vestige dreest:
The sprightly bird, whose carol sweet
Broke on the breath of early day,
The summer flowers she loved to greet
The bird, the flowers, O where are they?
Thou desolate and dying year!
Yet lovely in thy Refleteness,
As beauty stretched upon the tier
In death's cold and dark grasp;
Thou'st loneliness in thy decay,
Which breaths, which lingers round the shell
Like memories mild and cheering gay
Beaming upon thy night of ill.
Yet -- yet the radiance is not gone
Which shed a richness over the scene,
Which smiled upon the golden dawn
Where skies were brilliant and serene
Oh! still a melancholy smile
Gleams upon nature's aspect fair,
To charm thy eye a little while,
Or ruin spreads his mantle there:
Thou desolate and dying year!
Since time entwined thy vernal wreath,
How often love hath shed the tear
And knelt beside the bed of death;
How many hearts that lightly spring
Where joy was blooming but to die,
These precious chords by death unstrung,
Have yielded life's aspiring sigh;
And pillowed how beneath this day
