The wounded Hussar.
Alone to the bands of the dark, tolling Danube
Fair Adelaide hid when the battle was over:
Oh! Whither, she cried, hast thou wander'd, my lover;
Or how dost thou shelter, and bleed on the shore?
What voice have I heard? Was my Henry that sighing,
All mournful she hastened, nor rounded the yar,
With blestening, and low, on the Heath she distanced,
By the light of the moon, her poor wounded Hussar.
From his bosom that hour, the last torrent was streaming;
And fall was his visage, deep marked with a star;
And dim was that eye, once expressive, glowing;
That melted in love, and that kindled in wax!
How small was fair Adelaide's heart at the sight,
How bitter the weepings over the victim of war?
Hast thou come, my fond love, this last successful night
To cheer the lone heart of your devoted Hussar?
Thou shalt live; she replied, Heaven's mercy relieving;
Each conquishing wound shall forbid me to murmur,
Ah, no! the last pang in my bosom is hearing;
No light of the moon shall see Henry return!
Thou charmer of light, ever tender and true!
Ye babies of my love that watch me afar!
His fault-telling tongue scarce could murmure adieu;
When he sunk in her arms -- the poor wounded Hussar.
