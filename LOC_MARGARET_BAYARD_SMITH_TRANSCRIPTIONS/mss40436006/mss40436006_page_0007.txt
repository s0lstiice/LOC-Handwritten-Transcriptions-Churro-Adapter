The Epistle.
Here rests his head upon the lap of Earth
A youth to Truth and to Fame unknown
Fair Science found not on his humble birth
And Melancholy marketh him for her own.
Large was his beauty and his soul sincere
Heaven did a recompense as largely send
The giver to Misery all he had a tear
The ground from man's narrow thorns all be with'd by a friend.
No gutter sick nor merited to discover
Or draws his qualities from their common detail about
(Here they relate in trembling hope for reproof)
The bosom of his Father and his God.
Grey.
The Hermit.
Far in a wild, unknown to public view
From youth to age, a reverend hermit grew
The moss but hid, his drink the crystal well
Remote from man, with God be present days
Pray for all his business all his pleasures prince
A life so secret such calm repose
Shall I bear in myself till one suggestion rose
That vice should triumph virtue vice obey
This springing some doubt of Providence's sway
This hopes no more a certain prospect burst
And all the donor of his soul is lost
So when a smooth response remains impost
Calm Nature's image on its worthy trust
For ever bent the banks the trees depending green
And shears beneath with answering contours glow
But if a stone the gentle sea devolve
