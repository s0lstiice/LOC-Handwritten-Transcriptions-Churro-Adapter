They running grace will lose its power to charm,
They smile to vanquish, and they breast to warm,
The reign of beauty, like a blooming flower,
Is but the pride and fragrance of an hour;
To day its sweetest perfume the ancient air,
To morrow see it thrown, are longer fair:
Such the extent of all external sway,
As lost the glory of a short-lived day.
Then let the mind your restless eye engage;
In beauty lost begins the flight of age.
The mental charms protect each living grace,
And renew the bloom that deck'd the bounteous face.
Let every virtue reign within thy breast,
That heaven approves, or makes the crown blest
To candour truth and charity alone.
The modest, discreet, lovely virtues join
Let not well temper'd meet with coarse refin'd,
And every thought expresses the polished mind,
A mind above the meanness of deceit:
If honor serves -- in conscious virtue great.
In every change that hails one steady aim,
And feels that in one virtue are the same.
And O! by preidence one each thought prevails,
Direct in public, and in private guise;
Teach thus the stores of artifice to thine,
And know not feel how others are undone.
