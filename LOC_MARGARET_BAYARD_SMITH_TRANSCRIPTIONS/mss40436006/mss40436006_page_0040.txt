Have ceased to melt—to breathe—to burn—
The proud—the gibeless and the gay,
Gathered into the moulderling urn—
Whilst fretfully flowed the frequent tear,
For love burnt—affection fled—
For all that were our blessing due,
The loved—the lost—the sainted dead?
Thou desolate and dying year!
The mourning spirit finds in thee
Seasons impure and severe,
Of deep and stern morality
Thou teachest how the germ of youth
Which blooms in being's glowing ray,
Planted by nature—ruined by truth,
With thus like thee in dark decay.
Promise of youth! fair as the form
Of Heaven's benign and golden bow
Its smiling arch beglides the stone;
And sheds a light on every eye;
Hope awakes for thee, and to thy tongue
A tone of melody is given
As if her magic voice were strong
With the eternal fire of Heaven.
And love, which never can expire:
Whose origin is from on high,
Throe— O fire thy name a ray of fire
From the pure fountain of the sky—
That ray which glows and brightness still
Unchanged—eternal, and divine—
Whose seraphs own it holy thrall,
And bow before its glancing shrine,
Thou desolate and dying year!
Sophistic of an final fall,
Thy buds are gone—they leaves are bare,
Thy beauties shrouded in the fall:
And all the garniture that used
It brilliancy upon thy prime,
That like a morning vision lies
Unto the extended grave of Time,
Time! time! in thy triumphal flight,
How all life's phantom effect away
The smile of hope—and young delight
From's utter beam—and fancy's ray;
They fade and on thy heaving tide,
Filling its strong waves for,
The soul the cries of human pride,
The broken weeks of fortune's war.—
