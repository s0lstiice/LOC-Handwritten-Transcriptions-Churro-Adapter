deny the immortality of the soul but believe

all deviations from right to be equally wicked; that a man
could never forgive, nor be moved by anger, favor,
nor pity; never be deceived, never repent, never change
his mind.

What is necessary for the conduct of our animal
life, the beautiful Author of Nature hath made manifest to all men. But there are many other choicier
secrets of Nature, the discovery of which enlarges the
aperture of Immevall the state of man. These are
left to be discovered by the judicious use of our rational powers. They have hid, not that they may
be always concealed from human knowledge; but
that we may be excited to search for them. This
is the greater wisdom of a Philosopher, and it is
the glory of a man, and the best reward of
his labour, to discover what Nature has thus
concealed --
