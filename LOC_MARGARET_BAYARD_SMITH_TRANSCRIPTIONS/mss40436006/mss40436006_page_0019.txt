Of earthly happiness, -- romantic schemes,
And fought with industry; and it is hard
To feel the hand of death cruel and sharp,
Throw a still slight veil over all my hitherto hopes &c.
Forty years hence and who will know of Jenny?
Oh, none; -- another busy brood of being
Will shoot up in the vacuum, and none
Will hide him in remembrance --
In my quaking gown
The men of better times will curtsy head,
And read my name upon the painted stone;
As well the sound familiar to their ears,
Recall my vanished memory. I did hope
For better things! -- for Fate decrees
It shall be otherwise, and I submit.
Therefore, it would no more of the desire!
No more of hope! The canton vagrant hope!
I abjure all. -- Now other cares engage me,
And my tired soul with emulative haste,
Looks to its God, and prunes its wings for Heaven
He thus approached with many a heavy ear,
Though young yet sorrowful, I turn my face
To the dark woodland, -- longing much to greet
The form of peace, if chance she joyous there;
Dark thoughtless and dismal, verging to despair,
Fills me and beats, -- and bid with this vain veil,
I think dismay'd before life's uplifted toil.
And as amid the leaves the evening air,
Whisper still melody; I think in long
When I no more can hear, that wood will speak;
And then a sad smile plays upon my cheek,
And musical fantasies calm me thron;
And I do ponder with most strange delight
On the calm chambers of the head-man's night.
And at those fled, then welcome orb,
Of sweetly pleasant flies,
To mankind in darkness lost,
The beam of wisdom lies
