Then, lest to rest, the day's long sail they throw,
Deep sunk in sleep, and sail, and buoys of dawn.
At length the morning and, at the dawn of day,
Along the wide canals the sphynxes play;
Fires, over the gay, spartanis, the bargees creep
And shake the neighbouring wood, to vanish sleep.
Upon the guest, obedient to the call,
An early banquet decked the splendid hall.
Pick luxurious wine a golden goblet gras'd,
Which the kind master forced'd the guests to taste.
Then pleased, and thankful, from the porch they go,
But, but the landlord, none had cause of fear,
The cup was minister'd for, in secret guise.
The younger guest pursued the gushing foreign.
The one who sees a serpent in his way,
Clothing and bedding in a summer way,
Disorder'd hopes, to calm the danger near,
Then walks with faintness on, and looks with fear,
To seem'd the sire, Robert, far upon the road.
The shining sport his worthy partner should.
The stepl, with silence, walked with trembling heart,
And must be resisted, but dared not ask, to part:
Murmuring, he lights his eyes, and thinks it hard
That generous actions meet a base reward.
While thus they pass, the sun his glory abounds,
The changing skies hang out their cable clouds,
Or sound an air, preceded approaching rain.
That boats to convert, send across the plain,
Murmured by the signs, the wandering pair retreat.
To seek for shelter at a neighbouring seat,
Towns built with turrets, on a rising ground,
And strong, and large, and unimproved around,
Its owner's temper, timorous and severe.
Unkind and grating, amidst a desert there,
As near the miser's heavy doors they drew,
Three rising guests, with sudden fury, blow,
The nimble lightening, mail with showers, began,
And, over their heads, loud rolling thunders run,
More long they knock, but known or fall in vain,
Driven by the wind, and battered by the rain.
