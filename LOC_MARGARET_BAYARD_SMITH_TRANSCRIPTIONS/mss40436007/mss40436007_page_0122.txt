This Life is a dream, an empty show!
In the bright world to which I go,
With joys substantial & sincere,
When shall I wake & find me there?
Of glorious hour! In bliss abide!
I shall be near in like my god
And flesh & sin no more control
The sacred transport of my soul.
Then shall I see, I hear, I know,
All I desired or wished below
And every power find sweet employ,
In that eternal world of joy!
yes, then, I not tell them shall these desires
be satisfied, which from my earliest youth have
been searching for longing for an enjoyment which
they have never found -- which earth has
not to give -- it nameless something -- which
with an invincible attraction lures on the
hope, from heart to heart & as it follows
their yet in spite of dissimilar vest, loses not
its power, but deludes us still -- and deludes us
it well as love as we follow it on earth -- for
our view it draws us on & on to the soul's native
home -- this hope of this desire, of & hope
--hopes, in the instinct of immortality -- as
wise in it design -- as true to its end, as the instinct
& irresistible instinct which impels animals to
satisfy the appetites necessary to their existence
& devotes them in the choice of the food
most conducive to their sustenance to their
enjoyment & renders them aware & disfrisified, when
forced by circumstances, to take such food as
nature never designed for them --
precisely so, is it with our immortal souls.
They are led by a divine instinct to seek for
that existence & enjoyment suited to it nature,
& when, forced by its temporary connection
with its material body -- to forget its natural
instinctive wants -- it never can be happy --
In youth I thought otherwise -- the
world was then united, a when suffering from
these restless & unsatisfied desires of the heart
& the mind -- I looked to the future with
such an assurance of attainment, that I would
lose the sense of existing difficulty, in the
ardour of hope & endeavour -- that is, at times --
and those times not over frequent -- for generally
even in youth my heart was disunited with
dadreph -- his life was often a weary burden -- yet
I never have suffered from that watchful disease
called law spirits -- a disease worse of the body
than the mind, from which I have been forever
by a continual flow of unusual spirits -- which
gives courage & electricity to the feelings, which
enables them to resist the pressure & weight
of care & trouble -- or when forced under, quickly
to rise again on the surface of the stream.
Different & distinct from the weakness in disposition
& the animal spirits -- is that sadness in melan
coly which during my whole life, even in the
happiest period I have felt -- may, loved to
feel -- or sadness such as is felt by the earle
when he thinks of the poor instant home of
his love -- a sadness the immortal soul
must feel, while it, is an exile from its
Home.
