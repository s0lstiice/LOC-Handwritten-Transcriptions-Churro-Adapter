These are thy glorious waters, Fountain
Of Godliness, thine universal frame,
Thy wondrous fair; thyself her wonders
Unspecifiable, who sitteth above these heavens
In us invisible, or divinely seen
On these thy lowest walks; yet these de-
lusive goodness beyond thought, & power divine,
I read God's awful name, embossed on
With golden letters on the illuminated sky;
Nor less the mystic characters I see
Wrought in each flower, inscribed on every
In every leaf that trembles to the breeze
I hear the voice of God among the trees,
With thee in shady solitudes I walk,
With thee in busy crowded cities talk;
In every creature own thy forming power,
In each event thy Providence adorns,
Thy hopes shall animate my drooping soul
This precept guides me & thy fear contracts,
This shall I nest, unmarred by all alarms
Secure within the temple of thy arms.
From anxious cares, from gloomy terrors free
And feel myself omnipotent in thee.
When when the last, the closing hour draws
And earth recedes before my swimming eye
When trembling on the doubtful verge of fate
I stand & stretch my view to either state
Teach me to quit this transitory scene
With decent triumph & a last serene
Teach me to fix my ardent hopes on high
And having lived to thee, in thee to die.
Barbauld.
May - 1828
Life is still prolonged -- This is
more than I expected, for from symptoms
which I now indicate sudden death
I believed that and this should
have left this frail tenement of
day mouldering to its natural decay
I have been exploring other worlds
But I am still a prisoner in this --
Prisoner! -- ungrateful word, let me
recall it -- Child, rather, favoured
child, still kept in this infancy
of being -- heir of immortality,
not yet in possession of my brightest
inheritance. -- Oh my heavenly
Father, they will be done. -- I am
ready, if the time is come when
I shall approach nearer to thy
throne & hold closer communion
with thee -- But if that happy
period is to be delayed & I must
still linger on my journey home
I will and ever will, cheerfully to
perform the duties, cheerfully to
suffer the trials of my then
remaining years of life. -- I will
struggle with the bitterness &
inactivity of this infirm & often
suffering body, as far as possible
& separate my spirit from it &
employ my mind, though diseased
may prevent my employing
my hands. -- To be useless
