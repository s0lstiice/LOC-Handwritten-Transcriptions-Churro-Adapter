January—Oh! it flames as if parting,
Were like the last beam of the light,
That's left, like the day star, departing,
To rest in the darkness of night.—
Sam Brunswick
October 18th 1829
Oh! why does the Heart the reflection retain,
Of the Sunshine whose Splendor has fled,
And why does the scent of the roses remain
When their blossoms are faded and dead?—
Why Anna, in vain doth my fancy rebel,
Recollections of days that are gone,
I remember the days when I loved thee so well,
And forget that I love thee no more—
The flame of affection be wasted away,
And its embers burn dimly slow,
The sigh of remembrance that mourns its decay,
Too often extinguishes the glow—
And fateful death is that languishing flame,
Which the embers of passion impart,
It comes, like a sickle vanishing dream,
And gladdens the desolate heart.—
Has till the trace of the past been mord'd,
The struggles of reason are vain—
Oh! let me forget that I ever have loved,
Or I feel I must love thee again—
