How happy with your friends you've been
While summer smiled upon the scene.
How often plucked its glowing rose
To twine around your youthful brows.
That youthful brow so smooth and fair!
Unfavored yet by pain or care,
And must it bear the deep impress,
Of dimming age, and dark distress?
That fragrant rose, without a thorn
Sparkling in the dew of morn;
Must it then wither drop and die,
Beneath a cold and flowering sky?
That sunny hill, that woodland shade,
In Nature's loveliest hues arrayed;
How bright they seem! as if decay
Could never wear their charms away.
Those evenings cloaked with social mist
Those cheering friends around the hearth
Those devout joys I wish for you
These - these, alas must vanish too!
Since charms and joys, which spring from earth
Return to whence they take their birth.
— Yet sigh not at the sad presage
Since there's a charm to brighten age
Since there are joys which in decay,
But last when Time has past away.
Then let me dear Louisa say
Forget not these when far away,
When to kind heaven your prayer ascends
Remember then your Sidney friends.
M. H. S.
Sidney November 1826-
