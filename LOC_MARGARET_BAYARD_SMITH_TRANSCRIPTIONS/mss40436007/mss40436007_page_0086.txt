placed in my heart by God himself,
and which affirms me that all
men, of all countries & all times
are equally precious in his eyes
& if obedient to this law, which
he has written in their hearts, they
shall enjoy his favour.
Does not religion then consist in
loving God with all our hearts &
souls & with all our strength &
loving our neighbour as ourselves?
Has not Christ, said that on
these two things, hang all the
law & the prophets?
Why then puzzle myself with
darker mysteries -- incomprehensible
doctrines, which if I did believe
them would lessen my love to God,
& love to man?
For it would be impossible for
me to love him, as I now love
him, as the Father, the merciful
Father of the human race -- if I believed
that out of innumerable millions he
created, he chose a few, very few
for eternal happiness & condemned
the rest to eternal misery --
or could I love these condemned
wretches, as I now love my brethren
children of the same Father?
Moral Sense -- or instinct
Do not all systems of religion or
Philosophy also recognise the exist-
ence of this internal principle, that
for they give to it different names?
Smith, in his theory of moral sen-
timents, says,
"Upon whatever we suppose our
moral faculties are founded, whether
upon a certain modification of reason
upon an original instinct, called a
moral sense, or upon some other
principle of our nature, it cannot
be doubted that they were given us
as for the direction of our life -- They
carry along with them the most evi-
dent badges of this authority, which
denote they were set up within
us to be the supreme arbiters of
all our actions to superintend all our senses
passions & appetites, & to judge how far either
of them was to be indulged or restrained. The
rules therefore which they prescribe are
to be regarded as the commands of Deity --
Laws, promulgated by those vice-governors which
he has set up within us."
And Cicero observes: "Right reason
is itself a law, congenial to the feelings
of nature; diffused among all men, uniform;
clinical; calling as imperiously to our duty
& peremptorily prohibiting every violation
of it
