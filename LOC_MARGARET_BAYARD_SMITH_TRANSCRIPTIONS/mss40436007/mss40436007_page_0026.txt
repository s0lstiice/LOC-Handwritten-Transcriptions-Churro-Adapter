The bloom of spring, the summer's glow,
But transient pleasures can bestow,
Even Autumn riches soon decay
And life itself must pass away!
But Friendships can on life bestow
More sweet a bloom, more bright a glow
Harvests that never know decay
And joys to cheer life's winter day.
These are the gifts to the we bring,
Warm from the heart our wishes spring,
That every year you may enjoy,
The charms of love without alloy
Friendship as perfect and sincere,
As that my friend you meet with here.
Call ye a wreath for the maidens hair,
The Rose hath unfoldded its blush to air
The violet springs in the grassy vale-
And the sweets of summer sprout the pale-
Twine young flowers, while the morning dew
Beauties with light each sparkling hue,
Their beauty fleets in the midday glare;
- A wreath for the youthful maidens hair.
Call ye a wreath for the aged man
Who is slowly lengthening his span-
A leaf of moss from the ancient rock
Leaves which cling and the Tempests shock,
A plume from the bird's weary wing-
And faded remnants of vanish'd Spring-
For his hopes and wishes are with the dead:
- A wreath for the old man's drooping head.
Frederick E. - A.
