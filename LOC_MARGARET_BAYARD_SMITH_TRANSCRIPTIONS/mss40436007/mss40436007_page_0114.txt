a bundle of nerves vibrating to the
slightest touch -- and could send a sensation
very fine in this hand world without
suffering -- oh no -- no -- I rejoice she
is released from such a state of being.
Then too -- she thought so minutely of herself,
that she never enjoyed the delightful con-
-sciousness of being beloved, as the -- she was
so tenderly & fondly by her family &
friends -- she could not be personated
that any one could for her -- This diffidence
in her own powers of pleasing & entertaining,
was a source of much iniquity to her -- I
believe the love of pleasing -- she grew
insufficient to her endeavours to do so & yielded
to a little self indifference -- that shelled her
naturally warm affections -- an indifference
which as it increased -- extended itself to
all the interests, as well as pleasures of life,
but religion, if ever to its duties -- for its
duties, she looked upon as one of the ties
which connected her, or rather as one of the
guises that lead her to that other & better
world for which she so ardently long -- In her
father therefore, she was diligent & persevering
in the performance of those she successfully
struggled against her feeble & inactive body
& her timid & tender disposition -- But
on all her other relations with the
world she was cold & indifferent --
Oh my beloved one, when I think of these
things, I think our father who is in Heaven,
that he has taken thee home -- taken thee
from a cold, barren cheerful world, in which
there didn't live an a soul, genuine & a stranger --
yes -- taken thee home -- dear child of God --
from I joy in thy joy -- this I feel so lonely,
& so desolate without thee --
Happily soul thy days are ended
all thy mourning days belong
to, by angel bands attended
to thy blessed Jesus go.
Struggle through in thy latest passion
to thy dear redeemer's breast
to his glorious sweet salvation
to his ever lasting rest.
For the joys he sets before thee,
Dear a momentary pain
Die, to live a life of glory
Suffer -- with thy Lord to reign --
My blest is my darling -- benefit
of all that could burden her mind,
How easy he soul that hath left
The meagreous body behind.
That frame is affected no more,
with sickness -- or shaken with pain,
The war in the members is over
And never shall disturb them again,
