A drowning man will seize in vain
to save him -- This certainly is not
in such spirit, he would never think
of yielding such frail support --
So the dying man, will seize
on any hope, how weak or futile so
ever, that is offered to him as the
means of obtaining eternal redemption
or avoiding eternal misery.--
But can this shrewdness of spirit, the
offspring of fear -- the offspring of
disease, be as acceptable to our
creator, as the free spirit & full conviction
of a mind perfect as God
himself chose to make it? --
Certainly then, those decisions
the mind makes, those opinions which
it adopts, in the time of health, are
much more safely to be depended on
than those formed in times of sickness.
Repentance is another thing necessary
in our preparation for death --
This indeed may appear even in our
last moment -- if any, the least degree of
intellect is left -- This is not the work
of the understanding -- or the exercise
of the affections -- It is the work of the
conscience, or of that moral sense, which
often retains great strength, when all
the other senses & even the intellectual
faculties are much impaired --
It is that law which God himself has
written in the heart of man; it is a portion
of the soul -- it is that moral
consciousness, which constitutes our
immortal being -- 2 its power is felt
when every other human power is
decayed -- Even when reason is departed
its voice is heard in the ravings of despair
& sinfulness -- It never dies! -- Repentance
is its offspring & repentance may avail
in the last agonies of death -- For
our God -- is a God of mercy
& of truth -- 2 He has promised forgiveness
to the repentant sinner --
We should therefore certainly pray for
time to repent -- but at the same time
we cannot but feel assured, that repentance
while in health, must be
more profitable to ourselves & our fellow
creatures & therefore more acceptable
to God, than the momentary repentance
of a dying hour -- 2 instead of wishing
for a lengthened sickness -- a lingering
death, to give us time for repentance, we
should constantly remember that death
