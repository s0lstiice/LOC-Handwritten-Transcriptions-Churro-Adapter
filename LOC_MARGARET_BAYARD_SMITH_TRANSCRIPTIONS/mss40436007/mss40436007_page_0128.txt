Then let my soul still clench to Thee
That prostrate in the dust,
Hast thou not bid me see thy face?
And shall I seek in vain?
And with the ear of Sovereign grace
Be deaf when I complain?
No— still the ear of sovereign grace
Attends the mourner's prayer
Oh may I ever find relief
To breathe my saviour's Name,
Thy mercy seat is open still
Here let my soul retreat
With humble hope attend thy will
And wait beneath thy feet.
October 24, 1838
A year— yes a whole year has
passed away, since my dearest
Anna went to heaven! A year
I yet no record of her worth &
of my affection— no outpouring
of that grief which lays heavy
on my heart— there I still preside
with a weight that humbles
& deadens every feeling, every faculty
— after have I espayed to
its commemoration the hour of
my bereavement— but I could
not— I cannot— The state
of mind that followed that
dreadful stroke, was a kind of
insensibility, like the stunning effects
of a severe blow— her is mind &
body yet recovered— Every faculty
physical, & moral seems paralyzed—
