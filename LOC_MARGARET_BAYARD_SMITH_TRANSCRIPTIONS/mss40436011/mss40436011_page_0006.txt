When the infantine age is past,
let the attentive mother watch
the moment to sow the seeds
of instruction, & attend to their
growth & culture. The pleasure
she will derive from watching
the progress of this tender plant,
of seeing it blossom, of reaping
its fruit, will more than
repay all her cares. But these
very cares are pleasures, for what
taste can be more pleasing
than to pour instruction on
a young & innocent mind.
This is all best by the
mother who sends her
children to school. She has to force
them to it, often to which I cannot
get them there— when
there, they learn many things which
they conceal from their mother,
hence, early do they learn art
& falsehood. They form friendship
from home, often very imperfect
ones; they learn to love others
more than their family. Home
loses all its charms; for when
they return, instead of having
their meals with the family,
some cold victuals are handed
down, they are put in a room
alone to learn their tasks
& then harnessed away again
