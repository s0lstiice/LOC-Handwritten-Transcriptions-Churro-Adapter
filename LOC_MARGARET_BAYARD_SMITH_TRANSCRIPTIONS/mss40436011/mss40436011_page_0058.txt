Mode for cultivating early Potatoes.
We take up all intended for seed next
year before they are ripe, just when
the outer skin feels off, and before
the stalk or stem begins to wither; they
are then laid upon a gravel walk,
fully exposed to the sun, for a month
or six weeks, when they becomes quite
green and soft, as if roasted, and often
much shi p pedled; they are then
put away, and protected as other pota-
toes are. In February we examine them,
when we generally find every eye full
of long sprouts fit to be planted; they
are then cut as described by R.W.; but
less economically, as we seldom make
more than two sets from each tuber,
— the eye, or tip part, and the root or
bottom part. We plant them as descri-
bed by him, the eyes upwards in
both parts; and we observe, as des-
cribed by him, that the potatoes from
the eye or tip sets are earthen by a
fortnight, and that those planted in
the common ground. And let me
observe, in by no means the warm-
est or richest part of our country, the
writer had a dish of ripe potatoes at
dinner last Whitsunday the 14th of
May, and the same every day since,
from successiveal crops, until the
20th of July, when the common
potatoes come in use.
