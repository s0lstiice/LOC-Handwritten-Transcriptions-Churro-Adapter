Has this room stood to sell,
And has it was by the owner found,
And all I felt, I need not tell,
Such shame would cut me to the
heart from this lesson I have learned
Or rather than thus to sin again,
That bread by honest labour earned
Or matter for
Whate'er the cost, whatever the pain,
Two cruel days have past
Since I this crime deplored,
This sane will be my last,
If not by you restored
Poison pity takes on me
You never shall repent,
While I can speak or see
For you shall life be spent
Oh one what do I hear
And do you then consent
Kind lady does not fear
You never shall repent.

The happiness which flows from
the virtues here spoken of are
the those sweet springs, which
are hid in some secluded thickets
concealed by foliage or trees
from the eye, but which spread
forthly their beauty over the
surface of the adjacent earth,
as tho they themselves are
invincible -- or like the Nile,
whose source is unknown
but whose waters carry plenty
of wealth wherever they
flow
Happiness is the man of a
generous and benevolent disposition
who feels the desire of blessing
others, but has not the means,
than the avancer who
has the means but
not the desire,
