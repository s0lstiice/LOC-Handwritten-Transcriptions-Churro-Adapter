children. It is only by feeding, dispensing
& playing with them, that we draw
forth their little hearts, that
we engage their affection, & are
entertained with their spurious tricks,
that we perceive their temper,
& become acquainted with
the development of their faculties.
These are the circumstances
which endear them to us, which
inspire a kindly interest, a tender
affection. -- On the contrary the
mother who consigns her child to
the care of others, never engages
its affection, or becomes acquainted
with its qualities. -- Its sounds
a little engaging tricks & endearing
movements are reserved for the
nurse, while with the mother
it is dull, & crude. -- How
sweet are the movements, when
lulling an infant to sleep!
its eyes, now closed, now opened with an
expression of tenderness & pleasure,
the smile of peace & complacency beam-
ing in its innocent face, its little
hands raised to stroke the mother's
face, & died with in her bosom;
now & then a kiss, given of its
own accord, as a reward for the
labor, its little voice humming
low & soft; & when at last it
has fallen asleep, its smiling
& tranquil countenance, its easy
& quiet posture, exhibit a picture
of comfort & enjoyment, which
no mother can see unmoved! --
