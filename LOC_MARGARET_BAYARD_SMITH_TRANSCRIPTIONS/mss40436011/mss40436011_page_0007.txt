There is nothing which children imitate or apply more readily than
expressions of affection & warning, of
approbation, hatred, resentment, &c. the
like; & when these passages & expressions are once connected, which
they soon will be by the same association which unites words with
their ideas, the passion will follow the expression & attack upon
the object to which the child has been accustomed to apply the epithet. In a word, when almost
every thing else is learned by imitation, can we wonder to find
the same cause concerned in the generation of our moral
sentiments?
Palley
