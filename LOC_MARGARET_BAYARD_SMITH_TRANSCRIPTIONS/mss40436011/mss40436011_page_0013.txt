What was enjoyment, in
the sense of that youth, who,
according to Tacitus, loved danger
itself, not the newards of courage?
What is the prospect of Pleasure
when the sound of the horn or
the trumpet, the cry of the dog
or the shout of war, awakens
the ardour of the sportsman or
the soldier? The most animal
occasions of human life are
called to danger & hardship, not
invitations to safety & ease;
and man himself in his
excellence, is not an animal
of pleasure, now destined merely
to enjoy what the elements bring to his
use; but like his associates, the dog
& the horse, to follow the exercises
of his nature it preference to what
are called its enjoyments; to find in
the lap of ease & affluence a to
court in the midst of alarms that
seem to threaten his being, in all
which his disposition to action only
keeps pace with the variety of
powers with which he is furnished
& the most respectable attributes
of his nature, mananimity, fortitude
& wisdom, carry a manifest
reference to the difficulties with
which he is destined to struggle.
