Chinese method of propagating various kinds
of fruit-trees:
They strip a ring of bark, about an inch
in width from a bearing branch; surround
the place with a ball of fat earth or loam,
and fast to the branch with a piece of mat-
ting; over this they suspend a pot or horn,
full water having a small hole in the bot-
tom, just sufficient to let the water drop,
in order to keep the earth constantly moist;
the branch throws new roots into the earth
at above the place where the ring was
stripped off. The operation is performed in
the spring, and the branch is drawn off and
put into the ground at the fall of the leaf:
the following year it bears fruit.
NEW BRIDG
I am a little child
my name is Sally Gray
And in these woods so wild
have been the line-long day.
No victuals have I had
But berries which I found,
If then I was night glad,
A handful to have found,
You ask dear ladies why,
Alone I wander here
And tell me not to cry,
But banish every fear
