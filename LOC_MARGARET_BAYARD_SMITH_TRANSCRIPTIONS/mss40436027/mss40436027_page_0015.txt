Sidney - 26- March - 1808-
When I calculate the time that has passed, since
I wrote to, or heard from you, I can scarcely believe it possible
that I have allowed so long a silence to remain unbroken.
You have heard from me, since I have for you; my
long letter directed to Maria, but written eagerly to
you, reached after Maria had left you, but I have
not had a line from Brunswick since that period.
It is no much reason to answer the letters
we receive, than to write to those we have not
heard from, that from I did quite, which was not often
it was to Maria a Susan. They say but of correspondence
is now required to have two, for you some given
me up- I find the little happiness in keeping up our mutual
intercourse with my best friends. How is it my dear
sister that you never write to me? If we cannot
our leisure, I think of the two, you have the most fancy
& Maria, & I suppose Margaret, in entirely late at
cane of every kind off your hands, & you are so little
interrupted by company, that I should suppose your
pen would afford an apparent relief to your
needles; for I have no idea of how you have spent
the winter, what work you have done, or what
books you have read. I suppose either you
or Mary read aloud while the Stars see and Jane
& Eliza's letters are now so advanced, that during their
respons, I suppose you are not interrupted in your
sewing. - When I allow myself to get a thinking
about you, I feel an indescribable impatience to take
a look at at you. If I try to convey myself
to your partner, & when I feel any strong emotion
of pleasure, surprise, admiration I cry to communicate
to my dear sister. The Maria's letters inform me
how your are, they do not afford that much
