Dear little Sue, just begins to learn her letters. She is
a sweet tender hearted little creature I would love you to give love
her dearly. —
I was really afflected at the account you gave me of
your Susan — I wish sincerely we could persuade her to come on
directly; her ill health & dejection excite all my tenderness &
I feel disposed to do every thing in my power add to her comfort
& happiness. — She was so well, so gay & so interested in her studies
while here, that I cannot but hope believe that she would be
quite restored were she to come to us. — I wish when you write
to Mary, you would advise Susan's coming on directly. —
I had written thus far, when the servant had brought sent
to town, returned & brought me your letter of the 14th, with the melan-
choly intelligence of Mr P's death. It will indeed make a great
difference in the society of Boursnich & Papa with most sensibly feel
the loss of such a companion & friend. —
