Sidney 24. 1828
This is the last letter I shall write to you dear
Maria from Sidney -- How melancholy is the word
last, when applied to what we love & dearly do I love
this Place -- every tree, every shrub, every hill & dale
I forest view, on which I have gazed with a pleasure
that while it delighted, purified my soul -- Never shall
I enjoy a more perfectly happy state of mind than
I have enjoyed for the last three years -- a physical
& moral revolution took place in my frame
after my return from New York -- since then
I have enjoyed a calm, a contentedness of mind
I never before experienced -- my health required
very few hours & the avoidance of all excitement
& this regimen no doubt had a salutary effect
on the moral system -- my pleasures have been
these of the heart & mind, almost, I dare not say
entirely independent of external circumstances, &
contemplating the beautiful scenery of which I have
a view from my windows, an inexhaustible source
of the purest enjoyment -- Oh this I leave for the bustle
the frivolous society & amusements of such a city as
Washington -- characterized by those Rechenries which
belong to a court -- or seat of government -- Now,
it seems easy to keep aloof from the interests &
intrigues of which pervade the whole mass of society
every individual being more or less connected with
the government -- but I throw myself too well to
hope to be cool & indifferent -- I remember past
scenes, & fear, to trust myself -- yet's will do my
best -- But I am so easily excited -- last winter the
Fair, cost me a fit of vertigo -- & there is to be
another Fair -- and there in the city my time will
not be at my own command -- the long, quiet morning
devoted to writing & reading which for years I have
enjoyed -- these I cannot have -- for myself, I would
not venture on the charge -- for my girls I am not
only willing, but desirous to live in Society -- It has
