Sidney June 24th 1809
It was my intention to write
the moment I had read your long & welcome letter
but as I had just finished reading writing to Jane
& as Betty contemplated leaving me in a few
days I thought it best to write by her. But the
situation of parting with a dear friend is not a very
instructive one for writing. The poor woman and I
feel in kind of reconciliation, a dislike of leaving
her a moment, that renders me unfit for writing.
betty has shown more affection to her husband
& every day has shown more affection to herself,
I never saw one who rendered themselves no useful
indeed I feel as if she had done too much &
often by force taken her from her mother. But
she never thought she could do enough for me
or the children to whom she has been a mother.
She has a most generous disposition to the servants
Mrs. Jones will have reason to remember both her
kindness to her favours. She is greaty very greaty
self was never thought of. I have had other friends more
entertaining & more interesting, but it would be impossible
to have one more kind & affectionate. Her
example is to me a new confirmation of the
preference which should be given to a noble rather
than brilliant character. I will furnish you with
a new indowment to try to render my family, industrious,
a useful, rather than accomplished. They must
enter at once into the approaching separation, & I do not believe
they will leave me more than a sense of duty to her mother, would
have induced her to leave me with me and Susan
have done. But she is nearly anxious to be with her
mother the 3 ensuing months, as she says she suffers from
heat. I hope my dear sister you will both lead
Betty to a greater intimacy than has ever obtained
between you & she is dependent & reserved, but I never
was a heart warmer or more constant in its affection & I
am acquainted with every thing that concerns you or my
parties & will speak to you of all that interests you
