I have just put Bayard to sleep, the girls are
sitting up stairs with their hymn books, & while all
around me is quiet, I will devote a little while to con-
versing with my dear sisters. I arose at the time intended
& execute it with much greater pleasure, since I found
a letter from each of them on the breakfast table.
I visit them regularly some of them, since I found
my attention in the city is diverted by such
a variety of objects, that I have not time for one upon
which I am under much to a distance, or to create
anxiety. Here "the world forgetting, by the world's stream"
my time passes as tranquily as the calmest stream.
The dear children comprise all my business, amusement
& comfort, & if ever matter had reason to be grateful
I have -- yesterday the rain poured down in torrents &
prevented our intended excursion to the city, which at
first was a great disappointment to the girls, who
were all prepared for the ride; but they were now
reconciled to it, by talking of the pleasure of a rainy day.
They went through their lessons with great
delicacy & delight, & then eagerly seated themselves
to their sewing, they put Bayard in one corner of the
sophia, & Matty between them at round him & while
Julia sewed on her foot, Matty cut out patch work,
& I was sewing by them, Susan read aloud to us from
the "Parents assistant" a very favorite book of theirs.

overflow with such sweet pleasure, I could not help
