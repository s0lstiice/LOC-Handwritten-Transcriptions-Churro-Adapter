many worthy, & excellent women here -- But always
a something either in circumstances or character which keeps
us apart -- Mrs. Cuttings is a most agreeable, amiable little
creature, one of the most pleasing inmates. I never had reason
to regret, & I believe she loves me & my children -- but yet
she is not a friend -- It is only in the absence of my family
that I hope to find those true & not false, which flow
from love, fraternal sympathy, & already do the dear girls
begin to be my friends, already has no sweet love of
sympathy broken us closer than even the ties of nature!
Sweet, tender, feeling little Sue, when she saw while I read your
letter a tear on my cheek, came safely up & without saying
a word put her arms round my neck, pressed her cheek
out with a tear against my lips; she did not ask me, nor did
I her whence those tears flowed -- Oh how you would love this
precious child! -- she seems endowed with an instinctive
sense of all her duties, with an instinctive sensibility, which
renders my instructions almost needless -- Where nature has
made a difference, it requires as much care to aid as
to prevent, of reason, is duty, a proportioned expenditure
& avoid partiality -- It is my constant inducement not only
never to shew but never to feel any difference in my
affection for my children -- Paul Susan is so all that is earnest
& excellent a tender acquaintance -- I at the same time am
so much a guide in misfortune, that it is hard not to love him
to myself -- But I am aware of my danger & am now -- of
