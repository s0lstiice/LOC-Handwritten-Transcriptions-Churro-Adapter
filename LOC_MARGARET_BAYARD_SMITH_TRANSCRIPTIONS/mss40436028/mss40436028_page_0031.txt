I always rejoice on awaking on Sunday if I find
it a rainy day, & more still if it is stormy & what
others call gloomy. This kind of weather has the happiest
effect on my mind tranquili zing & elevating the soul, attaching
the scattered thoughts & softening the heart & love
while the rain beats against the windows & the winds
roar amongst the trees to sit silent a solitary listening
to this music of the elements. At such moments
the mind wanders over the past & finds a moun tain
pleasure in pondering on days forever gone. A bright
sunny day dissipates the thoughts, quickens the anim al
spirits, & leads the busy fancy thro' scenes of
future life; in which the mind, animated by the
society of nature delights to exhastiate, instead
of sitting in serious meditation on times now
murkly with eternity. It is when the soul is suddenly
we best enjoy all the pleasures of intellect & the ten der
dose of affection. How kind is Providence, thus
to make even affliction sweet, as well as medicinal,
like the fond parent, who when obliged to administer
a bitter potion, accompanies it with some
drops of honey to lessen the disgust.
It is only on Sunday that I feel at liberty
to with draw myself a few hours from the children
& enjoy silence & solitude, & even these rare
preious hours seem to be stolen & are often
relinquished to the dear girls, who are so acc ustomed
to be with me, that they deem it a
great hardship to be sent from me. Susan
