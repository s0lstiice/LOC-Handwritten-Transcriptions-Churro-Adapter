Bernard, Dec. 9th 1812.
Your letter my heart expressed truly expressed my heart. I heard of your misfortunes and sublime feeling and felt with only sorrow about your 40 hundred times as I was going to write but I have not done it in a gentlemanly and humane manner in writing when my feelings are in such a state of turbulence. I wanted to know how I could inform you if you were here and how I could hope to help that one man who was so hard pressed and willing to give up his intelligence. At length your welcome letter came. I wrote my thanks, yours to you but you are again expressing your gratitude and asking to give me another. I hope you will help well and give yourself sufficient time to recover from the difficulty which you have in another. I am glad to see that in the matter of the Mexican boundary question you are more anxious to secure the Mexican people than to secure the United States. It is impossible to appreciate the advantages of your present situation which affords all the pleasure you formerly enjoyed with about complete independence. May it be possible from the various individuals who have about you, your presence will at last enable you to manage them with like success. I shall feel interested in all your arrangements and long to know the different scenes in which you are engaged. With you would make it a point to mention your love in all your business and give me a sketch of the incidents that will you meet the events of circumstances that occur and in which you are interested.
