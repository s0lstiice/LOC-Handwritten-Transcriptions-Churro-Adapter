From Norwich May 12, 1855.
I am exceedingly sorry my dear Margaret that
you so impatiently ask me to mention our alarm and apprehension to
you. It shall be a lesson to me in future. It is not right to cast
the piece of our friends after us over such a distance. God in
the time I was writing forgetting that circumstances should not
be I should have told you and communicated all my fears. For many
days we were indeed extremely gloomy. I heard of various
accidents and deaths and symptoms of insanity in our own
family brought it all home to me. This did not last long. I
have been greatly distressed and I have been able to write, and moved to her own home. On the 1st
of May, I was greatly distressed on a new scene in connection with
the family. I hope they will do well. The man, who has married Hannah, is
a comforter & joiner by trade. He appears already capable. Smithson
had united labour. I think will be sufficient for a comfortable
support if they are favored with health. It would very strange
to part with Sally. I thought I should have told you more, but
for some time past she has been quite satisfied by her own
comments so that I have no need to speak of the charge. I am very
much favored with the women I have got, and if they come
to me as they have begun I shall not wish to look further.
