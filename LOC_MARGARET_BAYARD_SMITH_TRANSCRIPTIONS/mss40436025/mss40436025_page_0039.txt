Thursday 7 May 1800
Did not intend writing to you this morning, after the long
letter I wrote to Maria, in which I mentioned several things
to you, but I feel in such a wabbling mood, that I cannot
say quite any firm. What a charming season is the present!
I have seldom enjoyed it so much as this year.
This Island has all the luxuries of hill & valley & water
seats of gentlemen, which are so thankfully exhibited, give it an
air of elegance in some places which is finely contrasted by
the wildness of others. I have read out every other thing
& generally taken different sides. I have too had some pleasant
talks with my friend Francis. During these rides I walk
my dear little Mary Ann is always thought of, how often do I wish
to meet little Constance, & it is seldom my absence from
Brunswick produces regret, but when I think of her I do wish
we should take more I at home. Infact tells me the situation
of your house is changed, the situation of fine, & its necessity to
be shut up every day. I fear not many regret the little land
Royard & Hyattston I fear will quite forget me. It is almost
six months since I left them! I shall not leave Julia, until
he is quite recovered. I began to wish the time of my return
had arrived, for if I go to Maryland I should wish to have
some time frequently with you. This however is doubtful
