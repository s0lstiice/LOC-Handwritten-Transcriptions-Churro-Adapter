I have been in some expectation of a letter from you, which I suppose the
Times here has received from New York might satisfy me, but I who have no sentiments
or disposition in every thing else am never satisfied in this point.
So long as I am writing to you I will be one of my chief and most
agreeable employments, it is still one of my most agreeable
but I have to make it subject to others. Indeed I have
so little time for drawing that I am almost tempted to give
it up and not to attempt French. I find much more employment
for my needle than I expected. But I will endeavour
to preserve, & make the most of my time. But I cannot find
anyone now nor will he pretend to give me any regular lessons
so that I did not see him all last week tho he called
almost every day & when he did come, company prevented
me in a few days. Sister goes to the assembly tonight if she can procure a hour's report I have been
employing these 2 days for her she is very desirous that I
should accompany her, but according to your advice I
decline it. I am still receiving added obligations from the
kind attentions of the her family, & the attention her father has
made of me agreeable but I perceive a very great differ-
ence in the manner of the people here & those in New-
York from the present appearance of things, I expect to make
but few acquaintances & no very intimate intimate friends
