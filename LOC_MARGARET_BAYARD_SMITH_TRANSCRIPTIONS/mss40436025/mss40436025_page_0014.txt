Until this time he has been in every respect utterly
indifferent to any thing but his own interest and
happiness. But it appeared to me that there were
two great interests which he might have to consider
which were not only incompatible with each other
but which he might have to choose between. The first
was the preservation of the Union. The second was
the preservation of the Constitution. If the Union
could be preserved without the Constitution, I would
not have been willing to sacrifice the latter. If the
Constitution could be preserved without the Union,
I would not have been willing to sacrifice the former.
But I have now come to the conclusion that the Union
and the Constitution are inseparably united together
and can never exist with the dissolution of either. I am
not aware of any instance in which a government
has been established upon the principle of the Union
and the Constitution without the consent of the people
and without the support of the people. It is the
principle of the Union and the Constitution that
the people are the only source of all power and
authority. It is the principle of the Union and the
Constitution that the people are the only judges of
