Life is a stream, subject to the varying tides, & exposed
to all the resistances of storm & whirlwinds, of tempests &
colds. Sometimes when its surface is smooth, we go
pretty quiet with the elements, pleased with the reflection
of a bright day, the beautiful scenery of its shores &
spending in the gentle breezes which pass us along.
Then, we can never be little men, without experience,
to be safe & happy thus alone. But when storms
come, when the billows roll, & the opening waves displace
rocks & quicksands, then when our strength is exhausted
the soul dejected by fear & alarm, then we realize
our timely 2 helpless state, & not for the satisfaction
hard of companions, for the nothingness of friend,
& above all, nothing assistance, where such cheering
forces, as that which true love affords!
There is in extreme pleasure, an excitement of sensation, which
by increasing our sensibility, enables us more perfectly to enjoy
our transient moments of happiness. In periods of great sorrow
or the contrary, the mind is deadened by languor & the eyes
covered with weeping, naturally close & find relief in sleep.
This is the goodness of providence, though even amidst
its irritations.— Alaska
