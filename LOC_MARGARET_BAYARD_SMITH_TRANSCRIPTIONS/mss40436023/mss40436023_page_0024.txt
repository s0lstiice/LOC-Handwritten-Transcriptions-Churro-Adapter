to Washington led me into more society than usual.
She & Fanny passed a social day with us, which I greatly
enjoyed. I could scarcely realize any time, much less
a year had intervened since we had been passed a
day together -- The season was the same -- the same pleasant
truths -- we sat in the same places & talked of
the same friends -- How wonderfully well Mrs B. looks for
a woman of her age & what spirit & activity -- Sue
and Fanny, made hardly any appearance. To pass away the
long manner, I read them the little MSS. I had got to
you -- The deserted Child, Whether Mrs Bradford's tears
were as much at her command as her smiles I knew
not, but whether voluntary or not, my narrative required
her a fancy of some kind more than once -- He & she
--- motions of excellent taste & beautiful time & I
did not flatter me half so much as their time & un-
interrupted attention -- I know of fewer pleasures that
satisfy that of exciting this kind of interest & attention
The Prairie sheltered by a hundred indifferent people
or paragraphs would not equal it -- No praise can give
me pleasure, unless bestowed either by good judgment on dear friends --
& the latter is for the sweetest -- Your dinner, we were
joined by some of your friends -- acquaintances I mean -- Mrs &
two sisters Rushie, Mrs Johnson, Bradford, Clarke & a dozen
more -- making a social & agreeable little party
Mrs Bradford seemed pleased. Mrs Bradford asked us all to
her house the next after noon -- We went -- the weather very
delightful -- Kalorama lovely in her flower garden in all
its pride & glory -- It was a charming afternoon -- 2 charming
girls -- Mrs Johnson then invited us -- It was an evening
pretty, very large & crowded in eleven rooms -- Mrs Rush after
wards gave a larger party to Mrs Brown (Mrs B's daughter)
& Mrs Bradford -- it was elegant & agreeable -- I suppose social
at the Mayo all the evening -- I suppose social
evening for water Mrs B -- at Mrs Brown's -- So you see how
I returned with increased delirium to the solitude of our dear
room -- which I have since seldom been tempted to
leave -- Mrs Brown I have seen more of, & in
a more social manner than I ever have before -- as
I called it, to Sister -- we have had quite sentimental
