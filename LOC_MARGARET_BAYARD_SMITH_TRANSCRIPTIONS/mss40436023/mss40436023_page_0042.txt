't her consolation & support -- of her mother, whose fine

memorv while it drew for the tenderest sympathy
of my heart -- raised me above this world, of cheer
& sorrow -- But even in that excited moment, I felt,
my sympathy could not be so true, as that of a heart
that had been wounded with similar afflictions -- I
merely laid my tear upon hers -- but effective words of comfort
had not to give -- for these experience only can teach
what I could do -- I did -- & she is comforted by such
sympathy as I can feel -- & such few acts of kindness as
can perform -- I often take her out & riding & often
cheerful fire-side -- But poverty -- poverty! -- Oh it
is hard, very hard for those to sustain, who have been
accustomed to affluence -- Her husband, not only failing in
his establishment here (a commissary store) but by his fault
involved in debt to a large amount -- has advanced in age,
without any prospect, of any possibility of getting any
occupation -- I have tried & other friends have tried, to procure
a clerkship for him -- Mrs Sister, did her very best & I used a
manner of persuasion & argument with the mind, good natured Sec'y
