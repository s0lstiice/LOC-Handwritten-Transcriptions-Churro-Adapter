Christmas day. 1835
Merriment, dear Maria, would not be
engagement to us -- Therefore I will not wish you a merry
Christmas but most sincerely hope this anniversary of our former
happy holiday may find you in possession of substantial, rational
tranquil happiness -- or, what is the highest degree of felicity
we can possess on earth, a contented mind. Contentment
founded upon a full persuasion of the government of a good
& merciful God, whose dispensations, whether in accordance or
in opposition to our desires, we feel to be best for us, -- This
conviction can give us a contented mind even in affliction.
They will be done! Oh when we can say this with all
our heart, what matter it whether the world smiles or frowns
Although my case, is not one of unmix'd sweet
ness, yet so few are the bitter drops mingled in the draught
of life, that it requires little effort to say, "they will be done"
"since I have so much more to enjoy than to suffer --
"yet the return of a day, that is separated with the
recollections of so many dear departed ones, is to me far
more saddening than enlivening -- Oh yes, -- I feel at this
moment more inclined to weep over days that are
gone, than to gaily enjoy the present, or with smiling hope
look forward to the future -- The future! who can count
on any future in this state of existence, when so
many -- are daily snatched from life without any warning --
Mr Henry -- he whom I left in full health, in
the full enjoyment of all that gives value to life --
a beloved family, friends, fortune, active & extensive usefulness --
he is gone! -- Anne Erwin, the gay, the happy, the respected
beloved, the darling of so many fond hearts -- alike the favorite
of fortune & of nature, without one moment wavering, snatched
from her husband -- her children & her idolizing parents --
