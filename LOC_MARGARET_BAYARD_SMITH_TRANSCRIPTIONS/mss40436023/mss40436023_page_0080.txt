but society to which Miss More introduced you --
I have read Stewart's sketches & agree with you in thinking
there was an indelicacy in addressing the letters to
your Virginia, whether betrothed or not betrothed. -- The family
in South and especially, absolutely & seriously deny there being any
truth in the rumour of any desir'd connection. The letters
are very injurious to those Mrs Stewart wrote from the
Sandwich Islands -- One reason, Virginia says, is, that they
are all mutilated -- None of them are given, as they were
written; the most interesting & confidential parts were
omitted. -- I am sorry I did not write by Capt Riley
but I did not know until he called to bid us farewell, that
he was going to New York -- When I mentioned you to him,
he said if I wrote, he would with great pleasure carry
my letter over to Brooklyn & go to see my sister -- He
is a kind, warm hearted man & since his first introduction
to me by my dear Mrs Beecher, has felt like a friend -- I
have quite domesticated in our family -- He has no talents
for conversation & need, being thrown out -- a general
dispersion of the recent inhabitants has taken place
& Washington is now as quiet as a village -- How many
of the hundreds who have separated -- will never meet
again. Our, in a strange state of society -- made up of
persons from all parts not only of this country, but of Europe
throwing here in crowds -- eager -- bustling -- agitated by various
& often conflicting interests -- no monotony here -- every reason,
may every week & month bring change & variety -- new
faces -- new interests -- new objects of every kind -- in pictures,
fashions, -- works of art & nature -- for exhibitions -- as all descents
