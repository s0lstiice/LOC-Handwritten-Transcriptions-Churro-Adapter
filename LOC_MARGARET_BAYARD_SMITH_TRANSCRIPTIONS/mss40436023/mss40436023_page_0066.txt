from Washington, Feb. 19th Wednesday.
Poor Dr Stevens! what a dreadful blow has he received
what a shock! Can this unfelted frame sustain it? Scholastic
amazes, very amazes, to hear of him -- this sad reflection reminds
the immortal genius & his devoted & kind attention to me, unfounded Scholastic
is the sympathy it has awakened, that I feel as if some affliction
had befellten myself. If even in the midst of health, gratitude
prosperity, life, falls upon the taste, becoming not only wasted,
but often thoroughly wearying, like a fruit to a man
without an appetite -- an uninteresting or a thorn told tale
that a heavy load must it be, under the weight of such an
affliction -- How can those who do not receive support, beyond
any this world can give them, how can they endure it? Even
in the best estate, it seems to me, this life would be intolerable
if it were the whole of man's existence. It is only in its connection
with a future promising life, that its barrenness intensely
appears a deep felt interest -- An interest sufficient
to stimulate us to the performance of duty, or strengthen
us for the endurance of calamity. It has been in my happiest
moments, I have most deeply realized the importance of these
things of this world -- their ineffability to satisfy the demands of my
mind -- what even at the same time of most complete enjoyment,
consisted in craving for, I turned, disheartened by
disappointment from the best things the world had to give -- It is
only by contemplating our mortal being, as a state of discipline, of purification
for a spiritual life, that it becomes valuable & useful.
