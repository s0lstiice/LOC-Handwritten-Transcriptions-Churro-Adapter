January 17, 1834.
It is a rainy day, & therefore I may hope it will
be free from the continual interruptions to which I am
subject on fine sunshiny days- though as they have been
over the winter, you may imagine according to that rule
that most of my days were an interrupted leisure. But I
know not how it is, I have very little leisure for either
reading or writing; interchanging visits with about a 100
people consumes a great deal of time- now I think
at of 12 visits which I had, & were pleasant interviews-
with Mrs Parham I eat I am sure more than
an hour & when I left by, thought to myself our
conversation might have furnished two interesting biographical
sketches, with the effect of perfect contracts. She began
by making inquiries about Mrs Clay, which from the interest
he took in the subject led me into many details
and gave a continual list of speakers, which made Mrs
Parham only a listener; but we exchanged our parties
& she became the speaker, I, the listener, where she
entertained me exceedingly with the story of the
Mrs Stis- theasher Mrs Stis- & most sensible, but informed, frank. I hope to improve
our acquaintance into intimacy, particularly, as she
likes, Miss Ledg with comes on to stay with her- I offer

daughter Mrs Mandal- But she sees her friends-
