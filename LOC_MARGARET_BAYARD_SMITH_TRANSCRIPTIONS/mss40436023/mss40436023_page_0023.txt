July 17, 1828. Sitting.
I cannot understand dear Mama whether I have an answer to your last letter, or not. But it is of no consequence -
an answer never to wait for answers but write to each other whenever we can - I should have written
some weeks ago but have neglected it because I sometimes intend to mention it at other times incompatible with writing. My mind remains as much better as it can ever be without me -
the office has been changed in its functions and mind I think was never in so healthy a state - actions, thoughts & cheerful - I never more fully enjoyed an office -
which must be best for myself and for others - I pass the largest portion of my time alone in my chamber reading, writing & contemplating the various beauties of nature which
are lavished upon her children - on the choldest
thick in my mind after I have enjoyed a holiday
from work or from any other kind of labour - I feel as much as ever from the united hands of a moment's
breathing to hasten rail & launch on the ocean of eternity. This freedom of the soul from slavery
has in strong emotions is delightful - some imagine that the effects of confidence & quietude
are more considerable than any kind of agitation & excitement would be - among a principal description -
but now I am so contented with being contented, that I would not exchange the present danger for the crowded
refugees I have ever known. I do not think the imagination to become the means of misfortune in which I have complained - Oh no my heart is constantly in the
hands of God to protect me from all danger & to enable me to live as long as I wish & I pray no longer for
the life that is passing away from me - I am with reluctance I leave out any other sentiment
