So how many may to all the sentiments of your
last letter how truly can I respond more to repay your very
words they would express better than any I could select, my thoughts
& feelings; especially that passage where you describe the satisfaction
you anticipated from our reunion -- from the hour unburdening of those feelings which cannot be entrusted to a letter.
To me, even more perhaps than to you, it has ever been the best relief of any troubles & sorrows, doubts or difficulties that
expressed my mind, to communicate them to a friend who could fully sympathise with me. Such perfect sympathy can only exist between those who have had similar experience; whose condition in life has been alike -- Only a mother can perfectly comprehend & participate in the hopes & fears, joy &
sorrows of a mother -- The same rule holds good in all the other relations of life -- as I have frequently often expressed to you, it seems to me no friend who can be so perfect as that which unites sisters -- and often have I recorded it as the peculiar felicity of my life, that my sister more my earnest, most faithful & confidential friends --
Absence has loosened the ties which united me to other friends but seems to have strengthened that which binds me to my situation -- The friends of my childhood remain the friends of my age -- they are still spanned! And shall I indeed once more see you -- I hope, most sincerely hope you -- you will understand what are my wishes -- in the same circumstance which prevented my visiting you in June when I was all ready to do so, may again prevent me -- Should it prove so I will then explain to you the cause, as I shall have little hope beyond the autumn of placing another visit for next spring when the cessation of the Bank exigencies, Mr S -- office will cause a change of course. He obliged to new model our mode of life --
I trust on a much more unresisted plan --
