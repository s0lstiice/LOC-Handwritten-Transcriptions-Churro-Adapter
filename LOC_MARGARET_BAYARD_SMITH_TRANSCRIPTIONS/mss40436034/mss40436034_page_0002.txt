[1833]
Yesterday Anna & I went into the city to make some
purchases preparatory for her journey -- your letter was given
me at the Bank, containing information which put all our plans
out of the question & the only alternative that remained was
to give up the wedding or give up the preparations --
I preferred the latter, as I wished Anna to be at this
happy re-union -- I must therefore depend on dear
cousin Mary Ann, to advise Anna as to what is necessary
which in the great Empression of fashion, she had I presume
she can get at short notice -- and I do her freely, & she
will get whatever her cousin thinks be coming & convinced
her intention was, to have gone on the last of
this week, (expecting the great event, would not take
place until 1st of November) to have stayed the inter-
vening time with her friend Mrs Hewat; to have
gone directly to her house, & not to have gone to
Eighth Street, as James Bayard, who knew that Anna
& Bayard proposed coming on, told him, he was sorry
he would not have a house to ask him to, as
he anticipated going to lodgings -- Now, we knew not
how our friends in Eighth Street or at Mrs Astley's
are exactly situated, & to which house she should go --
But as Mrs Astley told Bayard she should expect both him & Bayard to stay with her, I have
advised Anna to go first there -- to see you, & be
guided by your advice -- In all things dearest sister
treat her as your own. -- Yesterday one did not get
back until four o'clock, having to wait for Mrs Smith,
& one therefore in a little of a hurry -- Flurry -- our
friend Mrs Ellison who undertakes yesterday to find an
escort, was here before breakfast. This morning to
talk as of a lady & gentleman going on at two o'clock
today -- you will easily perceive I have no time
for writing a letter -- it is now past ten & we
must be off in less than three hours.
