I never saw him in better health, & spirits
Mr Bourdaine continues very ill -- if peace of mind remains
never the use of her limb, & even if her life is spared --
most -- what added affliction will this prove to her family and
that ever, midday ceremonies with her mother -- of course this must be
never be demanded -- but try to save her every day in her time --
settling with her by the side of Mr. D. who is not able yet to leave
bed -- George & S. have had some attention of cold, their chairs of
iron replaced on the fire -- but both have come up enough to ride
but not to walk out -- though more easily by jumping over the
swamps & gorges -- & though they are very strong & healthy
and open to any opportunity -- & I hope they will not
The Exhibition of Pictures has produced about 200 dollars clear of all
expenses -- the is divided between the Trustee & the Catholic organization --
Bourne -- who has not sold all for $6000 dollars -- of the rent to Mr. C. by
Bourne -- has been paid out to our friends Cable & Bailey -- he would have
been glad to have called on you -- my paper is paid -- The girls have
come in with their work & our book accounts as you are reading this
day -- weather the wind & rain -- it is very interesting to regard the
talents of the day --
Farnwell -- that which gives a great part of
the stores without doubt -- as an admirer
& friend of the country
It is a pleasure to me to write to you
from Sidgery, 10 April, 1858 Thursday
My health perfectly
From your affectionate
BRONSON
W. H. BRONSON
