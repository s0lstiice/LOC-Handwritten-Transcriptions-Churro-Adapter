March 23, 1833.
A few days since I received my dear sister's
letter, and your expected letter; from which I heard occasionally
from your Bayard. But you were rude, I was generally
impatient for some honest communication, some
interchange of thought a feeling that now that the
communication has been made I cannot quite understand it. That the breaking up of your relationship
should be a most painful business, events of past
time would be the case. The idea of it has
been painful to me & though convinced of
its eligibility to publicity I can hardly recommend
myself to the thought of your leaving a home
so pleasant & endearing -- and the garden too!
I am sure that I sympathize not only
understand but perfectly sympathize in all the
tender regrets you feel. But decent rules, your
letter implies something beyond these natural
feelings. You speak of such a pitiful scene --
