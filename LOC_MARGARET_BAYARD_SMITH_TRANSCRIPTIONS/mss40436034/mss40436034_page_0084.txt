I can scarcely see to guide my pen.— a The girls will soon come
in— your & my son left me after dinner & for a wonder 5 hours
been quite alone. Anna passed part of last week at that
& continues quite well. I thank you my dearest sister for your
suggestions in regard to her. So late week a vigilant look out at
present there is no cause of anxiety— Julia's health remains as
I found it on my return— By Dr Sewall's advice she deserves a very leni
gement & takes a great deal of exercise & her mind is annexed to
interests by the secret of her constant visitor Mr. Gauledet— He is a
good young man— but more 5 cannot say. He is too poor to have any
serious designs in his attentions & 5 see no prospect of his getting on
any where. Whether his manner or appearance are improving— but
seems truly from & he & Julia go frequently in the week to different
churches & few days pass without a long visit—
— what a dreadful fine they have had in N.Y.— The intelligence has
just arrived— but no particulars— Another example of the negligence
of this world's hopes & schemes— "Our life now poor a trifle time." Dear sister
we are drawing to its close— This I realize & my thoughts dwelle
often on the solemn change. A Dear dear sister & friend I ever love
your affectionate M. H.S.
Mr. Washington City of Len
Bernard
Fig.
