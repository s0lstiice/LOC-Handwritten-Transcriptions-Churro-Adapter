We had a very pleasant evening. Collett read
aloud to us Mr. Smith's article on
Col. Howell from the San Francisco Post of
July 17. Collett said he, Wilson had
never seen or read "the real Col. Howell"
and if nothing had been sent to Collett
it must have been done by some
one in the admiralty office whom he knew
and without his knowledge.
Collett said she had a little built day
present when she would give me now,
but when I left we both forgot it.
I called her up later & she said she
would send it to me tomorrow.

at the Board meeting in Georgia sent me from Atlanta a copy of a letter, from Winston to
Wilson to give Wilborn enough time to do everything in his power to arrest
the invasions, because in his district,
he said, this idea was to go the limit.
He then also sent a copy of Wilborn's
letter, dated July 20, giving a review of
what the Atlanta bank had done in the
past and was doing to assist embarrassed
banks, also quoting an old letter to
him from E.W.C. praising his work to
the limit.
If there is an ordinary that Winston
should have written such a letter, it would
have been just as much on our Board
to write the collector of customs at V.J.
and give him instructions as to training
the baggage of returning tourists.
The Board then took up Curtis's application
of one extra month's leave to go abroad.
