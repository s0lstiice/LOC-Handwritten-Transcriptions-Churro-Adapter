May 31. Leicester. 1861, 1641
Musten wrote Bel asking it to enter N.Y.
Res too to give 2000 gold in London at 4.96 +
a senior claim. He replied it looked to
best web, had helped Musten & that Gent.
Musten had offered N.Y. Bank to buy
4.98. Musten said Equit. It is only offered
web. N.Y. bank said if it did not it would
break off whole transaction.
Harding said N.Y. bank had been very liberal
that it cost 5.04 to ship gold & that it
had been offered 5.15; that 4.98 was
shipping cost &c. I, however, think actual
cost of shipping gold.
Compt. Bullions said Rolling steel had
a deal w. Musten; Clint B told him
he did not know why his combination
was continuing or not.
I told him it was his duty to tell the
Presidt. En H said he intended to
tell him.
see Quincy, Aug. 1924; Believing quite a reputable
man & satisfactory blunt of his relations &
muscle.

June 3. Harding
to W Harding was letter from Presidt W. Harding
to me June 2 protesting use change of
method of N.Y. to Genl. U.S. for gold to be
delivered at Bk of England. President said whole line
now little about it Bel stood courageously
inquest web. It was not treating Musten
unjustly. En H said he would write President
fully telling him all about Balling.
Bel voted to nobody except U.S. to meet if it
did not buy for this gold w. in 10 days
the amount could be withdrawn.
En H said Cowley told him Musten had
interwitted to him that was great
deceit for keeping money out of this
financial situation. & Cowley replied
shortly that such rumors should not
be made to him.
En H in a previous letter to President
said Musten had speculated in Shanghai
of change on his own account as well
as on his own. I fear he will apply
this gold to his own financial account.
En H said he believed Sen. Lee was
acting as ally for Musten & that
he would receive support for his
services.
