In evening I began a letter to Catherine which I
omitted the next morning. Sunday, Dec 25 and
immediately mailed it.
It read as follows:
My dear Catherine:
Christmas Eve.
The sun has set but I can not let it
rise again before trying to tell you how deeply your
gift has touched me. Returning from a brief walk
this afternoon we found it awaiting us,
delivered through the courtesy of Mr. Amory
I embrace my hands briefly, humbled as I
looked thru water glasses, but when I read
the beautiful message, I can only say
that the tears rose to my cheeks and gathered
to my eyes as I thought of the "deep that
cut me more".
And as I said I felt that dear Annie
was hovering over me with a smile of
pleasure in her face.
She was, as you know, the idol of our

little, and what would we get and neither one of us
utter despair in the deep feeling and conviction
that she has not really left us, but that we
merely lack some cause or source of seeing her,
and that she is constantly by our side.
Here are the feelings the message brings
to me. It is well done and we shall always
look upon it as one of our most beautiful
homilies.
As to your second thought of me, I can only
say it has deeply touched me and that
you hold us in a bond of sympathy and
obligation which time can never break.
With our heartfelt wish that the new year
may bring you every happiness, and with
much love to you and yours, believe me,
affectionately yours,
Charles S. Hamlin
