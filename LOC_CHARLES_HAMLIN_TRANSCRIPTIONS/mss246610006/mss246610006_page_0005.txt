I also felt worn out and really sick.
finally I went home and told
Berlin I could not wait until Sunday
but must go tomorrow Friday by the
day train. I then telephoned Dr
Clute who said he would see me
at his office Friday evening.
We had a difficult Friday evening for
the Belgian Ambassador & I begged
Berlin to stay over on it. She also had
to make arrangements for sending her
servants home as we decided it would
be better to have the hotel house ready
for me after my operation. She did not
want to do this but I told her the
operation undoubtedly would not depend
on some day & she could
leave Sunday or Monday. I informed
her this would be no obligation
before she came, unless it were
some testimony of will. The
night went on at one.
Finally I found her to consent &
took the day train for Berlin --
Friday the 16th. The day before I
told Harding & Houston and they
were very proud & sympathetic.
1920
Dr. Houston told me in confidence that
he also found her had the same trouble
and seemed much worried about it.
On the way to Boston (my water was
drawn at 7 am by Dr Fowler) I began to
have water about 1 PM and it was
continued almost every 20 minutes.
We were stopped at many stations &
the U.S. C's were locked and I suffered
tortures. Until I began to vomit
was about, I had to remain in the
berthhall between the cars.
I finally reached Boston when I found
me & we went to Dr Clutter. He
did not go 14 or 15 told me an operation
was absolutely necessary. I told him
to go ahead & he telephoned the City
Hill Hospital & engaged a room on
the next morning. That night I slept
at Congress house.
Sat. morning April 17 I went to the
hospital, after breakfast at 9 o'clock,
& immediately went to bed.
At about 10 am Dr Clute arrived
& I at once went up to the operating
room and stretched out on the table.
