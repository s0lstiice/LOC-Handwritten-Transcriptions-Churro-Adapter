Sept 15
En H. told me that Miller sent for him yesterday
and said he was still confident P. H. will
support him, and would give him a recess
abhorrently often congress adjourned, with
a few weeks. En H. told Miller not to trust
him too implicitly and not to hesitate to
drop his name at any time, but that Miller
said he certainly would not drop his
name. Miller also said he informed the Dean
had told the President it would be foolishness
to support him and that the
President seemed very much distressed.
Platt showed me a copy of a letter to Sen.
Sumner in reply to a letter from him asking
me to certain of states of Williams revolted
by Sen. Hepburn in an attack on En H. &
the Board delivered in Senate last
Thursday Sept 12. Platt's letter was personal
& not official. In it, he said that Lincoln
was guilty of deliberate falsehood --
source of his statement, & suggested
sobbing this somewhat by saying intentionally
misleading or something to that effect.
