Tuesday.
United with Mr. Cyrus Meyer to meet
Dr. Bergmann, an official of the German
Exch., over here to establish a plan for
stabilizing the mark.
He said that Germany industrially was
in splendid condition; that she
should now bring in all the cotton and
cattle and grain they could and
had no difficulty in paying cash (
gold) for them.
He said Germany -- leaving out Rheinland
Pfaltz -- now had a balance of budget.
He denied emphatically that Germany was
now earning more to pay expenses. He
said the German people were all
chickens up in marks, that they
turned out the bread they had
into lemons and bought foreign exchange
with it and that they were what
caused the demand for marks;
that the trouble of Germany -- not
the Exch. -- held out about a billion
dollars in dollar exchange.
His opinion was to borrow 50 million
