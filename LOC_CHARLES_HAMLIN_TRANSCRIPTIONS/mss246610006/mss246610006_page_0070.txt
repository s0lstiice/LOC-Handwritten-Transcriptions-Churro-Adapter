Born Slidehara, the J. Ambasssador, called
this (Sunday) 11.45 and said really
two things. He is leaving on Tuesday in
Sunday to undergo an operation for
"stool." We talked in a general way
about the Powell Treaty and I informed
him correctly that Johnson and Taussig
would ratify it at once, notwithstanding
the Senate's reservations.
He said he had no doubt but that John
would do so today, without any reservations
on its part.
He also said his Govt was considering the
purchase of an Embassy building and that
the British House in Chicago circle could
be bought, at any price, I understood
him to say, of one million dollars.
Bertie and I told him that this house
would undoubtedly make a very
good Embassy building but that we felt
the French was far too high; and also
architecturally it was very crude and
unartistic.
I suggested that it would probably be
cheaper to buy land and build such a
building, and suggested that the Govt
consult Ralph Crane, of Boston, and ask
him to come to Washington and look over
possible sites for the erection of an
artistic building of the best light of
American art, in harmony with Japanese
artistic principles, and with a typical
Japanese Garden attached thereto.
I said Crane would probably be glad
to come to work, on a very reasonable
sum and advise the Govt and then at
which he would be satisfied for himself whether or not
to employ him to design it. I pointed
out that Crane designed the building,
at West Point and had been called
into consultation on Temple N.Y. Catholic
and was a very eminent man in his
profession. I also told him that Crane
had, within a few years, published a
book on Japanese art and was intimately
interested in the subject.
I also said that John should erect
a beautiful, monumental building
sent Slidehara a note giving him Crane's
