April 10, Sat.
During a conversation with Sen. B. and Mr. Peabody, I said that I would be reluctant to act as a matter of course, and send him to the Senate if there was anything he could do to help me in my command. I suggested that I should talk with Sen. Eliot, who, I said, he would do at once. He said he was on very cordial terms with Mr. Coolidge. He also assured me that he would guarantee that I should get every item Senator could do with him to give Collett a House and ask for my support.
He said Sen. Peabody was more partisan, slightly, and intellectually dishonest; that he had made a fatal failure in the Senate.
Mr. Bruce said Peabody once told him that he was not a prohibitionist and did not believe in prohibition, but that it was good politics for him to work to be dry.
Sen. B. said Peabody can not be responsible.
Sen. B said Sen. Reed (Pa.) was a good lawyer, but somewhat wanting in integrity. Heambled that Pa. politics was making its mark upon him and not on the good.
