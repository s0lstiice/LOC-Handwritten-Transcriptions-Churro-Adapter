The term Petrus was then taken up by
Harding who moved to adjourn Petrus that
no action would be taken until return of
Meade & Warburg but that matter could be
settled not later than June 15. I agreed
to this as I felt if cleanups were not in
force by that time, I should declare of
them ever being in force. I said we ought
to study the evidence before the Bk. Clay.
Court & that this question was squarely in
issue there, almost every witness being
asked as to West. Corn. by see readers.
I agreed, however, that a final vote
could be taken not later than June 15,
altho I did not commit myself
as to how I should vote.
I asked authority to take up with
Cong. Hill the attitude of Eastern Corn.
as if it were made clear that they
will content w. Boston it would make it
easier for me to let West. Corn. so if I
found that it sufficed anyone under the
post. Res Act, which however I believed it
would not. I also wanted out that 10 out
of 21 banks in West. Mass voted to go to
N.Y.
Harding said he would hold vote to
amise any part of Mass. to N.Y or to
assign Eastern Corn.
Warburg said he would agree also never
to vote for either and especially he would
never put the N.Y. line farther east
than the Corn. river
