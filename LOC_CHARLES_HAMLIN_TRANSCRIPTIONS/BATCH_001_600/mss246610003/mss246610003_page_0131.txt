Sen. H. never deceived politics as well as any
way, except that after our vote, at which
my vote was defeated -- in the afternoon
of Wednesday July 26, in presence of Harding
Sen. H. began to cheap of the political effect
of doing justice to the cause. Senator H at once
interrupted him and said I shall absolutely
decline to consider the political effect of
any decision of the Board, that every question
would be decided on its merits. Harding
laughed & said he had seen so much of
politics, it did not stand him a influence
him in any way.
This was the only wise indirect mention of
politics made in the matter.
During at Met. Club w. Sen Brandegee. Delano
& Harding discussed together near me and
Delano never came over to comprehend
me.
He is evidently very mad with me.
I fear it is too guilty consequence on the
matter.
Had talk w. Elliott as to my suggestion that West. boys be
allowed to buy ships presented to them by them
by remitting cash or claim to chief. Bk. for account
of them. Bk. He said he thought this would not
solve the difficulty; that unless strictly
limited to West. claims it would permit
arbitrary exercise of exchanges and tend to
ruin clearing system. Did not favor the
suggestion at all.
Also had a talk w. Harding on Wednesday,
I think. He said he had no doubt but
that the transfer would leave them Res Bk.
credibly strong enough to have cast of all
demands from its District. Also said the
District was a wheat district and that
he was inclined to think, considering the
adulterated flour, it was not well to
errate diversified districts. We spoke
of Boston as a district where development
of resistance business could probably
be its chief work in future.
