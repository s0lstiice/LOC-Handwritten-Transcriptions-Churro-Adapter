May 9 Sunday.
Lunched at Boys Town Club with
the Hon. Milton T. Sis. Met Justice McReynolds
him. Speaking of sending of Sentinels he
said he was for war & that Justice Sis
told him he was also.
May 11. Tuesday
Called up Meadow 6.30 P.M. He said he was
sorry I had Collected so frantically w Brandreth
who had told him of our talk. He also
said he wanted me to send him affidavits.
I went right over. He seemed very distressed
at my letter w. Brandreth's call. with my stand
that I might have to testify as Williams in
this long drawn matter. I told him that I
felt it my duty to tell B. all I knew
about this matter -- that it was hardly
necessary to say that I had no desire to
testify at all but that in the necessity
of being summoned I should testify
to the actual facts, but that it could be
here thrown out my business to say
anything on the matter. I also told him
that B. told me -- before I said a word --
him that an honorable & equitable conduct
was not justifed. I again strongly advised
an honorable settlement of the matter
as a local -- however it might result
technically -- would inevitably drag on and
injure the President, the Administration also.
He said he could not object to a fair
settlement & I assured him that the
Editors must see that many of his
& forms allegations -- even if not relevant
to the Bill -- would greatly injure the
Bill & I felt sure they would make
some offer of settlement.
