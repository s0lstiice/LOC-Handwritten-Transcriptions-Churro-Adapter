59
7 P.M.
Called at 2340 S. St.
at dinner Mr W. Clarke to Randolph, referring
to me as Mr. Hamlin, after dinner I asked him
"you were very formal," and she laughed and
said, "that was an inadvertance."
I then said, "you have never asked me to carry
you by your first name, although I have done
so in several letters; I feel I ought to
abolish it to you." She quickly replied, "you
must absolutely have the right to do this without
any request, and I want you to do it."
I told her that I should do this only in
the family, but when outside an individual
"believes and I look on you in a double
personality," an almost a Queen Dowager
and will treat you accordingly, but in the
interest of the family, it is most important
for me to think of your utter personality."
She made it perfectly clear that she
wanted and expected me to carry her
title, whenever I chose to do so.
When I was leaving, she said before
Randolph, "Good night, Charlie! Charlie
and I have been too formal, and this
settles it," or words to that effect. I then
whispered, "Good night, lady."
