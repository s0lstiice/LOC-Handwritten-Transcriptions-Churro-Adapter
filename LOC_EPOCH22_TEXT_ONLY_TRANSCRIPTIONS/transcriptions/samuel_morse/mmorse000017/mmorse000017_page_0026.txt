Copy to Prof. Henry Corwin in reply to his of Jan 24th 1844
My dear Sir,
Baltimore Feb 7. 1844
You must think it strange that I have not answered your
letter of the 24th ult. before this but I have this moment received it in passing though
this place on my way to N.York which I trust will be a sufficient apology for my
apparent neglect. I have read your letter with much interest and it has determined
me to make a visit on my return from Mulberry which will be the beginning of the
week perhaps on Tuesday morning 13th inst. If any thing in N.York should prevent
me from being with you then, I will drop you a line on Monday morning.
Spiritual questions occur to me in this hurried moment, (as I have in
the morning and carry this with me to Princeton on my way to N.York) suggested
by your results.
1st How does the result you have arrived at affect the experiments made
on my 80 feet of wire of 160 miles?
2d. What distance apart must the wires of a circuit be to prevent
the cross cut? Will any insulation in a tube prevent it?
3d. If my conductors are placed or poles suspended in the air, will there
then be any danger of cross cut?
4th. Now is it that Wheatstone, who has extended his conductors
for 20 miles and more, has not discovered this difficulty of cross cut?
I found the difficulty which you apprehend in the insulation of
my wires, but this I will explain when I have the pleasure of seeing you.
In the mean time believe me
With sincere respect Your obliged
Sam T Brown
Princeton
