Washington May 17th 1844
Sir,
I have time but for a few words in answer to yours
this morning. I am under the necessity of visiting Norfolk
for two days and leave in the morning.
I cannot spend time in disabusing your mind of
the illusion which presides it in suspecting me of

there is no agreement in any shape whatever, on the
subject of which it treats, you made a proposition through
Mr. Cornell to be submitted to the Secretary, I acted
as the mere reporter of your proposition of reference
and of the Secretary's answer, I am no party to the
matter and know nothing in regard to it, and if
you wish any information on the subject, you
know where to apply for it. It certainly is not
to me. I have never seen the Secretary's report
to the Committee nor have I been his adviser; I heard
in incidentally that it had been sent in to the Committee,
and I am as ignorant of the contents as yourself.
