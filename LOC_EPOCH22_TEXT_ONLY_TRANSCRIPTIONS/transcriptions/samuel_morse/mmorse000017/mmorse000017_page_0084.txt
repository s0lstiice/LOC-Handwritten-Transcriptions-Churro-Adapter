Washington March 4th 1844.
Dear Sidney,
I have just received letters from the
children at Pats Rice, and I presume you have
letters also by the same arrival. They are well, except Mr. Lind whose health seems not quite established
They advise me of a barrel of oranges for me,
if they arrive in good order please ship them to me
to Baltimore to the care of D. Barback Esq. 14
Commerce St., but also direct the one at Washington
at Hon. H. L. Ellsworth's. They also say that there
are some sweetmeats coming by the "Joseph" in about
10 days, if these arrive, they are for my friends the
Ellsworths, please direct them in the same way.
I have nothing new. Smith continues to annoy
me, but I think I have got him in check, by a
demand for compensation for my services, for seven
months for doing that for him which he was bound to do.
The agreement stipulates that I give my services for
"three months and no longer", but at his earnest solicitation I remained seven months longer, and was his
Agent in "negotiating the sale of rights," which by
the articles he was obliged to do; consequently I have
a right to compensation, and Mr. E and others think
my claim a valid one; if it is sustained, the table
is completely turned on him, and he is debtor to me
for the amount of 6 or 700 dollars. I have commenced my operations with posts which promise
well at present. I have no time to add only
