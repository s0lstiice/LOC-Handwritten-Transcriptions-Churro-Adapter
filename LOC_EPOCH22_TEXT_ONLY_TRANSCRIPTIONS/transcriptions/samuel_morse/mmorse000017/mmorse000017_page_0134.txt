My telegraph letters go on well at present, the whole matter
is now critical, or as our good father used to say, there is a
crisis at hand. I hope for the best, while I endeavor to prepare
my mind for the worst. Smith if he goes forward with his claim
is a ruined man in reputation, but he may sink the telegraph
also in his possession; but when he retires, we hope through his
friends to persuade him to withdraw it, which he may do from fear of the
consequences. As to his claims privately on me, I think I have him
in check but he is a man of consummate art, and in principle
he will, therefore, doubtless give me trouble.
I wrote you yesterday giving you a little trouble to
verify the copy of a letter of Smith's to Sathan V brothers
which it is important I should have. — Please drop one
line respecting the oranges which Susan says she sent me in
the "New Haven", and the box of sweetmeats sent by the "Joseph"
I am anxious to have them especially the former lest they
spoil.
Sove to all again,
Truly Yr Aff. brother
Finley.
