him, when I learn that he is engaged, with those who
are attempting to supplant & infringe upon my inviolate
thoumy obstacles in the way of my interests, before legislators
& league with hostile interests against me. At first I
had not listened to the fact, for I did not believe it possible.
This own letter to Mr Kendall left no doubt in my mind
what now can I do? Let him put his finger on a
single act in which from the moment I have known
him, to the present, I have not acted his friend.
As to L.J. I rather pity him. With such a weak mother
nothing can be done for him, that I see -- If then can he
I shall be glad to do it. The only way in which I can
previve myself from a desolation, which injustice suggests,
not to interest myself for any one again, when my kindness
& my exertions in behalf of others, are thus rejected, is
to reflect that this Conduct towards me, is wholly to dis-
gracing, nor ungrateful nor unworthy, as my own con-
duct towards my Heavenly Father, & Redeemer. It is
but one of those revolting manifestations of human nature,
which we feel keenly enough, when we are matted the
Sufferers, but to which our own selfish hearts are blind
while we daily & hourly show the same deformity to our
entirely benevolent Friend & Father. It is this reflection
alone, that takes out the sting who in gratitude has planted
in the heart, & wages on in the state of him who makes
his sun to shine on the unjust as well as the Just, &
the rain to fall on the thankful as well as the
unthankful.
In haste to tell as well
S F B Mott
