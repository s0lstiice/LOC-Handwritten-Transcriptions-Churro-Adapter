In all these cases the intention was to mark the dots and dashes
comprising the Conventional alphabet, & under the hood on a cloth and
paper specimen of the marking to make being that place, his present Telegraph.
I recollect my telling him that the plan of marking by decomposition
was designed on board the ship, that I was familiar with having Dr. Jackson
innumerable papers disposed into a solution of Platinum salts, was an agent at that time in Paris
who was asked to have the experiment tested upon a telegraph line
and many times afterwards both in Station and Telegraph.
Blodgood remembers the result of these experiments in 1835 or 36.
I never used but one circuit in my Telegraphic marking. My characters
are only adapted to a single circuit, and were devised expressly to accomplish my
purpose of imprinting at a distance, with a single circuit. This is an important
feature, and bears on the subject essentiality.
From a notice in the N.Y. Sun newspaper this time must
have been previous, because the Sulph. Acid which I had obtained
for making gun cotton, was shown to M.K. with the remark in my
part that it had been crystallized, and my salts were purchased
previously and were on my table when M. Kent was shown
the acid.
