The Machinery for printing the ordinary Roman or other
characters, as far as I have described it consists of the following
parts. There is also a simple thread revolving over two
pulleys and moistened in the Solutions A.B.C. thus in Fig. 2. When
the marking is to be on metals, the thread over the pulleys p.q, is kept
moistened with the Salts in Class C. and a as in Fig. 1. is the
metal cylinder which is marked by the thread either of at the point of its
contact with the Cylinder, whenever the electricity passes
through the cloth. In the first case the thread is marked black, in the 2d case the thread colored purple
or otherwise is bleached forming the letter in white.
I use also a simple thread revolving over two
pulleys, the upper one grooved, and the under one plain
and kept moistened with salts Class. C. when marking
is desired on metals. But the cylinder a is then much
enlarged or a metallic plate or strip is passed between
the cylinders a and the under pulley.
Sam W. B. Muny
Note to description in caveat.
Although I have named in the above specification
several kinds of salts and substances which may be used for
the purpose of marking as aforesaid I do not confine myself
to these but I use any known substance decomposed by
electricity to produce my effects.
S.F.B.M.
