I have been longing to publish your masterly pamphlet on
American Slavery, but have been deterred by your request
that if one should arrive in the country, not to publish till
we got your revised edition. So I have waited till now, I
have today sent it to the Union, and Richard writes that it
will appear in the Observer also this week. I had read it to
several Members of Congress, and they were so desirous of
having it published that I have been for some time tempted
to publish notwithstanding your request to the contrary. I have
taken care that proper credit should be given to you, and
have not hesitated to give your name in full. I
believe it will be read with equal interest in every
part of the country. I think you have most effect-
ually closed the mouths of English men on that subject,
and in so doing have paved the way for a peaceful, and
proper abolition of the evil as fast as providential circum-
stances will allow. No foreign interference. Especially
we will allow no interference from England. We can do
without her help. Indeed her help is hindrance. The
odium of Slavery recoils upon her tenfold from her arrogant
and officious, and impertinent interference in the matter, after
the centuries of wrong she has inflicted upon us, in forcing it
originally upon us. You say right that she must approach us
in the attitude of penitence. Yes upon her knees.
I hope you will pursue the subject. You will be listened to
at the South, when others will not be heard.
Good night, I almost fear I have missed the mail.
Truly Your Aff. brother as ever
Sam. P. B. M'Nary.
Sidney L. More Esq.
