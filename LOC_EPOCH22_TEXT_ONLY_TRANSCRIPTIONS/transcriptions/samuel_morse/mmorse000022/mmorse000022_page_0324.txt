Washington May 17th 1847
Dear Morse
I had hope on Mr Kendall's
return he could tell me something of the progress
you had made with your printing Telegraph
But all he knew about it was that you were
working at something - I hope to see you very
soon and see it work. Of Mr Houses I hear
nothing and guess a little that he can find no
one advance money for such visionary operations.
To me and no doubt to it will be a cause
of grief to learn a little incident which has occurred
with in a few days passed - You are
well aware that the Corner Stone of the Smithsonian
Institute was laid a few days since - On the
announcement that such would be the case, I
addressed a note to B B French Esq inquiring of him
If a copy of my work on the American Telegraph could
would be worth depositing, To which he replied
"certainly" and stated that Mr Mill Atchelert was
the proper person to address upon that subject - I
accordingly addressed Mr Mills a note upon the
subject and he at once called upon me &
stated it was both novel and very desirable to
deposit a copy describing an invention so
purely american and so astonishing and beneficial
in its results. & he cheerfully and thankfully
