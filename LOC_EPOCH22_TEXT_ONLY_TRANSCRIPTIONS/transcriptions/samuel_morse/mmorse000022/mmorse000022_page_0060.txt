I have already devised a mode of applying this effect to mechanics
for printing either the Common Roman letter, or any other character.
My apparatus as far as it now consists of two instruments
to which I shall give the names already given to my
Telegraphic instruments for printing my Correspondent,
to wit, the Register and the Correspondent. The Register is at
one extremity of a single line of electrical conductors, and the
Correspondent at the other.
The Register consists of a type wheel, with letters or characters
of any kind and size, revolting on an axis in a frame; on the same axis
are two ratchet wheels, one larger than the other, having the teeth
equal in number to the letters or characters of the Register, and
the corresponding ratchet wheels are the reverse of those on the other. The type wheel is moved
around its axis by the force of a lever attached to an electric
magnet like the pen magnets of my present Telegraph, in place of the
pen, however, I put a ratchet wheel, which plays on the cog of the smallest
