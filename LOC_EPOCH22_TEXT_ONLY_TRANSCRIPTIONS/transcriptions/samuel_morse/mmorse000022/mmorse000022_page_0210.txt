New York March 23 1847
My dear Sir
In answer to yours just received I want say that
I do not wish my letter to you published. I wish not to
attack Dr Jackson nor even defend myself in public from his
private attacks. If in any of his publications he
attacks the constitutionality of his claim, which I consider settled by default, then it will be time, and proper for me to notice
it him. If on looking over back numbers of the Boston
Post any one should notice that my challenge was never
never answered by Jackson, and the question should be
put to him in a public journal why did he never reply? He
wanted either to be silent or to reopen the subject. If he
chose to be silent, you might make your own inference
and the public would also make the same. If he wanted
to reopen the subject, he would have to admit that he
had prepared to defend himself, but I wish not to be instrumental in reopening a controversy
which is settled in the public mind. The most charitable construction of the Dr's conduct, as a monomania induced by excessive vanity.
I enclose you an article in the Herald of this morning from Paris, which shows the matter of the Lethon in a new and to you certainly an interesting light. Please send me any publications respecting the Dr which may appear. If you visit New York I shall be happy to see you.
Edward Warren Esq.
100436
Respectfully yours
S B M
Abner Doubleday
