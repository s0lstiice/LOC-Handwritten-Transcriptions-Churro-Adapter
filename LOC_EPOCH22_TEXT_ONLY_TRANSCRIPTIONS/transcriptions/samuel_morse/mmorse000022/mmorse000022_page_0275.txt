New York April 27th 1847
Dear Sidney, I received your short letter by the Cam-
bria, and was rejoiced to learn that you were all enjoying such
excellent health. My own health was never better; I am
actually growing stout, not quite a Daniel Lambert yet, but
never to him than I was a year ago. This you will recognize by
the date is my little dog, 36 years old, only think I shall
never be 26 again; dont you wish you were as young as Sam?
Well, if feelings determined age, I should be in reality what
I have above stated, but that leaf in the family Bible, those
boys and that daughter, and those nieces & nephews of younger birth
and especially that grandson, they all come in putting twenty
years more to those 36, I cannot get them off, there they
are, 56! I have just received letters from Susan, they are
all well in Guayama, and she will probably come to New York
in June; she says she has written you or Aunt Catherine
by Steamer from St Thomas. Richard and family are well
he went with Richard & Mary down to Quogue last evening
in a slop, I was at Aunt Salisbury's a few days, week
before last. She is well; in her new house; quite comfortably
and elegantly arranged. Pres. Woolsey is popular, and Old
Jail is flourishing. I was also at Utica last week to attend
Board of Directors of Peleg. Co. We are thriving. Cousin Tan-
ny is very ill, although I have just received answer to Telegraphic
