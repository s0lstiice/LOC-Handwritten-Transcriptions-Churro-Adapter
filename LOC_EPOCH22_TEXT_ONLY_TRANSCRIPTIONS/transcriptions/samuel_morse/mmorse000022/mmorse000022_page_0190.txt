Copy of Judge P. Staples' opinion in my Patent. Re
New York March 16th 1847
Charles Frederick Johnson Esq
Dear Sir: My absence for the week past
attending a court at Norwich has prevented an earlier answer to the questions proposed by you for my consideration and opinion. The first question is as follows; "Can the claim for the use of the motive power of magnetism when developed &c. as the means of operating a printing mechanism which may be used to imprint signals upon paper or to produce sounds for telegraphic communications be sustained in this general form; or would his claim be limited to his (Morse's) method of applying this power for this purpose. In connection with this question you refer to the fact that Steinhil in Germany in 1838 applied this motive power to imprint signals upon paper & to produce sounds. His method of operating was described in a public work published in London in 1839 - Sa Surgeons annual of Electricity, and also Vail's work.
Second Question. Supposing Mr. Moris claim is to be limited to his method of imprinting signals or producing sounds what is regarded as his method? The suggestion he is entitled to hold his specific machinery with which he operates to make dots and lines - do he also entitled to - or can he hold the system of signs consisting of dots and lines for this purpose Steinhil recorded by means of dots alone upon two lines and vary by combination of marks upon different divisions of the Paper. Then would it be an infringement of Mr. Moris rights to make signs consisting of dots and lines by the motive power of magnetism in a manner different from his? For example by the deflection of the needle applied to press the paper against a stationary pen or pencil or by demagnetizing a permanent magnet by passing the current around it.
