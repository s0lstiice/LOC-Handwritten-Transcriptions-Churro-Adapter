I made arrangements sometime ago with a gentleman in Washington
Jacob Bigelow Esq. to procure me a special act of Parliament
for my Telegraph in England. I shall write to him immediately to urge
action. Your plan if I write very
impious, and would be well
to make for Curiosity's sake. Perhaps we will make it when
you come home. For practical purposes, of course, it would be of
no use. I wish you could get me some account of the action of
Bains Printing Telegraph, or any other if there are such. If a letter
from London saying that they are exploded (if such be the fact,) or
that they are useless, as compared with mine, it would have good
effect here.
I have left Wash t altogether, and am settling in
New York. I have fixed up my office in the old place in
your buildings, am having a snug little bedroom attached
and am preparing for work & study, on economical principles.
I have just tried to see Mr. Munson, but he has gone out.
He may write you by this opportunity.
I sent through Mr. Kendall your letter on Slavery to be
inserted in the Union. I have not heard yet whether it will
be published by the Editors. You struck just right there.
What do you think came out by last Steam? Petitions
from Sunday schools in England signed by Teachers who
some of them made their mark, praying that Slavery might
be abated in the New England States!! I saw them at the
