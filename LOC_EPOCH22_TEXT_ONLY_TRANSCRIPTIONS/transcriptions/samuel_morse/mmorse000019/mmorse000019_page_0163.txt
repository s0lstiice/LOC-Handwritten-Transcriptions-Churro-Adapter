Dear Sir,
I am aware of the intention of the French Government to appoint
to the Telegraphs, and to telegraph through the Journal
that the government of France had appointed
an inquiry Commission into the various plans
of Electric Mail Telegraphs, and I was then the
point of addressing a note to M. Arago, one
of the Commissioners of the subject, when
your acceptable letter was received. I take
the liberty of sending copies of the documents that have
at various times been laid before Congress.
In closing my letter to the subject of Kearney's first publication, I
refer to page 627 that in 1838 my Telegraphs
in Report of the Senate, was examined in Paris by a
Commission from the Minister of the Interior, and reported upon favorably. I have better Patent in France
and experience has suggested to me many simplifications. I have no drawings of these or I would send
them to you with pleasure. I will add that what
I have communicated also to M. Arago, that
should the French Government be pleased to adopt
my telegraphs and should my experience be tested
to establish it in France, I will if proper inducements are offered me either give or my personal
attention a familiar competent experienced person as my substitute.
With the honor I be with
respects
Yours
S.G.D. Mase
Sup. Elec. Mag. R.
Washington Dec. 31st 1844.
M. Frederic de Burggrafe
2d Consul de France en mission
à Boston
