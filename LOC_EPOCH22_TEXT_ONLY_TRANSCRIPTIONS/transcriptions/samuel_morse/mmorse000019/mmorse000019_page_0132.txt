Copy to F.O., J.S. W. Dec 20. 1844
I read, of 12 inst. on the road as it is in your
answer in Bank Mag.
Do comply with fine on the subject of having laws to protect
Tele. conduct from injury they all agree that any agitation of that subject
would be detrimental at present to our enterprise. Their heretofore receptly
yet for such laws and experience had better dictate them when the necessity
exists. I think likewise that any proposal to purchase attachents present bill and opinion
its progress through Congress. But I am fully of opinion that after this bill has
passed a resolution appointing a Com. to negotiate with Rep. for furnishing of the
entire right would come with effect and be likely to pass if I could give our
terms. Will you not give me by return of mail what you would be willing to take
for the part we own. While I wish a proper compensation I think a bid in the
hand i write ten in the bush, and if a the whole right could be set down at
$250,000.00 and we could be paid that by the Government would you
not be satisfied? Please write me briefly on this subject.
Respectfully I
B.B.M.
Received Washington Dec. 21. 1844 of Prof. Mase
Superintendent of Telegraphs Fifteen dollars in
full for wages to date.
$15.00
H. A. Brown
