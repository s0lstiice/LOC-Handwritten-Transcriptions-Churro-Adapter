The operator must give
the usual sign of acknowledgement that all is right
in. . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . .
and understood, and no other operator must commence
writing until he hears this acknowledgement, if any part of
the message is obscure, the usual repeat must be given, and when all
is understood the finish signal must be given.
When any one operator writes he marks the same
character on all Registers at every station though
the entire line will be written for when the message
is intended. If it is intended will
stop then Register & paper reel, and listening to the
strokes of the pen will wait the finish signal.
In acknowledgements of the receipt of a message must be made at once, either by sign or figure 1 which is intended or by figure 2 which is intended will
stop the Register & paper reel, and listening to the
strokes of the pen will wait the finish signal.
When a message is intended to be sent from the other station
to Washington, every thing must be for a longer or shorter
time than intended, and then writing the respective station sign, and
signifying that it is ready to receive. Each operator must be prepared
for a longer or shorter time than intended, and all are prepared
for the sending of the message. Communication intended for any particular station
shall in any account be copied or preserved for any reason by any person belonging to
the Company shall be visited by the instant principal of the Company.
