New York Jan 24 1845
My Dear Sir
Yours of the 20th reached here while I was from
home. sickness or death has been our constant companion since your life
until within the last few days.
I have just returned from New York where I went to aid in
the last kind offices to the late Mrs Conner who died at our
house on Sunday morning last and was buried at N.Y.
on Wednesday
Thus am my apology for not answering, or rather writing you
before. I shall probably write again in a day or two –
I enclose to you a fifty doll. or note, No 143 Sent to, on
The Seventh Ward Bank. I regret that I have left you out
of the use of it so long – Mr Brown on whom I depended told me
sometime since he could give it me any day when I must have
it, but he has been unexpectedly delayed by the Insurance Company
in obtaining his losses so when I first applied to him he found
more difficulties than were anticipated – I beg you to accept my
thanks for the favor conferred.
Now for the petition. The day after your left for W– I
called on Mr Dudley, of the firm, (J. H. Hennion to consult
about the business of the petition. Mr C. said Mr Chepkin
