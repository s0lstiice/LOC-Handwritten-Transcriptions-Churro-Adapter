My dear Sir
In addition to what I sent by the morning
mail, I would observe that I do not despair of effecting a sale
of the concern in some way. -- Mr Smith told me what
he would do & take for his portion I walk out of the concern
at the time, I did not listen much to it. -- but on reflection,
there may be a possibility of accomplishing a sale so as to meet
the proposition I show you, as relates to an except Smiths.
And I am therefore anxious that you will get him to some
term in relation to what I sent him & myself this morning,
as by so doing I shall be constantly in the way of, I am one
capitalist thusiping the thing moving, and perhaps find some
who may at once close it, and want you own view comple-
tely -- I have it so well interwined & so much ground gone over,
& so punctual to their abundantly all, that with them it
only wants as it were the nail clinched. And sometimes
they will break out in a new place & close up quickly, and
then is much to widen them with me -- as you are aware to our
active vigilance in order to effect a sale, and to obtain I
must be at any our time & effort -- and I will submit to any if on
the whole this will not tend to aid the intent of the Telegraph, it
differs from me, and I am now believe that it will come out
right, yet, and I hope you will adhere to my having the proportion
I made this morning -- and I hope you, I shall endeavor
to aid you in this matter as well as my own -- I will have added,
that I feel grateful to you for the confidence & kindness taken
from me. I shall see Mr Smith as he passes through Baltimore
& he promised to announce when by Telegraph.
Yours respectfully & truly
David Barback.
