My dear Sir
Baltimore October 28th 1844
I thought of writing a line to you while in Wash
ington but had not time — I feel anxious to effect
something with you & the then proprietor in regard to the
privilege of constituting your line of Telegraph — I could not
arrange any terms with Mr Smith as respecting the right
to use it into a Company, — But I think he would
be willing to sell the right of building, apart from the right
of using, either for a sum out right, or for a percentage
on the contents, or a fix Cottage on the profits — if he and
the other proprietor could agree on the terms, I wish you would
talk with him on the subject now, to ascertain if any thing can be
done advantageously to all concerned, in this way, I don't wish
to interfere with the right which Congress will want to use the
invention, but only the right of contracting for building the
line — I did not name any rate. Would be willing to give
to either of you when I was in Washington — but will name
the sum of ten thousands dollars for the right of building
lines, to be paid as you & Mr Smith & the U. S. may think
should be from earnings.
I have written Mr Smith in the letter which we
refer to, another proposal — If he does not show it to you,
I wish you would ask to see it, that you may judge
whether it is not for the interest of all parties to do what
I then propose about the route to Buffalo — I think if I
could get him to agree to it, if you think favorably enough
of it, to encourage it. He professes to be willing to assist me
