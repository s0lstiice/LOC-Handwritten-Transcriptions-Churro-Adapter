The property in the Telegraph consists of the
Patent right owned in unequal shares by four
individuals.
There is money to be made doubtless by Contracts
for the erection of the Conductors & the instruments,
that is to say, Contractors profit,
The first is a definite property and can be appor-
tioned without difficulty, and is the only property
which I desire in the Telegraph.
The second involves risk, and complication of
details, for which I have no taste and no desire, but wish
to throw no impediment in the way of any of the
proprietors who wish to take the risk and secure
the profits of Contracts.
I am willing to part with my portion of the Patent
right at the rate of $200,000 for the whole right.
If the offer is taken before Congress assemble in Dec.
next (1844) that is to say $12,500. property secured to me.
With regard to the Contracts I
do not see how I can possibly receive any profits from contracts
made under the law of the United States, which
holds any Office under government, since the
law expressly prohibits it. And by accepting the
$10,000 you offer I should virtually violate that
law, however it might be technically avoided.
This may be all right, but I had rather not receive
my profits in that way, if it is a paternity, the called it is
the difficulty. I am Superintendent
of Telegraphs under Govt, and it is of course my
duty
