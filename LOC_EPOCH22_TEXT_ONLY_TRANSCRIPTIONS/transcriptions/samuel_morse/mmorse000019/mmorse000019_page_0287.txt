Feb. 18th 1845
Jo. Ladok Platt Esq. Chairman of the Com. on Public
Buildings.
In reply to your request that I would give you
my views on the subject of taking the Years & Keys in the
House of Representatives. I would observe that the present
mode seems to be generally admitted to be defective, especially
from the circumstance of it consuming so much valuable
time, and it is by no means a new thought that another
and better plan could be desired. American
The time consumed in taking the Years & Keys, even
with the experience & admirable tact of the present
Clerk of the House is however less than a quarter of an
hour, and more often 20 minutes. When it is considered
that the Years & Keys are called in an average
times in a day, some estimate may be formed of the
consumption of time during a Session of Congress.
While so much important public business is done over
from Session to Session in the plea of want of time, or
is hurried defectively, through for the same reason,
I think that any mechanical plan to prevent a needless
waste of time must meet with public favor. I have several mechanical modes which I think
would be effective in reducing the time to 3 minutes
which includes of course the reading off the names
by the Clerk if that should be deemed necessary.
