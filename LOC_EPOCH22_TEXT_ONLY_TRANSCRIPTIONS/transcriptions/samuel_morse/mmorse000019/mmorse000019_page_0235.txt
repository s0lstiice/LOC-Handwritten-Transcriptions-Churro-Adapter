New York January 25 1845.
Although an entire stranger to you the populace your name has acquired by the
introduction into use of your important Telegraph offers me the honour of
addressing you in a business way in reference to your
magnetic achievement.
It has been stated in this City that you design
charging your present made telegraph wire from eminent points & that you
propose conveying them under the surface of the
ground furthermore that you contemplate the use of
lead pipes through which to convey the wires.
If the statement is true you have doubtless made
selection of this metal with a full knowledge of its
objectionable properties particularly the corroding influences
to which it becomes exposed when placed under ground.
It is therefore presumed that contrivings consideration
have induced you to have the objections allowed to in
which case the undersigned who is by profession an
architect and civil Engineer would respectfully direct
your attention to a recent improvement of his intended
to supply the place of iron pipes for conducting water under
ground. The material of which around in this locality
