Mr Smith
An expression in this letter might easily present to the
present controversy to seem to sustain his argument that
I had not at that time a Telegraph prepared for use and ex-
hibition in the U. States. The passage is this "Can you tell
"me at what expense you can have a Telegraph made
"for me with your improvements at N.York? If you should
"leave of a sudden we should have a perfect instrument
"to operate with at home. That Deficiency was alone
"what delayed our whole wishes through the last win-
"ter - A perfect instrument should be exhibited at the
"earliest opening of Congress & by it an appropriation
"will be rendered certain. The proprietors must Con-
"tribute pro rata and have one at best made off hand
"for home use, under your own eye Direction. Will
"you write me the probable expense, and propose it to the
"Messrs. Rails I will contribute my proportion." -
On this I remark that the inquiry respecting the expense at
which a Telegraph could be made in N.York, was not of such a Telegraph
as had already been made, a set of which I had already made
"a Telegraph with your improvements." I had made some improvements
while studying upon it in Paris, reducing it in size, and making it of
brass instead of wood, and adding the necessary for a London Telegraph
with various other modifications. Mr. Smith was desirous of obtaining
these improvements, and it is evident from what he says after, that
the instruments he speaks of could not be doing which I was to procure without "charge or expense to Mr. Smith" for they were to be procured
by contribution from the Proprietors pro rata.
