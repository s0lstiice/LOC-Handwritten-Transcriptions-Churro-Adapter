I wish, when the blossoms begin to swell on the large
apple tree, just before they open into blow, that one or two
of the middling sized limbs might be carefully girdled
by taking out a ring of bark, completely round the limb,
a quarter of an inch in width; the ring should be made
on that part of the limb nearest the body of the tree; this
process performed at the time I mention (which is prob-
ably now,) will make those limbs bear larger fruit than
the rest of the tree and ripen sooner; I am anxious to pro-
duce this year the largest apple.—
Give my love to all friends and neighbors, and
many Kisses to the dear boys.—
Your affectionate Son
Sam'l L. V. Moss
