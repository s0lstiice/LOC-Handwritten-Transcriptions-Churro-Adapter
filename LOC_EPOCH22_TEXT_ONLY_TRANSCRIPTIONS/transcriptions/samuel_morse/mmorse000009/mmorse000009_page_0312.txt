Philadelphia July 9th 1828,
L. Morton Esq.
For
I had the pleasure, to day, of receiving your
Circular communication to which, I must mention the
7th but dated on the 4th inst. under other circumstances
and considerations than those in which I am now
placed, I should have been pleased to contribute
to the support of your infant institution My
attention was directed towards it from its first
commencement, I watched its progress with
intense interest, and as I had been labouring
long on a similar project in this City which
I intended to establish with a view to an
improvement of the Public Taste and rendering the
artist independent of an arbitrary existing tax,
which it lays the least of it, stood in the way of improvement
and knew only to debate the profession with a
view to effect my purpose, I commenced a series of
communications in the United States Gazette under
the signature of Natural Truth, in which I endeavoured
to prove that the arts and Public Taste were
