New York Nov. 3. 1825.
My Dear Parents,
You know just before I left you
I told you that I should be unable to write
you often, as every leisure moment must
be devoted to my lectures, which I hope to
complete by March if possible, but I have
made it conditional altogether whether I lecture
or not; I devote only my evenings to this
purpose and if I cannot complete them by
devoting this time to them I defer them until
another season. — My finger and leg are
(I may say) entirely well, they are a little tender
but give me no uneasiness; I am fainting
away with all my might; Our house keeper
is exactly what we could wish, a good econ-
omist and faithful in every thing; we are
getting along comfortably; I was professed
for money a few days since but have got through
the pressure for the present, by pumping hard
I expect to keep my head above water.
