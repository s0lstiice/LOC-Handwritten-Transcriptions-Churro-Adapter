New York Dec 9th 1823.
Dear Parents,
The check for $10 which you sent down by Holland
Coit was destroyed the moment that it was received, we having
been fully supplied with money for all our immediate want
from other quarters.
We received your letter by Mr Hillhouse, and also
one for to be delivered to Mr Henry, which I have directed Mr
Tracy to keep in his possession, and not deliver till further
orders. I have given this direction on the supposition that
it relates to books to be sold at auction. This way of
managing the business is so complicated and so expensive
that it has become vexations to all concerned. You send
down books to Mr Henry, and request him to deliver
them over to an auctioneer; and then comes letter after
letter to me and Richard to request Mr Tracy to call on
Mr Henry to call on the auctioneer to write you or
send you five millions through half a dozen
different hands again; and then there is the passage of
the books from the vessel to our room, and
from our room to Mr Henry's, and from Mr Henry's
to the auctioneer's; and then there is a double course
to be paid, the auctioneer's commission & Mr Henry's
commission. The books must bring pretty good prices, if
all these expenses do not eat up a good slice of the
prospects profits. – My advice is that the books be
left out of the circle, and that the
books be sent direct to Mr Tracy (not to Mr Tracy, case of John P. Haven) and
that Mr Tracy receive all the instructions.
With respect to funds, I have in my
possession Gocelyn's & Stiles' notes for $400 due some time
hence; Collins' note for $100 at ten months; Lockwood's
for $127 at eleven months; There will be due me from
Gocelyn's for U States Atlas in February (you say) $250,
all together and something from Converse on
