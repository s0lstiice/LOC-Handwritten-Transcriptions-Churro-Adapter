Mr. Saml. F. B. Morse,
Seventy, Sept. 13th 1824.
I received your letter in due
season, and regretted by Mr. Field, to make some further enquiry.
Perhaps it would be no more that justice for me to state
he is a young man, that he appears to possess an uncommon
aptitude for landscape & portrait painting -- He has very
recently had an application to paint the scene at Betsy's brae
where the Indians massacred a company of our men, while
they were on the trees, plucking grapes from the vines.--
This piece of painting was executed with taste & elegance, considering
the advantages of the young man -- as a compensation
for which he received 10 dollars.-- He has recently
resided a few weeks in Boston this summer with a man
who carries on the business of painting.-- He could have
married there & had his board & clothes paid him, -- but he
wished to attend to a different branch.-- He wishes to attend
particularly to Portrait painting.--
It would be gratifying to Mr. Field to have you
write & give him definite information as you should
think expedient on the following subjects of enquiry:
1. In what particular branches of painting do you expect
most likely to be engaged? 2. Should he commence with you, can
he have the liberty of quitting, at any time he wishes? 3. Would
there not be some prospect, if he should prove tractable, that he
might pay his expenses, after some months had elapsed? or,
whether you could not, perhaps, afford to loan him, after some
months, by having his services? -- Will you be so good as to
