see you all, and those dear children often make me
feel anxious, and I am frequently tempted to break
away and have a short look at them; but I am
tied down here, and cannot move at present; all
that I am doing, has some reference to their interest,
they are constantly on my mind. — I hope Susan
is a good girl, and will make Papa glad to see her
by her good conduct, by her kindness to her little
brothers, by her obedience to her Grandpapa and
Grandmama, and her obliging conduct to all. Little
Charles must not tease sister, nor strike, nor slap,
but must love Susan and little Finley; and as for little
Finley he must grow and eat milk, and be as fat and
goodnatured as he can. — Tell them all they must
give Nancy and all as little trouble as possible. I
shall bring them something when I come if I hear
good accounts from them. —
