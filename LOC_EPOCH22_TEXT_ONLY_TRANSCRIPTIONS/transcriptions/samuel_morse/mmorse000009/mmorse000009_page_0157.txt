My dear Henley
I have received but two letters from you
the last dated 17th Novr 1824. 13 long days have elapsed
since I last heard from my dear husband. I have been
in a state of almost constant suffering from
the ague and tooth-pain and full debilitation
from the pain in my husband's hand and not to receive
one comforting line from my beloved husband has
made me watch his death with despair. His description - do have
pity on me and write of him if it is but a line.
I wrote this to my dear husband, they had no
party there last night. I was confined to the house with
the ague and am still too weak to go out this
morning as the weather is damp. I regret very much that
I am so unfortunate as not to be able to see them
before they go. The ladies and Miss Fill, Charles grows pretty
every day - he is happy and soon he calls upon them
to come and keep his sick. Whenever I drop a word
he talks about the roughness of his father's face he
and seems to remember all about you.
If I do not hear from you I shall think
you have forgotten your promise to come to love him
Newhaven 30th Novr
1824.
