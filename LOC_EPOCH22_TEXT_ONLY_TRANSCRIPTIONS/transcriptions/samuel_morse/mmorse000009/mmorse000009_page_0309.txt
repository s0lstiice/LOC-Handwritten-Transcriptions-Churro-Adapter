Dear Aunt
I look to answer your last letter relating to the
effects of your family and to assure you that I too believe and myself
do feel a deep interest in your undertaking business which I am to say that
I always intended, when I looked upon it as a duty of your little house
to be open, or as long as you can but to give her with us as an
honorable friend, and indeed a family that it would not be just to take
a little girl into as they are not fond of children, but after the
legislation which is passed we shall have no house and should be glad
if he could come to us. It will be very gratifying to my feelings to
have her with us for it always loves her very much and now feel
a still greater interest in her since the loss of her brother he will do
every thing in our house to make her a useful person and give her
a good education as to Charles dear Aunt I could not at present
have him with me but I will endeavour to do every thing in my power
to assist you by relieving you from the care of little Susan
Mr. Smith father intends to do something for his grandchildren but he takes
a great while to think of it I will not look to him any thing of the
subject but I will endeavour to render him to his duty and I am sure
you have a very delicate and strong man upon his hands of late
years dear and I know the poverty of the large family
