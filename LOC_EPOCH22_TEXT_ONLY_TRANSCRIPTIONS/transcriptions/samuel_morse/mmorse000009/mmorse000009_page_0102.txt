New York, 19 May 1824.
Dear Brother,
I write in haste to state that I have
seen Mr. Morse of Cherry Valley. He says that he spoke
to a number of his friends, after seeing you at
Albany, & all to whom he mentioned the subject
expressed a cheerfulness to have their portraits taken,
should you visit the Western Country. He thinks in
some of the largest villages, a dozen might become
your sitters. There are wealthy men scattered
the whole of that country, & by frequenting
your residence, you would no doubt have constant
employment. Edw. asked him what price would
be most eligible. He said 30 or 40 dollars for his
portrait at Underthatch. This is the amount
I believe of what he said on the subject, which he in-
troduced, which was among other topics this evening.
I only add that if you will consent to
begin anew, as you did at Portsmouth Concord,
I think you will at least be in a fairer way
to discover what know if this the most eligible situation, than you are
at New Haven; & I hope, your success intended success might induce you to give
relinquish the plan of going to Mexico.
With love to Lucretia, & all the families
Your affectionate brother
Rich. C. Morse
