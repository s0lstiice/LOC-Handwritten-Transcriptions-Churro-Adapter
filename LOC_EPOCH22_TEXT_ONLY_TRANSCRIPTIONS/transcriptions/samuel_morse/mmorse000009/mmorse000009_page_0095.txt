New York, April 24. 1824
Dear Parents,
Letters from Richard have been received repeatedly,
since you were here, all in doing subscriptions as usual. The
number now, I presume, does not vary 20 from 2000. My
last letter was from Uncle Sidney's at Scandia dock. I wrote
to the Utica gentlemen, a fortnight ago, and told them
that I should wait a fortnight for an answer, and should
then feel at liberty to publish or to adopt any other course
which might seem just and expedient. I have as yet
received no answer. I will send you a copy of my letter
to them.
With respect to money matters, I have to pay one of my
carriers and agents $50 on the 1st of May, Mr Seymour $400 in cash and a note for
$1000 more on the 17th of May, rent & board for me
to Phyllis more than $50 on the 1st of May, besides some
smaller debts. To meet these demands I have only $40 in
hand, Bottling & Co's note at 12 months for $50 & your
check on the New Haven bank for $50. Where the rest is to
come from I do not know. We cannot collect from our
subscribers in the city, have nearly all paid and those in
the country are not acceptable. It is a great evil in commencing business -- I
am perfectly satisfied that if I had a capital of one or
two thousand dollars, I could make $600 or $700 a year if I
am now paying. -- I notice in your letter to Mr Tracy that
you give him a message to Mr Cord & Mr Pent on some
subject with you do not name. I do intimateat that you would
not publish any more pamphlets or books on Indian affairs at
your own risk. We shall never get rid of our embarrassments
97788 long as this is kept up. -- Mr Bell has 150 copies of my
Atlas pledged for to him on account of his note, and liable to be
told at the end of 40 days if not redeemed
