New-York April 22. 1825.
My Dear Parents, I have just rec'd your letter by the Miss Lawrence, and was glad to hear that you were all well, and that dear little Finley had recovered from his temporary illness I hope the children will not be burdensome to you. — Susan is very well, talks a good deal of Charles and wants to see him very much, I think I shall visit N.Haven the first week in May. I have as much as I can do and am employed all the time. I have heard nothing from Albany yet definitely as to the State picture of La Fayette, I am perfectly at ease on the subject, whether it is given to me or to another. — My new establishment will be very commodious for my Profession- al Studies; and I do not think its being so far "up town" will, on the whole, be any disadvantage to me.
Edu'dt & Richd. are well and doing well. I wrote this in haste expecting it will go by Mr. Boardman this afternoon. When I return I will bring a list of such things as I may want and see what I can have from home. — My dear children from Susan sends love and kisses to dear brother Tallashome and belongs to as many of your affection. Your S.F. Richmond
