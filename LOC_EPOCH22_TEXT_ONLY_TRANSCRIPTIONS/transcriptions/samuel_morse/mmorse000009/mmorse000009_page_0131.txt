if I am to live in poverty, it will be as well there
as any where, and if to make money, why there is the place;
I have run about long enough, I must settle down now.
Please write me, what your arrangements are, in regard to
our return, Lucretia and the children are well and send a great
deal of love. I leave a space for L. to complete this letter.
Dear Mother, I was much qualified to receive a letter from you
last week, and should have answered it had full, & not little
writing rendered it superfluous -- my friend here says, & that my visit
is likely to be shortened -- nothing could have happened more beneficially
than Shirley's return to Concord -- I am glad to find he has been
so well since the journey -- the country air seems to agree
so well with him as it does with the children and myself
as to our return, if Shirley must go to Hartford, it seems unavoidable,
as he has neither the time or money to expect an adequate
journey -- and it would be a source of unhappiness to him to be
again removed so great a distance from his family, with the
possight of their immediate return so uncertain -- I have not time
to add any more but will send love for you all I remain your affy
P.S. Lucretia feels very delicate about expressing a wish to return, lest
