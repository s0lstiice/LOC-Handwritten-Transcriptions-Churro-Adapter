for your good long letter of Christmas eve, it comforts
me much, I recieved it yesterday, I am glad to hear
of the health of you all; what grieved me most was
the loss of your hair, but it was necessary; will it grow
out pretty long before I come home? I regret I did
not paint you before I left home, if we live I
shall certainly do it when I return.—
Aunt and cousins & the Peronneaux send
love to you & a great deal of it, they say
you must come next winter, so I say, I
have very much to say but must defer till another
opportunity as I am in the greatest haste.
Henry is well. Rick? I have not seen.—
Give a great deal of love to all father mother sisters
& brothers; and kiss the dear child for me often; I
am glad to hear she grows; don't make any more
apologies for writing of her improvements, you are
not particular enough, I want to hear of everything
write often good bye — your most affectionate
Husband
