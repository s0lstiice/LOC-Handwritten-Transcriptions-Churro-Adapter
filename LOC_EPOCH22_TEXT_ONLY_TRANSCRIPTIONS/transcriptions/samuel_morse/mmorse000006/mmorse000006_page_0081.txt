June 3rd 1819-
Dear Sir,
I am truly sorry I did not receive your kind letter
until late to-night, & beg you will believe it does not put
me to the smallest inconvenience to pay the little sum
I owe you, be pleased therefore to call on Messrs Kershaw
& Lewis & You will now receive it — I have sent them
by this mail an order for the money — Could any thing
add to the respect & Esteem I have always entertained for you,
it would be the sentiments you have expressed — Those only
who are blessed with them can appreciate them — They jus-
tify the opinion I had of you — Could you instil such into
the minds of my Sons I would value them even more than the
productions of your genius —
A neighbour who had borrowed
a sum of me, returned to me with convenience to himself,
asmuch of it as was necessary to pay you — Messrs Kershaw
& Lewis are truly good & worthy men, but their customers are
all pressing upon them for money & they are unable as they
express themselves to "turn round" which is the reason your
payment has accidentally been delayed — I regret the
circumstance but we must expect now & then such slaps.
They had rice of mine on hand which it appears they were
unable to sell, & they had also the care of a very large quan-
tity more at Mr Paul Trapier's Mill, not yet founded out,
by the time this last arrives in Charleston, I hope we may
