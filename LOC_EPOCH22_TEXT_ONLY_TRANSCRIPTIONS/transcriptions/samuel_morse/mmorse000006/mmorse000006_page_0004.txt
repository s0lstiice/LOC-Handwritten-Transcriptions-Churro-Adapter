January 1st 1814.
My dearest friend, I am to you a happy hour
and not only this, but though many years, many years experience the
loving kindness of the Lord in you and your affectionate husband, gentle
kindness have been my best riches. More than two years have elapsed since an
intimate communication and it is a source of pleasing reflection to me that
not one doubt, has arisen, to darken or obscure the sunshine of our mutual
confidence and affection: time has seemed to strengthen every attachment,
and to give me a more satisfied opinion of the amicable and enduring
qualities of my friend. I trust, my dear, our friendship will never
experience diminution or change that when our Heavenly Father shall
be pleased to disunite it in time, it may be reunited in a blessed and
glorious Eternity. Did you not think of home the 24 of last month I left
it, a year previous, I spent at your house, the events of that period, are
fixed in my recollection as though they were of yesterday. It was then
both Christmas and Thanksgiving. You doubtless recollect the few words
you noted in my Memoirs a book, last New Years night, you and your
brother spent with your friend. I have read it repeatedly in the most
