Charlestown Thursday Jan 27th 1820
Dear Father
We received your letter of the 22 January this morning.
In the beginning you seem to speak of a letter which you had
written previous to which we have not received. We were glad to find
that you were so comfortably situated at Dr. Leavitts, & that your
Indian business was so prosperous. I have consulted the
Tract containing Dr Edwards Observations on the Muhhekaneeuw
language. He says nothing which you want him to say. His
remarks are confined to the language; he does not go into the
history at all. He says, moreover, that "The languages of the
"Delawards, in Pennsylvania, of the Penobscots bordering on
"Nova Scotia, of the Indians of St Francis in Canada, of the
"Shawaneses on the Ohio, and of the Chippewans at the westward
"of Lake Huron, are all radically the same with the Mohagan,
"or Muhhekaneeuw." -- And again "that the languages of
"the several tribes in New England, of the Delawards, & of Mr Elliotts
"Rible, are radically same with the Mohagan" (or Muhhekaneeuw)
"I abst from my own knowledge." -- You see, therefore, that
Edwards is dead against you.
I am progressing to my own satisfaction in the geography.
I go slow at first, but I shall go faster & faster as I proceed. It
will be very important that New York particularly should be
done well. I should be glad, therefore, if you would procure
the latest and best map of the State as you pass through the
City also the latest & best geographical description of the
State = the same in regard to Pennsylvania & Virginia &c.
I do not mean very expensive maps, but late & authentic.
