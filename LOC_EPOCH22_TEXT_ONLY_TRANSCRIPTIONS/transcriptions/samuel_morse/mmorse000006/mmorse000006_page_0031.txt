My dear Son,
Charleston Feb 26th 1819.
For the last month we have received no letters from you -- We have felt more than
your affectionate heart would wish us to feel, that hope deferred, and making the heart sick --
The mail has come -- I have no letters from you -- We know little what you are doing, or what are
your prospects -- I am not enabled to make a remittance to your Benefactors, with it is
very important to be made, very soon else. There will be some feelings excited, which should
not be permitted to exist -- The letter to me is to explain to you what I mean -- I am unable
to pay some debts here, till I receive a remittance -- I am sorry to be obliged to mention
these things, as I do in my letters, but such is the situation (I receive nothing here) that I
am constrained to do it -- I have good prospects of relief in other ways, after
the present crisis, which will last but a few months -- Money has been extremely scarce
this winter & have seldom heard more complaints -- A little relief is now afforded
by bank discounts -- I pray God you may have had a letter & a remittance in the mail
lately robbed of which you will have an ant in the papers. Of which will be necessary to have
your draft renewed & measures taken to prevent the mail from being robbed of the remittance
& your letter. I am your affectionate son
John C. Calhoun
