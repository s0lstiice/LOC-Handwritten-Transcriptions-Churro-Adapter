London. Thank to a and Providence, my absent Brother, for permitting me to
reach you affectionate letter and thank you every time for your kindness in writing
with your my friend, a long letter from your brother dated Lexington where he has
written at last after having been detained 3 weeks in consequence of Breach of
arms in an excellent opinion of your own conduct during all particulars.
I rejoice that your health remains unimpaired by the southern climate. I think
you must spend your time delightfully about improvements to be undertaken by every
man to improve dear England. I think your most affectionate brother
to your family will not be able to persuade me to break up a beautiful family
between you.
I often think how much I should be much obliged if
he would give me an interview in which I could
talk over the state of our affairs. I have not
seen him since the 21st of this month. I am
