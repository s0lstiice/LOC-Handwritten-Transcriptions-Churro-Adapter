My Dear Husband
We received your letter to Edwards dated the 5th ult. and
regret that you are so long detained at Washington which will prevent your
receiving our letters at Philadelphia which require an immediate and
very particular answer. We do hope that you will not have answered
the letter from the Comte to you requesting you to give them a statement
of what you intend shall be laid before the council till you receive
our letters at Philadelphia in which E has given his right
course to be pursued in this business I think you had better not get
home till the beginning of March, it would save you very materially
gaining to make E your agent to settle your pecuniary concerns
with them before you return and have nothing to do with them. I
have called on him to know when it would be convenient for
us to quit the house. told him by the first of May we had
made our arrangements to go and that he should wish to be left
to ourselves to pack up our things without the interunction of
a family with small children in the house at the time of so much
confusion as that would be to us and there could not accomodate
Mr. Fay with any convenience to ourselves he said he did not
think of it in that view before but thought it well enough &
he would mention it he believed some of the Comte was to call on
one of them here however for which I am glad. I hope we shall be
away long before then though I thought I would fix it to the last mo-
