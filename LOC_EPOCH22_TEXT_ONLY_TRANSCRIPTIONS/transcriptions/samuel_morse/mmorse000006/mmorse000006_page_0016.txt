Georgetown January 2d 1819
Dear Sir,
I have had the honor to receive your letter of the
22d instant & am much flattered & gratified with the attention
you have been pleased to show me. – It has always been my
intention to procure from you some first rate pieces & I have
been deterred only by the consideration of your being too much
engaged to bestir that attention upon one of them on which alone
I would be willing to receive it – I hope you will execute this
liberty I take, & the trouble I shall give you, it is the task
which distinguished citizens must pay the community for the
honors they receive – I wish to engage you to paint a full
length Portrait of a young person as large as life, of a very
young & small person, with the richest display of scenery,
that it is in your power to design and execute, the scenery to be
very extensive and large, to allow room for a full display
of your genius – a small part, say the face head & hands only to
be immediately painted, & another part of it to be touched until
you return to the Northward, & the picture not to be delivered
to me until the expiration of twelve Months or longer
if you choose – When you deliver the picture you are to
pledge me the honor of a gentleman that it is the
finest you ever painted, & in the execution of the landscape
you have exhausted all the powers of your invention –
This is requiring a great deal of you, & I would disdain
to say so much were I not fully determined to give you
