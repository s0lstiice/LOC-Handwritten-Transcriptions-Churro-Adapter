We are glad to hear that you have given us your last will &
testament on politics & suppose we are not the first who have
joiced at such an instrument the for different reasons. I hope
that we shall now hear more of yourself. I wish very much you
would tell us something of the divine art of Painting & re-
move somewhat the distance at which I presume an English street-
sweeper might put us to the blush.
Papa & Mama & Safford are writing you all that is interesting
here, as I wrote you so lately you will probably expect less from
me. There is both in your letter to Mr. Hinton's an indefiniteness with
respect to the time of your coming home. You will therefore un-
derstand that we consider the period of your stay lengthened one
year only & are secure in the expectation of seeing you the
next season, & that the old masters will all have been mas-
tered in that time.
