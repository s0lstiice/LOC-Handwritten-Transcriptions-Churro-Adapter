Since I wrote the above I have received your favour dated Bristol
September 9th 1814. from which I find you are probably at this mo-
ment in Paris; consequently the request contained on the other page may
be impracticable. -- We had letters yesterday from Mrs Hull at Cork-
mouth. Jannette is with them at present, and has perfectly regained
her health which during the summer was not very good. -- I do not
remember at this time what I wrote to you in my letter of May 4th.
I hope it was not more than I ought to have said for I would not for
any consideration trifle with your happiness by the encouragement of
falter hopes -- I believe you worthy my dear Sir of her affections and it
would certainly gratify me to see her yours. But while I think you ought
not to dishearten me I am fully sensible that your absence for so long a period
and the uncertainty of your future settlement in life, may prevent that at-
tachment on her part which I am inclined to think your presence and
the constancy of your affection might create. -- You will I am sure duly
appreciate the motives which lead me to use the language of caution.
I am not surprised at the feelings you exhibit with regard to England
or America. The English in general I have so contemptuous an opinion
of us, I am so exalted of themselves that every American must feel a virtu-
ous indignation when he hears his country traduced and vilified.
But my dear Sir it is natural on the other hand for an exile from
his native land to turn with fond remembrance to its excellencies, I
forget its defects. -- You will be able some years hence to speak with
more impartiality on this subject than you do at present. The
men who have involved the country in this war are wicked, corrupt.
