Bloomingdale November 2. 1815
My dear Sir
It is but a few hours since I received your letter
announcing your arrival in your Native land, and I hasten
to express my heartfelt pleasure at an event to me so unexpected
and so desired.— The intelligence gladdened our evening circle and
Mr. Jarvis and myself made it the occasion of recalling many
grateful recollections of the past, as well as that of anticipa-
ting pleasures which are still in store for us when you
shall become once more an inmate under our roof.—
We have lately removed to Bloomingdale, and are within
sight of the house in which you visited us just before your
departure for England. This of course brought to mind all the
associations connected with that visit and we could hardly
believe, so rapidly has time flown, that more than four
years had elapsed since you walked with us over the
lawn.— Your time must be so much engrossed by those
friends who have a stronger claim upon you than
we can possibly have, that I am afraid it will be
some time before we shall see you in New York. I
can only say therefore that there is a room for you
