Charles Town, Decr 6 1814
Dear Son
We have just received yours by Dr. Romain of oct 11th and
was glad to find by it that you were well at Bristol where you wrote
but regret exceedingly that you will make politics the constant
theme of your letters to us. doing thus after such repeated requests
& even commands from us, you wound our feelings exceedingly
& sometimes we are almost sorry that we permitted you to go
to England, as you appear so warped & contrary in your
views from what you would have been (as we trust) had you never
left your country. You must drop the subject of politics, if you
wish that your letters should afford us that satisfaction which
they are eminently calculated to do when they are turned
on any other subject than that of politics. Your descriptions
of the scenes that you witnessed are highly interesting to us & and all your Friends, but you the Discourse
which you manifest at finding that the people in England
are just like every other people on the Globe pleased with
the successes that they meet with over their enemies because
those enemies are your countrymen into one to one way very
perfectly ridiculous. It is a weakness which you ought to conquer
and not foster in the manner you do to the great Injury of your parents
as a painter and to the grief & mortification of your affectionate parents.
How does your persisting in thus one-sided with what does not belong to you
as a painter, and which must, from the manner you
have taken it up, prove extremely injurious to you accord
with the strong professions of gratitude which you
so frequently express in your letters to your parents for
all the attentions they are constantly paying to you
