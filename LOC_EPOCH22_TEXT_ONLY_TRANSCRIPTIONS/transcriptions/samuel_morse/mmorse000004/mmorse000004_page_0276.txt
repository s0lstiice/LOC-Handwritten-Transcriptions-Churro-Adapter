Charleston June 1st 1815.
Dear Son
Received your Letter of the 21st of March was happy to find
by it that you were in health and industriously pursuing your Profession
we were pleased to find that you were receiving some money to support
yourself we hope your means will increase and that you will be able to
do as Mr Leslie does, and be independent, in time we were relieved from
any further responsibility as to your support if Providence should enable
us to do as much for each of your Brothers as we have done for you
we shall be thankful that , but we know, not what is before us, in the
Cities we received from you during the two first years of your bringing
London you appeared pretty confident that by the end of the two years
you should be able to support yourself, this you find has not been the case, we
give you reason to expect that we would allow you $300 a year for three
years we have since added 200 a year to the thre fourth year also a 1000
for a fourth year, all of which except 100 or 150 have been transmitted to
you making as you will see $4000 which we have hired and are now
paying interest for, to gratify you in the permit of your favourite
Profession – I don't mention this by way of complaint, in the proportion
you have made in your Profession, from the reports we have had, and
with the accounts of your correct conduct otherwise we are well pleased
and also with your expressions of gratitude in your former letters to
us for making the many sacrifices which under our many circumstances
we have carefully made to aid your views, you requested in
some of your first letters that your affectionate parents would continue as
they ever had done to counsel, reprove and advise you from time to time
as they should in their wisdom deem necessary and give us reasons
