your sentiments coincided exactly with our own we should have advised you
to keep them to yourself your busying yourself with such things, could do no good
to anyone, and was calculated to do yourself, more harm, than any other person
could do you, by making enemies of those who might have it in their power, to serve you
spiritually, in the way of your profession, did you not offend their prejudices: I do not charge you with being so imprudent as to have
caused any alterations of the kind which have suggested, by expressing
such sentiments to those around you, as you have, from time, to time, expressed in your letters to us, but fearing you might in your zeal to defend
the character of your country, drop an unguarded expression, which would
injure you as to your prospects, we have thought it our duty to advise you, to
be prudent, in this respect, and not give your tongue, as much latitude, as
you did your pen, we should love you much less than we do, if we thought
you capable of not loving your country, with all the ardour which you
express for it, we consider this as a very amiable trait of character,
which we have not the least desire to eradicate, but would cherish,
still a person may be placed in a situation in which it is best to keep
this to himself. We have never in any letter we have written you given you the
least reason to believe we thought you criminal in regard to your politics, or
company, as your last letter intimates we have cautioned you with regard to
