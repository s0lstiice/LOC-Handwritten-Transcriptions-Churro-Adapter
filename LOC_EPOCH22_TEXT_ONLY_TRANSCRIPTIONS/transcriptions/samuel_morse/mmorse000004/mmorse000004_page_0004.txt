Charleston March 17, 1814,
My very Dear Son
It is very long since we have had any accounts from
you, which occasions much solicitude. We are anxious to know how
your healthy spirits are, and hope you succeed in your profession your
last letter was dated the 14 Sep. 1813 your success as to the reception
of your statue of Neptune was flattering & I hope your success
since we have announced your expectations. The little cast
you mention sending has not yet come to hand we wish much
to suit it but fear we shall not very soon for we are very desirous
of giving you this summer but do not wish it in such a way as
to have you injure yourself greatly in your profession to gratify
us, especially if by continuing a year longer you can get into
such business as will enable you to support your self afterwards
which I hope is an object with you, we wish much to see
our three sons in the situations to support them in comfortably
& as fast as should we need their help in our old age, and to
be able & willing to do good to their fellow creatures as opportunities
occur, you know perfectly our feeling on this subject and
that we would much rather see you all good & useful in every
situation into which your lot may be cast, than rich & great,
without a heart to do good & be useful to all your brother Common
has begun a letter to you but is prevented from finishing it just now
by a painter that you know he is a little subject to. I hope he will
be able to renew the letter before this goes. Richard is at Utica at
