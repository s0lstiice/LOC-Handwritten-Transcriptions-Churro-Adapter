to Dartmouth in time; An opportunity may soon
occur from Liverpool, and then it shall be sent; I wish
I had a good opportunity I should like to send you
a fine Stilton or Cheshire cheese. I know it would be
a luxury to you, but the times prevent and ten to one
you would never get it; this is also the season I am
afraid of sending books &c. &c. to you. I hope by
this time the receipt of my former letters will convince
you that I have not been so remiss in writing
you long letters, as you accuse me, in your last letter.
I have written repeatedly, and am really astonished that
so many should miscarry; I have lost the sum of the
number in my letters, for when I went to Bristol
I forgot to take my memo. of them, and so have lost
the regular number, this is somewhere about 38 and
I therefore will call this number 38 so as to get into
the numbers again; my next shall be 39, and so on.—
I fear I have forgotten to mention to all Friends, to Uncle and
Aunt Salisbury, Miss Russell, Mr. J. Brown, Mr. Evans,
Dr. Bartlett &c to the family in particular Brothers
Nancy Betty Caroline. De. De. De. De. De. De. De. De. De!!!
In some haste, I will write again soon.
Your very affectionate Son.
H B Mason
