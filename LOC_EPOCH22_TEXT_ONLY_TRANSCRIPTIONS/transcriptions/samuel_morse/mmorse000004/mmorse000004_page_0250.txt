and sorrow with which I read that part of
Mama's letter, I was so much hurt at it, that I read it to
Mr. Allston, and requested he would write to you, and
give you an account of my spending my time, he seemed
very much astonished when I read it him, and authorized
me to tell you from him that it was impossible for
any one to be more indefatigable in my studies than
I am! —
Mama mentions in her letter, that she hears
that Mr. Leslie supports his mother & sisters by his labors,
this is not the case; Leslie was supported by 3 or 4 individuals
in Phil: till within a few months past, about
a year ago he sold a large picture which he painted
(whilst I was on my fruitless trip to Bristol for money)
for a hundred guineas, since that he has had a number
of commissions in portraits and is barely able to
support himself, indeed he tells me this evening that
he has but 20 pounds left, he is very economical
and a most excellent young man, his expenses in
a year are on an average from 230 to 250 pounds,
Mr. Allston's (single) expenses not less than 300 Pounds,
and I know of no artist among all my acquaintance
whose expenses in a year are less than 200 pounds.
