My dear Son,
Capt Rogers of this town is to sail to-morrow
for Corwin, & thence be expectd to go by land to London
& kindly offers to take a panel for you. I prepared
in great haste. We have rec'd a few days ago your
letter of Decr. 22, just after your return from Boston.
Also yours of the 314th of Feb. announcing the
melancholy tidings of Mrs Alston's death. The
enclosed letter to Mrs McChanning was immediately
handed to the family whom I have since seen.
Everything proper has been done to soothe their
gloomy sorrow. They are most obliged to you for your
sympathies & kind attentions to Mr Alston & feel
toward you very affectionately. You will express
& Mr Alston our tender sympathies under his
afflictions. Your letter arrived was received
as I was going to the funeral of our friend
Mr Rockwood, who died of a rapid consumption
after about a month's confinement. His loss is
deeply felt & lamented. Mrs R. will go back
to her father's at Phila in a month or 6 weeks with
two of her 4 children the other two remain with
his father at Wilton. I do not now recollect any
other deaths among your acquaintance except of Mrs
Whitmore, who married you & your brother. The was buried
this week a good woman. We have just heard of the
death of Dr Ramsay who was shot by an insane man
a great loss to his family & to the public.
