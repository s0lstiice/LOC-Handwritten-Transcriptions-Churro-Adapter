Charlestown Nov 9th 1814
My Dear Sir
Your last letter that we have received was dated January
2nd and anxiously looking out for letters from you by every avenue
that we knew of in any of our seaports -- and next to hearing that you
had become a real Christian on his of an inheritance incomparably und
filed and that faith not away would be the pleasure experience
from hearing that you were industriously maintaining yourself by your
own earnings it appears to us that you have obtained so much celebrity
by what you have done in the way of your profession as that if you have
been and are prudent in hoping the friendship of Mr. West Mr. Alston and
others who appeared disposed duly to appreciate your talents that by
this time you must ought to be in a situation to do every thing for your
self that we could wish which would be a great relief to us at this
trying time, but your goodness and gentle father has seen proper to give to
less you a thousand Dollars for this year which I know not how or where
he will obtain it but he bid me be of good Cheer the Lord will provide
and our own one dear wife, he often says has conducted so well as
has been of trust will continue to be an honour to his family of
to his country and will most cheerfully support us when we are on
the decline of life if not able to support ourselves that we must exert
ourselves to the utmost to spirit just at this moment though it comes
very hard upon us at this time Edwards has gone to Kentucky to study
law he was invited or rather appointed a tutor in Yale College but
he did not except he took his second degree we have not heard
from him this six week, he is not asked in writing us frequently of
long letters as you or Richard are but his a great comfort to us of
I hope will be a great blessing to his country & in all his connections
