Charlestown June 12. 1814.
My dear Sir,
I have had the privilege of reading or hear-
ing read the letters which you have written to your
parents since your residence in England. They have
afforded me much pleasure, as they are written with
ease and elegance, contain important information, &
exhibit the character & prospects of one whom I highly
esteem: and in order to make some adequate returns,
I almost wish, that I had the exclusive right of
communicating intelligence to you from your
native country: a country which, with all its con-
fusion, political division, degradation, & misery, is
still dear to you. But your parents & brother have
a previous claim, and you will consider my letter
as valuable no farther, than as it is a testimony of
my affection & esteem. The news of the glorious events
which have recently taken place in Europe has
just reached us. We rejoice that, that man who aspired
at nothing less than universal dominion has been
humbled, & tied back by the way in which he came;
that the nations of Europe, after having suffered & bled
for more than twenty years, may at length hail
with transport the return of peace. We rejoice especi-
