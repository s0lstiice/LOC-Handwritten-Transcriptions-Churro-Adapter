Panorama of the Battle in two views. I send you a key to the defeated
Com. P. was first in the Lawrence & outsailing the rest of his
fleet was exposed to the whole fire of the British line, the vessel
was a perfect slaughter house, not one being unhurt except himself & a little brother. He then left it & went in
a row-boat to the Niagara with which he broke thro' the line & took the bold attitude exhibited in the second
scene, separated by the enemy from the rest of his fleet
encountering single handed their two principal vessels. In this position they struck to him, successively
followed by the rest. The Trippe of 1 Gun tho' at the end of the line sailing up had come first to his assistance
& encountering the Lady Prevost of 13 guns, that vessel struck to her! the Caledonia going to engage her
did not arrive in season. He distinguished himself on and, in this respect resembling Nelson, as also in his letters
containing the account of his victory. He is remarkably modest & shuns attention. He returned from the Lake
in a plain habit with no other badge but a button on his collar, on horseback. He rode in company with Uncle Sidney
about ten miles, who took him a Midshipman & converted with him desultorily as such. He was known first at Utica
& there a dinner was given him. To avoid the gaze of the crowd whom the news of his approach had drawn together in all
the towns along the river, he took a batteaux & sailed down the Mohawk to Schenectady accompanied by two or three
gentlemen & ladies. I saw him in Boston. He has a very engaging & interesting countenance. Let me stop here.
Your affectionate Brother R.E. Morse.
