My dear son
The letter enclosed from your good
Mother, and that from your brother Rich intends
to write you, will save me the labor of
writing you long letters by an app. 4 to direct
good, that we cannot omit it. I am glad
to be spared this labor at this time, as it is
the close of Election week which I have been
always with me a busy week has been un-
commonly crowded this season. I am very
tired. I have written by Mr Gallandett
a few days since I sent a small parcel
of papers, which I shall send you will give
you an account of our Spring Elections
of the news of the day. Of which we have
very little, that is interesting -- we wait for
important news from Europe --
We have just received a letter from your
Uncle James E.B. Finley of Carolina He fears
you will remain in Europe but hopes you
have "so much amor patriæ" as to return
& display your talents in raising the military
& naval glory of the nation by exhibiting
on canvas some of her late naval & land
actions & also promote the fine arts among
us -- He is, you know, an enthusiastic re-
publican & patriot & a warm approver of
the late war -- but an excellent man
I am by no means certain that it would not be
best for you to come home this fall, I find a few
or two in this country in painting some portraits,
