have other things to do, and think of, on their first
arrival; and it is generally a week or 10 days after
every one else has recieved their letters that you get
those sent by passengers, or otherwise sent by post from
the place where they arrive; so that on the score
of expence it is the same if not more, and the
delay is certainly vexatious; the expence of a letter
would be at most 1/2 sterling, now the beer which
I drink at dinner amounts to 4. dins. per day, so
that if you will only send the letters by the letter-box
I will, after the receipt of one, instead of drinking
beer, drink water for dinner for 3 days; for a letter
loses half its interest, when you know that all
your friends have received theirs by the same vessel
a week or ten days before you. I only mention
this subject, and you can do what you think will
be best?— Mr. Bromfield wrote me yesterday
that he had just recieved a line from you, enclosing
a bill for 105,, 13,, 6,, in this I have no doubt
will get me thro' the year very well; living here
is very expensive however; I am pursuing my studies
with increased enthusiasm, and hope before the
three years are out to relieve you from further expences.
Mr. Allston encourages me to think thus from
the rapid improvement he says I have made; you
may rest assured I shall use all my endeavors to
do it as soon as may be. I have kept a regular
account of my expences, and at the end of the year
