Dear Parents,
I have just received yours of the 12th inst. by Betsy
Allot & intend to send this by him with a no. of others to
my regret inance in Charleston & then South. I
had safe & sent the little trunk which you sent us some
time since, but was obliged to pay a monstrous price for
carriage & toll. We are very much obliged to Hannas
for sending us the cake & raisins, we have eaten them all.
I have given up the idea of having Major Hirst for a
sett has just been purchased for the Brothers' library & I
can have the use of that. I have not been to the President's
house yet to visit him, I will go soon; I don't wish to visit
much during the vacation as I have my hands full;
I am pursuing my Frenchy & Mr. Valued says, if Petition
I can nearly acquire the language in 3 Months
In the affair of my gun I don't think I made a very bad
bargain, I had used it considerably, it wanted many repairs,
the lock was out of order, & as I had not used the gun
for some time, it was getting no better by being kept in my room
The principal book I read was Young, Right Tho'k, the rest were plain
wh. I consider as trash & only took them that I might sell
them again.— The result of the Cooks trial is, one has been
dismissed, 2 remain on trial for poor behavior, the rest are
in their former standing— I have not got any trunk from
Williams yet, this I have asked him for it a no. of times—
We have had but one day in which there has been good
slighting; the winter has been uncommonly mild, the grass
is green in many places. This letter is rather short for a
private opportunity, but as I have so many to write I hope
you will excuse me. I think it is better to write all my
intelligence by this opportunity than to send them by mail. I am
Your affectionate Son S.G.B. More
