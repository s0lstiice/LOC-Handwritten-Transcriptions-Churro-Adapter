Consideration.—
Since writing the other letters I have
finished, a drawing wh ich I intended to offer at
the academy for admission, Mr. Allston told me
it would undoubtedly admit me, as it was better
than two thirds of those generally offered, but advised
me to draw another and remedy some defects in
handling the chalks (which I am not at all accus-
tomed,) and he says I shall enter with some
elat ion , I showed it to Mr. West he told me it
was an extraordinary production, that I had
talents, and only wanted knowledge of the art to
make a great painter; since giving him your
last letter to Dr. Waterhouse, he has been very
friendly and liberal to me, and says in any way
that he can benefit me he will do it with pleas-
ure, for the first, to economize, he told me a
way of preparing common paper to paint on
instead of canvas, which will be a great saving
of expence, for in my studies, I should have been
obliged to consume a great deal of canvas—
I feel perfectly content, am very comfortable
