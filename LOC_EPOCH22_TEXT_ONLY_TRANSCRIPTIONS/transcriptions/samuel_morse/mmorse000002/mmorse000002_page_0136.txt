New Haven March 31st 1811.
It is several weeks since I received your letter, my friend, accom-
panying the last volume of Scott, and though I have suffered them
to pass without bearing you my thanks, the only acknowledgements
I have it in my power to make, I cannot let you think I am
either ungrateful for these proofs of your friendship, or the flattering
regard you express for me. You did me justice when you said
"you are my friend, Mrs J." I am indeed, and no one who knows your
would be happier to prove himself such than she who has known and ad-
mired the delicacy of your conduct, and who has no need to take as painful
the friend in whom she has seen nothing to blame.
My sisters thank you for your remembrance of them, and
unite with mine their best wishes for your happiness.
We are just leaving this place for New York, but I could not depart
without expressing, my pleasure at a confidence, and my gratitude for a
kindness which I know not what I have done to deserve, except it be from an
appearance, which I am happy to repeal, that I am
your friend.
S. H. Jarvis.
