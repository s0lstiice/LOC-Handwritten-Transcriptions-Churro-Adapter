doubtless heard, are very annoying to me, as indeed
they are to all strangers, the noise of them is constantly in our ears from morning till midnight, &
with the exception of one or two they all appear to be
the cries of distress, I don't know how many times
I have run to the window expecting to see some
poor creature in the agonies of death, but sounds to my Surprise that it was
only an old woman crying "lardin apples," or some
thing of the kind; Hogarth's picture of the enraged
musician will give you an excellent idea of the
noise I hear every day under my windows

The English winter is now beginning to
set in, it is strange weather, at one moment the
sun is shining in a cloudless sky and the very next
a cloud passes over, so dark, that candles are almost
neccessary, it then rains, and the sun shines again &
this happen five or six times in the course of a day
there is a singular custom with respect to
knocking at the doors of houses here, which is strictly
adhered to, a servant belonging to the house brings the
bell only, at strange sent. knocks once, a market man
or woman knocks once & rings, the penny post knocks
