Wednesday Nov. 6. 1811
After leaving this letter so long, as you see by the
different dates, I again resume it; perhaps you
will be surprised when I tell you, that but
yesterday I heard that the Garden is still wind-
bound, it makes my letters which are on board
of her about 5 or 6 weeks old, beside the pros-
pect of along voyage; however, it is not her
fault; there are 3 or 4 hundred vessels in the
same predicament the wind has been such
that it has been impossible for any of them
to get under weigh; but I must confess I feel
considerably anxious on your account; I would
only request that you would always think the best,
when there is a long interval between hearing from
me, a thousand accidents may have prevented the
letters from reaching you; at such a distance too as
I am; you can't expect to hear from me so often as
if I was at New-haven; and if vessels arrive at
Boston, from England, and you have not letters by
them, I hope it will not make you anxious, even
if they came direct from London, for at such a dis-
tance -- I am from, hearing of vessels, that it is
almost by accident that I never hear of any, and it
would employ almost all my time to go into the
city every time I wished to know of vessels; if I have
business in the city, I generally wait till it accumu-
lates a little, and then take a day, to transact the whole
