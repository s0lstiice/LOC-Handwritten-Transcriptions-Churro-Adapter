in nature must be studied in the same minute
manner so you see that it is not so easy a thing
to be a painter, as some are apt to suppose;
I mention these things, that you may not raise your
expectations of my progress too high, for it must be slow
and sure; if within 8 or 10 years I can paint tolerably well
I shall think my time has been well employed; I am deter-
mined if application and study will make me a painter
to be one, no difficulty shall discourage me, the more
they increase, the greater is my desire to overcome them.
The most disagreeable part of the whole
is the necessity of my being so far from my friends; if
there were only a few more Miss Rupells in America,
American artists would not be obliged to leave their
own country; Tell Miss Russell she possesses an en-
graver of Leonardo da Vinci; that print cannot
be purchased here under 25 guineas, the painting
itself is considered as a masterpiece of painting, and this
print a masterpiece of engraving. But I must close
this letter; I am going to write my brothers soon: "Give my
love to all friends." I wish S.D.Ward would write me
he cant think how it would gratify me. I mentioned before
that I had received a letter from Mr. Vose of Charleston, I answered it a few days after I received it, remember me to him.
I have a great many things to tell you, and indeed too
many for a letter, so that I must wait untill I come home,
and then my visit will be made more interesting, as I shall
have so many things to tell you of
