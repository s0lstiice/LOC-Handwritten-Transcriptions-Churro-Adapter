I visited Mr. Copley a few days since, he is very old
and infirm, I think his age is upwards of 60
years, his powers of mind have almost entirely
left him, his paintings are miserable; it is really
a lamentable thing that a man should outlive his faculties,
he has been a first rate painter as you well know
I saw at his room, some exquisite pieces which he
painted 20 or 30 years ago; but his paintings of the last
4 or 5 years are very bad; he was very pleasant
ever, and agreeable in his manner. -- Mr. West, whom
I have visited a number of times, is still of I visit
now to then, he is very liberal to me, & gives me
every encouragement; he is a very friendly man, he
talked with me like a father, & wished
me to call & see him often, & be intimate intimate with him.
age instead of impairing his faculties seems rather
to have strengthened them, as his last great piece
testifies; he is soon coming out with another which
Mr. Allston thinks will far surpass even this last, the
subject is Christ before Pilate. -- I went last week
to Burlington House, in Piccadilly, about 4 o'clock, with
the residence I think of Lord Elgin, to see some of the
ruins of Athens, Lord Elgin has been at an immense
expence in transporting the great collection of splendid
original statues of Phidias
the celebrated ancient sculptor; they are very much
mutilated, however & impaired by time, still there
was enough remaining to show the inferiority of all
the moderns.
