My dear Son
Three days ago we received your acceptable
letter of the 30th June 7, your 14th. The two preceding ones by
Miss Lyman's, 2 Novr 13, we have not yet received. You do well
to number your letters, I to write often as we feel a
deep interest in every thing which concerns you, as well
as our other children. We have not numbered our
letters to you but shall in future -- we suppose we have
written you about as many as you have written
us -- We are pleased to find that your ardor for your
profession continues. A line from Mr Alston's brother
as to your proficiency would be very grateful to us.
We hope the change in your lodgment, & your connection
your self with Mr Leslie, will be for your advantage
but at this distance we cannot judge for you -- I trust you
confer with Mr & Mrs Alston, Mr Bromfield, & Mr Smith, in all your important
movements, & especially in the choice of your companions,
which is of infinite moment to you -- We are unacquainted with Mr Leslie, but presume
he will be helpful to you, not only in your professional pursuits, but in all virtuous counsels.
I in every thing which will tend to promote your best interests, your eternal welfare. We feel, my dear son,
be assured a very deep & tender concern for you
continually in regard to these things -- By regarding
our wishes in these things you will give us the most
comfort in neglecting them your will help to bring down
our body hardy in the honor of the brave.
