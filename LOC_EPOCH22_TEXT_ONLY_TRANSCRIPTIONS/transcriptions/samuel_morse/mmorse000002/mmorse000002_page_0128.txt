many a stubborn heart I had many a stubborn knee - I fear more
unhappiness you have her family in your tail - I fear she may not be able to
withstand every attack for new york stands with elegant & accomplished young
men -- you mention that you have again changed your mind as to the
bursary which you intend to pursue -- I really thought that the plan of becoming
a bursarite would be permanent because sanctioned by practical authority
but I am now convinced that your mind is so much bent upon
painting that you will do nothing else effectually -- it is indeed a noble
art & if pursued effectually leads to the highest eminence for painters rank
with poets & to be placed in the scale with Milton & Homer is an honor
that few of mortal men attain unless -- I really rejoice that you commence
your career with so fair a prospect Mr Alston is undoubtedly a strong artist
in his profession & to have him for a guide is a happy circumstance --
wild, the pencil now & then but generally in the humble walk of miniature
painting -- While in Newyork I was introduced to Wood I visited him at
his room he is indeed invincible. I never saw any thing in the painting line
that would bear the smallest comparison with his figures -- His coloring is more
exquisite & often a beautiful & his shades more delicately blended than the finest chinese paintings but the peculiar excellence of his figure is that energy &
soul animated from every point -- before I saw his painting I had the certainty to
