London for the robberies constantly committed
upon them; at Highgate we had a
vicissitude of London, I almost despair of giving
a description of it, imagine to yourself a
tract of country extending 9 or 10 miles in a
semicircle covered with buildings, whole forests
of spires & towers rising up in all directions
& in the midst of them conspicuous above
the rest, the noble dome of St. Pauls; if
you can conceive of this you will have some
thing like London; after passing thro' Islington
in the environs of the city, we at length entered
it, & after winding about thro' a labyrinth
of streets, we stopped at the York Hotel, near
Blackfriars Bridge & in full view of St. Pauls;
after resting from the fatigue of the journey
and arranging my affairs a little I went in
search of lodgings, in which I am at present
very pleasantly and comfortable settled, they
are in the West or Court end of the town at No 67.
