My Dear Parents,
As I understand there is a vesel up
for New York, and as I omit no good oppor-
tunity of writing you, I take advantage of a few
leisure moments to add another letter to the many
I have written you since my arrival; I have
written 10 letters to you since I have been in England;
this makes the eleventh: 1 from Liverpool
by the ship Aristomenes and another postal for the
District of Maine whose name I have forgotten; 2
from Liverpool by the ship Prince of Boston; 1
by the Lydia via Liverpool for New York; 2 by the Galena
dated Oct. 3rd 1 dated Nov. 3 sent by private opportu-
nity to Liverpool to go on board a ship for New York, and
1 I sent a few days since to young Mr David West
of Boston who was to sail in a day or two; I hope
at least some of them have reached you before
this, and that you have received information of
our safety; I have been anxiously expecting
letters from you for a long time, but have
not received any information from you since
the letter via Bristol; but I know how to make
allowances for accidents and delays, and only hope
in case of a long interval in hearing from me, you
will make the same allowance, and find a sign the
true cause of some accident or delay befailing the
letter, for I shall always write, in the space of 3
months, averaging 2 letters in three weeks; I assure
you I am extremely desirous of hearing that you
know of our safe arrival, and expect before you
