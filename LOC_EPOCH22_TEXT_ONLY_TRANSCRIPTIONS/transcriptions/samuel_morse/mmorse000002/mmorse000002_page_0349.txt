My Dear Friend,
Dr.
I received your letter of 4 Inst. same time
since, and have now a few moments to commence an
answer. I began to fear my letters had miscarried, as my
letters to my parents by that Line did not reach
them, but was glad to hear that you received yours
safe.
You mention that you are fearful your letters are
a source of great profit. You say in your letter that
you live a very sedentary life perhaps you will scan
by chance a number of books.
I can scarcely believe I have been in England almost
eight months, time flies very rapidly with me,
3 years will soon pass.
Mr. Palmer, has just sailed who leaves London
for France to-morrow in the cartel, he left with me
a list of books with prices annexed, with which he
requested him to obtain for you here, he told me he had
written you on the subject and that in the present
state of public affairs it was impossible to send
the one to you as soon as circumstances will admit,
I will endeavour to transact that little business
for you, and shall always be happy to render
you any service in that way which is in my power.
I have now been nearly eight months in England
during which time I have scarcely been out of doors, I
have been obliged to make the most of the daylight for
