Washington 3 Decr. 1841.
With the seal, after recognizing your will rem
and on the back of your letter and found your circular
-and yet you have done right - the mortification rests with us
- I trust you did not weigh your friends by the small measure
of the Missouri standard - now you ought to have sent the
man his money and let us alone until we could afford to
pay you better - I am surprised to learn the true cause
of your not going on with the picture - now for the first time
and I do think the mass of your friends have as little idea
of it or you would not have had the chance to get
off so easily - I do not know how it was that I missed paying
my small penny the third time, but when I called on Robinson
he said you had desired that he should receive no money
and I have waited anxiously to be told that you would have
it - and I am sure you have no friend who would have
sailed in this matter and the number might have been
readily increased - Can you not yet be tempted? - Let
me tell you in the sincerity of a true friend, and plead the
up and downs we have had together to excuse the freedom
with which I do it that there is too much in you to be
cowed up, and you must bring some of it out for our
sake and for your act - I will mind the grants grants that
swarm and buzz about and sometimes sting a little too - they
will do it - they are in the woods and must do their work
- let me continue - and keep the fever on the surface
