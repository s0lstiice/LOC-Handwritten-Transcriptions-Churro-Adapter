Philad. Augt 20th 1841
My Dear Sir
Your favour came to hand this
morning and I hasten to reply That it is a case
in which I think I ought to commit my brother as con
cerned with me, and will do so, as soon as I receive your
reply in regard to the case of conscience. As for myself
I can see no objection to giving you the power you
ask, for the object specified. Indeed I am ready to any
thing in my power to assist in advancing our mutual
interest in the Telegraph. It is too valuable, not to be
of some service to our country and pecuniary benefit to
the parties holding it. The last No of the Journal
of the Franklin Institute, contain the date of Wheatons
patent in advance of yours. How is that, will he not
receive the priority. I am perfectly willing to forgo patent
fee in the first trial of the Telegraph by any Co undertaking
it. – I was in N.Y. recently and called to see you, but
learned that you were not in the City. The Telegraph
could be shown to advantage in this city and I want
one very much.
I remain ever yours truly
Alfred Vail
To S.J.B.Morse
New York
98843
