unprecedented for the season, you remarked, that the only point
remaining to be adjusted, which presented any difficulty, was
the terms of admission of the members of the Association
to the Exhibition, of the shade my, & that you were confident, from the liberal & friendly disposition already evinced by your Council, this matter could be easily arranged.
Indeed, as we were willing to meet you on this subject,
not only on grounds of equitable reciprocity, but in the
most liberal spirit of compromise, I did not doubt our
negotiations would result in securing the objects we
had in view. I can now only regret that our recent
difficulties have prevented this happy issue.
Permit me to conclude this note, my Dear Sir, by
expressing the hope, that, as the Academy of Design & the
Metalls Association were both founded for one object, &
are both seeking one & the same grand result, & as the
members of each can have no personal interest to serve
that can come in competition with the public good,
they may both move on harmoniously in their respective spheres, & that their arduous labour may be
crowned by the happy event of making our City, as distinct
from the other cities of the United States, as it is already eminent for her commercial enterprise.
With high considerations of respect,
Your Obedt. Servt.
A. Heele
New York Decr. 12. 1840.
