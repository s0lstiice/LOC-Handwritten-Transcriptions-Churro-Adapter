Extract. NewYork Dec. 13. 1841.
My D: Sir.
I am endeavouring to do something with the Telegraph
at Washington &c. We all seem somewhat
supplied, and I most of all, being obliged
to superintend the setting up of a set of machinery complete,
and to make the greater part myself, and without a cent of
means -- I am now at work upon it, and feel to propose of be-
sturing upon it. If I should get it completed I may visit you with it
in Phila. sometime before the winter closes. Mr. Smith is without
means and cannot advance any thing. He suggests that if I could raise
200 or 300 dollars to enable me to visit Wash. with the new machines,
it would be well and he expects to my receiving the first receipt
from the Telegraph to repay the sum. If I give my time and
attention to the matter, is it not fair that I should receive
a compensation? All the burden now rests on my shoulders after
years of time devoted to the enterprise, and I am willing to offer as
I am able to bear my share, if the other proprietors will lend a
helping hand and give me facilities to act, and a reasonable
recompense for my services in case of success. -
Truly your friend, I am
Samt B. Morse
