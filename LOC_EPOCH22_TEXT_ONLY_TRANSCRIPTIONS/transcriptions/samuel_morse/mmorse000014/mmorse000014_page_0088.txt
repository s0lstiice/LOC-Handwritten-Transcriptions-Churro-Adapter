have returned from Europe, do not understand that I do not easily forget friends. I saw Miss
Benson yesterday, she gave me information respecting you & of
a nature which has given a new interest to you. Is it indeed
true that you have felt the utter hollowness of worldly pleasure
the phantom nature of the world's promises of happiness, have
found beauty to be true of happiness not in the variety
of the world, not in the friendships, or affections of earth, but in
the love of Him who is Love itself, who lives to bless and guide those who
unselfishly trust in him. If this is indeed so I shall be proud to see you, for if beauty, any natural
charm of character without religion are so lovely, what must
they be when mingled with the soul of beauty, the sun of love, riches, religion. A
woman never appears so lovely as when bending at the foot
of the cross, never so beautiful as when her countenance is lighted
up with the praises of her Savior. I have omitted to begin the letter to you lest you should mistake my silence, and think
me forgetful, when I was only consulting Providence.
