from you the true cause of my difficulty with the
battery.— Had I discovered the matter I should have
detained the Machine several days longer, but
finding I could not succeed, I determined to send it
to you presuming you needed some practice before
you sailed.— We have not yet received any of the
reports— I perceive that Mr Ruggles has made a
motion in the Senate in regard to establishing a system
of Telegraphs for the U.S.— This is a sort of feeling.
I forgot to tell you that I attempted to make a sort
of supplying inkstands for the pen, to stand over the
pen, but made such bad work with it. I gave it up
I should be glad if you would get of your brother
or elsewhere 4 or 5 lbs of old worn out type, for the
purposes of casting the type for your new port rules.
I am waiting anxiously for the appropriation
I may dive into the work.— I have tried my long
wound iron and with the end wound with wire and
do not find it to succeed well. I shall however
try it again soon.— Mr Bonnet is finishing off the
wood work. The boxes are almost finished so that
I think you may expect the whole on Saturday.
Will you be kind enough to say to the Dr that he
had better let the Machine in his room remain as
it is for the present.— I do not think it will be of
any advantage to have it brought out.— It is the
property of the Company.— George has been very
much indisposed for several days with a very pain
ful finger, and he had also a chill last night. He
together with Mary send their kind regards. Hoping
to hear from you soon I remain yours truly
PS. This is rather a long letter.
