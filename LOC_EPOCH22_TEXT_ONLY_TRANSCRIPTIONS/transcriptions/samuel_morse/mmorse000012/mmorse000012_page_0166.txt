Sidney E. Morse, Esq., Speedwell near Morristown N.J. Nov. 8. 37
My Dear Brother,
This will be handed you by Mr. Stephen
Vail the father of Mr. Vail who is engaged with me in the
Telegraph. He wishes to have some conversation with you on
the subject of Paper, as he has just become possessed of a
paper mill which is manufactured first rate paper. You
will of course know best whether it is desirable to make
a contract with him for a regular supply. Mr. Vail
is well known as a faithful man in his contracts and
entirely responsible.
You will perhaps be surprised to learn that I
came out here to be sick; I caught a severe cold the day
I left New York from the sudden change of temperature, and
was taken down with one of my bilious attacks, which under
other treatment and circumstances might have resulted seriously.
But through a kind providence, I have been thrown among
most attentive and kind and skillful friends, who have treated
me more like one of their own children than like a stranger.
Mr. Vail has been a perfect mother to me; our good Nancy
Thazard can alone compare with her. Through her nursing and
constant attention I am now able to leave my room and have
been down stairs to-day, and hope to be out in a few days. This
sickness well of course detain me a while longer than I intended,
