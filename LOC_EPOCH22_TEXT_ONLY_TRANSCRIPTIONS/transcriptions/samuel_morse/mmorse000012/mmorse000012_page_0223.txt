Professor Morse and W. Vail bring their electric-magnetic telegraph,
to the Institute on my invitation, to be exhibited to the Committee of Science
and the Arts. It was not my design to give judgment on it,
but merely to satisfy my own curiosity. W. Vail, however, acts a
defence of the invention in the usual way, and I will of course
appoint the former sub-committee on telegraphs, viz. Patterson, Park,
Irving, Walker, Rogers; and I will take leave to add to it, F. Hale
and Stanton.
Please call a meeting of this Committee for Wednesday evening
when the apparatus will be exhibited. Please also to call
a special meeting of the General Committee for the same time, even
timing that it is "to examine Professor Morse's electric-magnetic
telegraph."
I pray you to give every aid to W. Vail in making his
preparations and arrangements; and as the instrument is of great
interest, and brought on our invitation, I think that the expenses
of portage 1/2, within the building at least, should be borne
by the Institute.
Very truly yours
(signed) E. M. Patterson
W. Hamilton
