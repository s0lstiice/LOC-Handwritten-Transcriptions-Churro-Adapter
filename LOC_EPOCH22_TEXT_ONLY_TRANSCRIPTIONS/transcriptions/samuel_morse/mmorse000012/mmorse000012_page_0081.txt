Besides this piece of information,
which you ought to have had before this,
there is another subject on which I wish
to interest you, and which I have no doubt
will; that is, the destitute condition of poor
Stearns' widow. You may not know that her
sole dependence now is on the talent of her
daughter Jane; but I am well informed that
she has no other source to look to for maintenance.
And though the poor girl is indefatigable,
she avails of her industry, as you may
suppose, must be but a mere trifle in such
a place as Newport. It is true that the living
is cheaper there than in most places, but little
as they may require, yet when even that is wanting
they are no less exposed to suffering than if they
lived in a more populous and dearer city. And
this, I have been told by persons from Newport,
