New York June 13th 1836-
Dr Sir,
Well, our friend Horace has come, and he looks in fine health
and spirits. He goes to Washington today and must see his mother in
Boston before he can come to Cooperstown. I have told him that when-
ever he is ready I will go to the Hall with him.-
I send you by Mr. Pratt a small parcel of books. I am inclined
to think you will be pleased with Mrs Beecher's book, there is such a
fairness and truly liberal spirit (and unaffected) throughout, at the
same time there is such a masculine character of reasoning, freed from
masculine asperity of manner, as I think will commend itself to you.
I confess myself so far sectarian as she is, and no farther, her last
chapter contains my own sentiments in regard to other denominations per-
fectly.- In great haste but with real esteem
Your friend and serv.
Saml. F. B. Morse.
Please present my kind regards to Mrs. Cooper and the young ladies.)
