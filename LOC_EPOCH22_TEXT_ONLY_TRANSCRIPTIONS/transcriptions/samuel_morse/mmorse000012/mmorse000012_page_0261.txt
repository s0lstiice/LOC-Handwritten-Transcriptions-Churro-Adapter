Bardstown (Kentucky) April 7th 1838 —
Mr Samuel J. B. Morse, A.M.
I hope you will bear with me a moment, while I take the liberty to address you
for a few lines. I have seen by your publication of the
Confessions of a French Catholic priest, that the author is now
in New York. I myself am a Frenchman ordained Priest
by Bishop Foleyt of Bardstown Ky. in 1830. I have
acted in the capacity of a Priest, nearly five years, on the
two most conspicuous stations of the Diocese. But in consequence of an injustice done me by theadjutor Bishop,
I have ceased to officiate, and, in all probability, I will never
officiate again — and because I suppose to be bullied — into
what I conceived to be a flagrant wrong, they have attempted
to injure me in public opinion. Still in the camp, I have
lettered the gaulet, and taught them with their own means
I have already published from members in the Western Protestant, a religious paper of this place, in which, I have
confuted their charges with their own official Documents.
I have in my possession, letters of five Bishops,
and two archbishops of the United States, and several love letters of the men to their Priest, which go to confirm the
Statements made in the "Confessions". The Catholic clergy
