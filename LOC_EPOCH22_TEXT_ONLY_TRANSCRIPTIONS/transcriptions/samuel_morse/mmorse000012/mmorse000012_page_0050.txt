Cambridge, Jul. 18, 1896.
Dear Sir,
Your favor of Jul. 8, confirming a report, which I
had heard before, of Mr. Henniger's death, was received a few days
ago. The statement, contained in the Journal of Commerce, which you
had so kindly as sending to me, would certainly be curious and
interesting, if the matter communicated were real facts instead
of being, as I am inclined to think, a great measure of offspring
of poor Henniger's limited imagination. That you may not con-
sider this a mere assertion, I will point out some of the improbable lies,
anachronisms, exaggerations and erroneous statements.
First as to his name. Louis C. Henniger Hanning. If Hanning was
his family name, Henniger could never have been a part of his name,
as it is a custom altogether foreign in Germany to use a family name,
such as Henniger for example, as a Christian or middle name. He
may have substituted it for Hanning, but it certainly never was
a part of his own name.
The account says: "Young Hanning was at this time under arrest,
during the time of Napoleon's assassination and its immediate effects.
Sord, who was however never a student of Feudalism, assassinated
Napoleon in April 1819, now then seventeen years old. Henniger
was a boy of eight years. As to the effect which the deed of Sord pro-
duced upon especially upon the government of Baden, I am able to
express an opinion, in as much as I resided at Feudalism at Diff-
