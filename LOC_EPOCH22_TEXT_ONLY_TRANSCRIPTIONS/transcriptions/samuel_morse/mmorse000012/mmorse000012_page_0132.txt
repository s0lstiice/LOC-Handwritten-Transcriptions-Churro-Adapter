My Dear friend Catterson
Your welcome letter of the 21st I received on my return
from my journey to the Old Colony.
I have been about from Plymouth
about three weeks during which time I visited Boston, Plymouth,
and the extreme point of Cape Cod. I found myself in
all these places in the midst of important studies for my Historical picture, and I believe
I have made through search for the remains of the May-
flower and the furniture of the Pilgrims. I have been
thoroughly exhausted in all these places. I am
Thanks to you my dear Catterson for the concern you express in regard to my health. It has been perfectly good,
and I am now with the exception of a little anxiety in relation to the Telegraph and to my great pictorial undertaking, which
means the success of my fine oil work. The Telegraph in all
its essential parts is tested to my own satisfaction but the machinery
(all which I have been compelled to make myself) is imperfect, and
before it can be perfected I fear that other nations will take up
the hint and rob me both of the credit and the profit. There are indications of this in the foreign journals lately received. I have a de-
sign for a picture of the first voyage of the Pilgrims, and I have
the whole of the material for it in my possession. I have
also a design for a picture of the first voyage of the
English colonists in America, which I have in my possession.
