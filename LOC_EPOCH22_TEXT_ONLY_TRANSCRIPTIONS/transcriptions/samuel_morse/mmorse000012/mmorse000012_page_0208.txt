Philadelphia from Norfolk
February 3d 1838
My dear Morse: In your kind favor
come to hand in due course of mails
and I must beg pardon for delaying answer
so long Judge Dick has been from home
so much that I could not write
before. We have made up our minds to proceed
imperatively to get out the patent or rather secure
it in England and France and for that
reason I visit a copy of the cases and other
papers necessary for that purpose. I have written
at length to obtain and furnish them supposing
you to be so much engaged that you could
not well attend to it. If you can however
render him any assistance I will feel indebted
to you for it, the observer of that week gives us
a lift. I see and I hope the papers of the party
of Brotherly love will not be backwards,
your promising pupil has not been so industrious
as I could wish in her prophecies, having had
visitors ever since you left, she is to day without
any and is about to renew her studies.
The "Young Hawk" is well except a slight cold
the change in the weather produces it. I
am
Yours affecyly
your very affecyly
Samuel F. B. Morse
