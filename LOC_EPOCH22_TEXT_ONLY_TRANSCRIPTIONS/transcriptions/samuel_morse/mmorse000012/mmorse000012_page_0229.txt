I tried a straight magnet, but not satisfactorily to
myself, as the battery I used was poor, and the acid
weak, — I have been waiting to hear from you respecting
the type — that is, their exact proportion, — I cannot
do any thing until I know — I wish steel trasp to
make them of about the thickness of the little catet
on the port rule, which holds the lever up, — I would
thank you to get about 6 feet of it, of the ordinary
width, — also a cord for the weight — a little less than
the one we used for the old machine, and 6 feet in
length, — I have contrived a new port rule, which I
think must answer, and which will not require sticks
or rather but one stick, — It was brought forth after
much study and perplexity; the port rule is a difficult
affair — I shall make it to run by machinary; I have
had today three hands to work at the Telegraph, and shall
use every effort to get it done in time, — We have tried
the new printing machine, and find that it succeeds
in its imperfect state very well, — I think Mr Smith
better take a look at it, and he will no doubt desire
an interest in it, — I think that if he should see it he
will make up his mind to take out a patent for it
in Europe, — In case he should, I do not think that
it would in the least hinder the progress of the Telegraph.

machine will require no experimenting, — I would not
