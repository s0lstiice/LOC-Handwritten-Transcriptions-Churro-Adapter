Experiments on crossing rivers with the Telegraph without wires through, over or beneath the water. The first
experiment I made was on Dec. 16 1842 at Washington across the Canal 80 feet wide. Dr. Fiske was my assistant
and had charge of the magnet on one side while I was on the other with a galvanometer & the battery. The
result showed that electricity passed from one side to the other, my galvanometer indicated electricity when the circuit
was closed, and Dr. P. reported the magnet to be affected (which was doubtless incorrect,) the plates of copper
were each 1 foot square. Hon. Wm. Boardman of Penn. and a Penn. M.E. were present.
On August 19th 1844, an experiment was tried by running two lines of conductors parallel to each
other, 400 feet long and 175 feet apart, and at each extremity a plate of copper buried in the ground 16 by 13 inches.
The result was unsatisfactory owing doubletness to a defective Galvanometer.
