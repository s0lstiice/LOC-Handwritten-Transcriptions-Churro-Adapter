[Baltimore 22nd Aug 1844]
Prof. Morse
20th.
Thankful that I might not have expressed myself
with sufficient clearness while in conversation with you this afternoon,
having been somewhat embarrassed, I beg leave to repeat what I then
stated.
I suppose the earth to be a battery, having its concentrated force
of attraction at the magnetic pole; if this be so, the fluid will rush from
all parts of the surface towards that point; the reacting force of which
will produce a motion to the earth equal to its diurnal motion on it
axis: when the fluid becomes too abundant it will be discharged
at the magnetic pole, producing the "Northern Lights" which are
known to be most brilliant after a long period of dry and stormy weather
when the atmosphere is the least charged with electricity, hence acting
as a safety valve.
If this be so, the needle of the compass must be attracted towards that
point, and it will have precisely that "disp" which observation
proves it to possess. — Should the magnetic pole (about 11º from the
pole of the earth) be retratory, or have a certain motion round its own
centre, it would be "the regulating power", to keep the earth steady in
its motion. These are the prominent points in this theory, and I
have an sufficient to entitle it to investigation.
Very respectfully
Robert Leslie
P.S. I may add that your investigations as far as they have come to
my knowledge all tend to establish in my mind the truth of the theory
