Experiments on crossing rivers with the Telegraph without wires through, over or beneath the water. The first
experiment I made was on Decr 16, 1842, at Washington across the Canal 80 feet wide. Dr. Stocker was my assistant
and had charge of the magnet on one side while I was on the other with a galvanometer in the battery. The result
showed that electricity passed from one side to the other, which a galvanometer indicated electricity when
the circuit was closed, and Dr. F. reported the magnet to be affected, (which was doubtless incorrect) the
plates of copper were each one foot square. Hon. Wm. Boardman of Iowa and a Penn. M. C. were present.
On Aug. 19th 1844 an experiment was tried by running two lines of conductors parallel to each
other, 400 feet long and 7 1/2 feet apart and at each extremity a plate of copper buried in the ground 15 by 13 inches
each. The result was unsatisfactory owing probably to a defective galvanometer.
