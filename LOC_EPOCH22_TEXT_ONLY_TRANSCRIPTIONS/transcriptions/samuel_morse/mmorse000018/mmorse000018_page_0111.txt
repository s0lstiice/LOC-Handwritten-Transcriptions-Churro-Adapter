Baltimore July 1 1841
My dear Sir
I have had none of your esteem
for some time -- The Vail spoke of your
mjury sustained by a fall, from which we hope you
have recovered.
I have thought that I would like to learn if there
may not be some way that you would be willing for me to
dispose of yours (I offer of my well wishes) interest in the Times
office, say from him to a good -- perhaps I might be useful
to you -- in this respect, as well as to afford me an opportunity
of driving some business -- I should be far delighted in
company and as you remarked when I said you, that
you preferred what you had to be definite and fixed, and
that you would a perhaps give it a little thought that me
know. I am aware that things have formed a different
aspect from then -- but I should hope in the end as much
for your interest -- If you should be willing to give me such an
opportunity, my first object would be to have you come to our
seat as to your satisfaction and in the formation of a com
pany, to retain your counsel to advise by all means, in the execu
tion, so far as you might find opportunity & convenience, to
your usefulness & truly
David Dunbark
