to F. O. J. Smith, Esq. Wash. June 20th 1864
Dear Sir,
In the other letter I have copied a letter just received from Phila. What can we say in answer? I have expressed
that if we gave permission to construct the line for $10,000, or say $100,000 mile that sum to be secured and
paid to us six months after the work is commenced that this would be a simple and easy mode of arranging the
whole matter. Please give me your own views of what you think best. I think myself if we at once
fix the rate at 100 cents as our price for the construction of the line, we shall soon have orders from all parts of the Country, and our profits
will come in rapidly, and sure. If you think any of us should desire further to increase our own profits by contracting with any company or companies this might
be done without embarrassment to the rest. I think that $100 per mile might be substituted for $50 in forming a
company for the N.York & Boston line. I am still confined by my leg, but hope to be able to go out in a few days.
Respectfuly, J. Mose
By granting a privilege on a particular line we do not have ourselves from granting a privilege on another line between two points, so that unless we sell right in states (which we ought to avoid)
we can have a good profit from God at the same time that we accommodate private companies.
