I think not politic
at present, for the
following reasons:
1. It opens it to suspicion that
they cannot introduce the
bill without suspending the
judgment which may be respectively
given by the respective States
2. If the President should
fail of telling to either of
them whether they would
or would not consent to
the bill, the President
might be said, we have
not been consulted
3. Should there be done
in this matter, I have
been told by Professors
and through Intelligence
Congress might say
you have not given us
a fair chance. You
act
with us to the extent
you give us no time to
act or plan.
Propositions drafted by
M Smith for Congress.
Bath June 14. 1844
