Baltimore July 4th 1841,
Dear Prof. Morse
I returned to Baltimore
on Tuesday evening at 1/2 part 12 at night by
the steamboat with my family all well. I have
received both your letter, and am now getting
ready for experiments and will send you a
note tomorrow morning — I am sorry you
are still confined — I hope you will soon get
out, and find your leg entirely well. I regret
the hesitancy of the Depart. in regard to further
appropriation. If you wish any assistance
to induce Mr. Young to yield to your request —
there are persons here to whom he owns his
situation who will take the trouble to go to Washington
on that business. Let me know if you
wish any help — I shall not embarrass you in
any arrangement you may please to make for
constructing the Ele Mag. Yet from Phil to N.Y.
You have already made me promises which I
wish to consider as equivalents at least for the present.
I know I have no disposition to throw
any thing in the way of our success with the Yet for
the present I hope to see you soon and we will
have it all right — In haste Your obt. Servt
Alfred Bail.
