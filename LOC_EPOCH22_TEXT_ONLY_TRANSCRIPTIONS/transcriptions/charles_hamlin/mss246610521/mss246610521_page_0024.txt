There followed a general discussion of the atti-
tude of the postal authorities, as reflected in a letter
from the Third Assistant Postmaster General, addressed to
the Federal Reserve Board, dated February 14, 1935; and
while it was conceded that the use of the ordinary mail
for transmitting valuables of any considerable amount, if
it became a general practice, might result in substantial
losses, it was felt that the high surcharges exacted by the
postal authorities on registered mail, justified to some
extent, the use of the ordinary mails for transmitting val-
uables of this nature, where the amount involved was rela-
tively small. While this matter, in the opinion of the
Governors, does not necessarily require uniformity of ac-
tion on the part of the Federal Reserve banks, it was nev-
ertheless
VOTED that this Conference approves the princi-
ple that money, securities, and other valuables should be
adequately safeguarded by the use of registered mail for
transmittal, and that each Federal Reserve bank should co-
operate with the postal authorities to discourage the use
of ordinary mail for the transmittal of such valuables.
