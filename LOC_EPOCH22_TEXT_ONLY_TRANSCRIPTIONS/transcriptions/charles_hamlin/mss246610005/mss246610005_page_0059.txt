Jan 8. Thursday
Begin Newton, Clerk of Court of U. J called.
talked with him over an hour. He spoke
of his talk w. Mettler but did not say
what Mettler said - I gathered that
M. had rather put him off without
disclosing his opinion. He said Company
was moving Raven & Castle to get their
renter for Leffingwell; that he had
talked w. Corcoran who did not deny
that it was fit on the place but thought
Leffingwell was the man for U. J. now
what he said I gathered that Corcoran
only grudgingly admitted my opinion.
Newton seemed very haggard - I told him
what Williams had said and he
said he ought to write the President.
I think Newton rather than that I
may be adopted. He did not seem to
understand why Elam rested on no
money - that he had heard a rumor
that he wanted to hold on until the
sensational movements in Washington
had passed away
I formally told U. in absolute confidence
117
that I had good reason for believing that I
was in the running & that at one time it
was agreed I should have it, but that
of course, things might have changed
since.
He said if Wilson has ever made up his
mind to abandon you, it will take a
considerable force to change him.
He said he should see Kennedy at the
time of Naval Comm. tomorrow and ask
him how matters stood.
He also chose of an inc. Maylin feeling at
having Leffingwell jumbled over his
head on acting see; that he went to
see Elam about it who said Leffingwell
& said it was not a personal question.
I told U. if I went see, I should advise
President to have official rotator in town
keeping w. Maylin, otherwise I would do nothing
while Leff. stayed with. I believed would
only be for a short time.
