We dined with Mrs Sec. and Mrs Bruck at
Wom. Democratic club. We sat at Mrs Bruck's
table at which were seated Sec & Mrs Bruck,
Mrs Wilson, Mrs Holtsett, Sec & Mrs Burwell
and Sec & Mrs Richard Cleveland.
Mr Cleveland delivered a cheering address
on China; he spoke clearly and quickly
and made a fine impression.
I sat next to Mrs Wilson, she was very
polite and courteous but I fancied
she had something in her mind against
me growing out of the Pennsylvania
debate, when her demeanor and cheeks
were all she devote tends without
a word, she spoke in rather subdued
to a question of mine about the
judgment against Margaret Lincoln
and said it was a humorous adventure

boldly -- she several times refused
to eat on "Charles" but I felt she was
