He said he thought it better not to protest
as he wanted nothing in the record which
could even seem to embarrass him in the
future "when election events might take
place." (meaning I presume, the removal of
some member or members).
He was very bitter -- said it was a most
deliberate assault & an entirely unmerited
affront upon the President, which could not
be forgotten.
We also look up Elan letter sending us
Smith's will giving power to the board to buy
corn, notes in open markets,
Wachung bitterly attacked this as did also
Harding. Delano also opposed it. I said
I was for it & would so vote. Finally after
long discussion it was referred to Cornes
in law. I shall speak for a cheery
wharf.

Wednesday May 7.
attained minutes as to vote to pay Williams salary,
at Willis originally drew this the vote said that
whether the Court, have put in a clause for salary
etc. I told Willis vote should read, whether Court
has raised question of effective date of salary the
Willis corrected this and I explained the clause
to the Board. It was read twice and discussed
unanimously.
Later on Mr Willis came in with a statement
of the vote in writing restating the Court that
whether the Court had put in a clause for
salary etc. I told him this were not the
vote as passed. He said Delano as Chair of
Court in Expends had told him vote must
read as above before pay could be made,
and that he would have to bring it up
before Bd at next meeting.
We look up this Ann the question of assistant
of Mr. Res AY at Richmond. Harding at
last meeting moved appointment of Hardy of
Norfolk at salary of $1,000.00 and Delano
seconded it.
Diller moved to take whole question united
Delano & Harding returned from west; said
he was not satisfied that Hardy was
the best man, especially in view of
the rumor of a telephonic letter between
him & Wood Harding which had been
