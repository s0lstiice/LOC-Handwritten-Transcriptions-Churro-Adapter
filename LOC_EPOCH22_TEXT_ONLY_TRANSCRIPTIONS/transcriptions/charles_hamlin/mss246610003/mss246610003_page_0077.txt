Buter & I divided w. British Ambassador to meet
Sir Paul & Lady Harley. Sir Paul informed
British East in loan credit negotiations & is
staying in N.Y. running over to Washington only
for a day returning to N.Y. tomorrow Sunday
at 3 o'clock.
Present. Ant. McPherson Peters, Mr & Mrs Scott (State Dept.)
Mr Brack, Mr Scott (Embassy).
After dinner had very pleasant talks, all together
on to general topics but nothing as to the
business in which Sir Philip Cogges was.
Returning to Ladies Sir Paul asked me many
questions as to the Loan Act and I explained
it and said I would send him some of
my sketches explaining the text of the Act.
Sir Cecil at once said - why can't he drop
around to see you tomorrow morning. I,
of course, said I shall be glad to see him
and said I will be out from whole
morning - let him telephones me any time
he can come.

U.S. Sunday.
At 9.30 But. Embassy telephoned to ask if I
could see Sir Cecil & Sir Paul at 10 o'clock; I
sent back message that I should be glad
to. Later, I thought matter over, and felt
that to have the British Ambassador and
Sir Paul call might lend an appearance
of formality which neither of us intended
and, if matter still ever come up as to
whether we had officially had
anything to do with English banking credits,
it might cause some comment if it were
known that the Ambassador & Sir Paul
had called upon me, even though nothing
ever said about the credit matter
either formally or informally. I felt also
that it would be impossible for me to
later telephone without referring in some
way to these negotiations.
I accordingly telephoned the Embassy
sending a message that such seeing
I would be glad to see them, an important
matter had come up, which would
not, my seeing the Secretary of Treasury and
as this would take some time I
respectfully would have to answer to
postponing their call, but would
explain their fully when next I saw
them.
