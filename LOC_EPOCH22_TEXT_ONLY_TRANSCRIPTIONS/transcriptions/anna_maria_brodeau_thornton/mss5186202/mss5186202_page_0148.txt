Monday 7th. We were so obliged as to
put in at St. John's - where we found them
could not give us any thing but nothing which
with our hands I brought up breakfasted on -
as found such poor prospects here, that we
determined to go on to the settlements 8 miles
better than they did our richer - They think'd
of the road, which we soon lost, and rode
from about eleven till 4 in the afternoon to go
three miles. This word where there were only
fall paths, the carriage carried troops with
the trunks of trees, or walking a great part
of the way thru the burning sun, sometimes in
red hives - we at last came to one they
also told us one wrecked it thither and they
after a most distressing day in every respect
we reached the desired place - which we found
no better landly than St. John's - their log houses
made and one kitchen, the other occupied by the
family in the style of the mansion. - We took
30 men chosen to get rid of the hives which we
had obliged to go before the women and the daughters
and their three masters in two dirty looking beds
to rest - after a while we were summoned to dinner
in the kitchen which was in the women's room
