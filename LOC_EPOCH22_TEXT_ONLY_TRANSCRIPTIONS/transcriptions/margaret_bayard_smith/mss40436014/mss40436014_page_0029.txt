The memory of joys that are past.
There is a tear of sweet relief—
A tear of rupture and of grief;
The feeling heart alone can know
What oft emotions bid it flow.
It is when memory charms the mind,
With under images refuted;
It is when the magic spells restore
Departed friend and joys no more.
There is an hour—a provident hour,
And still how dear to nothing power!
It is when twilight spreads its veil
And dies along the silent vale;
It is when the fading Sleeps close,
When all is silence and repose;
Then memory watches and loves to mourn
The days that never can return.
There is a strain a pleasant strain,
The source of joy and yet of pain,
It is the song whose dying measure
Your friend beloved has heard with pleasure
Some friend who sin again may hear
The welling cry to memory dear,
Oh! take the magic spells restore
Sciences of Sleepful days no more.
St. Nancy, May 1858
