My Mother
My mother's voice, thro' often swept
Of its cadence on my lonely house!
Like healing sunbeams wings of blessed,
Or dew With the uncorrupted flowers.
I might forget her melting prayer,
While pleasant pictures rested there:
But in the still unbroken air,
The gentle breeze come darting by,
And years of sun and moonlight flee,
And leave me at my mother's knee.
The look of nature, and the print
Of beauty on the whispering sea,
Give still to me some sentiment
Of what I have been taught to be.
My heart is harder, and perhaps
My manliness has drunk too deep,
And there's a silence in the lapse
Of a few miserable years
That mother's book is never yet,
With all my mother's firmness.
January 1829.
Twenty eight and Twenty nine
Will not change, my mother's!
I heard a sick man's dying sigh,
And an infant's idle laughter;
The old year went with mourning by,
The new came dancing after;
Let them hide her lovely tear,
Let them clothe her tablet
With lengths of grass for the bee,
Filling wings on the candle!
With its wait on the funeral date!
Take to him the wine!
And a requiem for twenty-eight,
And a health for twenty-nine.
Mist for human happiness,
Blasphemy for human sorrow,
Our yesterday is nothing now,
What to-morrow will be one tomorrow?
