and do it over again. The stones are
from half an inch to three quarters in
depth, & in breadth, of all sizes, from
an eighth to half an inch in thickness.
After the picture is finished, & the
surface of the stones ground down to a
level & perfectly polished, the white
cement is carefully scraped out of the
intervals to a little depth. A variety of
painter's stones, in fine powder, are then
cast mixed with a small portion of
melted wax, & put on a palette. With
these, by means of a hot pointed iron,
like a furnace's lighting iron, the artist
melts a little of the cement wax to model
the stones, turns it from the front of
his work into all the crevices & then scuppers
off the superfluous wax, & cleans the surface
with spirits of turpentine.
Edmunds September 20th 1831.
Evening Song of the Kentucky Present.
(By Mrs. Edmunds)
Come be the sunset tree!
The day is past and gone;
The afternoon's are deep blue,
and the sunset work is done.
The twilight fire is thrown,
And the luminescent fountain,
And rest to me is given
By the cool soft evening breeze.
Level as the shore I rest!
