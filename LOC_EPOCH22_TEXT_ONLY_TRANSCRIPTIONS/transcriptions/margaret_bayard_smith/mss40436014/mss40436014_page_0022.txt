The Contest.
With gallant horse the warrior sprung
On his saddle and he would not stop,
But the hope of his returning danger rang,
As to battle he rushed away.
He rode most fast on the wanderer's power,
And the fellow's knee over the plain,
And the wounded though many a bout won
At the head of his troop again.
They came when the morning was old and pale,
With a warrior on his knee,
And his banner rent like a latter in nails,
And he died not the death of fear.
They brought him in piteous and sorrow's back
To the home he had left so gay,
When he gallantly by flew on glory's track,
Ours is battle rushed away!
They can still no more go down; neither shall they move
In their sleep for the land shall be with the sun everlasting light.
New twilight gray her ancient spires,
The orb of day has sunk to rest;
But together fable under shades,
Declining towards the evening.
And now most midnight's dusky wing
Veils nature's face in deeper gloom;
That it is the terminus of the day,
And all is silent as the tomb.
Now from her never-form the sunny
Point glimmering beams of borrowed light,
But order still the shade extends
Till earth is wrapped in sable night.
Thus like the sunflood more must set,
Thus like the moon his days must warn;
Even, soon his wingward wandering feet
Must humble head night's decayinger.
