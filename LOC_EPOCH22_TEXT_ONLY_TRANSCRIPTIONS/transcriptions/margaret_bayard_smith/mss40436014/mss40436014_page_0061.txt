Present the sounds low, deep,
and the clamour of the woods,
and the staff shaken on the
thorn burden and the heat
of labour's task are over;
and hands once free
The trouble one it has done.
Home to the Sunset Tree!
The day is past and gone
The sentiment are despoiled
And the reason work is done.
Grateful is the sound
That dwells in the whispering tangle;
Welcome the fresh leaf sounds,
And the gate that opens on doors.
And as, sweet and still
Then our joyful feet go,
Far longing thither that place
In the world beyond the grave.
There shall no tempter be,
By smoking wood side stand;
There shall be no more men,
By weary wandering feet.
But we will lift our trusting eyes,
From the hills, our fathers' bed,
To the vault of the skies,
To the shelter of our God!
Libby September 1869
Song - "In the Land of the Stranger".
In the land of the stranger, if ever there be a man,
The day following will go by unmarked,
Thanks the day, & the hour from here to our home at home,
Or the day of the stranger be breathed;
It will come the time we shall a home on the night
Or the distance of the day may surround here!
