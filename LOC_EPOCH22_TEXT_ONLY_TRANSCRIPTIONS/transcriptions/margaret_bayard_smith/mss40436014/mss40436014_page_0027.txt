The Rainbow at Purning.
'Twas evening, once the setting sun
Boke from the cloudy West,
Athwart the misty sky it shone
And gladdened all the East;
There, on the clouds which dense and low
Stung up the humid sky,
A beautiful majestic bow
Of nebeys met the eye.
The thunder seals were harn'd no more
The lightnings ceased to glow,
The winds had rushed its assistant rear
Along the vale below—
Nature, just bursting from her chain
Of winter, fresh & fair,
Set on her robes of liveliest green,
And fragrant was the air.
So may this life of toil and storm,
At evening passed me by:
So may my sun break out, anasform
To rainbow in my sky.—
May gratitude and love and praise,
Thin fragrancy impart,
To cheer the errings of my days
And fill my haptur'd heart.
And when life's latest beams recede
From earth and time away,
May glory, bless and blest endure,
In bright suffulgency:
Then, as the colors of your rocks,
Are blended in all in one;
May I with Christ's triumphant breath
Compose an everlasting arch
To rainbow round his throne.
