I received your letter yesterday, I answer it im
mediately, in hopes it will reach you before 2 o'clock
to 13- for I am anxious the business may be adjusted as
soon as possible - If I only were concurred, I would
patiently wait as much longer as my friends desired.
But every letter I receive from the family of my
dear & unhappy friend Jublet, makes me more anxious
to dispense of my mess - Since Mrs death, my
interecourse has been regular & frequent. Jublet has never
been able to unite herself, but I hear almost weekly
from Mary or Anna & I have procured the place
of private governess in the family of my friend Mrs Burnside
for Mary. She pretends to teach nothing but French English
& needle work - for that, she is to receive 100 dollars a
year & be exempt from any expense her board, washing &c &c.
I hope to procure an equally eligible place for Anna -
on the same terms - Their ignorance of French drawing,
music &c &c makes it difficult to procure any places of
parfaititude & I am I feel anxious lest they should be found
unequal to the tasks they have undertaken - Jublet's health
is untatched - her nervous system sadly disordered - yet she has
opened her school & has a few scholars - She is to accompany Mary
here & I expect her next week - Her daughter have persuaded her
to this, in hopes it would restore & strengthen her mind & spirit. Her
chief meansip is for John - she speaks highly of his talents & from
what I can learn he has never had any instruction, but lessons from
his mother & sister - If he could have two or even one years solid
& regular instruction, perhaps he could obtain a place out West
Point - if not. That of midshipman - or clerk in one of the hulls of
ships - but education - is to give a absolutely necessary provision, for
success in any thing else - Do them my dear Maria have it concluded immediately
& when she comes, let me have some comfort to give her.
