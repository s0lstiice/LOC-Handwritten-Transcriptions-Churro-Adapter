And so, after all, my poor MSS. was condemned for
its original faults, & they found the Doctor, had never seen
it, in its present corrected & improved state -- all my
later labour & my own, expended on it, were lost -- as to him
if I had not known it as I since before told you,
not only attainted, corrected & enlarged, all that our dear
editor's kind advice & judicious criticism suggested, but wrote
two new chapters, without placing it in Byron's hands, (for after his death, I felt as it would be impossible
to publish it) added some passages & adopted some alterations
supplied by sister -- & I own, I was both surprised &
disappointed after all this revision, that you & the Doctor
should yet condemn it. -- I find no fault with his not
reading the MSS. with such receptations, cares & anxieties
as he has had of late, for in them it is neither a proof
of indifference or untruthiness that he should not read these
MSS. -- which knowing what his situation, I should never have
sent him, had he not in several sacrifice letters, expressed
my complaints & insisted on my sending it -- But I do blame
him, for giving an opinion, which, without reading it, when he
knew its publication, was to depend on his opinion -- Mr
Samuel Bliss, White, offered to publish any thing he would
recommend, as well as he did the work -- Whether
these gentlemen will publish it, without his unanswerable
recommendation I know not -- but am determined to try --
for that purpose I enclose a letter, which I will have
Mr Boyd to give them as far as he conveniently can,
circulars & the business with them -- I do not like the idea
of having it printed in Province & selling it to Booksellers --
Smith says Booksellers interest themselves in the sale of
books they publish themselves -- I wish to run no risk -- I may
get more -- but I might likewise lose. In parting with
the Copy except of the first edition what I do gain is certain --
