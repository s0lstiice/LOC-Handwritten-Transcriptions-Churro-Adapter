These alterations are very trying -- I hope you will not

all very thoughtful to say quite well -- Mrs Blount is
complaining -- who is happy?
I have written a longer letter, than I thought I
should have been able to write -- for I feel as if
none person all over -- The loss of sleep two nights, has
quite exhausted me. After I came home this morning, I
sent myself to nothing -- at last your idea came, & I
sent myself to talk to you -- I am alone in the
house -- every one, even the dear baby have gone further to
enjoy the bright sunshine of the front of May -- I am about
with sad and thoughts -- continue to love me dear sir
as I love you --
Very truly yours
Brannock
A.J.
June 21st 1838.
Although it is past ten o'clock at night,
I will fulfill the expectation I formed when I
wrote this morning, of not allowing another day
to pass without my dear sister, answering your
letter. Various reflections, or rather interruptions
prevented my using my pen, though that with
my father has been kinder to me all day; and
this evening when I had taken my seat, with
a need to heart over-flowing, some company came
in & obliged me to lay down my pen -- I believe
the only way for me to do, is to answer your
letter the very day I receive it -- The pleasure
they afford, acts for a stimulus on my languid
almost torpid mind -- alas my dear sister, I am
very much in need of something -- a kind of confidence
to hope & every object in life, paralogues around a body.
It is in vain I rest on a foundation of truth --
this government -- that I try to create within my
self an interest in life -- an energy sufficient
for the discharge of my common duties & every
day duties -- I can not do this, but I can do little
else -- our little boy has become too much of a
little boy for my quiet mourning -- that he is much
too much with me -- than before he could speak a letter
about as he now does -- after playing the whole
day in my chamber I had a violent thought -- I
try to leave them though I know my family
are very weary, to do so with a cheerful voice &
with courage & this day to welcome the first
friend who continue to visit us -- Among these are your
father let me say, our dear Bayard is ever the most
reliance, & the greatest of loves to society, & to
