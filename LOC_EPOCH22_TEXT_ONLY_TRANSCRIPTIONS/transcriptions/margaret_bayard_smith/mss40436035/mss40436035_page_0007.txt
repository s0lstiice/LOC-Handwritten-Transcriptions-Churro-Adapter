Julia bids me tell you, she would have been delighted
to have had you there on the occasion, but as the
indisputable reasons precede her making such a request,
she feels happy in having a representative of her
family to whom she is so much attached, in her
cousin Bayard.
She is perfectly contented with the happiness
of being the wife of a poor - doggyman, in fact
happier, she says, than if marrying a gay and wealthy
rich man. The father-in - law seems now much
gratified to be connected, they are both very fond
of Julia, who in truth has been a favorite in the
old gentlemen's for three long years past -- whenever
he wanted us either alone or in the country (which he often did)
he used to get Julia to play a nice game for him
by the hour, while he sat by the instrument & accom-
-panied her -- she is to take her fiancée with her
to his house to pay homage to himself most pleasantly
from this source. The circle of society into which
she will be transplanted, will be far more congenial
& far easier to her now fashionable one -- much
use as it will be of solemn-minded religious folks --
Mr Hard, bearer at the Gaudelots, will be her intimatemost
companion & long has been an intimatemate friend -- Mr
Bondish is to her groom -- man & Mr Noble will officiate
several other clergyman will be present, as well as our
friend, Dr Seval & Mrs Browne -- Snygles, Wood are in
the only gay family, as they call them in central Kentucky to
religious Gadsdills will be our friends, the Bonsfields & Mr Thorton --
The next morning, the new made pair, will quietly resign
from Gaudelots, without any friends of racing company --
Shake's acquaintance, that with my increased doubtfulness
of any future event -- I write at this rate reluctant
to have no correspondence on a day -- But continually feel
such a strong conviction of the uncertainty of life & all human
schemes, that I scarcely expect this plan will be realized.
I felt as if something would occur to defeat it & I have
no doubt on that account feel an unwillingness to
communicate it to any one -- Think of me & from for
ever depart -- you will not wonder that I feel more
sadness than joy -- do not ask whether I feel so in similar
occasions -- But I will endeavour to shatter all these
expectations of spirits & hope all things that I wish --
Julia is so well -- so happy, why should I not be so? --
unless you were with her, you can scarcely conceive
the beneficial effects these new relations, objections &
prospects have had -- Is this many be imagined -- her
being so well while I was away & ever since --
the attachment commenced in the autumn --
she was so well, but suffering exceedingly from this
incident also -- this morning the thermometer was 2 below
zero -- It has risen a few degrees -- 50 on -- 5 must.
Mr G -- has just come in & is thanking his hosts for
provisions & clothing to me whilst I am writing -- I am
in the other room trying on the dress -- with the
exception of this white one, all the others are plain
& they should be -- I hardly know what I want -- I am
so interrupted -- Genereux dear writes a dear niece -- I
wish you were here with me -- at any rate think
of me & pray for me, when ever Wednesday evening you offer
up your family prayers -- I wonder if any single article
will be able to -- But -- no, I must have no
butts -- I will marry -- I will marry in great Consequence in which
direct Aggression all things -- yours most lovingly
W H S
