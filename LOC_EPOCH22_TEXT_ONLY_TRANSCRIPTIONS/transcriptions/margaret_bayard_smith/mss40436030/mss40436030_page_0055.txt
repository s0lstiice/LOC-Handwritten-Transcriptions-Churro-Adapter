To Mr Smith.
December 10th 1817.
A variety of little matters, in a different
way has so occupied my time, since I received your letter my
dear Harpster, that I have not been able to devote that
quiet hour to you which is essential to the execution of
philosophy converse. By this time I suppose you are quite
blind in the city -- and have completed your winter prepara-
tions. I hope you will pass an agreeable season -- but so
gay I presume as the Lord's Short -- will hardly
be in time be in time for general society -- but you may
easily compensate to her -- any loss sustained in that way --
indeed from what you say, if her present views retirement
will be the most acceptable. A heart that is occupied with
a new affection touched by grief -- and awakened to religion can
not find enjoyment in gay errands. This has been an interesting season to me -- I sincerely hope it may continue in her
lasting happiness. It will be delightful to you to have settled
near you -- and see her united to an amiable and deserving
young man. Her entering the family just at this moment
must endear her greatly to the parents -- She will fill up
the place in their hearts and be adopted as a daughter.
-- It would gratify Mary exceedingly to be with you again --
but at present -- I can not say when, nor how -- Robert's
Bayard returns in safety, and makes a prosperous voyage he
may complete his much wished for excursion to Washington &
take Mary with him. But this is all uncertain. As to
Gov. Ro. he says it is impossible for him to leave home
for another he has so much to do in his office.
