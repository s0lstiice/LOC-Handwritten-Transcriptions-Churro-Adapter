in such an affair merely from expediency. I never have calculated on
her marrying. It is a thing I refer to a higher power. If it is the will of God
mine I trust in his goodness it will be ordered so as to promote her happiness
and mine. If not as long as she has a father house I fondly hope
she will want no comfort but continue to be the joy of her family.
I have written to you with perfect confidence and unreserved feeling
assured that no eye but your own will see it. Receive a thousand
tender acknowledgements for your goodness to my children and all I
ask is to write to me as often as you can all that interests you or them
I beg you to tell me how your health is. I feel very silent
since I heard whether you find yourself better for your journey.
I hope Mrs S. is also relieved of the indisposition which you spoke
of some time ago. Now do not neglect to answer this when you
write again. You never think of replying to my interrogatories.
Adieu my dear Margaret with best wishes for your
health and happiness and that of all your dear family believe me
truly and affectionately your sister & friend.
Brother John is below.
J K.
Washington
Wm Harrison Smith
