It is not because I am in want of leisure, that I
do not write often to you my sister & my other friends
but, as tho' my time is not occupied it is not my own.
I cannot command that silence & solitude which enables
us to write with pleasure. In winter, when
in the whole family assembled in the same room,
a family, young, gay & light as sun, conversation
seldom knows any cessation, & one has not resolution
to interrupt its course & take up the hour.
I generally remain after breakfast in our little
dining room until near one, -- but that portion
of the morning is wholly devoted to my dear
little ones, who now take two rather three hours
in their lessons. They need, spell & cipher, &
conversation about what we read, long explanation
after the words they spell, & take up as much
time as he actual lesson -- this is the best diet
of my life -- & I forbid it often much
longer than necessary. I have a silver medal,
wheels in his desired round the neck of he one
who wins it by six successive perfect lessons,
& the emulation is as great as I ever saw it in
a school, accompanied with he slightest ill will -- it
is impossible for children to love such other more
than Anna Maria & Bayard. Bayard needs new
with considerable difficulty & adds a pretty long sum
correctly & rapidly. I have no pleasure equal to
instructing them, & on their parts, they seem equally to
do their school house -- From one to near four we all
go together & when not interrupted by visitors, sometimes
read aloud -- & this is not often, for amidst winter
station time & it is with difficulty we have read Madoc --
a most interesting poem.
