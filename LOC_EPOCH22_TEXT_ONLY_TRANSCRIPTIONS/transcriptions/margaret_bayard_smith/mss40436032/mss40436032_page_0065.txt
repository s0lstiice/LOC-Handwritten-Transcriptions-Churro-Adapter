Sidney. Oct. 20 1828
For the last two weeks I have been more
than usually occupied -- bored with the trifles
of this trifling life -- But little thing are
great in little men -- No, it is not so, I am
sure that it were -- for almost among the least
of this little race, I cannot perceive any great
interest in the objects of human pursuit; I so constantly
feel their littleness, that they excite
life interest than I would desire to feel.
My mind turns to generalize its ideas, to expand itself
through creation -- to contemplate the Deity as
the God of universal nature, rather than dwell
on his special providence to trace his power
in the details of human nature or human
life -- In the general survey, there is an
order, completeness & perfection, which fills
the soul with such a firm conviction of his
wisdom & his goodness, that no room is left
for doubt or for anxiety -- But when
with microscopic view, we examine
man & life -- the few mysteries & dark
are the ways of Providence! To see the young
ste nations & useful matches from life, & the
precious & idle left, to burden on themselves &
families a pest to society -- to see the young
of spring, blasted by an untimely frost -- to see
the labour of the husbandman & the bounty
of nature destroyed by drought or storm --
To see pestilence ravaging whole districts & un-
disturbingly laying low the young and -- the good
& bad -- To see all animal nature, sustaining
life by the destruction of life -- impelled by
an instinct given them by their creator -- &
there are mysteries, that cloud the mind in
darkness & fill it with doubt -- So painful
have I ever been found this state of mind to
be, & so instinctively so I shrink from pain, that
I have habitually shut such reflections, &
turn my thoughts to those expanded & general
views of Providence, in which
