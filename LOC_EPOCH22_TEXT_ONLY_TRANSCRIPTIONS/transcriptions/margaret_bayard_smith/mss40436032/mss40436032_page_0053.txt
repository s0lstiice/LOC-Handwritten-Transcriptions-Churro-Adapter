Until I had written to you -- my thoughts
have been most quiet, and I have not written to you -- I take my seat by you, talk with
you -- read to you -- write you cutting out
& arranging your patchwork -- I ask with
great pleasure to be quit of the idea
that while making it, you are thinking
of me, with impart a painful, but to me
a real value to the work, of which this
way it would be suitable -- remember
to put some matter of value in some
peculiar shape, the pieces of the quilt or your
designs -- when it is finished I receive
it I will use it on my own bed & think
of you -- Thank you many a time for their little
remembrances -- we want to do something
practical or experience in nature -- but at present
are not gifted enough -- Anna has to be most
of her time with Mrs. Shaw and the
children -- a Bayard -- Susan -- housekeeper
& my mind is entirely leaves for fancy work
& fashion has no turn for fancy work --
I know not how it is, the days slip by
& very little is done -- as for me, I cannot
do any thing but read & talk & would give
behind it I cannot even take much -- it
fatigues me & increases the internal soreness, from
which I suffer a good deal & my voice after a little
resting gets loose -- how if I should lose
my voice & even my feet -- & what
if you should lose those who say I have to talk, better
than to hide any thing else --
It is a sad thing however, at this season to
be confined to your chamber as you are -- the
reason of Florence -- I am truly glad of what you
tell me, that you do not suffer pain from the
swelling of your limb -- Maria tells me that when
it first came on he pain was very acute & that
you must have a great sufferer -- I am thankful that
you are relieved from this -- you have such various
responsibilities upon yourself, that you suffer less than
most folks -- your duties, your needs, your home --
about all your own mind -- worry about the
state of fortune that remains of life -- above the little
spot on which you dwell & expectably freely
in the unencumbered fields of fortune -- Oh, when I think
of the life that is to come -- how indifferent am
of the life that is to transpire to leave an anxious
feeling -- and if the memories of our lives, were to be
nothing but suffering, yet melancholy & despair
have few years to elude, they would be much
a more point that we think they can be
patiently endured -- there are not words of
content & satisfaction on an individual feeling --
have always felt a want of content in life & indeed
and especially in men of affairs --
with them generally to feel & complain & gnaw at
the remembrance of an unaltered imagination --
unfulfilled wishes -- I am writing you a most kind letter
in all respects -- I wonder if you will be
able to decipher it -- at any rate you will
not have more difficulty in reading, than I
have in writing it -- I have not attempted to
writen peace my last to you & shall now forget how
I said my love -- Maria is much better than
I expected to find her, but is far from strong -- I do
say that her journey has already greatly improved
her health -- she is in excellent spirits & I hope
will receive permanent benefit from this excursion
of the weather allows me to make ride 10 or 12
miles every day -- I wish my dear sister could
come on & take her place when she leaves me --
