Every day since my return from Lexington have I
desired to write to you my dear sister, but to do so, I should
feel an unutterable memory, when I could however sit alone
in the retirement of my own chamber -- but no such memory
has yet come -- domestic occupations, the presence of my
family or the visits of neighbours & friends have prevented
my indulgence of these respective 2 feelings, if my heart
desired to indulge in them writing to you. I am in
their service of whose tender sympathy, esteem or
sorrow, I feel so deprived of. The constant presence of
others, drawn from the mind away its own favourite & peculiar
objects & sacrifices to kindness, then remains in calm solitude,
peace. There is a tender sorrow, that it is a luxury to
indulge in -- but still, at the same time, it might be
sacrificed, when it interferes with our duty -- to
part of them -- It has been from the earliest days
that I have insisted my declaration to refuse from
my own chamber & their yield to sweet & sad
recollections -- The bitter wish of an anguish of affection
& sorrow -- in what I have never seriously thought
the nature of my constitution, probably never shall become
the late events, induces me to believe my heart is
measurable of their kind of sensibility, otherwise, the loss
of a friend or even dear to me, would have excited it --
But it has not -- kindness and self-sacrifice, loyalty, tranquil
sleep, a undisturbed slumber to the heart, from misfortune or joy,
is the only feeling excited by this event. Some times
the analogy has forcibly struck me, between my own
disposition & a mirror -- it reflects foreign objects in its
truths, but remains no permanent eminence in them, pass
by away, & other objects succeed them, and an entirely different
figure, the place of them which but the moment before
they were imperceptible --
