A Letter from Joel Barlow the year 1777.
Dear Sir,
I esteem each of your two
letters as a testimony of a friendship that
that is entirely disinterested. You can
expect nothing resulting from the converse
sentence but the satisfaction of doing you
without receiving a return; and from your
human indulgence I quite with as much
freedom as tho' it were in my power to
make the advantage mutual. I shall
myself the line of my letters and look
upon every Mentor, only to give directions.
If I were entirely at liberty to follow
my inclinations, the choice of all other
that would attract my principal attention would be Policy and Divinity. Whether I should ever enter upon the latter as
a profession is a matter of doubt and
either unprofitable, the study of it only
would be agreeable to me, the ministry
is too important for my abilities and
attended with too little prospect of success. It is not better even mentioned in some
other way. Those a regard to duty will
direct me to it. I have but little prospect
of much proficiency either in poetry
or divinity but determin to attend to the
former considerably while I am at
college, and among other aerial castles,
I am thinking of a subject for a poem
that shall be serious and important,
as I think I should best succeed in the
serious strain and would endeavour to
subserve the cause of Religion as well
a Literature. Some of those you mention have often struck my fancy, in
particular that of Daniel; his would
involve the great actions of Cyrus.
