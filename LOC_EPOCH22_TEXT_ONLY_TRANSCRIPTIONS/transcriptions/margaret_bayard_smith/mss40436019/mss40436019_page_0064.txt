I have ever loved, and will hold highly in esteem, and have
felt strongly, the principles of Bentham's philosophy. This was a system of
pure and holy self-interest, and I have much to complain of in
many parts -- innocent men of my age have been wronged,
without all that improvement which their felings should
naturally have produced, & have been deprived of many of
their rights, & their liberties which I desired to be made
use of as examples of what I thought and still think
terribly unjust and cruel. I have felt that I should be
good if I had done so -- indeed there was a strong
desire to hasten my own death, and I was all the
more anxious about the fate of my family. I told
my father and mother that I would not live -- I would
rather be taken by an executioner than live -- I would
not be put to death by a jury -- I would rather die
than be denied the benefit of my habeas corpus -- I
was dying -- I would not live to be found -- I would
