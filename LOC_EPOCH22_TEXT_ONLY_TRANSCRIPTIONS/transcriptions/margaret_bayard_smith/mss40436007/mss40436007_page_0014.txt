Thing, like a gem, on the brow of the sky;
Trampling its despots in the power of her might,
And turning the clouds, as they pass her, to light!
Look to the waters!— asleep on their breast,
Seems not the ship like an island of rest?
Bright and alone on the shadowy main
Like a heart— cherished home on some desolate plain.
Who— as she smiles in the silvery light,
Spreading her wings on the bosom of night,
More on the deep, as the moon in the sky.
Or phantom of beauty— could deem, with sighs,
That so lovely a thing is the mansion of sin,
And souls that are omitted lie bursting within?
Who— as he watches her silently gliding
Remembers that wave after wave is devoting
Bodies that scream and guilt could not sever,
Hearts that are parted and broken for ever?
Or deems that he watches, affront on the wave
The death bed of hope, or the young spirit's grave?
Like a rebel at sea, amid sunshine and song!
27
Gaily we glide, in the grace of the world,
With steamers afloat and with canvas unfurled;
All glad of, and gay, to wandering eyes,
Yet chartered by sorrow, and freighted with sighs—
Fading and false is the aspect it wears,
As the miles we put on part to our own loss;
And the withering thoughts which the world cannot know
Like heart— broken spirits, lie burning below;
Whilst the vessel drives on to that desolate shore
Where the dreams of our childhood are vanquished and dies!
