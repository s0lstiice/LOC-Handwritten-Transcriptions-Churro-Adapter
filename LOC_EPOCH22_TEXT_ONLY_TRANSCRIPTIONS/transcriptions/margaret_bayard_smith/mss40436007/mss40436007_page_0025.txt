To a Friend on his Birth day— by M.H.S.—
In vain I seek a wreath to find
Of various hues, and sweets combined,
To entwine around this humble lay
An offering for your natal day.
The bloom of spring, the summer's glow
The beautiful flowers can now bestow
This only rose does yet remain
Of God's lately smiling train!
The Spring is gone, the summer flies,
To light with joy some other skies,
For us my friend there yet remains
The harvest of an autumnal plains,
But autumn too, shall pass away
Its boasted riches shall decay
And winter spreads its chilling reign
On the once gay enamelled plain.—
Will be it so— we'll not suppose
Since better joys are yours and mine;
