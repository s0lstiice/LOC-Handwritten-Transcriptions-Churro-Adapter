The boasted endowments of speech &
reason are not inherent in man,
but acquired from intercourse with
his fellow men. "We have instances
(says Rattel) of persons, who, having
grown up to manhood among the bears
of the forest, enjoyed not the use of
speech or of reason, but were, like the
brute beasts possessed only of sen-
sitive faculties." Society, thus im-
portant, and arising from the necessity
of mankind for mutual assistance,
could never exist without gratitude
which implies an interchange of
favour. To judge of the import-
ance of gratitude in Society,
we need only imagine to ourselves
the wretched condition of a com-
munity where no man either re-
membered or returned the
the obligations he had received.
Gratitude creates obligations
in proportion to the benefits received.
Most love, most veneration, most
attention is due where we have received
most favours. Thus our duty to God com-
prises as being our first & greatest
benefactor. Then comes our duty to
our Country, our Parents, our Friends,
our Relations, our fellow citizens,
& last of all the whole human fam-
ily. Man is a being so constituted
that he seldom looks to second causes
