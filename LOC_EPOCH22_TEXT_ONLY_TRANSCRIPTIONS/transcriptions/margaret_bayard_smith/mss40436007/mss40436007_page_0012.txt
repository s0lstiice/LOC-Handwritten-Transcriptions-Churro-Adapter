than his love of the Duty, which

love, either that Country, or that
parent, or that friend, or that man
who has done little for our good
& much for our injury. And
in proportion to the goodness of the
government under which we live
to the parental attentions we
have received, to the love of our
brother or our friend, so will be
the ardour of our patriotism, &
the strength of our filial & fraternal
affection, & friendship. For these
affections are, as we have said,
founded in gratitude & gratitude
increased with the increase of
the favours confended.
I have but entered upon my
subject. But it is an old saying that
we never know our weaknesses till
experiment discovers them nor was
I aware of my incompetency to write
an essay on gratitude suited to
a Ladies portfolio until I had attempted
it. I shall cast that dry remark
upon a title topic with the observation of Cicero that "There is no Duty
more necessary than gratitude."
