On finding a second Bunch of Flowers more
beautiful than the first. placed in my expect-
ment at noon.
I too heard it said, in olden times,
That Spright's were seen to ramble
at twilight hour by pale moon-shine -
When groves the thorny Bramble.
But woman like, my Spright will come,
When mid-day suns are shining;
She thinks her Wreath so fault at noon,
May fade when Sol's declining.
Thou Lady fair this moral takes
While many a Bud you gather,
O splend at noon while in its Bloom
The Body that lives forever.
S.B.S.
On a third Bunch of Flowers - found at
evening.
Some witching spell -
An wood nymphs tell.
When Orpheus charmed the hours,
My Lyre has sprung -
The all unstirring -
Many nations' choicest Flowers.
Twain pity Sue,
Their Buds so pure,
To bend, or bristle or break -
For round the spot where in they grow,
A sweet perfume at eve they show
And sum-bring fancy wake.
Then take the Lyre,
Poetic fire
He now in strings may mend -
Perchance to listen to its Sound
My Siliph may rise from out the ground,
In shape - and form of Friend
S.B.S.
Lichfield June 1826
