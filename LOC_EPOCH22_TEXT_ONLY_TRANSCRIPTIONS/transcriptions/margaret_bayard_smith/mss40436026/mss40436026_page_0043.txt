August 15. 1802. Willow Cottage.
Here my dearest sister, seated beneath one of the finest trees in
Willow I have ever seen with orchards, woods & meadows extending
bide around me, do I take up my pen to act after years
concerned to give you some account of my own. Maria's
last letter informed me one from you, of which I have been
in daily expectahin. Since you last heard from me, I have
experienced the most trying anxiety. My little Julia has been
very ill. During the few days when she was at the worst,
in how did I wish for you! I thought that your presence would
have helped the danger, almost banished my fear, it would
at least have rallied my heart & enabled me to undergo
any trial with which I might have been afflicted.
She made every exertion to get lodging in the country &
at last the house kindly offered as the use of 2 rooms
on his farm; which I most gladly accepted. While my
little one was ill, all my friends were most kindly attend-
ing. Mrs. Tupper & Mrs. Thornton called every day to see me, Mrs.
Stoddard, Robinson, Robinson & others all who expressed an interest
in my situation; but all this was not equal to the right
of my dearest sister Jane. When any thing ails my child, I think
respectfully of you, I wish continually for you, because you would
feel for me as a daughter & I should confide in your care
& sympathy as in a mother. The only society from which
my heart derived any gratification was that of the St German
& Hudson Picher. Stewart died to give the tendency, the
sympathy expressed by their conduct & their means.
