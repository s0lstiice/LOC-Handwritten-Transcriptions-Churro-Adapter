Mrs Bell, the lady with whom she lives & Miss Bell called. Mr
Bell is a very wealthy & respectable man, & what is uncommon
here, they are professors of religion. They sat more than
an hour with me, asked us to their house, & offer'd us seats in
their pew. Told me Mr Balch, their clergyman, knew my family
& intended calling on me. I did not expect a single person to
call on me. Mr Smith's vessel has at last arrived at Alex-
andria. Last night & all this morning there has been a most
violent storm of wind & rain, but I trust our little property
will arrive safely. The carpenter work is almost finished &
two rooms are plastered. In two or three weeks more, I trust
I shall write to you from our own house. Let me end, as I began
my letter, by entreat ing you to write to us. Tell me a great
deal about my dear children & tell Mary Ann to begin her letter
a month before Mr S sets off, so that it may be a very long one.
My most affectionate regards to all my dear friends, -they are
oftener thought of than ever, and do not forget to remember me
to Betsy & Sally. Tell Betsy that if John comes here he will
soon make a fortune. If you go a hundred yrds, each one pays
a qr of dollar a seat for going & returning. U And this winter
no one will be able to walk. On reading these last lines to
Mr S he thinks I am wrong in promising a fortune, for on inquiry
he has found the backs did not make much. I will let her know
more on this subject. Adieu, -my paper is too full already.
