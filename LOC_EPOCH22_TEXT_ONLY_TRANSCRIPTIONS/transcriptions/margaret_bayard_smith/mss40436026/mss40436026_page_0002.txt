Washington, Sunday, Oct. 19,1800.
To Mrs Kirkpatrick,
New Brunswick, N.J.
I can scarcely believe that a whole week has passed
since I wrote you my last letter. x x x
On Wednesday evening last, we were just seated at our tea-table,
when the door was opened & Brother S & Sister entered. I felt
most truly rejoiced to see them. They passed the next day with
us, & left this on Friday morning. They spent the day in look-
ing about them, & will give you a truer idea of our situation,
than I have been able to in my letters. Mr, & Mrs, Huges left
this on Wednesday; the two preceding days, Mrs H. was well
enough to dine & sup below stairs, & these interviews made me
regret not having visited her in her room. She is a woman,
plain both in manners & appearance, very social & talkative, &
is what you would call a good kind of a woman. To me she was
extremely friendly, & one evening after tea came in my room &
sat more than an hour. She is to stay at George-town this win-
ter, & I have promised to visit her. I not only got over my
prejudice against Mr H, but before he left us, used to feel con-
siderable pleasure in talking & listening to him. He is very
communicative & seems to like talking as well as he does eating,
which is not a little. He is a moderate federalist, & he & Mr
Smith talked by the hour, of political affairs & political men.
As for Mr Myers, who is still here, he seems to be out of humour,
& since Mr S, has been unable to converse, he every day gets up
from the table, turns his back to the company & either ruminates
over his own cares, or pores over a book. Cap. Memmenger, a
Benjamin Huger, Representative from South Carolina
