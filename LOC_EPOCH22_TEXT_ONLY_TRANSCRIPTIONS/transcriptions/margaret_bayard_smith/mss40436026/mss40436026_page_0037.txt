of all the seasons of the year our spring I think the
most tedious & disagreeable, winter is still struggling to
keep its government, -- a few warm days which really
studded my heart, are succeeded by rain & frost again,
& while the chimney is crowned with Conquish, daffodils,
sweet-suckets run a winter fine blaze on the hearth.
It is with us a remarkably backward season,
& excepting the aspen, not a fruit tree in bloom.
The girls are with their usual enthusiasm
seeking for wild flowers; if I did not remember
the delight which I used to take in scrambling up
the steep banks of the River in search of flowers,
I should wonder at their raptures. But even now
I cannot collect without a throbbing pleasure, the
wild columbines, that grew in the crags of Rose
Island with what labour a resource gathered them.
I feel much disappointed I must confess in not
seeing Maryanis society this summer, for
you said, I so certainly counted on it, that
indulged myself in many pleasing plans -- but
sure your reasons must be good & for her sake
I would wish her visit deferred, as another summer
will find us much more agreeably fixed. Our garden
is in a most unpleasant & chaotic state & we
have as yet no prospect of putting up the magasins.
The want of labourers has disapponted all our projects.
I have not my dear Maria seen Mr Quinney -- Mr Gold
& Mr Bleeker have been here & spent the evening; from what
they say I do not think congress will rise until July -- I spoke
to Mr Gold about taking charge of the books for you; which he
said he would do with pleasure. Mr Bleeker made particular
inquiries, where you would pass your summer in July. 66993 I suppose my presence
