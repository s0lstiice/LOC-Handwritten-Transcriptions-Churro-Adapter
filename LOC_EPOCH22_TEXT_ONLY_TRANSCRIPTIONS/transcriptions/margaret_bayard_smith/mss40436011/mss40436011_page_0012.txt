When we reckon the cir-
-cumstances that connect us to
our nature, such as that of
safety, shelter, food, & the other
means of preservation & enjoy-
-ment, we sometimes think
we have found an sensible
& solid foundation for felicity;
but those who are least dis-
-posed to moralize, above
that happiness is not con-
-nected with fortune, although
fortune includes at once
all the means of subsistence & of
sensual indulgence. The circumstances
which require abstinence, courage,
& conduct, expose us to hazard &
are in description of the painful
kind; yet the able, the brave,
& the ardent, seem most to enjoy
themselves when placed in the
midst of difficulties & obliged to
employ the powers they possess.
How many are there, to whom
war itself is pastime, who choose
the life of a soldier, exposed to
danger & continual fatigues; of
a marine tumult & in conflict
with every hardship & benefit of
every convenience. Of a politician
whose sport is the conduct of parties
& factions & who, rather than be
idle, will do the business of men
& of nations for whom he has
not the smallest regard. Such
