Most of the sorts of Chrysanthemum succeeded admirable,
and continue in beauty for
a long period, trained against a
south wall; the late-flowering
sorts grow more vigorously and
flower better in this situation
than in pots under glass. Whether
against a wall or in pots, reducing
the number of flower buds, as recom-
mended by Mr Joseph Wells, enlarges
the size of the flowers. In training the
shorts to a wall, combine to have the
branches of different lengths, so that
the flowers which only grow at their
ends may not all appear at the
extremities of the plant.
The best sorts for an open border
are the peple, changeble
white, buff and rose.
